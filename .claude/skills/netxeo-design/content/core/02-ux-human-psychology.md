# 🧠 02. UX & Human Psychology (The 50 Laws)

This module outlines the 50 psychological heuristics, cognitive biases, and behavioral laws that govern human interaction with digital interfaces.

## A. Gestalt Principles (Visual Perception)
1. **Law of Proximity:** Objects near each other are perceived as a group.
2. **Law of Similarity:** Elements that look similar are perceived to have the same function.
3. **Law of Continuity:** The human eye follows lines, curves, or a sequence of shapes.
4. **Law of Closure:** The brain fills in missing parts of a design to create a whole (useful for minimal icons).
5. **Figure-Ground:** People instinctively separate objects (figure) from their background (ground) via contrast.
6. **Law of Common Region:** Elements enclosed within a boundary are seen as related (Cards in UI).
7. **Law of Uniform Connectedness:** Elements connected by lines are seen as more related than those sharing only proximity.
8. **Law of Symmetry:** Symmetrical elements are perceived as belonging together regardless of distance.
9. **Law of Past Experience:** Elements are interpreted according to the observer's past experience (e.g., a magnifying glass means search).
10. **Law of Prägnanz:** Complex images are reduced to their simplest possible shape.

## B. Cognitive Biases & Heuristics
11. **Anchoring Bias:** Users rely heavily on the first piece of information offered (e.g., original price vs discounted price).
12. **Confirmation Bias:** Users favor information that confirms their existing beliefs.
13. **Framing Effect:** Users draw different conclusions depending on how data is presented ("20% Fat" vs "80% Fat-Free").
14. **Endowment Effect:** Users value a product more highly once they feel a sense of ownership over it (e.g., custom profiles).
15. **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** Users continue a behavior if they've already invested time/money in it (e.g., long onboarding).
16. **Halo Effect:** If a user likes one aspect of a site (e.g., beautiful design), they will assume the rest is equally good.
17. **Decoy Effect:** Adding a third, less attractive pricing option forces users toward the most profitable option.
18. **Bandwagon Effect:** Users adopt behaviors or buy things because others are doing it (Social Proof).
19. **Mere Exposure Effect:** Users develop a preference for things merely because they are familiar with them.
20. **IKEA Effect:** Users place high value on products they partially created themselves (e.g., setup wizards).

## C. Memory, Time, & Decision Making
21. **Hick's Law:** The time to make a decision increases with the number and complexity of choices.
22. **Miller's Law:** The average person can only keep 7 (±2) items in their working memory. Chunk information.
23. **Zeigarnik Effect:** People remember uncompleted tasks better than completed ones (Progress bars create tension).
24. **Ovsiankina Effect:** The tendency to pick up an interrupted action again when it has still not been achieved.
25. **Peak-End Rule:** Experiences are judged based on the peak (most intense point) and the end, not the average.
26. **Serial Position Effect:** Users best remember the first and last items in a series.
27. **Recency Effect:** Items encountered last are remembered best.
28. **Primacy Effect:** Items encountered first are remembered best.
29. **Von Restorff Effect (Isolation):** When multiple similar objects are present, the one that differs is most remembered.
30. **Bizarreness Effect:** Bizarre or highly unusual imagery is remembered better than common imagery.

## D. Interaction, Effort & Physics
31. **Fitts's Law:** The time required to hit a target depends on the distance to it and its size. Make buttons big.
32. **Steering Law:** It takes longer to navigate a cursor through a narrow tunnel (like a nested dropdown menu) than a wide one.
33. **Doherty Threshold:** Productivity soars when a system responds in < 400ms.
34. **Tesler's Law (Conservation of Complexity):** You cannot reduce complexity completely; you can only shift it from the user to the system.
35. **Parkinson's Law:** A task will inflate to fill the available time. Design tasks to be faster than expected.
36. **Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule):** 80% of consequences come from 20% of causes (Focus UI on the core 20% of features).
37. **Goal-Gradient Effect:** Motivation increases as a user gets closer to a goal. Provide artificial early progress.
38. **Hawthorne Effect:** Users change their behavior when they know they are being observed.
39. **Labor Illusion:** Users value a service more if they can visually see the system "working hard" (e.g., a complex loading screen for flight searches).
40. **Weber-Fechner Law:** The noticeable difference between two stimuli is proportional to the magnitude of the stimuli.

## E. Emotion, Perception, & Motivation
41. **Aesthetic-Usability Effect:** Aesthetically pleasing design is perceived as more usable and forgives minor UX flaws.
42. **False Consensus Effect:** Designers assume users think and behave exactly like they do. (They don't).
43. **Dunning-Kruger Effect:** Novice users overestimate their ability to navigate complex interfaces.
44. **Cognitive Dissonance:** The mental discomfort of holding conflicting beliefs; avoid tricking users with dark patterns.
45. **Scarcity Principle:** Items are perceived as more valuable when they are limited in availability ("Only 2 left!").
46. **Authority Principle:** Users trust information endorsed by experts or authoritative figures (Trust badges).
47. **Loss Aversion:** The pain of losing something is psychologically twice as powerful as the pleasure of gaining it.
48. **Spacing Effect:** Learning is greater when studying is spread out over time rather than crammed.
49. **Forgetting Curve (Ebbinghaus):** Memory retention drops drastically over time unless information is repeated.
50. **Attentional Bias:** User perception is affected by their recurring thoughts at the time. (Contextual UI rules).
