DKA - my ward notes (pasted)

def: hyperglycaemia + ketonaemia/ketonuria + metabolic acidosis. usually T1DM, can happen in T2DM under stress. SGLT2 inhibitors -> euglycaemic DKA (glucose may be <250 mg/dL, still treat as DKA)

pathophys: insulin deficiency + high counter-regulatory hormones (glucagon, cortisol, catecholamines, GH) -> lipolysis -> free fatty acids -> liver makes ketone bodies (beta-hydroxybutyrate > acetoacetate). high anion gap metabolic acidosis. osmotic diuresis -> big water + Na + K loss

precipitants: infection (commonest), missed insulin, new onset T1DM, MI, pancreatitis, drugs (steroids, SGLT2i)

clinical: polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, abdominal pain (esp children), Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath, dehydration, drowsiness -> coma

diagnosis (ADA): glucose >250 mg/dL, pH <7.30, HCO3 <18, anion gap >10-12, ketones positive
- severity by pH/HCO3/mental status. severe = pH <7.00, HCO3 <10, stupor/coma
- serum K may be normal or high at presentation even though total body K is depleted (acidosis + insulin lack shift K out of cells)
- Na falsely low from hyperglycaemia -> corrected Na = measured Na + 1.6 x (glucose - 100)/100

management order: FLUIDS first, then K check, then insulin
1. fluids: 0.9% saline 15-20 mL/kg in first hour, then by hydration and corrected Na. add dextrose (5%) when glucose falls to ~200-250 so insulin can continue until ketoacidosis clears
2. potassium: if K <3.3 hold insulin and replace K first. if 3.3-5.2 add K to each litre (aim 4-5). if >5.2 no K, recheck 2 hourly
3. insulin: regular insulin IV infusion 0.1 U/kg/h (some give 0.1 U/kg bolus first). aim glucose fall 50-75 mg/dL per hour
4. bicarbonate only if pH <6.9 (debated)
5. phosphate not routine; replace if <1.0 mg/dL or cardiac/respiratory compromise

resolution: glucose <200 plus two of: HCO3 >=15, venous pH >7.3, anion gap <=12. overlap SC basal insulin 1-2 h before stopping the drip (stopping IV insulin without SC cover -> rebound DKA)

complications: hypokalaemia (most dangerous, arrhythmia), hypoglycaemia, cerebral oedema (children, too rapid fall in osmolality - headache, bradycardia, falling GCS -> mannitol), hyperchloraemic acidosis from lots of saline, ARDS rare

vs HHS: older T2DM, glucose often >600, osmolality >320, little/no ketosis, pH >7.30, more dehydrated, higher mortality
