What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How ammonia is handled. Protein breakdown by gut bacteria releases ammonia. Portal blood carries it to the liver, where the urea cycle converts ammonia to urea for excretion. In cirrhosis, liver clearance fails and portosystemic shunting lets ammonia rich blood bypass the liver and reach the systemic circulation and brain. This is standard teaching in medicine and hepatology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • What happens in the brain. Astrocytes take up ammonia and convert it to glutamine, drawing in water and swelling. Swollen astrocytes and disturbed neurotransmission produce the spectrum from mild confusion and sleep disturbance through drowsiness to coma. The grade slider shows that spectrum qualitatively, without any clinical scoring detail. This is standard teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Lactulose traps ammonia in the gut so it leaves in stool, rifaximin lowers ammonia making by gut bacteria, and treating the precipitant removes triggers such as infection, bleeding or dehydration that tip a patient into encephalopathy. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Glutamine chemistry detail, other toxins and inflammatory mediators, brain imaging, clinical grading criteria, precipitant lists beyond examples in words, transplant and shunt procedures, and all doses and drug choices are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
