What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How haemodialysis works. Blood is pumped out of a fistula, passes through a dialyser where small solutes move across a semipermeable membrane by diffusion and water moves by pressure (ultrafiltration), and returns to the patient. Heparin stops the circuit clotting. Dialysate flows opposite to blood (counter-current) to keep the concentration gradient steep. Air detection and pressure monitoring protect the patient. This is standard teaching in medicine and nephrology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.

What was simplified. Convective therapies (haemofiltration, haemodiafiltration), peritoneal dialysis, catheter access, dialysate composition and temperature, bicarbonate buffering details, dialysis adequacy measures, session length and frequency, complications (hypotension, cramps, disequilibrium) and anticoagulation choices are left out. Gauge levels and the speed of change are illustrative. The setting choices are teaching toggles: no flow rates, doses or schedules are shown, and the figure does not say who should have dialysis.
