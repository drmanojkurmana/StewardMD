What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How hepatorenal syndrome develops. In cirrhosis, scarring raises resistance in the liver and portal pressure climbs. Arteries in the gut circulation widen, driven in part by nitric oxide, so blood pools there and the effective arterial circulation is underfilled even though total body fluid is increased. The body reads this as blood loss and switches on the renin angiotensin system and the sympathetic nerves. Those signals protect the brain and heart but squeeze the kidney arteries intensely. The kidney then fails in a functional way: its tissue is structurally normal, blood flow and filtration fall, and the kidney holds on to salt so urine salt is very low. Ascites collects in parallel from portal pressure and salt and water retention. This is standard teaching in hepatology and medicine texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Albumin expands the effective arterial circulation and a vasoconstrictor tightens the widened gut arteries, together easing the drive that squeezes the kidney. Stopping diuretics and other kidney toxins removes common triggers that tip the circulation over. Liver transplant is the definitive step because it removes the cirrhosis and portal hypertension driving the whole chain. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Diagnostic criteria and urine tests in detail, infection and other triggers beyond the drug toggle, acute on chronic liver failure, dialysis and other bridging support, terlipressin and other drug choices by name, transplant assessment, and all doses and targets are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
