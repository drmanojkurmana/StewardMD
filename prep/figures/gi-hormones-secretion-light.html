What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Acid control. Sight, smell and thought of food start the cephalic phase: the vagus nerve primes parietal and G cells. Food stretching the stomach and peptides in it start the gastric phase: G cells release gastrin, which acts in blood and locally to drive parietal cells, helped by histamine from ECL cells and by vagal acetylcholine, all converging on the parietal acid pump. Somatostatin from D cells restrains gastrin, histamine and acid. This is standard teaching in physiology and medicine texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Intestinal phase. Acid and fat entering the duodenum start the intestinal phase and slow the stomach. I cells release cholecystokinin (CCK), which contracts the gallbladder and drives pancreatic enzyme secretion. S cells release secretin, which drives bicarbonate from pancreatic duct cells to neutralise acid chyme. K cells release GIP and L cells release GLP-1; both act as incretins, boosting glucose-dependent insulin release from pancreatic beta cells. A proton pump inhibitor blocks the parietal acid pump, so acid falls and gastrin rises by feedback. A somatostatin analogue broadly inhibits hormone release, acid, enzymes, bicarbonate and insulin. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Chief cell pepsin, motility patterns in detail, enteric reflex wiring, bile chemistry, enzyme names, glucose transport and insulin action in tissues, other gut peptides such as motilin and ghrelin, and every drug dose and protocol are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
