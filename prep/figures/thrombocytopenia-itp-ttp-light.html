What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Why platelets fall. Platelets can fall because the marrow makes too few, because they are destroyed or trapped after release, or because they are used up in clots. ITP is immune destruction: antibody coated platelets are removed by macrophages, mainly in the spleen, while the marrow tries to compensate. TTP follows severe lack of the enzyme ADAMTS13, which normally trims von Willebrand factor; the long strings trap platelets into microthrombi in small vessels and red cells fragment as they pass (haemolysis). HIT follows an antibody to heparin with platelet factor 4 that activates platelets, so the count falls at the same time as new clots form. This is standard teaching in haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Steroids and IVIG calm immune removal in ITP, plasma exchange removes antibody and replaces ADAMTS13 in TTP, and in HIT heparin is stopped and a non-heparin anticoagulant is used so platelet activation settles without leaving clots untreated. They are teaching toggles that show which step each approach acts on.

What was simplified. Marrow failure detail, inherited platelet disorders, drug causes other than heparin, splenic pooling, laboratory testing and diagnostic thresholds, complement and neurologic or kidney injury detail, transfusion, surgery, second line immune treatments and all doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
