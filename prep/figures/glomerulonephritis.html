What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How the glomerulus is injured. In glomerulonephritis the filter is attacked by the immune system: immune complexes deposit in the capillary wall (as after some streptococcal infections), IgA deposits in the mesangium in IgA nephropathy, antibody binds along the glomerular basement membrane in anti-GBM disease, or small vessel inflammation injures the tuft with few deposits (pauci-immune disease). Complement is activated and neutrophils are recruited. The damaged wall lets red cells and protein through, giving a nephritic picture of haematuria, hypertension, oedema and falling filtration. This is standard teaching in medicine and nephrology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Patterns and the nephrotic contrast. Post-infectious disease follows an infection and often settles; IgA disease tends to smoulder with episodes of visible blood; crescentic disease fills Bowman space with a crescent of cells and fibrin and destroys filtration rapidly, so it is called rapidly progressive. Nephrotic injury is different: the podocyte and its slit diaphragm fail, protein leaks heavily, but there is little inflammation and little blood. Flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Supportive care alone (salt, fluid and blood pressure control), a steroid, or a steroid plus broader immunosuppression, which damp complement, neutrophils and crescent formation. In podocyte disease steroids can also reduce protein leak. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. The many named diseases, biopsy patterns and stains, complement genetics, infection treatment, plasma exchange, dialysis, relapses, and all doses and targets are left out. Pauci-immune and anti-GBM injury are shown together at the severe end. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
