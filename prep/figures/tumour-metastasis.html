What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How metastasis happens. A primary tumour grows and its cells change so they can detach, digest and push through the basement membrane and surrounding tissue (local invasion). Cells that enter a lymphatic channel travel to nearby lymph nodes (lymphatic spread). Cells that enter a blood vessel (intravasation) become circulating tumour cells; most die, but some survive, arrest in a small vessel of a distant organ, leave the vessel (extravasation) and, if the organ tissue suits them, grow into a new colony (colonisation). Which organs are colonised depends on blood flow patterns and on fit between tumour cell and organ tissue, often called seed and soil. This is standard teaching in pathology and oncology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Surgery removes the primary tumour and nearby nodes but does not remove distant colonies that are already established. Systemic therapy travels in the blood and can reach circulating cells, nodes and distant colonies. Radiotherapy is aimed at the primary site, so it shrinks the local tumour but does not treat distant colonies. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Angiogenesis, immune evasion, dormancy, pre-metastatic niches, organ specific patterns for each cancer, staging scans, and all doses and regimens are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
