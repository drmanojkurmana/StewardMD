What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Growth signalling and proto-oncogene activation. Proto-oncogenes are normal genes that promote cell growth and division. Growth factors bind receptors that switch on RAS and a downstream kinase cascade, sending a divide signal to the nucleus. A change that locks a proto-oncogene on (for example a RAS change that leaves RAS active) turns a regulated signal into an always on signal, so the cell is told to divide even without the usual growth cue. This is standard teaching in pathology and cancer biology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Tumour suppressors, checkpoints and repair. Tumour suppressor genes normally restrain division. p53 pauses the cell cycle when DNA is damaged, allows repair, and can trigger apoptosis (programmed cell death) when damage is too great. The Rb protein holds the cell at the G1 checkpoint until it is safe to copy DNA. Loss of p53 or Rb removes those brakes: damaged DNA is copied, repair falls behind, and further changes accumulate. Cancer usually needs several such hits in the same cell line (multi-hit accumulation), not one change alone. This is standard teaching in pathology and cancer biology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • From one cell to a clone, and treatment ideas shown. A cell that divides without checkpoint control and evades apoptosis passes its changes to daughter cells, so an abnormal clone expands and can become dominant. A targeted kinase inhibitor blocks the overactive kinase cascade downstream of the growth signal, so it lowers signalling but does not restore lost p53 or Rb or repair past damage. Chemotherapy harms dividing cells, so it shrinks the dividing clone but also damages DNA and harms normal dividing cells. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Specific oncogenes and suppressors beyond RAS, p53 and Rb, angiogenesis, invasion and metastasis, immune evasion, metabolic change, tumour heterogeneity and resistance, screening and staging, and all drug choices and doses are left out.
