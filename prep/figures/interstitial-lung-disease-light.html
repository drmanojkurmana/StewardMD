What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How interstitial lung disease develops. The interstitium is the thin support tissue between the alveolar epithelium and the capillary endothelium, where oxygen crosses into blood. Repeated alveolar epithelial injury and abnormal repair activate fibroblasts, which become matrix making cells and deposit collagen and other extracellular matrix. The interstitium thickens and stiffens, alveolar walls distort, and in advanced disease clustered cystic spaces called honeycombing replace normal lung. Causes and patterns include idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, connective tissue disease, hypersensitivity and inhaled exposures such as asbestos, and some drugs; in many cases the cause is unknown. Thickened walls increase the distance for diffusion, and a stiff, low compliance lung gives a restrictive pattern with reduced lung volumes. Typical findings stressed in standard textbook teaching such as Harrison Interstitial Lung Disease include cough, progressive breathlessness, fine inspiratory crackles (rales), and finger clubbing in some patients, with impaired gas transfer on lung function. This account was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. An antifibrotic slows fibroblast driven scarring and decline but does not remove established collagen. Immunosuppression helps mainly where inflammation drives the pattern, for example some connective tissue disease related disease, and is not a treatment for established scar alone. Oxygen supports blood oxygen when it is low without reversing fibrosis. Transplant referral is the definitive option considered for suitable patients with end-stage disease. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. The many named patterns and causes, imaging and biopsy criteria, lung function numbers, infection and malignancy exclusion, pulmonary hypertension, acute exacerbations, rehabilitation and vaccination, transplant assessment, and all doses and targets are left out.
