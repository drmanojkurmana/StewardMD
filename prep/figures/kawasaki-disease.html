What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How Kawasaki disease develops. Kawasaki disease is a childhood vasculitis of medium sized arteries, most importantly the coronary arteries. Days of fever come with red eyes without discharge, red and cracked lips and mouth changes, a rash, red and swollen hands and feet, and one large neck lymph node. Inflammation through the artery wall weakens it, so the wall can bulge into a coronary artery aneurysm. Blood swirls slowly inside an aneurysm and can form a thrombus, which can block the artery and injure heart muscle. This is standard teaching in paediatric and cardiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. IVIG given early calms the inflammation and makes coronary aneurysms less likely, aspirin makes platelets less sticky while the artery is inflamed or dilated, and anticoagulation is used for a large aneurysm where slow moving blood is likely to clot. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. The trigger and immune mechanism, other arteries, myocarditis and valve changes, shock and macrophage activation presentations, laboratory markers, echocardiography follow up, refractory disease and extra treatments, and all doses and timing are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
