What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How gallstones form. The liver makes bile carrying cholesterol held in solution by bile salts and lecithin (phospholipid) in tiny micelles. When bile is supersaturated with cholesterol compared with bile salts and lecithin, cholesterol crystals can nucleate, helped by gallbladder stasis when the gallbladder empties poorly, and grow into cholesterol stones. Pigment stones are different: they are dark stones of calcium bilirubinate that form when excess bilirubin reaches bile, for example in haemolysis where red cells break down faster. A stone that sticks in the cystic duct while the gallbladder squeezes gives biliary colic. If the cystic duct stays blocked the trapped bile and pressure inflame the gallbladder wall as acute cholecystitis. A stone that passes into or forms in the common bile duct blocks bile reaching the gut, so bilirubin backs up and causes obstructive jaundice, and bacteria ascending from the duodenum can infect the held back bile as cholangitis. This is standard teaching in medicine and surgery texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Analgesia eases the pain of colic but does not move the stone. Cholecystectomy removes the gallbladder and its stones and so cures gallbladder colic and cholecystitis, but it does not by itself clear a stone already in the common bile duct. ERCP passes a scope through the duodenum to open the duct opening and clear a duct stone, relieving jaundice and cholangitis, while gallbladder stones can remain. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Gallbladder polyps and cancer, sludge without stones, acalculous cholecystitis, Mirizzi syndrome, gallstone ileus and pancreatitis from a duct stone, imaging, antibiotics and drainage details, surgical risks and all doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
