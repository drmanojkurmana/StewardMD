What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How meningitis develops. Organisms reach the meninges, usually through the blood, and multiply in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) of the subarachnoid space, which has little local defence. Inflammation follows: the blood to CSF barrier leaks protein, white cells enter, and bacteria and white cells use glucose so CSF glucose falls in bacterial and tuberculous disease. Bacterial CSF is typically cloudy with many neutrophils, high protein and low glucose; viral CSF is typically clear with lymphocytes, mildly raised protein and normal glucose; tuberculous CSF is in between and more chronic, with lymphocytes, high protein and low glucose. Inflamed meninges and raised intracranial pressure cause headache and neck stiffness from meningeal irritation. This is standard teaching in medicine, neurology and microbiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. An antibiotic kills susceptible bacteria, an antiviral acts on viral infection, and a steroid dampens inflammation and pressure without killing the organism. These are teaching toggles only and do not cover urgent assessment, organism specific choices, resistance, or adjunctive care.

What was simplified. Routes through the sinuses and middle ear, newborn and post neurosurgical infection, fungal and parasitic causes, seizures and focal deficits, hydrocephalus and cranial nerve palsies in detail, lumbar puncture technique and opening pressure measurement, microbiology testing, and all drug choices, doses and timing are left out.
