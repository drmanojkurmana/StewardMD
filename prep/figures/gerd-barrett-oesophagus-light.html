What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How reflux injures the oesophagus. The lower oesophageal sphincter is normally closed between swallows. Weakness of the sphincter and transient relaxations at the wrong time let gastric acid and pepsin flow back into the oesophagus, and a hiatus hernia makes this worse by trapping refluxed fluid above the diaphragm and impairing clearance. Repeated mucosal injury causes oesophagitis, ulceration and peptic stricture. This is standard teaching in medicine texts on diseases of the oesophagus and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Barrett oesophagus and cancer. In some people the injured squamous lining is replaced by columnar metaplasia at the gastro-oesophageal junction: Barrett oesophagus. Barrett mucosa can develop low-grade and then high-grade dysplasia, and then oesophageal adenocarcinoma. Endoscopic surveillance and endoscopic or surgical treatment are teaching toggles in this figure only.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Lifestyle measures that reduce reflux, acid suppression that makes the refluxed fluid less injurious and lets oesophagitis heal, and endoscopic or surgical treatment that can remove or ablate Barrett tissue and tighten the junction. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Swallowing and peristalsis detail, bile reflux, eosinophilic and infectious oesophagitis, detailed endoscopic grading, surveillance intervals, staging and all doses and targets are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
