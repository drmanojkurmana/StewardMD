What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How cholesterol moves. The liver makes cholesterol, with HMG-CoA reductase as the controlling enzyme, and packages lipid into VLDL. In the blood VLDL loses lipid and becomes LDL, the main cholesterol carrier to tissues. Liver LDL receptors bind LDL and pull it back into the liver for clearance. HDL does the return journey, carrying cholesterol from peripheral tissues back to the liver (reverse cholesterol transport). The gut also absorbs dietary and biliary cholesterol. This is standard teaching in medicine, biochemistry and pharmacology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Familial hypercholesterolaemia and treatment ideas shown. A faulty LDL receptor gene leaves few working receptors, so LDL stays high from early life. A statin blocks HMG-CoA reductase; the liver responds by making more LDL receptors. Ezetimibe blocks cholesterol uptake from the gut. A PCSK9 inhibitor stops PCSK9 marking LDL receptors for destruction, so more receptors survive; it works best when some receptors can still be made. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Chylomicrons and their remnants, IDL detail, bile acid loss and medicines that act on it, lipoprotein(a), triglyceride pathways in detail, receptor genetics beyond the single faulty receptor idea, drug choice, combinations and all doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
