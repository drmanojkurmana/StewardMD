What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How protein-energy malnutrition develops. Too little food overall gives marasmus: fat and muscle are broken down for energy, growth stops and the child is severely wasted, while blood albumin is relatively preserved and oedema is absent because adaptation still protects visceral protein. Too little protein with relatively more carbohydrate, often after weaning or during infection, gives kwashiorkor: albumin and other visceral proteins fall, fluid leaks into tissues as oedema, fat cannot be exported from the liver so the liver becomes fatty and enlarged, and hair, skin and mood change. The mixed form, marasmic kwashiorkor, has severe wasting with oedema. In all forms immunity is weakened, so infection is more likely and more severe, and infection in turn reduces appetite and increases losses, closing a vicious cycle. This is standard teaching in nutrition and paediatrics texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Careful gradual refeeding (abrupt full feeding can be dangerous), prompt treatment of infection, and replacing missing micronutrients. These are teaching toggles only, with no feeds, products or doses shown.

What was simplified. Hormonal adaptation in detail (insulin, cortisol, growth hormone), refeeding syndrome electrolytes, specific micronutrient deficiencies, HIV and tuberculosis associations, appetite stimulants, ready-to-use therapeutic food composition, and all admission criteria, feed volumes and doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
