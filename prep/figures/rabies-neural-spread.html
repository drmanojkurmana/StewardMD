What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How rabies spreads. Rabies virus deposited by a bite in muscle is taken up at nerve endings and carried by retrograde axonal transport along sensory and autonomic nerves to the spinal cord and brain. There is usually no viraemia. In neurons the virus causes neuronal dysfunction rather than widespread neuronal destruction, with Negri bodies in infected cells. Limbic and brainstem involvement gives the encephalitic form with agitation and hydrophobia; a paralytic form gives flaccid paralysis. Centrifugal spread along nerves reaches the salivary glands. Antibodies appear late, and laboratory tests detect viral antigen or nucleic acid. Once encephalitis is established the course is almost always fatal, so suspected exposure should be assessed urgently. This is standard textbook teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Prompt wound washing and post exposure vaccine plus immunoglobulin before symptoms can prevent virus reaching the brain. Washing alone is only partly protective in this teaching model. Treatment started once symptomatic is shown with no reversal of established brain involvement.

What was simplified. The long and variable incubation, differences between animal hosts, the exact molecular transport machinery, diagnostic test selection, supportive intensive care details and all doses and schedules are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
