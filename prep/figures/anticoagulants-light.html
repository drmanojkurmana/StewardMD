What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Warfarin. A vitamin K antagonist: it blocks the vitamin K cycle that the liver needs to activate factors II, VII, IX and X (and proteins C and S), so its effect appears over days as existing factors are cleared (Warfarin, NCBI Bookshelf).
  • Direct oral anticoagulants. Rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban and betrixaban inhibit factor Xa; dabigatran, argatroban and bivalirudin inhibit thrombin directly (Anticoagulation, NCBI Bookshelf).
  • Heparins. Heparin acts through antithrombin, speeding its inhibition of thrombin and factor Xa; low molecular weight heparins and fondaparinux act mainly on factor Xa because their chains are too short to bridge antithrombin to thrombin. This is standard teaching in the pharmacology texts the PrepNucleus lessons cite (Katzung; KD Tripathi) and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.

What was simplified. The intrinsic and extrinsic pathways are drawn as one step; factors V, VIII and XI, thrombin's own feedback, protein C and S, tissue factor pathway inhibitor, fibrinolysis, platelets, monitoring (INR, aPTT, anti-Xa), reversal agents, interactions and bleeding risk are left out. Levels, flow and the size of each block are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
