What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How AV conduction works and fails. The sinoatrial (SA) node fires first and the signal spreads across the atria, giving the P wave. The atrioventricular (AV) node is the normal gateway to the ventricles and holds the signal briefly so the ventricles can fill; that pause is most of the PR interval. The signal then runs down the bundle of His, the bundle branches and the Purkinje fibres, so the ventricles fire together in a narrow QRS. In first degree block every signal still gets through, but the PR interval is long. In Mobitz I (Wenckebach) block, usually at the AV node, the delay grows beat by beat until one signal is dropped, then the cycle repeats. In Mobitz II block, usually below the AV node in the His-Purkinje system, beats are dropped suddenly without lengthening first, and it is more likely to progress. In third degree (complete) block no atrial signal reaches the ventricles: the atria and ventricles beat independently and a slow, wide escape rhythm from lower down keeps the ventricles going. This is standard teaching in medicine and cardiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Observation leaves the rhythm as it is. Atropine parks on the AV node, so it can shorten the delay in first degree and Mobitz I block, but it does not repair Mobitz II or complete block below the node. Pacing takes over the ventricles when dropped or blocked beats make the ventricular rate too slow, and it sits on standby when conduction is still adequate. These are teaching toggles only.
