What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How red cells are destroyed. Haemolysis is shortened red cell survival. Extravascular haemolysis is mainly removal of rigid, damaged or antibody coated red cells by macrophages in the spleen. Intravascular haemolysis is rupture inside the vessel, releasing haemoglobin into plasma. Breakdown of haem raises unconjugated bilirubin, which travels to the liver; the marrow responds with reticulocytosis, and chronic splenic removal can enlarge the spleen. This is standard teaching in haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • G6PD deficiency and warm antibody disease. In G6PD deficiency, oxidant stress from a trigger injures red cells, denatured haemoglobin forms Heinz bodies, and rigid cells are pitted and removed in the spleen or burst in the vessel. In warm autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, warm reactive antibody coats red cells and splenic macrophages remove them. Removing an oxidant trigger, steroids for autoimmune disease, transfusion support and splenectomy in selected cases are shown only as teaching toggles for which step each acts on.

What was simplified. Haptoglobin, lactate dehydrogenase, urinary findings, direct antiglobulin testing, blood film details beyond Heinz bodies, cold antibody disease, inherited membrane and enzyme defects other than G6PD, infection and drug lists, and all doses and thresholds are left out.
