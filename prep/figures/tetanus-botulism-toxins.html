What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Tetanus. Clostridium tetani spores enter a wound and germinate in low oxygen tissue. Tetanospasmin is taken up at the nerve ending and travels retrograde inside the motor neuron to the spinal cord, where it blocks release of the inhibitory transmitters glycine and GABA from interneurons. Without that braking signal the motor neuron fires unchecked, giving spastic paralysis, rigidity and lockjaw (trismus). This is standard teaching in microbiology and neurology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Botulism. Botulinum toxin reaches nerve endings and blocks acetylcholine release at the neuromuscular junction by cleaving release machinery proteins. The muscle receives no signal, giving flaccid paralysis and weakness that can reach the breathing muscles. This is standard teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Antitoxin neutralises toxin that is still free and unbound; it cannot undo toxin already inside neurons. Wound care removes the spore source and vaccine builds protection against future toxin. Supportive ventilation breathes for the patient while the nerve endings recover. These are teaching toggles only.
