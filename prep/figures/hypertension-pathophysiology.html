What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How blood pressure is set. Arterial pressure is the product of cardiac output and total peripheral resistance. Cardiac output rises with heart rate, contractility and blood volume. Resistance is set mainly in small arteries and arterioles, whose smooth muscle tightens under sympathetic drive and angiotensin II and relaxes when calcium entry into that muscle is blocked. The kidney links volume and resistance: it releases renin when perfusion falls, starting the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, and it holds or loses sodium and water. With time, raised pressure and hormonal signals thicken and stiffen the vessel wall (remodelling), which keeps resistance high. This is standard teaching in medicine, physiology and cardiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Organ damage and treatment ideas shown. Sustained pressure thickens the left ventricle and can lead to heart failure, injures brain vessels (stroke and cognitive decline), damages kidney glomeruli and vessels (chronic kidney disease, which itself raises pressure further), and injures retinal vessels. Diuretics help the kidney lose sodium and water. ACE inhibitors and ARBs block the renin-angiotensin system. Calcium channel blockers relax arteriole muscle. Beta-blockers reduce sympathetic drive to the heart. These are teaching toggles that show where each class acts, not how any person should be treated.

What was simplified. Secondary causes of hypertension, blood pressure measurement and thresholds, white coat and masked hypertension, isolated systolic hypertension, drug choice by comorbidity, side effects, resistant hypertension workup, acute hypertensive emergencies, and all doses and targets are left out. Wall change reverses only slowly, so the figure keeps some resistance from remodelling even when a treatment is on. Counts and speeds are illustrative.
