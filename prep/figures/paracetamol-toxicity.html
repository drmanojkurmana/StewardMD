What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How paracetamol is handled. At therapeutic intakes most paracetamol is conjugated in the liver by glucuronidation and sulphation and excreted. A small fraction is oxidised by cytochrome P450 enzymes to the reactive product NAPQI, which is normally joined to glutathione and made harmless. This is standard teaching in pharmacology and medicine and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • What happens in overdose. The sulphation and glucuronidation pathways saturate, more paracetamol is diverted to NAPQI, and glutathione stores are used up. Free NAPQI then binds to hepatocyte proteins, causing centrilobular (around the central vein) hepatocyte necrosis, which can progress to acute liver failure. This is standard teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Activated charcoal in the gut, given early, reduces absorption. N-acetylcysteine provides cysteine to rebuild glutathione and detoxify NAPQI. Liver transplant replaces a liver that has failed and cannot recover. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Absorption timing, blood level measurement and risk assessment, other causes of glutathione depletion, kidney injury, acid base and clotting detail in liver failure, transplant selection, and all doses, timings and targets are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
