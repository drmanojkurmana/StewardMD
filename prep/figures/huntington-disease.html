What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Gene and inheritance. Huntington disease is caused by an expanded CAG repeat in the huntingtin gene (HTT). It is autosomal dominant: each child of an affected parent has a substantial chance of inheriting the expansion. The repeat is unstable and often grows when passed on, especially through fathers, so later generations may develop symptoms earlier, a pattern called anticipation. This is standard teaching in neurology and genetics texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Brain mechanism and signs. Mutant huntingtin gradually harms medium spiny neurons of the striatum (caudate and putamen). Loss of striatal neurons that drive the indirect pathway weakens the normal brake on movement: the globus pallidus and subthalamic nucleus signalling changes, the thalamus is released, and unwanted involuntary movements (chorea) appear. Thinking, planning and mood are also affected, often before or alongside the movement disorder, and the condition progresses over years.
  • Treatment ideas shown. No treatment shown here slows the underlying neuron loss. Symptomatic drugs can ease chorea and some mood symptoms, genetic counselling helps the person and family understand inheritance, testing choices and planning, and supportive care (therapy, nutrition, speech and social support) helps daily function. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Direct pathway changes, cortical and other brain region involvement, repeat length thresholds and exact risks, predictive testing protocols, psychiatric detail, drug names and doses, and disease modifying trials are left out.
