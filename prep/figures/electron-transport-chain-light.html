What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How the chain works. NADH donates electrons at complex I and FADH2 (from succinate dehydrogenase) at complex II. Electrons pass through complex III and complex IV, where oxygen is the final acceptor and is reduced to water. Complexes I, III and IV use the energy to pump protons from the matrix into the intermembrane space. The resulting proton gradient (chemiosmosis) drives protons back through ATP synthase, which makes ATP from ADP and phosphate. Rotenone blocks complex I, cyanide blocks complex IV, and oligomycin blocks ATP synthase; uncouplers carry protons back across the membrane so energy is released as heat instead of ATP. Supportive oxygen keeps the final acceptor supplied, and an antidote is shown as a conceptual removal of the blocking agent. This is standard teaching in biochemistry and pharmacology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. An antidote that removes the inhibitor or uncoupler, and supportive oxygen. These are teaching toggles that show which step each idea acts on, not a recommendation for any patient or any specific drug.

What was simplified. Mobile carriers (ubiquinone and cytochrome c), the Q cycle, proton counts per complex, ATP yield per donor, reactive oxygen species, brown fat and regulated uncoupling proteins, specific antidotes for each poison, and all doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
