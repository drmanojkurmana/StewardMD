What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How asparaginase works. Asparaginase is a bacterial enzyme used in ALL therapy in children and adults. It breaks down asparagine in plasma to aspartate and ammonia, and leukaemia blasts, which lack asparagine synthetase, cannot make their own, so protein synthesis stalls and they die. Healthy cells make their own asparagine and survive. This is standard teaching in pharmacology and oncology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Side effects shown. Hypersensitivity to the foreign protein; pancreatitis with islet failure and high sugar; less liver protein output causing low antithrombin and fibrinogen, so thrombin runs free and fibrin clots form while wounds ooze; fatty liver; and high triglycerides. Allergy cover, clotting support and insulin ease some harms but not pancreas or liver injury. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Erwinia switch after allergy, desensitisation, dose holds and stopping rules, clotting factor monitoring, ammonia toxicity, combination chemotherapy, and all doses and schedules are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
