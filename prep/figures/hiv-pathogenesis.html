What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How HIV infects and damages immunity. The virion's gp120 binds CD4 on a T helper cell and then a co-receptor (CCR5 or CXCR4); gp41 drives fusion. The capsid opens, reverse transcriptase copies the RNA to DNA, integrase inserts it into the host genome (the provirus), host machinery makes new viral RNA and proteins, and the virion assembles, buds and matures when protease cuts its precursor proteins. Over years the CD4 count falls, so opportunistic infections and some cancers appear as the count gets low (AIDS). This is standard teaching in medicine and infectious-disease texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Entry inhibitors, reverse transcriptase inhibitors, integrase inhibitors and protease inhibitors each block one step; combination antiretroviral therapy blocks several and suppresses the virus while CD4 cells recover. A single drug alone is shown only as a teaching contrast, because resistance develops quickly on one drug.

What was simplified. Latent reservoirs, resistance mutations, other co-receptor details, mucosal transmission, acute syndrome details, HIV-2, prevention, drug interactions and all doses, regimens and monitoring targets are left out. The over-the-years plot is a generic illustration of an untreated course and of the effect of starting treatment; it is not a prediction for any person.
