What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How tuberculosis takes hold. Inhaled Mycobacterium tuberculosis is taken up by alveolar macrophages, where it blocks fusion of the phagosome with the lysosome and survives. Infected cells and monocytes gather, and antigen-specific Th1 cells release interferon gamma, which (with TNF) activates macrophages. These form a granuloma: epithelioid macrophages, Langhans giant cells and a rim of lymphocytes, with caseous (cheese-like) necrosis at the centre. In most people the granuloma contains the infection (latent TB) and a primary (Ghon) focus with nodal involvement heals. When immunity fails or falls later (HIV, TNF inhibitors, malnutrition), the core liquefies into a cavity, bacilli spread through airways, lymph and blood (miliary TB), and active disease follows. This is standard teaching in medicine and microbiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Multi-drug combination therapy for active disease and preventive therapy for latent infection are teaching toggles that show effects only; the drug-by-drug mechanisms are in the antitubercular drugs figure.

What was simplified. Drug resistance, extrapulmonary sites other than lymph node and blood, the Ghon complex and Ranke complex staging, latent testing methods, immune reconstitution, and all regimens, doses and durations are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
