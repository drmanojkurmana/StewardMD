What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Graves disease. A stimulating antibody against the TSH receptor (often called TRAb or TSI) binds the receptor on thyroid follicular cells and switches it on without TSH. The gland grows diffusely (goitre), makes and releases too much thyroid hormone, and the high hormone suppresses pituitary TSH by negative feedback. A related autoimmune inflammation behind the eye expands orbital tissue and muscle, pushing the eye forward and causing Graves eye disease. This is standard teaching in endocrinology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Hashimoto thyroiditis. Antibodies against thyroid peroxidase (TPO) mark a lymphocytic attack on the gland. Lymphocytes infiltrate, follicles are destroyed and, over time, fibrous tissue replaces working gland. Early on the gland may be enlarged and hormone may still be held near normal while TSH rises to compensate. Late, with little follicle left, hormone is low and TSH is high (primary hypothyroidism). A short release of stored hormone early in destruction is left out here.
  • Treatment ideas shown. An antithyroid medicine blocks new hormone making, which helps Graves but deepens a Hashimoto deficit. Radioiodine or surgery removes the overactive gland in Graves, after which hormone falls and replacement is usually needed, while eye disease may persist. Levothyroxine replaces missing hormone in Hashimoto and restores feedback, but adds to an already high hormone level in untreated Graves. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Hashitoxicosis and other transient phases, blocking and neutral TSH receptor antibodies, TPO antibody titres and testing, ultrasound and biopsy, pregnancy and neonatal effects, thyroid storm and myxoedema, eye disease grading and its own treatment, drug choice and monitoring, radioiodine precautions, surgical risks, and all doses and targets are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
