What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How PCOS develops. In polycystic ovary syndrome the pituitary releases LH in stronger pulses while FSH is relatively low. Insulin resistance raises circulating insulin, and insulin acts with LH on ovarian theca cells to increase androgen production. Androgen excess and the altered hormone pattern arrest follicle growth, so many small follicles collect in the ovary, ovulation is infrequent, and periods are irregular. Androgens acting on skin and hair follicles cause acne and hirsutism. Insulin resistance also raises longer term metabolic and diabetes risk. Not everyone has every feature, and the balance differs between people. This is standard teaching in endocrinology and gynaecology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Lifestyle change and weight loss where relevant, and metformin, mainly improve insulin resistance and downstream androgen excess. Cyclic progestogen protects the womb lining and brings on regular bleeds, while ovulation induction aims to restore ovulation when pregnancy is wanted. They are teaching toggles only and the figure does not choose between them.

What was simplified. The inherited and genetic contribution, hyperandrogenism from the adrenal glands, FSH and anti-Mullerian hormone detail, diagnostic criteria, fertility and pregnancy care, endometrial cancer risk detail, mood and sleep apnoea links, and all doses and drug choices within each class are left out.
