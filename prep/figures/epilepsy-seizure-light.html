What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Balance of excitation and inhibition. Cortical pyramidal neurons excite each other mainly through glutamate acting on AMPA and NMDA receptors. Fast sodium currents drive their action potentials. Interneurons release GABA, which opens chloride channels (GABA-A receptors) and steadies the network. When excitation outruns inhibition, through altered ion channels, loss of inhibition or excess glutamate, neurons become hyperexcitable and fire together. This is standard teaching in neurology and pharmacology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Focal and generalised seizures. A focal seizure starts in a local cluster and may stay local or recruit neighbouring cortex. A generalised seizure engages networks on both sides from the start or by spread, with loss of awareness and a spiky brain wave trace (EEG). Only the spread idea is drawn here.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Blocking voltage-gated sodium channels reduces rapid firing, enhancing GABA strengthens inhibition, and blocking glutamate reduces excitation. These are teaching toggles only, not a guide to choosing a medicine.

What was simplified. Single-neuron bursting, thalamic circuits, absence and other seizure types, status epilepticus, triggers, genetics, imaging, EEG interpretation in patients, and all doses and drug choices are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
