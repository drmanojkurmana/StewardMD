What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • The circuit. Dopamine from the substantia nigra pars compacta excites D1 neurons of the direct pathway and inhibits D2 neurons of the indirect pathway in the striatum. The direct pathway inhibits the GPi, which releases its inhibition of the thalamus and so promotes movement. The indirect pathway runs through the GPe and the subthalamic nucleus (drawn here as one stop lane): it ends by exciting the GPi, which increases inhibition of the thalamus and suppresses movement. This is standard teaching in neurology and basal ganglia physiology and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • What goes wrong in Parkinson disease. Dopamine neurons of the substantia nigra are progressively lost, so striatal dopamine falls. The direct pathway becomes underactive and the indirect pathway becomes overactive. The GPi brake therefore holds the thalamus down, thalamic drive falls, and movement becomes slow and small (bradykinesia), with rigidity and a resting tremor. Non motor features, Lewy pathology and tremor circuitry in detail are not drawn. This is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Levodopa is a dopamine precursor that is converted to dopamine in the brain. A dopamine agonist stimulates dopamine receptors directly. A MAO-B inhibitor slows dopamine breakdown in the brain, and a COMT inhibitor slows levodopa breakdown, so each prolongs dopamine action; on its own the inhibitor needs some dopamine to be present. None restores the lost neurons. These are teaching toggles only, not a guide to choosing a medicine.

What was simplified. Alpha synuclein and Lewy bodies, the exact cause of neuron loss, tremor generation in detail, the separate GPe and STN relays (merged into one stop lane), non motor symptoms (mood, sleep, smell, cognition, autonomic features), gait freezing and falls, dyskinesia and wearing off with long term treatment, deep brain stimulation, physiotherapy and all doses and drug choices are left out.
