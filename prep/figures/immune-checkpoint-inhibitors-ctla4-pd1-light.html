What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How checkpoint blockade works. Priming a naive T cell needs signal 1 (its receptor meeting antigen on MHC) plus signal 2 (CD28 on the T cell meeting B7 on the dendritic cell). CTLA-4, which appears after activation, binds B7 more strongly than CD28 and dampens priming early in the lymph node; blocking CTLA-4 with ipilimumab keeps priming going. In the tumour, exhausted T cells carry PD-1, and tumour PD-L1 binding it switches killing off; blocking PD-1 with pembrolizumab or nivolumab reinvigorates killing. Reinvigorated T cells can also attack self tissue, giving immune related effects such as colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis and hormone gland failure. This is standard teaching in immunology and oncology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Anti-CTLA-4 acting early on priming, anti-PD-1 acting later on exhausted cells in tissue, and the combination giving more killing with more self tissue injury. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Regulatory T cells, other checkpoints (LAG-3, TIM-3), memory formation, antigen loss and other resistance routes, steroid and other management of side effects, tumour types and combinations used in practice, and all doses and schedules are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
