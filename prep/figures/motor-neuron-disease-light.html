What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How motor neuron disease develops. In amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, the commonest adult form, upper motor neurons in the motor cortex and lower motor neurons in the brainstem and spinal cord die together, so brisk reflexes and spasticity sit beside wasting, weakness and fasciculations. Most cases are sporadic, with no family history. A minority are inherited: mutant genes include an expanded intronic repeat in C9orf72, where short repeats are expanded many times over, and changes in genes for proteins such as the survival and RNA handling proteins. Inside dying neurons, TDP-43, a protein that normally lives in the nucleus, clumps in the cytoplasm as aggregates, and this protein pathology is a hallmark of the disease group. Excitotoxic drive, faulty RNA handling, and failure of protein clearing are among the mechanisms proposed, because no single cause explains every case. Other patterns in the group include primary lateral sclerosis, where upper motor neurons dominate, and childhood spinal muscular atrophy, where loss of the survival motor neuron protein weakens lower motor neurons early in onset. This is standard teaching in neurology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • What the disease spares and what it threatens. Sensation and eye movements are characteristically spared, because sensory pathways and the eye movement nuclei are largely untouched, which helps separate this disease from multiple other causes of weakness. Bulbar involvement weakens speech and swallowing, and death most often follows respiratory failure when the diaphragm and breathing muscles fail over the course of the illness.   • Treatment ideas shown. Riluzole is an approved drug thought to calm excitotoxic glutamate drive; the concept is shown here as slower neuron loss, without any claim about size of benefit. Multidisciplinary support (physiotherapy, speech and swallowing help, nutrition, aids and planning) supports function without changing neuron death. Ventilatory support rests and assists the breathing muscles and supports breathing without changing the underlying course. These are teaching toggles only.
