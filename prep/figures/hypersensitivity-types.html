What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • The four types. The Gell and Coombs scheme groups immune-mediated tissue reactions. Type I: allergen cross-links IgE on mast cells, which release histamine and other mediators within minutes (allergy, anaphylaxis). Type II: antibody against a cell-surface or tissue antigen causes damage through complement, phagocytes, or by disturbing a receptor (transfusion reaction, autoimmune haemolysis, Goodpasture, Graves, myasthenia gravis). Type III: soluble antigen-antibody complexes deposit in vessel walls and trigger complement and neutrophils (serum sickness, SLE, post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis). Type IV: delayed, T cell and macrophage mediated (contact dermatitis, tuberculin test, graft rejection). This is standard teaching in immunology and pathology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Adrenaline for anaphylaxis, antihistamines for histamine effects, corticosteroids and immunosuppressants for broader immune reactions, and removing the trigger. They are teaching toggles that show which step each class acts on.

What was simplified. Real reactions overlap (many diseases mix types), Th2 and other cell types, the late-phase type I response, the full complement pathways, genetics and every drug and dose are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
