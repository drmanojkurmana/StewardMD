What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • The pathway and the drugs. Phospholipase A2 frees arachidonic acid; COX-1 and COX-2 make prostaglandins and thromboxane; 5-lipoxygenase makes leukotrienes. Corticosteroids lower the first step, aspirin acetylates COX irreversibly, other NSAIDs block COX reversibly, COX-2 selective drugs spare COX-1, and zileuton inhibits 5-lipoxygenase. This is standard teaching in the pharmacology texts the PrepNucleus lessons cite (Katzung; KD Tripathi) and were not checked against a linked source here, so they are flagged for review.

What was simplified. Differences between individual NSAIDs, the central action of paracetamol, COX-3 and other isoforms, the gastric and cardiovascular risk balance of COX-2 selective drugs, the effect of prostaglandins on the kidney and blood pressure, aspirin's low-dose antiplatelet use (see the antiplatelet figure), leukotriene receptor antagonists, and the many non-eicosanoid effects of corticosteroids are left out. Levels and the size of each block are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
