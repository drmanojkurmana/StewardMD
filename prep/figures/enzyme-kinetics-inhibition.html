What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How the plots work. In Michaelis-Menten kinetics the reaction rate rises with substrate and levels off at a maximum rate (Vmax) when the enzyme is saturated. Km is the substrate level that gives half the maximum rate, so a higher Km means more substrate is needed to reach the same rate. The Lineweaver-Burk plot draws the reciprocals (1/rate against 1/substrate) as a straight line, which makes changes in Vmax and Km easier to compare: the line meets the vertical axis at 1/Vmax and its steepness reflects Km. This is standard teaching in biochemistry and pharmacology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • How inhibitors differ. A competitive inhibitor resembles the substrate and competes for the active site, so it raises Km and leaves Vmax unchanged, and adding more substrate can overcome it. A non-competitive inhibitor binds at a separate (allosteric) site and distorts the enzyme, so it lowers Vmax and leaves Km unchanged, and more substrate cannot restore the maximum. The modulator toggle is shown as a positive allosteric modulator that raises the maximum rate and lowers Km, helping against either inhibitor. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Uncompetitive inhibition is not shown. Multi-substrate reactions, cooperativity and sigmoidal kinetics, reversible binding steps in detail, enzyme concentration effects, and all doses and assay conditions are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
