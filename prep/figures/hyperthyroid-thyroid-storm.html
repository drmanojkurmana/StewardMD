What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How hyperthyroidism develops. In Graves disease, autoimmune antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor (TSH-R) on thyroid cells, so the gland grows and releases excess thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3) despite a suppressed TSH. Other causes are a toxic nodule or toxic multinodular goitre making hormone on its own, and thyroiditis leaking stored hormone. The same Graves immune process can affect the orbit: extraocular muscle and orbital tissue swell, leading to proptosis, lid retraction and, when severe, sight threatening disease. Hormone excess raises metabolic rate and adrenergic sensitivity: tachycardia, atrial fibrillation (notably in the elderly), weight loss, tremor, heat intolerance and muscle weakness. Thyroid storm is the severe, decompensated end of thyrotoxicosis. Low hormone after treatment or disease can tip toward hypothyroidism, so levels are monitored. Inhibitors of hormone synthesis (antithyroid drugs), beta blockers for adrenergic signs, and definitive radioiodine or surgery are the standard treatment ideas. This is standard textbook teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. An antithyroid drug lowers new hormone synthesis, a beta blocker blunts adrenergic signs such as racing heart and tremor without lowering hormone much, and radioiodine or surgery definitively reduces the gland. These are teaching toggles only.
