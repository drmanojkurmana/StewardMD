What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Primary response. Dendritic cells carry antigen to a lymph node and present it to helper T cells. Activated helper T cells help antigen-specific B cells, which enter a germinal centre, multiply and refine their antibody. Some become plasma cells that secrete antibody; others become long-lived memory B cells, alongside memory T cells. The first response is slow and relatively weak because it starts from rare naive cells. This is standard teaching in immunology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Secondary response and boosters. Memory cells are more numerous and quicker to react than naive cells, so a booster dose or later exposure to the real pathogen produces antibody faster, in larger amount and of better quality, and the pathogen is controlled sooner. That is why the stage slider shows the response building at the first dose, strengthening at the booster and peaking on later exposure only when a vaccine was given.
  • Vaccine types shown. A live attenuated vaccine uses a weakened microbe that replicates briefly, so it drives strong helper T cell, germinal centre and memory responses. A conjugate vaccine links a sugar coat antigen to a carrier protein, which recruits helper T cells to what would otherwise be a T-independent sugar antigen, giving germinal centres, antibody and memory. An inactivated vaccine, not offered here, uses killed microbe and mainly drives antibody with booster doses. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Inactivated and subunit vaccines as separate toggles, adjuvants, mucosal and maternal antibody, waning and variant escape, cell-mediated killing in detail, affinity maturation steps, and all doses, schedules and product names are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any person should be vaccinated.
