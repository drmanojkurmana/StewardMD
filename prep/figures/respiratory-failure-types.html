What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Type 1, hypoxaemic failure. Oxygen fails to reach the blood while ventilation is preserved. The usual mechanisms are ventilation perfusion mismatch, where some alveoli get blood but too little air, shunt, where blood passes alveoli that get no air at all, and a diffusion defect, where a thickened alveolar capillary membrane slows oxygen transfer. Because carbon dioxide is far more soluble and ventilation often rises in response to low oxygen, arterial carbon dioxide is normal or low. Causes sit mainly in the lung, such as pneumonia, pulmonary oedema, collapse and lung fibrosis. This is standard respiratory teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Type 2, hypercapnic failure. The ventilatory pump fails and alveolar ventilation falls, so carbon dioxide cannot be cleared and respiratory acidosis follows. The pump can fail at the drive (brainstem and sedative effects), the nerves and respiratory muscles (weakness and fatigue), the chest wall (restriction and deformity), or the airways (severe obstruction, where airflow cannot sustain ventilation). Mixed failure combines a lung gas exchange defect with pump failure. This is standard respiratory teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Oxygen raises inspired oxygen and helps hypoxaemia from mismatch or diffusion defect, less so from true shunt, and does not by itself clear carbon dioxide. Non-invasive ventilation supports the pump through a mask, raising alveolar ventilation so carbon dioxide falls, and can also improve oxygen. Invasive ventilation takes over the pump through a tube in the airway, giving the most control of ventilation and oxygen. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Renal compensation for chronic acidosis, the alveolar arterial gradient, oxygen targets and controlled oxygen, recruitment and positive end expiratory pressure, high flow oxygen, treating each underlying cause, blood gas sampling, and all doses, pressures and targets are left out. Chronic type 2 failure with compensated pH is not separated from acute failure. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
