What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How diabetes develops. In type 1 diabetes, autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells leaves little or no insulin, so the liver is not switched off and muscle and fat cannot take up glucose well. In type 2 diabetes, muscle, fat and liver resist insulin; beta cells at first compensate by making more insulin, keeping glucose near normal for a time, but over years beta cell function declines (exhaustion), insulin becomes relatively insufficient, hepatic glucose output rises and peripheral uptake falls, so blood glucose rises. This is standard teaching in medicine and endocrinology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Insulin replaces what is missing and acts in every type, most clearly in type 1. Metformin mainly lowers hepatic glucose output and modestly improves insulin sensitivity, so it helps most where resistance and liver output drive the problem and cannot replace missing insulin. Incretin-based therapy boosts glucose-dependent insulin release and lowers glucagon, so it needs surviving beta cells and helps most in early type 2. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Genetics, obesity and lifestyle drivers, glucagon and kidney handling in detail, gut and brain signals, complications, monitoring, drug choice and combinations in practice, and all doses and glucose values are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
