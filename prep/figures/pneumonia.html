What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How pneumonia develops. Bacteria reach the air sacs (alveoli) and multiply. Neutrophils and fluid flood into the sacs as exudate, filling air space so that part of the lung becomes solid, which is consolidation and cannot exchange gas, so blood oxygen falls (hypoxia). Filling that starts patchily around small airways is bronchopneumonia; filling of a whole lobe is lobar pneumonia, and a freshly filled lobe is red and firm like liver, called red hepatisation, before it gradually clears in resolution as the infection is treated and debris is removed. Infection reaching the lung surface inflames the pleura and can collect pleural fluid beside the lung. This is standard teaching in medicine and respiratory texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. An antibiotic to treat the infection itself, and oxygen to support blood oxygen while the sacs clear. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Viruses and other organisms, white cell and immune defects, aspiration, fever and cough signs, tests and imaging, complications such as abscess and sepsis, vaccines, other supportive care, and all doses and targets are left out.
