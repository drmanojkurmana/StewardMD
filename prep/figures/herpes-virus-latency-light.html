What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Latency and reactivation. Herpes simplex virus first replicates in skin or mucosal epithelium. Capsids travel by retrograde axonal transport to sensory neuron cell bodies in the ganglion, where the viral genome persists in the neuron nucleus as latent DNA with very little viral protein made. Triggers such as stress, fever, ultraviolet light and immunosuppression can reactivate the virus. New capsids then travel by anterograde transport along the same axon to the skin and replicate in the epithelium, forming grouped vesicles. Reactivation of varicella zoster virus follows the same ganglion idea and gives vesicles in the dermatome supplied by that ganglion (shingles). This is standard teaching in virology and infection texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment idea shown. Aciclovir is a nucleoside analogue. After activation in infected cells it is built into growing viral DNA and stops the chain, so replication and new vesicle formation fall. It does not remove the latent genome from the neuron nucleus, so reactivation can still happen later. This is a teaching toggle only.

What was simplified. The full latency gene programme, immune control in the ganglion, differences between herpes simplex types and varicella zoster in detail, antiviral resistance, and all doses and durations are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
