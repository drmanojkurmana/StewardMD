What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How cell injury develops. Loss of oxygen stops oxidative phosphorylation and ATP falls. The sodium-potassium pump and calcium pumps fail, sodium and water enter, and the cell and its organelles swell. Anaerobic glycolysis lowers pH. If oxygen returns at this stage the changes can reverse. With continued injury, membrane damage, calcium entry and free radical injury (lipid peroxidation, protein and DNA damage) make injury irreversible: membranes rupture, enzymes leak out and the cell dies by necrosis. Necrosis patterns include coagulative (architecture kept, most solid tissues), liquefactive (digested to liquid, brain and abscesses), caseous (cheese-like, tuberculous granulomas) and fat necrosis (enzyme action on fat, chalky deposits). This is standard teaching in pathology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Restoring oxygen early can rescue a reversibly injured cell but cannot revive necrotic tissue. An antioxidant limits free radical membrane injury but does not by itself restore ATP while hypoxia continues. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Apoptosis and its pathways, reperfusion injury in detail, calcium signalling, lysosomal rupture, inflammation after necrosis, specific free radical species and scavengers, autophagy, and all drug names, doses and timing are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
