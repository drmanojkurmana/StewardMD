What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • ABO acute haemolytic reaction. Preformed recipient IgM against donor A or B antigens fixes complement on donor red cells, causing rapid intravascular haemolysis, free haemoglobin, hypotension, disseminated intravascular coagulation and kidney injury. Fever, flank pain and dark urine are classic teaching features. This is standard teaching in transfusion medicine and haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Febrile and allergic reactions. Febrile non-haemolytic reactions follow recipient antibody to donor leucocytes and cytokines that accumulate in stored components, producing fever and rigors without haemolysis. Allergic reactions follow recipient responses to donor plasma proteins, with histamine-mediated urticaria and flushing; severe reactions can progress to anaphylaxis with airway swelling and bronchospasm. This is standard teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • TRALI and TACO. In TRALI (transfusion-related acute lung injury), donor anti-leucocyte antibodies and other mediators activate neutrophils sequestered in lung capillaries; the injured capillary leaks protein-rich fluid into alveoli, causing hypoxaemia without circulatory overload. In TACO (transfusion-associated circulatory overload), transfused volume raises cardiac filling pressures, especially with cardiac or renal susceptibility, causing cardiogenic pulmonary oedema with raised venous pressure and hypertension. Telling them apart matters because a diuretic helps TACO and not TRALI. This is standard teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Delayed haemolytic reaction. A previously sensitised recipient mounts an anamnestic IgG response to a minor donor red-cell antigen (classically Kidd) days after transfusion. Antibody-coated donor cells are removed extravascularly, mainly in the spleen, with jaundice, fever and an unexpectedly falling haemoglobin. This is standard teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Stopping the transfusion is the first step in any acute reaction and prevents more incompatible or offending component entering. Fluids support the circulation and kidney perfusion in haemolysis but can worsen lung fluid in TRALI and TACO. Antihistamine with steroid acts on allergic and febrile features. A diuretic unloads the circulation in TACO. Adrenaline for anaphylaxis, oxygen and ventilatory support, and component rechecking are essential in practice but are only noted here. They are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Transfusion-associated graft-versus-host disease, post-transfusion purpura, bacterial contamination, iron overload, alloimmunisation without haemolysis, hypotensive reactions, oxygen and ventilation, adrenaline, antipyretics, laboratory rechecking and reporting, and all doses and thresholds are left out.
