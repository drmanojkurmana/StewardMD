What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Two defects, one weak plug. Primary haemostasis: platelets stick to exposed collagen through von Willebrand factor (vWF, platelet GPIb), activate and clump into a plug. In von Willebrand disease vWF is low or works poorly, so platelets stick badly, mucosal bleeding and heavy periods are typical, and bleeding time is prolonged. Secondary haemostasis: the intrinsic cascade (XII, XI, IX with cofactor VIII) feeds factor X, thrombin and fibrin, which stabilises the plug. Missing factor VIII (haemophilia A) or IX (haemophilia B, Christmas disease) gives haemophilia: the plug forms but is not stabilised, so bleeding is deep, into joints (haemarthrosis) and muscles, often after a delay, and aPTT is prolonged because aPTT tests the intrinsic arm. PT, which tests the extrinsic arm, stays normal in both. This is standard teaching in haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Factor replacement supplies the missing VIII or IX (aPTT and fibrin correct, but a pure factor concentrate does not fix a vWF adhesion defect, so bleeding time can stay prolonged). Desmopressin releases stored vWF and factor VIII from vessel lining cells, helping most in mild disease and much less when little is stored, as in severe disease. Avoiding NSAIDs protects platelet function, because NSAIDs block platelet cyclo-oxygenase, so it eases the platelet side but does not correct the cascade or aPTT. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Haemophilia carriers and inheritance (X linked), inhibitors against replaced factor, the types and laboratory subtyping of von Willebrand disease, acquired von Willebrand syndrome, platelet receptor detail, tranexamic acid and other adjuncts, prophylaxis schedules, joint damage over time, and all doses and factor levels are left out. One vessel and one joint stand for many bleeding sites, and counts and speeds are illustrative. The figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
