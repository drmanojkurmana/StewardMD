What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Normal production and how leukaemia blocks it. Haematopoietic stem cells in the marrow make progenitor cells that mature along myeloid and lymphoid lines into red cells, neutrophils, other white cells and platelets. In leukaemia a single altered stem or progenitor cell expands as a clone. In AML (acute myeloid leukaemia) and ALL (acute lymphoblastic leukaemia) immature blasts accumulate and normal maturation is blocked, so the marrow fails quickly, causing anaemia, infection from lack of neutrophils, and bleeding from lack of platelets. This is standard teaching in haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • The chronic leukaemias. In CML (chronic myeloid leukaemia) a swap between chromosomes 9 and 22 (the Philadelphia translocation) joins BCR to ABL and creates a constantly active kinase that drives excess myeloid cell production; the disease can progress from a chronic phase with few blasts to blast crisis, which behaves like an acute leukaemia. In CLL (chronic lymphocytic leukaemia) long lived, mature looking lymphocytes accumulate slowly and marrow failure, when it comes, is late. This is standard teaching in haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Chemotherapy kills dividing cells including blasts. A targeted kinase inhibitor blocks the BCR-ABL kinase, so it helps in CML where that target is present and does little where it is absent. Stem cell transplant replaces the marrow stem cell population after the leukaemic marrow is cleared. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Subtypes and risk groups, cytogenetic and molecular variants beyond the Philadelphia change, staging systems, lymph node and spleen enlargement, immune dysfunction in CLL, tumour lysis, transplant conditioning and graft effects, and all drug choices and doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
