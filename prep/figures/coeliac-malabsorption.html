What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How coeliac disease develops. In people with HLA-DQ2 or HLA-DQ8, gliadin peptides from dietary gluten resist digestion and cross the small bowel epithelium. In the lamina propria the enzyme tissue transglutaminase deamidates gliadin, which makes it bind HLA-DQ2 or DQ8 on antigen-presenting cells much more strongly. Those cells activate gluten-specific CD4 T cells. T-cell cytokines and cytotoxic attack injure enterocytes, producing villous atrophy with crypt hyperplasia and a flat mucosa that absorbs iron, folate, fat and other nutrients poorly. Antibodies to tissue transglutaminase and to endomysium are markers of the same response. This is standard teaching in gastroenterology and immunology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. A lifelong gluten-free diet removes the trigger and lets the mucosa recover. Nutrient replacement corrects deficiencies such as iron and folate while the mucosa is damaged, but on its own it does not stop the immune attack or heal villi. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Increased epithelial permeability pathways, intraepithelial lymphocyte killing in detail, B-cell maturation, dermatitis herpetiformis, refractory coeliac disease, lymphoma risk, biopsy grading, antibody test performance, dietetic support and all doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
