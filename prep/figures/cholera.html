What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How cholera causes diarrhoea. Vibrio cholerae is swallowed, survives to the small bowel and colonises the enterocyte surface without invading it. Cholera toxin enters the enterocyte and locks a stimulatory G protein in its active form, so adenylate cyclase stays on and cyclic AMP rises. Cyclic AMP opens CFTR chloride channels on the luminal membrane and reduces sodium chloride absorption. Chloride leaves into the lumen, sodium and water follow, and the result is profuse watery rice-water stool with rapid dehydration. This is standard teaching in medicine, physiology and infectious-disease texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Oral rehydration solution uses sodium glucose co-transport, which cyclic AMP does not switch off, to pull sodium and water back into the blood. Intravenous fluids refill the circulation directly when dehydration is severe or drinking is not possible. An antibiotic can shorten the illness and reduce bacterial shedding in cholera, but it does not replace lost fluid. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Gastric acid defence, toxin subunit structure in detail, other chloride and sodium transporters, potassium and bicarbonate loss, dehydration examination signs and fluid calculations, ORS composition, resistant organisms, vaccination, and all doses and targets are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
