What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • The synthesis steps and the drugs. Iodide uptake (NIS), thyroid peroxidase, hormone release and peripheral T4-to-T3 conversion are the four steps; perchlorate blocks the first, thionamides the second, iodide and lithium the third, and propylthiouracil, propranolol and glucocorticoids the fourth; levothyroxine replaces the hormone. This is standard teaching in the pharmacology texts the PrepNucleus lessons cite (Katzung; KD Tripathi) and were not checked against a linked source here, so they are flagged for review.

What was simplified. The pituitary and TSH feedback (which raises the gland's drive when the hormone falls and is the main way levothyroxine is monitored), the Wolff-Chaikoff and Jod-Basedow effects, thyroid storm, pregnancy, radioactive iodine, selenium, the different actions of propranolol on symptoms, and drug adverse effects are left out. In the figure, thionamides and conversion blockers are drawn as partial blocks because dose is titrated to a normal level. Levels and the size of each effect are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
