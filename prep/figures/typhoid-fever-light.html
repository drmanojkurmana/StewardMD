What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How typhoid fever develops. Salmonella Typhi is taken in with contaminated food or water, reaches the small bowel and invades the lining over the Peyer patches. It is taken up by macrophages but can survive inside them, riding to lymph tissue and into the blood. The resulting bacteraemia gives a fever that climbs in steps and then stays high, with faint pink rose spots on the trunk. As the swollen Peyer patches break down, the bowel wall ulcerates and may bleed or perforate, usually later in the illness. After recovery, bacteria may persist in the gallbladder and be shed in stool as a carrier state. This is standard teaching in medicine and infectious-disease texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. An antibiotic clears bacteria from the blood and bowel; fluids alone support the patient without clearing the infection; surgery is shown for perforation, closing the hole without clearing bacteria elsewhere. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Stomach acid and dose effects, lymph-node and bone-marrow spread in detail, relative bradycardia and blood-count changes, bowel bleeding as a separate event, resistance patterns, vaccine and public-health measures, and all doses and drug choices are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
