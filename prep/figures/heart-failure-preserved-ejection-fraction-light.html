What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How this type of heart failure develops. Long-standing hypertension, diabetes, obesity and ageing promote concentric hypertrophy: the left ventricular wall thickens inward and the chamber stays small. The thick muscle relaxes slowly and is stiff, so filling needs a higher pressure even though the squeeze, and therefore the ejection fraction, stays preserved. Raised filling pressure enlarges the left atrium and backs up into the pulmonary veins and lungs, giving breathlessness and congestion. This is standard teaching in medicine and cardiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Where treatments act in the drawing. A diuretic removes salt and water and lowers congestion and filling pressure. Blood pressure control lowers the load the wall thickened against and slows further hypertrophy. SGLT2 inhibitors are shown as a concept: they ease congestion and are part of modern teaching for this condition. These are teaching toggles for the mechanism only.
  • Contrast with reduced ejection fraction. In that type the ventricle is dilated with a thin wall and a weak squeeze, so the ejection fraction falls. Here the chamber is small and thick and the squeeze is preserved; the problem is filling, not emptying.

What was simplified. Atrial fibrillation, pulmonary hypertension and right heart involvement, kidney disease, anaemia, amyloidosis and other specific causes, valve disease, natriuretic peptides, diastolic grading, exercise testing, and every dose and target are left out. Sizes, speeds and levels are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
