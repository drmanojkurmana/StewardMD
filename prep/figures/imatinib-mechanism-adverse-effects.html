What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How imatinib stops the tumour. Imatinib occupies the ATP pocket of inactive BCR-ABL (and also KIT and PDGFR), so substrate phosphorylation and downstream signalling stop and CML and GIST cells die. The T315I gatekeeper change blocks drug binding while sparing ATP, so the clone resists. This is standard teaching in oncology and pharmacology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Why the side effects happen. PDGFR blockade loosens vessel walls and causes fluid retention with periorbital oedema; marrow, gut and muscle effects give cytopenias, nausea and cramps; the liver strains under drug breakdown; rarely the heart weakens. Diuretics ease swelling and organ checks catch liver and heart injury early. This is standard teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Diuretics for fluid retention plus regular organ checks for liver, heart and blood counts. These are teaching toggles only.
