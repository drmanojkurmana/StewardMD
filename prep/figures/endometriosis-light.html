What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How endometriosis develops. Endometriosis is endometrial-like tissue growing outside the uterine cavity, most often on the pelvic peritoneum, ovaries and the tissues behind the uterus. One route is retrograde menstruation, where menstrual tissue passes back through the fallopian tubes into the pelvis and implants; immune, hormonal and inherited factors also play a part, and retrograde flow alone does not explain every case. The implants respond to the hormonal cycle, bleed in place, and provoke inflammation. Repeated bleeding causes scarring and adhesions that can distort the tubes and ovaries. On an ovary, repeated bleeding can form an endometrioma, often called a chocolate cyst because of its old blood content. Typical features are period pain, pain during sex, chronic pelvic pain and subfertility. This is standard teaching in gynaecology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Pain relief blocks pain signals without changing the implants. Hormonal suppression pauses the cycling that drives bleeding and inflammation while it is used. Surgery removes or destroys implants, divides adhesions and can remove an endometrioma. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Other proposed origins of implants, staging scores in detail, bowel and bladder disease, infertility investigation, recurrence after treatment, drug choices and dosing, and fertility treatment are left out.
