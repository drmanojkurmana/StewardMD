What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How salicylate poisoning works. Salicylate directly stimulates the respiratory centre, so ventilation rises and carbon dioxide is blown off, giving a respiratory alkalosis. It also uncouples oxidative phosphorylation in mitochondria, so energy is released as heat rather than stored, oxygen use rises, and metabolic acids accumulate, giving a high anion gap metabolic acidosis that can dominate later. Hyperthermia and tinnitus are classic features. This is standard teaching in medicine and toxicology and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Why acid makes it worse. In acid conditions more salicylate is in the non-ionised form that crosses membranes into tissues and the brain, so tissue acidosis worsens toxicity. In alkaline urine more salicylate is ionised and trapped in the urine for excretion. This is standard teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Activated charcoal in the gut to reduce absorption, urinary alkalinisation to trap salicylate in alkaline urine and limit tissue entry, and dialysis to clear salicylate directly from the blood. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Mixed acid base detail over time, dehydration and electrolyte disturbance, kidney and lung injury, cerebral and pulmonary oedema, assessment and monitoring, antidote dosing, and all doses, timings and targets are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
