What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How blood is made. Haematopoietic stem cells sit in a marrow niche of stromal, bone and vessel cells that hold and instruct them. A stem cell gives a common myeloid progenitor and a common lymphoid progenitor. Erythropoietin, made mainly by the kidney when oxygen delivery falls, drives red cell production. Thrombopoietin, made mainly by the liver, drives megakaryocytes to release platelets. Colony stimulating factors drive granulocytes and monocytes and rise in infection and stress. Lymphoid progenitors give lymphocytes. Mature cells leave the marrow and enter the blood. This is standard teaching in haematology and physiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. A growth factor boosts progenitor output in the lines it acts on, but it cannot rebuild an empty stem cell pool. A transplant replaces the stem cell pool itself, so every lineage can recover. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Detailed progenitor stages, other growth factors and interleukins, iron and vitamin requirements, spleen roles, cell lifespans, laboratory counts and all drug names, doses and transplant procedures are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
