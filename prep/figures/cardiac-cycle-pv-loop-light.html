What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Phases and valves. During atrial systole the atria top up ventricular filling through the open mitral valve. The ventricle then contracts with both valves shut (isovolumetric contraction) until its pressure opens the aortic valve and ejection follows. When the ventricle relaxes and its pressure falls, the aortic valve shuts; pressure keeps falling with both valves shut (isovolumetric relaxation) until the mitral valve opens and filling resumes. This is standard teaching in physiology and cardiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Heart sounds and the loop. The first heart sound follows closure of the mitral and tricuspid valves at the start of ventricular contraction; the second follows closure of the aortic and pulmonary valves at the start of relaxation. On the pressure-volume loop, filling runs along the bottom, isovolumetric contraction up the right side, ejection across the top and isovolumetric relaxation down the left side; the width of the loop is the stroke volume. Extra filling widens the loop to the right, tight arteries raise and narrow it, and an inotrope empties the ventricle further so the loop widens to the left and reaches a higher pressure. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. The right heart, the tricuspid and pulmonary valves, atrial and ventricular pressure traces over time, valve timing differences, coronary flow, conduction timing and every pressure and volume value are left out. Only the left heart is drawn, the loop is a smooth schematic, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
