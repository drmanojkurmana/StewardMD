What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How carbon monoxide poisons. Carbon monoxide is breathed in, crosses the lung and binds haemoglobin much more strongly than oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin. Fewer sites carry oxygen and the oxygen haemoglobin curve shifts left, so the oxygen that is carried is released less readily to tissues. Carbon monoxide also binds cytochrome oxidase in mitochondria and impairs cell energy production. A standard pulse oximeter cannot tell carboxyhaemoglobin from oxyhaemoglobin, so its reading can look normal while tissue delivery is poor. Symptoms range from headache and dizziness to confusion, collapse and coma, and delayed neurological sequelae can follow apparent recovery. This is standard teaching in medicine, physiology and toxicology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Removing the source and giving high flow oxygen speeds clearance of carbon monoxide from haemoglobin. Hyperbaric oxygen, where available and selected, speeds clearance further and is shown as a teaching toggle. Supportive care supports airway, breathing and circulation without directly clearing carbon monoxide. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Carbon monoxide from different sources, myoglobin binding, inflammation and reperfusion injury after poisoning, exact indications for hyperbaric treatment, pregnancy and fetal haemoglobin, investigation thresholds, and all doses, pressures and durations are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
