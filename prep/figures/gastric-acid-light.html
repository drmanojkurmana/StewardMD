What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • The three signals and the pump. Histamine (H2), gastrin (CCK2) and acetylcholine (M3) all stimulate the parietal cell, and the H⁺/K⁺ ATPase is the final step; H2 blockers and antimuscarinics each block one input, and proton pump inhibitors bind the pump irreversibly. This is standard teaching in the pharmacology texts the PrepNucleus lessons cite (Katzung; KD Tripathi) and were not checked against a linked source here, so they are flagged for review.

What was simplified. Somatostatin and prostaglandin inhibition, the mucosal defence (mucus, bicarbonate), Helicobacter pylori, antacids and sucralfate, the delay and acid-activation of proton pump inhibitors (which are best taken before meals), rebound acid, nocturnal acid and the effect of long-term use are left out. The three inputs are drawn as equal in strength for teaching; real contributions vary. Levels and the size of each block are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
