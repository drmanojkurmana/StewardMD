What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Where each class acts. Non-depolarising blockers compete with acetylcholine at the nicotinic receptor, suxamethonium depolarises the end plate and holds it so it cannot respond, and anticholinesterases such as neostigmine raise acetylcholine in the gap to reverse a non-depolarising block. This is standard teaching in the pharmacology texts the PrepNucleus lessons cite (Katzung; KD Tripathi) and were not checked against a linked source here, so they are flagged for review.

What was simplified. Fasciculations and phase II block, plasma cholinesterase and prolonged suxamethonium action, sugammadex, antimuscarinic cover with reversal, train-of-four monitoring, drug and disease interactions, myasthenia and adverse effects are left out. Contraction is drawn as a teaching score. Levels and the size of each effect are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
