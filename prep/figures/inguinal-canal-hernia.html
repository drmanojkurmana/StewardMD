What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Anatomy shown. The inguinal canal is an oblique passage in the lower abdominal wall. It opens at the deep inguinal ring laterally and the superficial inguinal ring medially, has an anterior wall and a posterior wall, and transmits the spermatic cord in this drawing. The inferior epigastric vessels lie medial to the deep ring. Hesselbach triangle is bounded by those vessels laterally, the rectus abdominis medially and the inguinal ligament below. This is standard teaching in anatomy and surgery texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Hernia ideas shown. An indirect hernia enters through the deep ring, lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels, and follows the canal, sometimes reaching the scrotum. A direct hernia pushes forward through a weak posterior wall inside Hesselbach triangle, medial to the vessels. A reducible hernia can be pushed back, an irreducible hernia is trapped outside, and a strangulated hernia has its blood supply cut off, with swelling and bowel obstruction. A truss can only hold a reducible hernia in and does not cure it. Elective repair strengthens the posterior wall for a hernia that is not strangulated, while strangulation needs emergency surgery. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Femoral hernias, the detailed layers and muscles forming each wall, conjoint tendon and reflexions, cord structures in detail, women and the round ligament, infant and congenital cases, operative technique, mesh choices, complications and all clinical decision rules are left out. The two sacs are shown together to compare routes and would not usually be drawn as one patient. The figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
