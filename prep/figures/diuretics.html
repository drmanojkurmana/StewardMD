What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Loop diuretics. They inhibit the Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ cotransporter NKCC2 on the luminal membrane of the thick ascending limb; without a hypertonic medulla water has less pull to leave the collecting duct, so urine flow rises. They also disturb potassium recycling and so magnesium and calcium reabsorption (Loop diuretics, NCBI Bookshelf).
  • Thiazides. Hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone and indapamide reduce sodium reabsorption in the distal convoluted tubule by blocking the sodium-chloride cotransporter (Thiazide diuretics, StatPearls).
  • Aldosterone antagonists. Spironolactone blocks the mineralocorticoid receptor, so aldosterone has less effect on the collecting duct (Spironolactone, StatPearls).
  • Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors and ENaC blockers. Acetazolamide inhibits carbonic anhydrase in the proximal tubule and so bicarbonate reabsorption, making the urine alkaline; amiloride blocks ENaC in the late distal tubule and collecting duct. This is standard teaching in the pharmacology texts the PrepNucleus lessons cite (Katzung's Basic and Clinical Pharmacology; KD Tripathi, Essentials of Medical Pharmacology) and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.

What was simplified. The shares of sodium returned by each segment and the size of each drug's block are illustrative and are not shown as numbers; real values vary with the person, the diet and the dose. Compensation (more reabsorption downstream after a block, the effect of lost volume on renin and aldosterone), potassium, magnesium, calcium, uric acid and acid-base effects, the secretion of loop and thiazide diuretics into the tubule, tolerance, osmotic diuretics (mannitol), vasopressin antagonists and SGLT2 inhibitors are all left out. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
