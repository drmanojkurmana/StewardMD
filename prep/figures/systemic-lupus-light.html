What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How lupus injures tissues. In systemic lupus erythematosus, tolerance to nuclear self antigens is lost. Nuclear material from damaged and dying cells is presented to helper T cells, T cells help B cells, and B cells make anti-nuclear and related autoantibodies. Antibody and nuclear antigen form immune complexes that circulate, deposit in small vessels and tissues, activate complement and recruit inflammation. Complement is consumed, so complement levels fall in active disease. Deposition and inflammation affect skin and joints, the kidney glomeruli, blood cells and other organs. This is standard teaching in immunology, rheumatology and pathology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Hydroxychloroquine dampens immune activation and autoantibody drive over time. A steroid calms tissue inflammation quickly while autoantibodies and complexes persist more. An immunosuppressant suppresses B cell and T cell activity more strongly, so autoantibodies, complexes and organ inflammation fall. These are teaching toggles only and name no individual drug beyond the classes requested or any dose.

What was simplified. Genetics and environmental triggers, interferon pathways, neutrophil extracellular traps, individual autoantibody specificities, complement genetics, organ specific staging and biopsy classes, infection and other treatment risks, and all doses and targets are left out. Blood involvement is shown only as falling cell counts. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
