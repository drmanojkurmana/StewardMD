What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How psoriasis develops. In susceptible skin, triggers and injury activate dendritic cells. Their IL-23 sustains Th17 cells, and Th17 cytokines IL-17 and TNF act on keratinocytes, which proliferate and mature too fast. The epidermis thickens with elongated rete ridges, the granular layer is lost, and nuclei persist in the stratum corneum (parakeratosis), giving a silvery scaly plaque over dilated dermal vessels. Pinpoint bleeding when scale is lifted is the Auspitz sign; new plaques at sites of trauma are the Koebner phenomenon; a related inflammatory arthritis can affect joints. This is standard teaching in dermatology and internal medicine texts covering eczema, psoriasis and other common skin disorders, including topical agents and systemic options for characteristic lesions, and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Topical steroid with a vitamin D agent calms inflammation and slows keratinocyte growth on the skin, phototherapy dampens cutaneous immune activity, and a biologic that targets TNF, IL-17 or IL-23 interrupts the cytokine axis and can also help joint disease. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Genetic susceptibility, triggers such as infection and medicines, the full cytokine network, nail disease, plaque subtypes, cardiovascular and metabolic associations, infection and malignancy screening for systemic therapy, and all doses and product choices are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
