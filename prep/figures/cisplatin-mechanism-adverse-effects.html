What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How cisplatin kills tumour cells. Inside the cell cisplatin loses its chloride groups and binds DNA, linking two neighbouring bases on the same strand. The bend stalls replication and the cell dies by apoptosis. This is standard teaching in oncology and pharmacology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Why the side effects happen. Kidney tubule cells pull cisplatin in through the OCT2 transporter, where it makes radicals; hydration flushes the tubules and magnesium replaces what the kidney wastes. Hair cells of the inner ear, sensory nerves, and the gut serotonin plus brain substance P sickness pathway are injured in the same courses. Antiemetics that block 5-HT3 and NK1 calm vomiting. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Other harms (marrow suppression, allergy, vein irritation), monitoring (hearing tests, kidney checks, magnesium checks), exact schedules, other platinum drugs, and all doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
