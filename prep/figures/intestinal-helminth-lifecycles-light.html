What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Roundworm and hookworm. Ascaris eggs are swallowed from soil, food or hands. They hatch in the small bowel, larvae pass through the bowel wall and blood to the lungs, break into the air spaces, are coughed up and swallowed, and mature in the gut. Hookworm eggs hatch in soil; infective larvae penetrate skin, usually of the feet, travel in blood to the lungs and then to the gut, where adults attach to the bowel wall and feed on blood, causing iron loss and anaemia. This is standard teaching in parasitology and medicine texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Taenia solium, cysticercosis and Strongyloides. Eating undercooked pork containing Taenia solium cysts gives an adult tapeworm in the gut. Swallowing Taenia eggs shed by a human tapeworm carrier gives cysticercosis, with larvae forming cysts in tissues such as muscle and brain; that is a different outcome from having the adult worm. Strongyloides larvae can become infective inside the gut and re-enter through the bowel wall or skin around the anus, so infection can persist without new exposure from soil (autoinfection). Treatment ideas shown are by class and prevention only: albendazole, praziquantel, and sanitation with footwear. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Worm burden, species other than those named, larval lung symptoms, seizures and eye disease from cysts, immune suppression and severe Strongyloides infection, drug choice by species and site, resistance, and all doses and schedules are left out.
