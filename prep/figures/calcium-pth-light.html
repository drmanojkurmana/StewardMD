What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Calcium and PTH physiology. Extracellular calcium concentration is sensed by the G-protein-coupled calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) on parathyroid chief cells. When calcium falls, CaSR inhibition is relieved, triggering secretion of parathyroid hormone (PTH). PTH acts directly on the bone (stimulating osteoclasts to resorb bone mineral, releasing calcium and phosphate) and the kidney (enhancing distal tubular calcium reabsorption and proximal tubular phosphate excretion, while stimulating 1-alpha-hydroxylase to convert 25-hydroxyvitamin D to active 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D / calcitriol). Calcitriol in turn drives active intestinal absorption of dietary calcium and phosphate, and feeds back to suppress PTH synthesis. This is standard teaching in endocrinology and physiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Hypo- versus hyperparathyroidism and calcium states. In hypoparathyroidism (often post-surgical or autoimmune), lack of PTH causes hypocalcaemia, hyperphosphataemia, low calcitriol, and neuromuscular irritability (tetany, carpopedal spasm, Chvostek and Trousseau signs). In secondary hyperparathyroidism (vitamin D deficiency or chronic renal failure), hypocalcaemia elicits a compensatory surge in PTH, causing bone demineralization (rickets or osteomalacia). In primary hyperparathyroidism (usually a benign solitary adenoma), autonomous PTH drives hypercalcaemia, hypophosphataemia, hypercalciuria (nephrolithiasis / kidney stones), and bone resorption ('bones, stones, abdominal groans, and psychic moans'). Flagged for review.
  • Conceptual treatments shown. Calcium and active vitamin D (calcitriol) to treat hypocalcaemia; volume expansion with intravenous saline to promote calciuresis in hypercalcaemia; bisphosphonates to inhibit osteoclasts and halt bone breakdown; calcimimetics (such as cinacalcet) to allosterically stimulate parathyroid CaSRs and turn down PTH; and surgical parathyroidectomy to excise the adenoma. These are teaching toggles only, with no doses or drug regimens.
