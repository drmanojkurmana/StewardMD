What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Plaques, tangles and cell loss. In Alzheimer disease, amyloid beta peptide collects outside neurons as plaques, and abnormally phosphorylated tau protein collects inside neurons as neurofibrillary tangles. Synapses fail and neurons die, including basal forebrain cholinergic neurons that supply acetylcholine to the cortex and hippocampus. Brain shrinkage (atrophy) tends to involve the hippocampus early, with wider cortical thinning later, and the fluid spaces (ventricles) enlarge as tissue is lost. This is standard teaching in neurology, pathology and pharmacology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Cholinesterase inhibitors slow the breakdown of acetylcholine by acetylcholinesterase, so more of the remaining signal reaches the synapse. NMDA blockers damp excess glutamate signalling through NMDA receptors, which may otherwise add to neuron injury. Neither treatment is shown removing plaques or tangles or restoring lost neurons. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Genetics and risk factors, amyloid production and clearance pathways in detail, microglial and vascular contributions, other dementias, brain imaging and biomarkers, disease modifying antibody treatments, and all doses and drug choices are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
