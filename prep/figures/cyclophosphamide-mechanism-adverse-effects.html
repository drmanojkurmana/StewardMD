What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How cyclophosphamide works. Cyclophosphamide is a prodrug switched on by liver CYP enzymes into phosphoramide mustard, which cross-links the two strands of DNA so cells cannot copy them, and acrolein, which is toxic. Tumour cells die, and fast-dividing normal tissues suffer too. This is standard teaching in pharmacology and oncology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Side effects shown. Acrolein passing in urine causes haemorrhagic cystitis, prevented by mesna (which binds acrolein) plus fluids; marrow suppression as blood counts fall; SIADH with the kidney holding water; infertility from gonad harm; and, late on, second cancers from mutated healthy DNA. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Other toxicities such as nausea, hair loss, immune suppression and bladder effects beyond bleeding, combination regimens, the exact timing of count nadirs and recovery, and all doses and schedules are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
