What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How lung cancer develops and differs by type. Inhaled carcinogens, most often from tobacco smoke, damage airway lining DNA. Small cell cancer arises from neuroendocrine cells, is usually central, grows and spreads early, and can release ADH like and ACTH like activity causing SIADH and cortisol excess. Squamous cancer is usually central, can cavitate, and can release PTHrP causing raised calcium. Adenocarcinoma is usually peripheral and arises in glandular tissue. Large cell cancer is a poorly differentiated non small cell type, often peripheral. Local growth can obstruct the superior vena cava or, at the apex, cause Pancoast effects. This is standard teaching in medicine and oncology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Surgery can remove a localised non small cell tumour but rarely helps small cell cancer that has already spread. Chemotherapy with radiotherapy is a main approach for small cell and is also used in non small cell disease. Targeted therapy can control adenocarcinomas with a targetable driver change. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Staging, imaging, biopsy and molecular testing, immunotherapy, screening, performance status, operative fitness, specific drug choice and all doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
