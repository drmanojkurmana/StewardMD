What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How the heartbeat is conducted. The sinoatrial (SA) node fires first. The signal spreads across the atria (the P wave), is held briefly at the atrioventricular (AV) node so the ventricles can fill, then travels down the bundle of His, the two bundle branches and the Purkinje fibres to depolarise the ventricles together (the narrow QRS). The T wave is the ventricles resetting. In complete heart block no signal crosses the AV node, so atria and ventricles beat independently and a slow, wider escape rhythm drives the ventricles. In atrial fibrillation the atria quiver chaotically, only some signals reach the ventricles, and the QRS is irregularly irregular; blood pooling in the left atrial appendage can form a clot that embolises to the brain. This is standard teaching in medicine and cardiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Rate control slows conduction through the AV node. Rhythm control tries to restore sinus rhythm. A pacemaker supplies the missing ventricular beat. An anticoagulant lowers the stroke risk from AF. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Other rhythms (flutter, supraventricular and ventricular tachycardias, long QT, pre-excitation), accessory pathways, drug classes and their cautions, cardioversion details, risk scores for anticoagulation, bleeding risk, pacemaker modes and all doses and targets are left out. The ECG is one schematic lead, and rates and timing are illustrative. The figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
