What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How cervical cancer develops. Persistent infection of the cervical transformation zone with high risk human papillomavirus types, most often HPV 16 and HPV 18, is the central cause. Virus reaches basal epithelial cells. Viral E6 protein targets the tumour suppressor p53 for degradation, and viral E7 protein binds and inactivates Rb. Loss of these controls lets damaged cells survive and divide. Abnormal cells first occupy part of the epithelium in cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN 1 to CIN 3), then breach the basement membrane as invasive carcinoma, usually squamous cell carcinoma. This is standard teaching in pathology, gynaecology and oncology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Prevention, screening and treatment ideas shown. HPV vaccination prevents new high risk infection. Screening by cervical cytology and by HPV testing finds precancer so it can be treated. LEEP excision removes the transformation zone in CIN. Invasive cancer is shown treated by surgery and by chemoradiation. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Other HPV types, immune clearance and regression of CIN, adenocarcinoma and glandular lesions, staging, imaging, biopsy, fertility sparing choices, radiotherapy planning, chemotherapy choice, HIV and immune suppression, and all doses and test intervals are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any person should be screened or treated.
