What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Why the cells become large. Folate, helped by vitamin B12, supplies one carbon units needed to build thymine and other DNA building blocks. Without enough of either vitamin, DNA copying slows while RNA and protein making in the cytoplasm carries on. The nucleus lags behind the cytoplasm (nuclear cytoplasmic asynchrony), marrow precursors grow large and divide fewer times, and the blood shows large red cells (macrocytes, raised mean cell volume) and neutrophils with extra nucleus lobes (hypersegmented neutrophils). This is standard teaching in haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • How B12 is absorbed, and pernicious anaemia. Vitamin B12 from food binds intrinsic factor, a protein made by parietal cells of the stomach. The combined complex is absorbed in the terminal ileum. In pernicious anaemia the immune system attacks parietal cells or intrinsic factor itself, so intrinsic factor is missing and B12 cannot be absorbed even when the diet contains enough. Folate is absorbed higher in the small bowel and does not need intrinsic factor. This is standard teaching in haematology and gastroenterology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Nerves, and the treatment trap. Vitamin B12 is also needed to keep myelin healthy, so B12 deficiency can damage the spinal cord and peripheral nerves, while folate deficiency alone does not do this. Giving folate to a person who lacks B12 can repair the blood picture while nerve damage carries on, which is why the missing vitamin must be identified. Treatment choices in the figure are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Causes of folate and B12 lack beyond diet and pernicious anaemia (bowel disease and surgery, medicines, pregnancy demand, alcohol), homocysteine and methylmalonic acid testing, antibodies and gastric biopsy, other blood lineages and pancytopenia, glossitis and other clinical features, how B12 replacement is given in practice, and all doses and schedules are left out.
