What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How hepatitis B infects and injures the liver. The virion binds the NTCP bile-acid transporter on a hepatocyte and enters. Its partly double-stranded DNA is completed and kept in the nucleus as cccDNA, a stable mini-chromosome that host enzymes transcribe into viral RNAs. One RNA (pregenomic RNA) is packaged with the viral polymerase, which reverse-transcribes it back to DNA; new virions and large numbers of empty HBsAg particles are then secreted. The virus itself does little direct damage: liver injury is mainly immune-mediated, as cytotoxic T cells kill infected hepatocytes. Strong, broad immunity clears HBsAg and brings anti-HBs (resolution); weak or tolerant immunity leaves chronic infection, and years of injury and repair lay down scar tissue (cirrhosis), which raises the risk of liver failure and hepatocellular carcinoma. This is standard teaching in medicine, hepatology and infectious-disease texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Markers and treatment ideas shown. HBsAg marks current infection and persists in chronic disease; anti-HBs marks recovery and vaccine immunity; HBeAg marks active viral replication and infectivity, and its loss with anti-HBe is seroconversion. Nucleoside analogues block the viral polymerase and suppress replication and inflammation, but they rarely clear cccDNA or HBsAg, so stopping can relapse. Vaccine induces anti-HBs and prevents infection; it does not clear established chronic infection. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Mother-to-child and other transmission routes, genotypes, HDV coinfection, occult infection, HBeAg-negative variants in detail, liver biopsy and elastography, transplant, hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance and treatment, resistance, and all doses, regimens and monitoring targets are left out. The marker curves are generic illustrations of each course and are not predictions for any person.
