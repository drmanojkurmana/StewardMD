What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How COVID-19 develops. SARS-CoV-2 is a betacoronavirus first recognised in an outbreak of respiratory illness. Its spike protein binds ACE2 on airway and alveolar cells and TMPRSS2 primes spike for entry, then the virus replicates in the respiratory tract. The innate response recruits macrophages and neutrophils and releases cytokines. In severe disease in older adults and others at risk, a strong cytokine response, diffuse alveolar damage with hyaline membranes and flooded alveoli, and endothelitis with microthrombi impair gas exchange and cause hypoxia, sometimes needing ventilation. Variants differ in transmission and severity. This is standard teaching on coronavirus infection in humans and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Supportive oxygen helps hypoxic disease without fixing the alveolar damage. Dexamethasone calms inflammation in hypoxic disease. An antiviral given early limits viral replication. These are teaching toggles only and show direction of effect, not how any patient should be treated.

What was simplified. Variant specific behaviour, adaptive immunity and antibodies, thrombosis beyond microthrombi, bacterial superinfection, organ effects outside the lung, vaccination, and all doses, timing windows and thresholds are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
