What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How influenza infects the airway. Haemagglutinin on the virion binds sialic acid on respiratory epithelial cells, the virion enters by endocytosis, acidification frees its segmented negative sense RNA genome, and replication and transcription happen in the nucleus, unusual for an RNA virus. New haemagglutinin and neuraminidase reach the cell surface, virions bud there, and neuraminidase clips sialic acid so progeny virions can leave and spread instead of sticking to the dying cell or to mucus. Infected epithelial cells die and are shed, cilia are lost, and the denuded airway is prone to secondary bacterial infection. Fever and systemic illness reflect inflammatory mediators, and complications occur primarily in the lung as primary viral pneumonia or secondary bacterial pneumonia, particularly in young children, older persons and people with chronic illness, where pulmonary findings and mortality are highest. Cleavage activation of haemagglutinin by host proteases is needed for infectivity. Immunity to haemagglutinin and neuraminidase protects, but antigenic drift (small changes) lets the virus partly escape it each season, and antigenic shift (reassortment of segments, for example in a co infected host) can produce a subtype with little population immunity and pandemic potential. This is standard teaching in medicine and infectious disease texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment and prevention ideas shown. Neuraminidase inhibitors block release and work best early, vaccine induced antibody to haemagglutinin blocks binding and prevents illness, and supportive care eases fever and breathing without a direct antiviral effect. These are teaching toggles only.
