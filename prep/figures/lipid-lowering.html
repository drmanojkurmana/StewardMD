What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Where each class acts. Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase and raise LDL receptors; ezetimibe blocks NPC1L1 absorption; bile acid sequestrants bind bile acids so the liver uses more cholesterol; PCSK9 inhibitors stop receptor destruction. This is standard teaching in the pharmacology texts the PrepNucleus lessons cite (Katzung; KD Tripathi) and were not checked against a linked source here, so they are flagged for review.

What was simplified. Triglyceride-lowering drugs (fibrates and omega-3), niacin, the effect of statins on HDL and triglycerides, VLDL and the lipoprotein pathway, hepatic LDL receptor feedback through SREBP, familial hypercholesterolaemia, pleiotropic effects of statins, adverse effects and interactions are left out. LDL cholesterol is drawn as a teaching score. Levels and the size of each effect are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
