What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • What cardiomyopathy means. A cardiomyopathy is disease of heart muscle where the cardiac muscle itself fails, without valve, coronary or pressure overload explaining it. The three classic patterns are dilated (ventricular dilatation, thin wall, systolic failure), hypertrophic (septal hypertrophy, dynamic outflow obstruction, diastolic dysfunction and sudden death risk) and restrictive (a stiff ventricle, classically from amyloid, with poor filling). This is standard teaching in medicine and cardiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Causes stressed for dilated disease. Multiple causes are implicated, often together in a patient with an initial insult and systemic involvement. Stressed examples are alcohol, viral myocarditis including COVID-19, inflammatory and immune disease including immune checkpoint therapy, systemic disease with cardiac involvement, and genetic disease. Myocarditis is inflammation of heart muscle that can heal, scar or progress to a dilated ventricle. These are teaching associations only.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Heart failure therapy supports a failing ventricle, a beta-blocker or septal myectomy relieves outflow obstruction in hypertrophic disease, and treating the cause means removing alcohol, treating myocarditis or treating amyloid and other systemic causes. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Genetic subtypes and testing, arrhythmia devices and anticoagulation, transplant and mechanical support, specific amyloid therapies, imaging measurements, exercise advice and all doses and drug choices are left out. The heart is a simple two chamber schematic and does not show the right ventricle separately. The figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
