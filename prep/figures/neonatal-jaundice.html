What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Why newborns become jaundiced. Newborn red cells turn over quickly and the newborn liver has immature conjugation (low UGT activity), so unconjugated bilirubin accumulates. Prematurity makes both worse. Breast milk can increase enterohepatic reabsorption and reduce conjugation, prolonging physiological jaundice. This is standard teaching in paediatrics and neonatology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Pathological causes and brain injury. Haemolysis from ABO or Rh incompatibility or G6PD deficiency greatly increases bilirubin production. Unconjugated bilirubin that is not bound to albumin can cross the blood brain barrier and injure basal ganglia and brainstem nuclei (kernicterus). This is standard teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Feeding and monitoring supports gut excretion and reduces reabsorption. Phototherapy converts bilirubin in skin and blood to forms that can be excreted without conjugation. Exchange transfusion removes bilirubin and, in haemolysis, antibody coated red cells. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Albumin binding detail, direct and conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia, cholestasis, sepsis and metabolic causes, gestational age charts, bilirubin thresholds, phototherapy irradiance and exchange volumes, and long term neurodevelopmental follow up are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any baby should be treated.
