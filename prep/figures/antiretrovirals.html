What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • The classes and where they act. Entry inhibitors interfere with HIV binding to CD4 and the chemokine co-receptors; nucleoside or nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitors compete with the enzyme and non-nucleoside ones bind it directly; integrase strand transfer inhibitors prevent integration into human DNA; protease inhibitors block the maturation of virions. Combinations of two to four drugs from different classes are usually needed to suppress replication fully (Antiretroviral treatment of HIV infection, MSD Manual Professional).

What was simplified. Pharmacokinetic boosters (ritonavir, cobicistat), the capsid inhibitors, attachment inhibitors, pre-exposure and post-exposure prophylaxis, resistance mutations, latent reservoirs, drug interactions and toxicities, and the immune recovery that follows are left out. Levels and the size of each block are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
