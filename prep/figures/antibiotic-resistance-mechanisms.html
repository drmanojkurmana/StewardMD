What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How resistance works. Bacteria resist antibiotics by destroying the drug (beta-lactamase breaks the beta-lactam ring), changing the target (altered penicillin-binding protein PBP2a in MRSA, or a modified ribosome binding site), pumping the drug out (efflux pumps), or letting less drug in (fewer or altered porin channels). Resistance genes often sit on plasmids and can pass between bacteria by conjugation. Under antibiotic selection pressure, susceptible bacteria die and resistant ones multiply and spread. This is standard teaching in microbiology and pharmacology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. A beta-lactamase inhibitor protects a beta-lactam from the enzyme but does not fix an altered target, efflux or a closed porin. A drug from a different class bypasses beta-lactam defences but can still be reduced by efflux or by modification of its own target. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Gene mutation and regulation, every enzyme and pump family, biofilms, persisters, tolerance, resistance in viruses and fungi, infection control, stewardship, and all doses and drug choices are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
