What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How rheumatoid arthritis damages a joint. In a genetically susceptible person, immune tolerance to self antigens (including citrullinated proteins) is lost. Presenting cells activate T cells, T cells help B cells, and B cells make autoantibodies such as rheumatoid factor and anti-CCP. T cells, B cells, macrophages and fibroblast-like synoviocytes release cytokines, chiefly TNF and IL-6. The synovial lining thickens (synovitis), forms invasive pannus, and releases enzymes. Pannus and osteoclasts erode cartilage and the bone beneath it, which is the permanent joint damage. This is standard teaching in rheumatology and pathology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. A DMARD slows the immune attack itself and protects the joint, but works slowly. A TNF blocker neutralises TNF, so TNF-driven inflammation, pannus and erosion fall while some IL-6 activity remains. A steroid calms inflammation and symptoms quickly, but pannus and erosion are much less well controlled, so damage can continue behind the relief. These are teaching toggles only and name no individual drug or dose.

What was simplified. Genetics and environmental triggers, citrullination in detail, complement, angiogenesis, individual DMARDs and biologics, infection and other treatment risks, extra-articular disease, and all doses and targets are left out. Existing erosion is shown as largely irreversible. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
