What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How arsenic trioxide works. In acute promyelocytic leukaemia the PML-RARa fusion protein clamps maturation genes shut. Arsenic trioxide binds the PML part, raises oxidative stress, and tags the fusion with SUMO and then ubiquitin chains, so the cell shreds it. The freed genes let blasts mature and die by apoptosis. This is standard textbook teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Harms shown. Maturing cells can flood the lung with fluid (differentiation syndrome); arsenic blocks potassium channels in the heart, stretching the QT until the rhythm twists (torsades), which keeping potassium and magnesium up and avoiding other QT drugs guards against; the liver strains, nerves tingle and go numb, and fatigue builds. This is a teaching summary only.

What was simplified. Combination therapy with ATRA, the bleeding tendency of acute promyelocytic leukaemia, white cell and ECG monitoring, electrolyte checks, liver checks, nerve checks, relapse care, and all doses and thresholds are left out. Speeds, counts and gauge levels are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
