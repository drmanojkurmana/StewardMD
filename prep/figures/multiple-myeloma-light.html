What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • The clone and the paraprotein. Multiple myeloma is a malignant clone of plasma cells in the bone marrow. The clone makes a single monoclonal antibody, the paraprotein, and often excess free light chains. MGUS is a small clone with a low paraprotein and no organ damage. Smouldering myeloma is a larger burden without organ damage. Active myeloma is defined by organ damage or high risk features. This is standard teaching in haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • CRAB features. Bone resorption driven by the clone leaves osteolytic lesions and releases calcium, giving bone pain, holes in bone and high calcium. Free light chains injure kidney tubules and can form casts, giving renal injury. Marrow crowding lowers red cell production, giving anaemia, and normal antibody and white cell defence falls, giving infection risk. This is standard teaching in haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Proteasome inhibitor-based therapy kills plasma cells and shrinks the clone and paraprotein. A bisphosphonate coats bone, slows bone resorption and calcium release but does not shrink the clone. Supportive care supports blood counts, kidney and defence without attacking the clone. These are teaching toggles only.
