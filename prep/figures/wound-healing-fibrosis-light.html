What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How a wound heals. Bleeding is stopped by a clot of platelets and fibrin (haemostasis). Neutrophils arrive first, then macrophages, and together they clear microbes and dead tissue. Fibroblasts then make granulation tissue, new blood vessels grow in (angiogenesis), and new skin cells crawl across the surface (re-epithelialisation). Later the early type III collagen is replaced by stronger type I collagen, myofibroblasts pull the edges together (contraction), and a scar remains. A raised scar that grows beyond the wound is a keloid. This is standard textbook teaching in pathology and surgery and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • What delays healing, and treatment ideas shown. Infection, poor blood supply (ischaemia), diabetes and steroids can all delay healing. Cleaning and dressing protects the wound and keeps it moist. Treating infection and restoring blood supply removes two major blocks to filling and closure. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Diabetes and steroid effects are described in words rather than drawn as separate toggles, nerve and hair regrowth, wound dressings by type, sutures and grafts, and all doses, timings and targets are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
