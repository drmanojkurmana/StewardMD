What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease develops. A mutation in PKD1 or PKD2 makes polycystin faulty. Polycystin sits on the primary cilium of tubular epithelial cells and helps the cilium sense flow and control cell growth and fluid movement. When signalling fails, tubular cells multiply and secrete fluid, a cyst forms from the tubule wall and grows, and over years many cysts enlarge the kidneys and replace working nephrons. This is standard teaching in medicine and nephrology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Effects in the body. Enlarging cysts cause flank pain and can bleed, giving blood in the urine. High blood pressure is common and speeds kidney damage. Filtration falls and waste is retained as nephrons are lost, ending in kidney failure. Cysts also occur in the liver, and there is an association with a berry aneurysm on a brain vessel, which is shown as a small sac on the vessel. Flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Blood pressure control protects the remaining nephrons and the circulation. A tolvaptan concept is shown slowing fluid secretion and cyst growth, which slows enlargement. Dialysis or transplant replaces kidney function when filtration is very low and waste is retained. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. The difference between PKD1 and PKD2 severity, cyst infection and stones, heart valve and bowel associations, screening of relatives, aneurysm screening, eligibility and side effects of tolvaptan, and all doses and targets are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
