What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Replication. Helicase unwinds the double helix at the fork. Primase makes a short RNA primer. DNA polymerase extends from the primer, building the leading strand continuously toward the fork and the lagging strand in short Okazaki pieces away from the fork; DNA ligase seals the pieces. Polymerase proofreading (its exonuclease activity) removes most copying errors. This is standard teaching in molecular biology and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Damage and repair. Ultraviolet light joins neighbouring thymines into a thymine dimer, fixed by nucleotide excision repair; failure causes xeroderma pigmentosum. A base mismatch left by copying is fixed by mismatch repair; failure causes Lynch syndrome. A double strand break is fixed accurately by homologous recombination using the sister chromatid; failure of BRCA1 or BRCA2 pushes the cell toward error prone repair and cancer. This is standard teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Interventions shown. Sunscreen and avoidance cut the ultraviolet damage reaching DNA. The PARP inhibitor idea: blocking PARP leaves single strand breaks unrepaired, which become double strand breaks at the fork; cells lacking homologous recombination (BRCA defect) cannot cope and are lost, while normal cells can. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Single strand binding proteins, topoisomerase, sliding clamps, telomeres, base excision repair, non homologous end joining detail, checkpoint signalling and every enzyme name and step inside each repair pathway are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
