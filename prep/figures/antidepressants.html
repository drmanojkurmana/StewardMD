What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Where each class acts. SSRIs and SNRIs block the serotonin or noradrenaline transporters, tricyclics block both, MAO inhibitors block monoamine oxidase so that more transmitter is stored and released, and mirtazapine blocks alpha-2 autoreceptors and so increases release. This is standard teaching in the pharmacology texts the PrepNucleus lessons cite (Katzung; KD Tripathi) and were not checked against a linked source here, so they are flagged for review.

What was simplified. Receptor blockade by tricyclics and mirtazapine (muscarinic, histamine, alpha-1, 5-HT2 and 5-HT3), the delay of weeks before benefit and the adaptations behind it, neurotrophic and other hypotheses of depression, the serotonin syndrome, the tyramine reaction with MAO inhibitors, discontinuation, bupropion and other agents, and the difference between serotonin and noradrenaline neurons are left out. Levels and the size of each effect are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
