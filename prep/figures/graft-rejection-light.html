What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How rejection starts. Recipient T cells recognise donor tissue by its HLA (MHC) molecules. In the direct pathway, donor dendritic cells carried in the graft travel to recipient lymph nodes and show intact donor MHC to recipient T cells. In the indirect pathway, recipient antigen-presenting cells take up shed donor proteins, process them and show donor peptides on recipient MHC, mainly to CD4 T cells, which help B cells make donor-specific antibodies. T-cell activation needs signal 1 (the T-cell receptor meeting MHC with peptide), signal 2 (costimulation, CD28 on the T cell with B7 on the antigen-presenting cell) and signal 3 (cytokines such as IL-2 driving division). Activated CD8 T cells kill graft cells; antibodies and complement injure graft vessels. This is standard teaching in immunology and transplant texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Drug targets shown. Calcineurin inhibitors stop IL-2 being made after signal 1. Antiproliferatives stop lymphocytes dividing. Costimulation blockers stop CD28 meeting B7. Anti-IL-2 receptor antibodies block the IL-2 receptor. Anti-thymocyte globulin removes T cells. Steroids dampen cytokine and macrophage activity. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Memory and regulatory cells, the semi-direct pathway, hyperacute and chronic rejection, tolerance, infection and cancer risks of immunosuppression, drug levels and combinations used in practice, and all doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
