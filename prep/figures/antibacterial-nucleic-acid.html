What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Rifampicin. It inhibits bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, obstructing the growing RNA chain (Rifampin, NCBI Bookshelf).
  • Sulfonamides, trimethoprim and quinolones. Sulfonamides are PABA analogues that block dihydropteroate synthase, trimethoprim blocks dihydrofolate reductase far more strongly in bacteria than in human cells, and fluoroquinolones inhibit DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. This is standard teaching in the pharmacology texts the PrepNucleus lessons cite (Katzung; KD Tripathi) and were not checked against a linked source here, so they are flagged for review.

What was simplified. The sequential block that makes co-trimoxazole synergistic and its bactericidal effect, bacterial resistance (altered enzymes, efflux, target protection), human folate and the need for folinic acid rescue in some settings, pyrimidine and purine detail, rifampicin enzyme induction and other drugs of this kind (metronidazole, nitrofurantoin) are left out. Levels and the size of each block are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
