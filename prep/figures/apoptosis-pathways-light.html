What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How apoptosis starts. Apoptosis is programmed cell death. In the extrinsic pathway, a death ligand such as Fas ligand binds a death receptor such as Fas on the cell surface. The receptor recruits and activates initiator caspase 8. In the intrinsic or mitochondrial pathway, cell stress shifts the BCL-2 family balance so that BAX and BAK open the outer mitochondrial membrane, opposing anti-apoptotic BCL-2. Cytochrome c leaves the mitochondrion, joins the apoptosome and activates initiator caspase 9. This is standard teaching in pathology and cell biology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • How the cell ends, and treatment ideas shown. Both initiator caspases converge on executioner caspase 3, which dismantles the cell from inside. The cell shrinks, its nucleus condenses and it breaks into membrane-bound apoptotic bodies. Phagocytes clear those bodies quietly, without the inflammation seen when cells burst. A BCL-2 inhibitor frees BAX and BAK and favours the intrinsic pathway. A survival signal raises BCL-2 protection and opposes it. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Bid and crosstalk between pathways, other BCL-2 family members, inhibitor of apoptosis proteins, caspase-independent death, necroptosis and other death modes, engulfment signals in detail, and all doses are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
