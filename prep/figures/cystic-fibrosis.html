What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How cystic fibrosis develops. Variants in the CFTR gene fault an epithelial chloride and bicarbonate channel. Mutation classes range from no protein made, through misfolding and failed trafficking to the cell surface (F508del, the common class II variant), defective gating once the channel reaches the surface (class III), reduced conduction, reduced amount of protein, and reduced stability. In airway epithelium the faulty channel gives reduced chloride and bicarbonate secretion and, with sodium hyperabsorption through the epithelial sodium channel, salt and water transport goes the wrong way. The airway surface liquid that cilia beat in is dehydrated, secreted mucus is thick and sticky, mucociliary clearance fails, and infection, classically with Pseudomonas, and chronic inflammation damage the airway wall into bronchiectasis. The same exocrine channel defect blocks pancreatic ducts so enzymes fall and digestion suffers, and stops salt being reclaimed in sweat glands so sweat chloride is high, the basis of the sweat test. This is standard teaching in respiratory and general medicine texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. A CFTR modulator helps the faulty protein fold, reach the surface or open its gate, partly restoring chloride secretion and surface liquid. Airway clearance loosens and moves thick mucus out without fixing the channel. Antibiotics reduce Pseudomonas infection without fixing the channel or undoing bronchiectasis. Combined they act on channel, mucus and infection together, while established airway damage reverses little. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. The full mutation class table and variant specific modulator eligibility, newborn screening, liver and sinus disease, diabetes, fertility, nutrition and enzyme replacement in detail, other infecting organisms, exacerbation care, transplant, and all doses and targets are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
