What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Beta-lactams. They block the final step of peptidoglycan assembly: they bind the penicillin-binding proteins (the transpeptidases), stop cross-linking, and the cell loses viability and lyses (Beta-lactam antibiotics, StatPearls).
  • Beta-lactamase inhibitors. Clavulanate-type agents are given with a beta-lactam to prevent resistance by inhibiting serine beta-lactamases (Beta-lactamase inhibitors, StatPearls).
  • Fosfomycin, cycloserine, bacitracin and vancomycin. Fosfomycin inhibits MurA, cycloserine the enzymes making D-alanyl-D-alanine, bacitracin the recycling of the lipid carrier, and vancomycin binds the D-Ala-D-Ala end of the precursors. These are standard teaching in the pharmacology texts the PrepNucleus lessons cite (Katzung; KD Tripathi) and were not checked against a linked source here, so they are flagged for review.

What was simplified. The Gram-negative outer membrane and porins, efflux pumps, altered penicillin-binding proteins (MRSA), other beta-lactamases (ESBL, carbapenemases, metallo-enzymes), vancomycin resistance by D-Ala-D-Lac, autolysins, the tolerance of non-growing cells, and the differences between penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems and monobactams are left out. Lysis is drawn as the opposite of wall strength in a growing cell. Levels, flow and the size of each block are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
