What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How anaphylaxis develops. In a sensitised person, allergen cross-links IgE fixed to Fc receptors on mast cells and basophils. The cells degranulate within minutes, releasing histamine, tryptase, prostaglandins, leukotrienes and other mediators. Histamine and related mediators dilate blood vessels and make them leaky (falling blood pressure and shock, flushing, urticaria and angioedema), contract airway smooth muscle (bronchospasm and wheeze), drive mucus secretion, and swell the larynx (stridor and airway obstruction). This is standard teaching in immunology, allergy and emergency-medicine texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Adrenaline is the rescue treatment: it constricts vessels, relaxes airway smooth muscle, reduces further mediator release and supports the heart. Antihistamines and steroids are slower adjuncts that blunt histamine effects and later inflammation, and fluids support the circulating volume when vessels have dilated and leaked. Airway support and removing the trigger matter in real care but are not modelled as toggles here. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Basophils, tryptase and the other individual mediators, urticaria and skin signs in detail, gut and heart effects, biphasic reactions, airway devices, oxygen, and all doses, routes and timings are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
