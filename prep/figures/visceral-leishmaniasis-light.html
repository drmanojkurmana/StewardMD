What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How kala-azar develops. A sandfly bite injects promastigotes of Leishmania donovani into skin. Macrophages take them up, and inside the macrophage they change into amastigotes that multiply and spread through blood to macrophages of liver, spleen and bone marrow. Heavy parasite load in these organs brings prolonged fever, massive splenomegaly, pancytopenia from marrow involvement and a large spleen, and hypergammaglobulinaemia with many antibodies that do not clear the parasite. After or alongside visceral disease, parasites in skin can give the post kala-azar dermal form with patches and nodules. This is standard teaching in infectious disease and tropical medicine texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Liposomal amphotericin and miltefosine kill the parasite by different routes, shown here only as a qualitative fall in parasite load. Supportive care only can help fever and blood cells a little but does not clear parasites. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Sandfly biology and the animal reservoir, lymph node and gut involvement, HIV coinfection, relapse and resistance, drug toxicity and monitoring, splenic aspiration and other tests, and all doses and schedules are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
