What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • What the figure shows. The Philadelphia chromosome is a swap of material between chromosomes 9 and 22 that fuses the BCR gene to the ABL tyrosine kinase. The fusion protein is constitutively active: its ATP binding pocket keeps loading ATP and tagging substrates with phosphate, which drives proliferation and survival of the leukaemic clone. Imatinib occupies the pocket when the kinase rests in its inactive (closed) form; dasatinib fits both the active and inactive forms and so also works against some imatinib resistant mutants. A single gatekeeper swap (T315I) changes the pocket mouth so that both drugs are rejected while ATP still fits. Fluid retention and pleural effusion, low blood counts from marrow suppression, and QT prolongation are shown as treatment related effects. This is standard teaching in haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. No drug, imatinib, or dasatinib, crossed with an untreated, responding, or gatekeeper mutant clone. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Second and later generation drugs beyond dasatinib, combination regimens, transplant, blast crisis, monitoring of transcript levels, and all doses, targets and schedules are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
