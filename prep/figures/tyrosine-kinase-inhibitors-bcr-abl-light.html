What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • What the figure shows. The Philadelphia chromosome is a swap of material between chromosomes 9 and 22 that fuses the BCR gene to the ABL tyrosine kinase. The fusion protein is constitutively active: its ATP binding pocket keeps loading ATP and tagging substrates with phosphate, which drives proliferation and survival of the leukaemic clone. Signals reach the marrow clone through the vessel. Dasatinib fits the pocket in both its open and shut forms while imatinib locks only the shut form, so dasatinib also works against some imatinib resistant mutants. A single gatekeeper swap (T315I) changes the pocket mouth so that both drugs are rejected while ATP still fits. Fluid retention and pleural effusion, low blood counts from marrow suppression, and QT prolongation shown on the heart trace are treatment related effects. This is standard teaching in haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. No drug, dasatinib, or imatinib, crossed with an untreated, responding, or gatekeeper mutant clone. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Second and later generation drugs beyond dasatinib, combination regimens, transplant, blast crisis, monitoring of transcript levels, and all doses, targets and schedules are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
