What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How chronic pancreatitis develops. Repeated pancreatic injury, most often linked with alcohol and in some people with genetic susceptibility affecting trypsinogen activation and enzyme inhibitors, leads to progressive fibrosis. The pancreatic duct becomes distorted and may contain stones. Loss of acinar tissue reduces digestive enzyme secretion, so fat is poorly absorbed, causing steatorrhoea and weight loss. Loss of islet tissue reduces insulin and can cause diabetes. Pain may arise from duct obstruction, stones, inflammation and nerve involvement. Serum amylase and lipase are often not strikingly raised in established chronic disease, unlike an acute flare. This is standard teaching in medicine and gastroenterology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Stopping alcohol and controlling pain, replacing pancreatic enzymes to aid digestion, and endoscopic or surgical drainage when a blocked duct with stones drives pain. Drainage clears the blockage so juices flow again, but established fibrosis and lost tissue do not come back. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Gallstones, severe hypertriglyceridemia, drugs and other causes of pancreatic injury, acute flares on chronic disease, pancreatic cancer risk, detailed duct anatomy, exocrine testing, and all doses and procedural choices are left out. Fibrosis is not reversed by the treatments shown. The figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
