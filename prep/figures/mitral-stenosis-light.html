What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • The mechanism. Rheumatic scarring thickens the mitral leaflets and fuses their edges, leaving a small diastolic opening. The left atrium must generate more pressure to fill the ventricle, so it dilates and hypertrophies, and atrial fibrillation becomes common with loss of the atrial contribution to filling. Raised atrial pressure is transmitted back through the pulmonary veins to the lung capillaries, causing congestion and breathlessness, and onward to the pulmonary arteries. Pulmonary hypertension then overloads the right ventricle, which hypertrophies and may fail. A low diastolic murmur is heard as blood crosses the narrowed valve. This is standard teaching in medicine and cardiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Slowing the heart lengthens diastole and improves filling, and a diuretic lowers congestion. Anticoagulation addresses clot risk from a dilated, fibrillating atrium with sluggish flow, without opening the valve. Valvotomy or valve replacement relieves the obstruction itself. These are teaching toggles only.
