What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How acne develops. Four linked steps are standard teaching: androgens at puberty enlarge sebaceous glands and increase sebum; follicle lining cells shed abnormally and stick together (follicular hyperkeratinisation), plugging the follicle as a comedone; Cutibacterium acnes (formerly Propionibacterium acnes), a normal skin resident, multiplies in the plugged sebum rich follicle; and inflammation follows, from early inflammatory signals around the follicle to papules, pustules and, when deep and intense, nodules and cyst like lesions that can scar. Whiteheads are closed comedones and blackheads are open comedones, the dark colour coming from exposed keratin and lipid, not dirt. This is standard teaching in dermatology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. A topical retinoid mainly normalises follicle shedding and unplugs comedones. An antibiotic with benzoyl peroxide mainly reduces C. acnes and inflammation (benzoyl peroxide also limits resistance). Oral isotretinoin, shown as a concept only, shrinks sebaceous glands and cuts sebum, and indirectly reduces plugging, bacteria and inflammation. They are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Hormonal detail beyond the androgen signal, diet, cosmetics, picking and squeezing, hormonal acne patterns, rosacea and other acne look alikes, antimicrobial resistance detail, maintenance therapy, and all doses, durations and safety monitoring are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
