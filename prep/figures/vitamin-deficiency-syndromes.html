What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Fat soluble vitamins. Vitamin A supplies retinal for the vision pigment and keeps eye and skin surfaces healthy, so lack gives night blindness and xerophthalmia with a dry, keratinised eye surface. Vitamin D, through its active hormone form, drives gut calcium absorption, so lack impairs mineralisation and gives rickets in growing bone. Vitamin K is the cofactor for carboxylation of several clotting factors, so lack weakens clotting and causes bleeding. Fat malabsorption can lower all three. This is standard teaching in nutrition, ophthalmology, bone and haematology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Water soluble vitamins and cofactors. Thiamine (B1) becomes thiamine pyrophosphate for pyruvate and keto acid energy steps, so lack gives beriberi with peripheral neuropathy, heart failure or brain signs, classically with alcoholism, poor intake or bariatric surgery. Niacin (B3) supplies NAD and NADP for redox reactions, so lack gives pellagra with dermatitis, diarrhoea and dementia. Vitamin B12 is a cofactor for methionine and methylmalonyl steps, so lack damages spinal cord and peripheral nerves and makes large red cells, often from malabsorption. Vitamin C (ascorbate) is the cofactor that keeps the iron centre working for collagen hydroxylation enzymes, so lack makes weak collagen and scurvy. Riboflavin, other B vitamins, trace minerals and excess vitamin toxicity are noted only in the simplification list. Flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Replacement restores the missing vitamin directly, dietary correction restores intake over time, and treating a malabsorption cause restores absorption from the gut. Which route matters depends on why the vitamin is low. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Riboflavin and other B group members beyond thiamine, niacin and B12, folate (covered in its own marrow scene), vitamin E, trace mineral deficiencies, vitamin excess and toxicity, amino acid and enzyme detail for each cofactor step, absorption transporters, body store sizes, laboratory assays, and all doses, routes and schedules are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
