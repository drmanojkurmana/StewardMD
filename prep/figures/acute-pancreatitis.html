What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How acute pancreatitis develops. The common triggers are a gallstone that blocks the pancreatic outlet and alcohol. Inside acinar cells, digestive enzymes that should only be activated in the duodenum are switched on early: trypsinogen becomes trypsin inside the cell. Trypsin activates more enzymes, which digest cell membranes and pancreatic tissue (autodigestion), injure blood vessels, and damage surrounding fat (fat necrosis, where enzymes split fat and chalky deposits form). Inflammatory signals then spread through the body, making vessels leaky; in severe disease other organs can fail. This is standard teaching in medicine and gastroenterology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Removing a blocking stone relieves the obstruction, fluids support the circulation while vessels leak, analgesia eases the severe pain, and nutrition support by feeding helps recovery. Definitive care also looks for the cause and treats complications. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. The two triggers are shown together for teaching, although usually one cause is found; gallstone removal in real care depends on where the stone sits and on specialist procedures. Enzyme cascades beyond trypsin, infected necrosis, collections around the pancreas, organ failure scoring, antibiotics, surgery and all doses and targets are left out.
