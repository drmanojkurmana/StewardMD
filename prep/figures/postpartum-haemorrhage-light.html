What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Why bleeding happens after delivery. When the placenta separates, the spiral arteries are left open. A firm uterine contraction compresses them and stops the flow, so failure to contract (tone, uterine atony) is the leading cause of postpartum haemorrhage. The other causes in the four Ts are trauma (tears of the cervix, vagina or uterus), tissue (retained placenta or membranes preventing full contraction) and thrombin (coagulopathy, where clots do not form). Heavy loss lowers circulating volume and blood pressure and can progress to shock. This is standard teaching in obstetrics texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Uterine massage and a uterotonic restore tone, repair of a tear or removal of retained tissue removes that source, and tranexamic acid with clotting support helps a firm clot form and hold. Resuscitation and transfusion run alongside in real care but are not drawn here. They are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Resuscitation, transfusion, balloon tamponade, surgery, embolisation, the full coagulation workup, rarer causes such as placenta accreta and uterine inversion, and all doses and thresholds are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
