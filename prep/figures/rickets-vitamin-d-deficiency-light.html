What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Where vitamin D comes from and how it is switched on. Vitamin D is made in skin exposed to sunlight and taken in from diet. It is first changed in the liver and then activated in the kidney. Active vitamin D lets the gut absorb calcium and phosphate. This is standard teaching in endocrinology and paediatric texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Why bone softens. When active vitamin D is lacking, gut calcium and phosphate absorption falls and blood calcium tends to drop. Low calcium raises parathyroid hormone. With too little calcium and phosphate available, new cartilage at the growth plate and new osteoid laid down by bone forming cells cannot mineralise properly. In children this is rickets at the growth plate; in adults the same failure in osteoid is osteomalacia. Growing bone bends under load, giving bowed legs, and swollen rib ends give the rachitic rosary. Flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Vitamin D with calcium restores the missing hormone and mineral supply, sunlight restores skin production, and surgery can straighten a fixed deformity but does not by itself restore mineralisation. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Phosphate handling in detail, fibroblast growth factor 23, parathyroid bone resorption in depth, inherited and kidney related forms of rickets, vitamin D resistant forms, growth failure, dental changes, muscle weakness, fractures, and all doses and targets are left out.
