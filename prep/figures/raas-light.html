What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • The cascade (well established). Renin cleaves angiotensinogen to angiotensin I; ACE converts it to angiotensin II, which raises pressure by constricting arterioles and drives aldosterone release and renal sodium retention. Renin release by the juxtaglomerular cells sets the pace (Physiology, Renin Angiotensin System, StatPearls).
  • ACE inhibitors and bradykinin. ACE also breaks down bradykinin; the build-up when ACE is blocked is the usual explanation for the dry cough and for angioedema (substance P may add to the cough). Cough affects roughly 5 to 20 percent of people on ACE inhibitors and angioedema about 0.1 to 0.2 percent, usually within hours to a week of starting (Case report and review of ACE-inhibitor angioedema, PMC).
  • Angiotensin receptor blockers. They block angiotensin II at the AT1 receptor and can replace an ACE inhibitor when cough or angioedema is a problem; hyperkalaemia, hypotension and kidney injury are the main cautions (Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers, StatPearls).
  • Aldosterone antagonists. Spironolactone blocks the mineralocorticoid receptor and is non-selective, so it also binds androgen and progesterone receptors (Spironolactone, StatPearls).

What was simplified. Angiotensin II also acts on AT2 receptors and is broken down to other peptides (angiotensin 1-7 acting on Mas receptors); chymase and other enzymes make angiotensin II without ACE, which is why ACE inhibitors do not remove it completely; the feedback of angiotensin II on renin release, so renin and angiotensin I rise when ACE or the AT1 receptor is blocked, is not drawn; aldosterone is also released by potassium and ACTH; aldosterone has non-genomic and non-renal effects (heart, vessels); sacubitril and neprilysin, and the effect on potassium, creatinine and pregnancy, are left out. Levels, flow and the size of the block are illustrative. The drug choices are teaching toggles of a class: no named drug, dose or schedule is shown or implied, and the figure does not say who should or should not take any of them.
