What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How the cranial nerves are organised. Each cranial nerve has a nucleus or ganglion, a course from the brainstem, and sensory, motor or parasympathetic fibers to a target. The trigeminal nerve (fifth) carries facial sensation from three divisions and motor fibers to chewing muscles; its ganglion holds sensory cell bodies. Trigeminal neuralgia gives brief severe facial pain with numbness usually absent, and carbamazepine often gives relief. The facial nerve (seventh) moves the face, carries taste from the front of the tongue, and supplies a tear and saliva gland. Upper motor neuron facial weakness spares the forehead because the forehead nucleus receives fibers from both sides; a lower motor neuron seventh lesion weakens the whole face and risks the cornea. The third nerve moves most eye muscles and lifts the lid, and its parasympathetic fibers narrow the pupil, so compression gives a down and out eye with a dilated pupil. The sixth nerve supplies only lateral rectus, so its lesion stops abduction. The vagus affects voice, palate and swallowing, and the hypoglossal nerve moves the tongue, which points toward a lower lesion. Large myelinated fibers carry touch and position sensation, and superior cerebellar artery compression is one proposed cause of trigeminal neuralgia. Headache, weakness, sensory loss and neuropathy patterns help locate a lesion. This is standard textbook teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Treat the cause means removing compression or treating inflammation or infection where present. Protect the eye means lubrication, taping or shielding when lid closure fails. These are teaching toggles only, used daily in principle to prevent corneal damage while the cause is treated.

What was simplified. Individual foramina, detailed brainstem cross sections, reflex arcs, vestibular and hearing testing, olfactory and visual field detail, and all drug choices and doses are left out. Upper and lower motor neuron facial patterns are shown as one schematic contrast. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any person should be treated.
