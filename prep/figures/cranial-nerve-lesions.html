What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How the cranial nerves are organised. Each cranial nerve has a nucleus or ganglion, a course from the brainstem, and sensory, motor or parasympathetic fibers to a target. The trigeminal nerve (fifth) carries facial sensation and motor fibers to chewing muscles; its ganglion holds sensory cell bodies. Trigeminal neuralgia gives brief severe facial pain with numbness usually absent. The facial nerve (seventh) moves the face, carries taste from the front of the tongue, and supplies tear and saliva glands. A lower seventh lesion weakens the whole face and risks the cornea, while an upper lesion spares the forehead. The third nerve moves most eye muscles and lifts the lid, and its fibers narrow the pupil, so compression gives a down and out eye with a wide pupil. The sixth nerve supplies only lateral rectus, so its lesion keeps the eye from turning out. The vagus affects voice, palate and swallowing, and the twelfth nerve moves the tongue, which points toward a lower lesion. This is standard textbook teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Treat the cause means lifting pressure or calming inflammation where present, so signals flow again. Protect the eye means drops and a shield when the lid cannot close, guarding the cornea while the nerve heals. These are teaching toggles only.
