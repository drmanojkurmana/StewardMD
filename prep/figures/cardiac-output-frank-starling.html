What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Cardiac output. Cardiac output is stroke volume times heart rate. Stroke volume depends on preload, afterload and contractility. This is standard teaching in physiology and cardiology texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Frank Starling relationship. Within limits, a ventricle that is filled and stretched more before it contracts ejects more blood. Plotting stroke volume against end diastolic filling gives a rising curve that flattens at high filling. Stronger contractility shifts the whole curve upward, while a failing ventricle follows a lower and flatter curve for the same filling.
  • Preload, afterload and the treatments shown. Preload is how full and stretched the ventricle is before contraction, set largely by venous return. Afterload is the load the ventricle ejects against, set largely by arterial resistance. A positive inotrope raises contractility, a diuretic reduces circulating volume and preload, and a vasodilator reduces afterload. These are teaching toggles for the mechanism only.

What was simplified. Heart rate control, valve function, ventricular interaction, pericardial constraint, the falling limb sometimes drawn at extreme filling, pulmonary circulation detail, and every dose, target and monitoring step are left out. Sizes, speeds and levels are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
