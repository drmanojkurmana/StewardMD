What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Latency and reactivation. Herpes simplex virus first copies itself in skin or mucosal cells. New virus travels up the sensory fibre to the nerve cell body in the ganglion, where the viral genome persists in the nucleus as resting DNA with very little viral protein made. Triggers such as stress, fever, ultraviolet light and low immunity can wake the virus. New virus then travels back down the same fibre to the skin and copies itself in skin cells again, forming grouped blisters. Reactivation of varicella zoster virus follows the same ganglion idea and gives blisters in the dermatome supplied by that ganglion (shingles). This is standard teaching in virology and infection texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment idea shown. Aciclovir is a nucleoside analogue. After activation in infected cells it is built into growing viral DNA and caps the chain, so copying and new blister formation fall. It does not remove the resting genome from the nerve cell nucleus, so reactivation can still happen later. This is a teaching toggle only.

What was simplified. The full latency gene programme, immune control in the ganglion, differences between herpes simplex types and varicella zoster in detail, antiviral resistance, and all doses and durations are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
