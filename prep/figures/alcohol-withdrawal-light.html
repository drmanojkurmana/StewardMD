What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • Why withdrawal excites the brain. Alcohol enhances inhibition through GABA receptors and blocks excitatory NMDA glutamate receptors. With chronic use the brain adapts in the opposite direction: GABA signalling weakens and glutamate signalling strengthens. When drinking stops abruptly, the adapted brain is left with too little inhibition and too much excitation. This is standard teaching in pharmacology and medicine texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • How it unfolds over time. Early withdrawal brings tremor, sweating, a racing pulse and anxiety. Seizures may follow as excitability rises. Delirium tremens is the most severe stage, with marked autonomic hyperactivity, agitation and confusion. Only this broad sequence is drawn here, without any timing numbers.
  • Treatment ideas shown. A benzodiazepine restores GABA inhibition and calms excitability. Thiamine replaces a vitamin store that chronic alcohol use depletes and protects the brain, but it does not by itself calm withdrawal excitability. Supportive fluids support circulation. These are teaching toggles only, not a guide to treating any person.

What was simplified. Wernicke encephalopathy and Korsakoff syndrome in detail, kindling, hallucinations, electrolyte and glucose problems, withdrawal scales and monitoring, other sedatives, and all doses and drug choices are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
