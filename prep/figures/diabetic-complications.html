What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How chronic complications develop. Long standing hyperglycaemia glycates proteins and lipids to form advanced glycation end products (AGEs). AGEs cross link collagen, thicken capillary basement membranes and bind receptors that drive inflammation and oxidative stress. Glucose is also converted by aldose reductase through the polyol pathway to sorbitol and fructose, consuming NADPH, weakening glutathione defence and adding osmotic stress, especially in lens, nerve and kidney cells. Protein kinase C and other pathways add vessel injury. This is standard teaching in medicine and diabetes texts and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Small and large vessel patterns. Microvascular disease gives retinopathy (pericyte loss, microaneurysms, leak, bleeding and new vessels), nephropathy (glomerular basement membrane thickening, mesangial expansion, albumin in the urine and falling filtration) and neuropathy (sorbitol and ischaemic injury to peripheral nerves, with sensory loss). Macrovascular disease is faster and more extensive atherosclerosis, giving coronary heart disease, stroke and peripheral arterial disease, and in the foot it combines with neuropathy to raise ulcer and amputation risk. Flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. Glucose control slows AGE and sorbitol formation and most clearly protects the small vessels. Blood pressure control protects the kidney, retina, brain and heart. A statin lowers lipid driven plaque growth and most clearly protects the large arteries. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Diabetic foot care, eye screening and injection treatments, kidney protective drug classes beyond pressure control, lipid and glucose targets, erectile and gut autonomic problems, and all doses and drug choices are left out. Damage built up over years is only partly reversible, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
