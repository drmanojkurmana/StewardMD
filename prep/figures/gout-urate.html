What the figure shows, and how sure we are

  • How gout develops. Purines from the diet and from cell turnover are broken down to uric acid, with xanthine oxidase catalysing the final steps. Humans lack uricase, so uric acid is the end product. Blood urate rises when production is increased (overproduction) or, more commonly, when the kidney excretes too little (underexcretion). Sustained hyperuricaemia lets monosodium urate form needle-like crystals in and around joints. Resident macrophages sense the crystals, activate the inflammasome and release IL-1, which recruits neutrophils and drives the hot, painful acute attack. Over years, crystal deposits with surrounding tissue form tophi in chronic tophaceous gout. This is standard textbook teaching and was not checked against a linked source here, so it is flagged for review.
  • Treatment ideas shown. An NSAID or colchicine calms the inflammation of an attack without lowering urate. A xanthine oxidase inhibitor lowers urate by reducing production. A uricosuric lowers urate by increasing kidney excretion. These are teaching toggles only.

What was simplified. Genetic and drug causes of underexcretion, gut excretion of urate, crystal coating proteins, resolution pathways, kidney stones and urate kidney injury, and all doses and targets are left out. Counts and speeds are illustrative, and the figure does not say how any patient should be treated.
