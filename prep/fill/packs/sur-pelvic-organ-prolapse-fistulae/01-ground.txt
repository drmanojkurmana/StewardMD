The POP-Q system quantifies vaginal descent against the hymenal ring as the zero reference plane, grading anterior, apical, and posterior compartments in centimetres above or below the hymen. Standardised staging directs procedure selection and permits outcome comparison across surgeons and series.

Apical defects demand suspension. Abdominal or robotic sacrocolpopexy fixes lightweight polypropylene mesh from the vaginal apex to the anterior longitudinal sacral ligament at S1, restoring the vaginal axis. As the gold standard repair for apical prolapse, it outlasts vaginal native tissue repairs in durability.

Vesicovaginal fistula, VVF, aetiology splits by development: prolonged obstructed labor crushes the vesicovaginal septum into ischemic necrosis where obstetric care is scarce, while in developed practice 80% to 90% of VVFs complicate hysterectomy, usually abdominal, through unrecognised bladder injury.

The double dye test distinguishes VVF from ureterovaginal fistula without imaging: oral phenazopyridine stains renal urine orange while intravesical methylene blue marks bladder urine blue. A blue tampon implicates the bladder, an orange tampon the ureter, steering vaginal versus abdominal repair.

Supratrigonal post-hysterectomy fistulae suit Latzko colpocleisis, a partial vaginal closure achieving over 95% cure: peritract vaginal denudation with layered burial leaves the ureteral orifices and bladder neck undisturbed through an entirely vaginal route.

The O'Conor technique addresses larger defects via transvesical or transabdominal bisection of bladder from dome to fistula, mobilising vascular flaps for separate bladder and vaginal closure cushioned by an omental flap interposition graft that bars reapposition.

Ischaemic and reoperative beds require imported blood: the Martius flap of labial fibrofatty tissue serves as a vascularized interposition graft for complex, recurrent, or radiation fistula repairs of vesicovaginal and urethrovaginal type.

Radiation-induced VVF management adds patience: delayed repair at 6 to 12 months following radiation cessation, always with vascularized tissue flap transfer, since irradiated edges heal only when fed by unirradiated vessels. Postoperative drainage keeps both suture lines dry for two to three weeks, and continence outcomes are reviewed with repeat dye testing before catheter removal.
