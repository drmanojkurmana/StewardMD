Cancer of the gallbladder follows stones. Gallstones over 3 cm, porcelain calcification and polyps over 10 mm raise risk, and the disease clusters endemically in North India with striking female predominance.

Surprise cancers split by depth. Incidental T1a disease confined to mucosa after simple cholecystectomy needs no further surgery with 5-year survival near 100 percent, while T1b muscle invasion needs extended resection with nodal clearance.

Extended surgery takes liver with nodes. Extended cholecystectomy removes liver segments IVb and V with 2 cm margins plus portal nodal clearance of at least 6 nodes for T1b and T2 disease, converting an incidental finding into a curative operation.

Advanced disease starts with drugs. T3 disease breaching serosa and T4 with vascular invasion get neoadjuvant gemcitabine-cisplatin first, and surgery follows only for responders without distant spread on restaging.

Yellow skin usually means no surgery. Jaundice at presentation usually marks unresectable hilar invasion, and palliation uses endoscopic metal stenting with 8 to 12 month patency plus pain and sepsis control.

Port sites seed. Port-site recurrence after laparoscopic cholecystectomy for unsuspected cancer mandates excision of port sites with the definitive resection specimen, planned at the index reoperation.

Chemotherapy follows clean surgery. Adjuvant capecitabine for 6 months after R0 resection improves survival per the BILCAP trial, and surveillance with 3-monthly imaging catches the early recurrence peak when re-resection is still possible.

Inflammation mimics cancer. Xanthogranulomatous cholecystitis mimics cancer with wall thickening and infiltration, distinguished by intact mucosa on imaging and frozen section at surgery, which spares a radical operation when benign.
