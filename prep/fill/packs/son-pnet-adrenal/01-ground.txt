Functional pancreatic NETs: overview
- Islet-type hormone cells give rise to pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors. Most stay silent and come to light by chance or because they press on a neighboring organ.
- Roughly 20 percent of these tumors make enough hormone to produce a recognizable clinical picture.
- Insulinoma is the most frequent functional type. Glucagonoma and gastrinoma follow in frequency.
- One hormone usually dominates the symptoms.

Insulinoma
- Surplus insulin drives glucose down, producing confusion, sweating, tremor, and in severe cases seizure or coma.
- The Whipple triad rests on three findings: typical symptoms, a precisely measured low glucose, and relief once glucose is restored.
- Diagnostic confirmation comes from observed fasting: insulin readings beyond 3 microunits per milliliter, often beyond 6, coincide with glucose under 40 to 45 milligrams per deciliter.
- Proinsulin and C-peptide climb along with insulin. Injected insulin leaves C-peptide low, which helps identify factitious use.
- The majority are smaller than 2 cm, and endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) is the best local tool.
- Nonmetastatic cases are benign in 95 percent of patients and are typically about 1 cm. The usual operation is enucleation, with the capsule removed intact, and no nodal dissection is needed.

Gastrinoma and Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (ZES)
- Excess gastrin stimulates acid secretion, producing refractory ulcers, diarrhea, and reflux.
- Fasting gastrin is typically above 600 pg/mL, and nearly all patients exceed 100 pg/mL.
- A value of 1,000 pg/mL or higher combined with a gastric pH of 2 or less confirms the syndrome.
- Proton pump inhibitors raise gastrin levels and can mislead the test, so timing matters.
- The duodenum is the most common site, and these duodenal tumors are often under 1 cm.
- Up to 25 percent of ZES patients have multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN 1). Gastrinomas in that setting are small and numerous, and partial pancreatectomy can leave behind new growths.
- Gastrinoma carries the clearest high malignant share among the functional types: more than 60 percent are judged malignant, based on local invasion or distant spread.

Glucagonoma and VIPoma
- Glucagonoma produces a migratory rash of necrolytic erythema in skin folds, along with glucose intolerance and weight loss. Plasma glucagon is generally above 1,000 pg/mL.
- VIPoma secretes vasoactive intestinal peptide. It causes profuse watery stools, low potassium, and reduced stomach acid, a triad named Verner-Morrison syndrome. In adults most arise in the pancreas.

Malignant potential
- Most pNETs are malignant. Insulinoma is the principal exception.
- Reviews report that 50 to 80 percent of glucagonomas have spread at diagnosis and that more than half of VIPomas have.
- Somatostatinomas are frequently malignant, with more than 60 percent judged so.

Primary aldosteronism
- Autonomous aldosterone suppresses renin and drives hypertension. Low potassium appears in only 10 to 40 percent of cases.
- Among hypertensive people in systematic screening, it is found in 5 to 12 percent.
- The screening ratio is aldosterone divided by renin. Its cut-off reads over 20 when renin is low or suppressed and aldosterone exceeds 12 ng/dL. Spironolactone is withdrawn for 4 to 6 weeks beforehand, and potassium is corrected first.
- Confirmation uses saline loading of 2 liters over 4 hours. Aldosterone staying above 170 pmol/L while seated indicates autonomy.
- Fine-cut CT is the standard image. Aldosterone-producing adenomas are often under 1 cm, and CT can miss lesions under 5 mm.
- Endocrine-inactive adenomas are the commonest adrenal mass, forming 40 to 70 percent of unilateral lesions. Aldosterone-producing adenomas make up about 2 to 5 percent.
- Under-35 patients with a one-sided lesion and no familial suspicion may proceed to surgery directly. Those over 35, or without a visible lesion on CT, undergo adrenal venous sampling (AVS) first. Cortisol checks catheter position, and lateralization needs a twofold aldosterone-to-cortisol difference between sides.
- Medical therapy with spironolactone or eplerenone suits bilateral disease and patients unfit for operation.
