Filling cystometry records vesical pressure Pves from the bladder catheter and abdominal pressure Pabd from a rectal balloon, with detrusor pressure Pdet calculated as Pves minus Pabd after cough subtraction checks. Compliance divides Delta-V volume change by Delta-Pdet pressure change, and values below 12 to 20 mL per cmH2O mark dangerously stiff bladders that threaten kidneys.

Detrusor leak point pressure DLPP above 40 cmH2O predicts upper tract damage with hydronephrosis, so storage is kept below that line and DLPP is rechecked after therapy. Suprapontine lesions from stroke or Parkinson disease give detrusor overactivity with coordinated sphincter relaxation and preserved synergy, managed by bladder training. Suprasacral cord injury above T6 produces detrusor-sphincter dyssynergia DSD with pressures spiking against a closed outlet, demanding reliable drainage.

Bladder overdistension in T6 cord lesions triggers autonomic dysreflexia with paroxysmal hypertension, headache, bradycardia, and diaphoresis; the patient is sat up and drained at once. Sacral and infrasacral damage of conus medullaris or cauda equina, or pelvic plexus surgery, leaves an acontractile detrusor with low compliance for scheduled catheterization. Clean intermittent catheterization CIC of Lapides empties the bladder four to six times daily and remains the gold standard for incomplete emptying.

Cough checks separate rectal contractions from true detrusor rises, and small capacity with rising filling pressures warns of poor compliance before damage is established. Sphincter electromyography confirms dyssynergia when traces equivocate, and timed voiding with measured residuals proves CIC adequacy. Night and day storage pressures are charted against capacity, because silent kidney failure follows neglected high pressure bladders. Anticholinergic therapy is added when overactivity breaks through training, with botulinum toxin reserved for refractory neurogenic cases. Video urodynamics pairs fluoroscopic anatomy with pressure traces when reflux or obstruction is suspected, and ambulatory monitoring catches phasic overactivity missed on standard fills. Detrusor pressures with compliance values are documented every fill, because trends across visits reveal silent deterioration earlier than symptoms.
