TIGAR-O lists the risks. Alcohol and smoking drive most chronic pancreatitis with TIGAR-O risk factors, and Cambridge grades ductal changes from equivocal to marked with chain-of-lakes dilation.

Wide ducts drain sideways. Dilated duct over 7 mm with head mass under 4 cm suits Partington-Rochelle lateral pancreaticojejunostomy, draining the whole duct to relieve pain.

Beger spares the duodenum. Beger duodenum-preserving head resection cores out the pancreatic head for inflammatory mass with portal hypertension, preserving duodenum and bile duct.

Frey adds drainage to coring. Frey combines head coring with lateral drainage for head mass with dilated duct, giving pain relief matching Whipple with lower morbidity.

Groove disease mimics cancer. Groove or paraduodenal pancreatitis with duodenal wall cysts and Brunner hyperplasia mimics cancer, and pancreaticoduodenectomy is chosen when malignancy cannot be excluded.

Genes change the plan. Hereditary PRSS1 pancreatitis carries 40 percent lifetime pancreatic cancer risk with surveillance from age 40, and total pancreatectomy with islet autotransplant suits intractable small-duct disease.

Leaks need stenting first. Pancreatic ascites and pleural effusion with amylase over 1000 units per L need octreotide with ERCP stenting of the duct disruption, and surgery when leaks persist beyond 3 weeks.

Left-sided pressure bleeds. Splenic vein thrombosis with sinistral portal hypertension and gastric varices needs splenectomy, while pseudoaneurysm of the splenic artery over 2 cm needs embolisation.
