# Imaging in rheumatology (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison; ASAS criteria)
MSUS detects subclinical synovitis and tenosynovitis. Power Doppler signal indicates active hypervascular synovial proliferation and correlates with structural damage in rheumatoid arthritis, guiding treat-to-target intensification.
The double contour sign is a hyperechoic irregular band over hyaline cartilage with above 90 percent specificity for monosodium urate deposition in gout, distinct from cartilage calcification.
MRI of sacroiliac joints with STIR or T2 fat-suppressed sequences shows periarticular bone marrow edema, which is required for the imaging arm of ASAS axial SpA criteria.
DECT uses 80 kVp and 140 kVp attenuation differences to color-code urate deposits with sensitivity and specificity above 85 percent to 90 percent, suiting atypical tophaceous gout.
Early RA radiographs show periarticular osteopenia and soft tissue swelling, then marginal erosions at bare areas of the second and third MCP and third PIP joints.
AS spinal radiographs show vertebral squaring, Romanus shiny corners, and thin vertical syndesmophytes from the annulus fibrosus giving bamboo spine, unlike thick coarse horizontal DISH osteophytes.
18F-FDG PET-CT is the modality of choice for large vessel vasculitis in Takayasu arteritis and giant cell arteritis, showing circumferential aortic tracer uptake above hepatic uptake.
Nailfold capillaroscopy at 200x magnification shows the scleroderma pattern of giant loops above 50 micrometers, microhemorrhages, avascular areas, and neoangiogenic arborization, separating primary Raynaud from systemic sclerosis.
Plain radiographs of gout show punched-out erosions with overhanging edges and preserved joint space until late disease. Tophaceous gout in atypical sites such as the spine is an indication for DECT rather than biopsy.
Color Doppler ultrasound of temporal arteries is the first-line test for cranial giant cell arteritis, with the halo sign of hypoechoic wall thickening carrying specificity above 90 percent in experienced hands. MR angiography suits Takayasu arteritis of the aorta and arch branches.
DISH shows thick coarse flowing hyperostosis across at least 4 contiguous vertebrae with preserved disc height, unlike the thin vertical syndesmophytes and disc narrowing of ankylosing spondylitis.
18F-FDG PET-CT requires a 6-hour fast and brief steroid-free window for best sensitivity, since glucocorticoids started more than 3 days earlier blunt arterial uptake. Uptake at or above hepatic intensity in the aortic wall is read as active large vessel vasculitis.
