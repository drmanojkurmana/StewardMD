Mixed connective tissue disease, also called Sharp syndrome, blends lupus, scleroderma, and polymyositis features under one roof. Its serologic anchor is extremely high titer antibody against U one small nuclear ribonucleoprotein. Overlap defines it; U one RNP confirms it.

Diagnosis demands high titer anti-U one RNP aimed at the sixty eight kilodalton, A, and C proteins of the spliceosome. All validated criteria sets (Alarcon-Segovia, Kasukawa, Sharp) require it. Equally, antibodies of other diseases (anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, anti-Scl seventy) must stay absent to keep the MCTD label distinct. One antibody in, all others out.

Hands tell the story first: Raynaud phenomenon in more than ninety five percent, diffuse puffy sausage fingers, non-erosive inflammatory polyarthritis, esophageal hypomotility, and mild myopathy with raised muscle enzymes. Puffy hands with white fingers point upward.

Lung vessels kill most often: pulmonary arterial hypertension strikes fifteen to twenty five percent, creeping in quietly even without severe interstitial lung disease. Annual echocardiographic screening is mandatory. Echo yearly catches silent pressures.

Kidneys stay calmer than in lupus: severe progressive renal disease is uncommon in pure MCTD. When kidneys suffer, membranous nephropathy or mild mesangial disease appears; diffuse proliferative lupus nephritis is rare and signals transition toward definitive systemic lupus erythematosus. Membranous stays; proliferative strays.

Time reshuffles labels: over decades, thirty to fifty percent of patients first called MCTD evolve into one well-defined disease, most often systemic sclerosis, systemic lupus erythematosus, or Sjogren disease. Labels drift toward single diseases.

Scleromyositis fuses scleroderma with myositis, flagged by anti-PM-Scl seventy five and one hundred or anti-Ku. Proximal weakness joins mechanic hands, sclerodactyly, and severe interstitial lung disease. Two diseases share one patient.

Treatment follows the dominant organ: mild arthritis, serositis, and puffy hands yield to hydroxychloroquine with low-to-moderate glucocorticoids, while severe myositis, rapidly progressive lung disease, or major organ hits demand mycophenolate mofetil or cyclophosphamide. Mild disease gets gentle drugs; severe disease gets strong ones.
