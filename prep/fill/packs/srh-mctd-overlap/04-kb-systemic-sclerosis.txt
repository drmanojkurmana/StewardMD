# Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma)
Systemic Sclerosis (Scleroderma), Rheumatology.
# Pathophysiology
SSc is a chronic, often progressive disease of unknown etiology defined pathologically by widespread microangiopathy (fibroproliferative vasculopathy with capillary loss and obliteration) combined with multiorgan fibrosis, on a background of autoimmunity. An initial vascular insult in a genetically predisposed individual (modest HLA class II and immune-regulatory gene associations) triggers inflammation, generation of SSc-specific autoantibodies, and sustained activation of fibroblasts/myofibroblasts driven by mediators such as TGF-beta, IL-6, CTGF, PDGF and endothelin. Maladaptive repair leads to replacement of normal tissue with rigid, avascular, acellular connective tissue affecting skin, lungs, heart, GI tract, kidneys and other organs.
# Prognosis
Highly variable; dcSSc carries a more rapidly progressive course and worse prognosis than lcSSc. SSc confers a fivefold to eightfold higher age/sex-adjusted mortality, with median survival ~11 years; 5- and 10-year survival are ~70%/55% in dcSSc and ~90%/75% in lcSSc. Major causes of death are PAH, pulmonary fibrosis, GI involvement and cardiac disease. Scleroderma renal crisis carries ~30% 3-year mortality (markedly improved by ACE inhibitors). Poor-prognosis markers include male sex, African ancestry, older age at onset, extensive truncal skin involvement, palpable tendon friction rubs, significant/progressive visceral involvement, and laboratory findings of elevated ESR, anemia, proteinuria and anti-topoisomerase I antibodies.
# Severity classification
ACR/EULAR classification criteria are >90% specific and sensitive but are intended for research rather than clinical practice; weighted items include skin thickening proximal to MCP joints (score 9), sclerodactyly/puffy fingers, fingertip lesions, telangiectasia, abnormal nailfold capillaries, ILD/PAH, Raynaud's phenomenon, and SSc-specific autoantibodies (anti-centromere, Scl-70, RNA polymerase III). Clinically, patients are stratified into limited cutaneous and diffuse cutaneous subsets by extent of skin involvement.
# Clinical pearls
Two major subsets: limited cutaneous SSc (lcSSc) with skin confined to fingers, distal limbs and face (trunk spared) and an indolent course; and diffuse cutaneous SSc (dcSSc) with rapidly progressive skin involvement of fingers, extremities, face and trunk.
