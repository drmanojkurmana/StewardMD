# Septic arthritis and infectious joint disease (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison)
Septic arthritis is bacterial invasion of synovial space, most often hematogenous, destroying cartilage within 24 to 48 hours through bacterial enzymes and pressure.
Staphylococcus aureus causes 50 to 60 percent of adult native-joint infections; risk concentrates in rheumatoid arthritis, prosthetic joints, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, and injection drug use.
Knee leads at 50 percent, then hip, shoulder, ankle, and wrist; axial sacroiliac, sternoclavicular, and symphysis pubis infections follow bacteremia and injection use.
Hot swollen restricted joint with fever indicates urgent diagnostic arthrocentesis with Gram stain, culture, crystal search, and cell counts; synovial leukocytes above 50,000 per microliter with 75 to 90 percent neutrophils are typical, but lower counts never exclude infection.
Blood cultures precede antibiotics in every suspected case; synovial fluid inoculated into blood-culture bottles raises yield, identifying the germ in 80 to 90 percent.
Empirical intravenous therapy is vancomycin at 15 to 20 mg per kg every 8 to 12 hours or daptomycin, with ceftriaxone 2 g daily or a fourth generation cephalosporin, narrowed by cultures; total courses run 3 to 4 weeks for native joints, longer for methicillin-resistant MRSA and gram-negatives.
Daily closed needle drainage with repeat aspiration until sterile is mandatory, with arthroscopic or open washout for hip (mandatory), shoulder, loculated, or prosthetic infections.
Gonococcal arthritis hits sexually active young adults with migratory polyarthralgias, tenosynovitis, and sparse vesiculopustular lesions; NAAT of genital, rectal, and pharyngeal sites plus single-dose ceftriaxone 1 g with doxycycline cover treat it.
Prosthetic joint infection within 3 months of implantation usually needs debridement with implant retention plus rifampin-based combinations; late chronic infections need one- or two-stage exchange.
Lyme arthritis gives episodic large-joint oligoarthritis months after tick bite with positive two-tier serology; a 28-day course of doxycycline 100 mg twice daily cures most cases.
Fungal arthritis from Candida or Aspergillus follows prolonged antibiotics, central lines, or immunosuppression with indolent effusions; synovial fungal cultures with prolonged incubation plus tissue biopsy prove it, and therapy runs for 6 to 12 weeks.
