# Cardiac amyloidosis
Cardiac amyloidosis, Cardiovascular, Cardiology.
Aliases: amyloid cardiomyopathy; ATTR cardiac amyloidosis; AL cardiac amyloidosis; transthyretin amyloidosis.
# Pathophysiology
Cardiac amyloidosis results from extracellular deposition of misfolded protein as insoluble amyloid fibrils within the myocardium. In AL amyloidosis the fibrils derive from immunoglobulin light chains produced by a clonal plasma cell disorder, whereas in ATTR they derive from transthyretin, either mutated in the hereditary form or wild-type in older patients. Progressive infiltration stiffens the ventricular walls, producing restrictive physiology with impaired diastolic filling, and disrupts conduction tissue; light chains additionally exert a direct cardiotoxic effect, contributing to the more rapid deterioration seen in AL disease.
# Prognosis
Prognosis depends on subtype and stage; untreated AL cardiac amyloidosis has a particularly poor outlook, whereas earlier diagnosis and modern ATTR therapies can slow progression and improve survival.
