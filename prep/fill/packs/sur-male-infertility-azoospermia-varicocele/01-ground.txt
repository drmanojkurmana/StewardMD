Semen is judged against WHO 2021 semen analysis limits from the sixth edition: volume 1.4 mL or more, total motility 42% or more, progressive motility 30% or more, concentration 16 million per mL or more, and normal morphology 4% or more. A single abnormal result is repeated after a full spermatogenic cycle before any label sticks, since transient illness and collection errors distort counts.

Azoospermia divides at the bedside. Obstructive azoospermia shows normal testicular volume with normal serum FSH alongside turgid epididymides, marking intact production behind a blockage. Non-obstructive azoospermia shows small soft testes with raised FSH, marking primary spermatogenic failure. The split decides reconstruction versus retrieval before any genetic test is ordered.

Congenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens, CBAVD, with impalpable scrotal vasa signals CFTR mutation in over 95% of men, so cystic fibrosis counselling with partner carrier screening precedes assisted reproduction. Associated mesonephric anomalies prompt renal imaging, particularly for unilateral variants.

Y-chromosome microdeletions cluster in the AZF region of Yq11. Complete AZFa or AZFb deletions forecast 0% sperm retrieval and close the surgical route, whereas AZFc deletion preserves retrieval in 50% through testicular extraction. Sons conceived from AZFc fathers inherit the deletion and its infertility.

Klinefelter syndrome, 47,XXY, leads genetic causes of non-obstructive failure through hypergonadotropic hypogonadism: elevated gonadotropins with low testosterone, small firm gonads, and frequent gynecomastia. Fertility preservation precedes androgen replacement, since exogenous testosterone extinguishes residual spermatogenesis.

Palpable varicocele with abnormal semen earns microscopic subinguinal varicocelectomy: 10x-15x magnification with arterial and lymphatic preservation yields the best pregnancy rates, recurrence under 1%, and negligible hydrocele formation. Subclinical lesions with normal parameters are observed.

Micro-TESE scans the opened testis for dilated opaque seminiferous tubules, harvesting only productive segments for sperm retrieval of 40% to 50% in non-obstructive disease while conserving androgenic tissue, outperforming blind sampling.

Epididymal obstruction with preserved production suits microsurgical vasoepididymostomy by the intussusception technique, joining vas to a single tubule with 10-0 and 11-0 sutures for natural conception after restored patency.
