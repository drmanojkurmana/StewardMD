# Traumatic brain injury (adult): acute management
Adults
Emergency department, ICU, neurosurgery
Classify by GCS, scan with CT using NICE 2023 criteria (derived from the Canadian CT Head Rule), prevent secondary brain injury by avoiding hypoxia and hypotension, give tranexamic acid within 3 hours for mild to moderate TBI, and treat raised ICP in tiers with early neurosurgical input.
Aliases: TBI; head injury; head trauma; raised ICP; intracranial pressure; Canadian CT Head Rule; CT head criteria; hypertonic saline; extradural haematoma; subdural haematoma.
# Classify and assess
Severity by GCS after resuscitation: mild 13 to 15, moderate 9 to 12, severe 3 to 8. Record eye, verbal and motor scores separately; the motor score carries the most weight.
Check pupil size and reaction: a unilateral fixed dilated pupil with a falling GCS suggests herniation and needs immediate action.
Warning signs: falling GCS, new focal deficit, seizure, repeated vomiting, severe or worsening headache, and hypertension with bradycardia (Cushing response).
Assume a cervical spine injury until cleared. CT cervical spine within 1 hour if GCS is 12 or less, the patient is intubated, there is polytrauma, or neck injury is suspected with age 65 or over, a dangerous mechanism, or limb neurology (NICE 2023).
# CT head criteria (NICE 2023)
CT head within 1 hour for any of: GCS 12 or less on first ED assessment, GCS below 15 at 2 hours after injury, suspected open or depressed skull fracture, or signs of basal skull fracture (haemotympanum, panda eyes, CSF from ear or nose, Battle sign).
Also CT head within 1 hour for post-traumatic seizure, focal neurological deficit, or more than 1 episode of vomiting.
CT head within 8 hours if there was loss of consciousness or amnesia plus any of: age 65 or over, bleeding or clotting disorder, dangerous mechanism (pedestrian or cyclist hit by a vehicle, ejection, fall from more than 1 m or 5 stairs), or more than 30 minutes of retrograde amnesia.
On anticoagulants or antiplatelets other than aspirin alone with no other indication: consider CT within 8 hours of injury (within 1 hour if presenting after 8 hours).
Also send glucose, FBC, coagulation, group and save, and a blood gas. Do not attribute a low GCS to alcohol or drugs until CT is normal.
# Prevent secondary brain injury
Intubate for GCS 8 or less, loss of protective laryngeal reflexes, hypoxaemia or hypercarbia, irregular breathing, or before transfer with a falling motor score, copious oral bleeding or seizures.
Avoid hypoxia: keep SpO2 above 90% at all times; in ventilated patients aim for PaO2 above 13 kPa (about 100 mmHg) and PaCO2 4.5 to 5.0 kPa (34 to 38 mmHg).
Avoid hypotension: keep SBP 100 mmHg or more at age 50 to 69 years and 110 mmHg or more at 15 to 49 or over 70 years; in severe TBI with bleeding, keep MAP 80 mmHg or more rather than permissive hypotension.
Tranexamic acid within 3 hours of injury: 1 g IV over 10 minutes then 1 g over 8 hours (CRASH-3; benefit in mild to moderate TBI, greatest when given early). NICE 2023 alternative for GCS 12 or less without extracranial bleeding: 2 g IV bolus within 2 hours.
Head up 30 degrees, neutral neck, loosen tight collars and ties, treat pain and agitation, keep normothermia and normoglycaemia, and use 0.9% sodium chloride rather than hypotonic fluids.
Reverse anticoagulation at once if there is intracranial bleeding: warfarin with 4-factor PCC plus vitamin K 5 to 10 mg IV; dabigatran with idarucizumab 5 g IV; apixaban or rivaroxaban with andexanet alfa, or PCC 25 to 50 units/kg if unavailable.
# Raised intracranial pressure
Treat ICP above 22 mmHg and aim for CPP 60 to 70 mmHg. ICP monitoring is indicated for salvageable severe TBI with an abnormal CT.
First tier: adequate sedation and analgesia, head up, normocapnia, CSF drainage if an external ventricular drain is in place, and osmotherapy.
Mannitol 20% 0.25 to 1 g/kg IV bolus: avoid in hypotension or hypovolaemia, replace urine losses, and hold if serum osmolality exceeds 320 mOsm/kg.
Hypertonic saline (for example 3% sodium chloride 250 mL IV bolus) is an alternative, preferred when the patient is hypotensive; check serum sodium at least every 4 to 6 hours.
Hyperventilation only as a brief bridge for signs of herniation; avoid prophylactic hyperventilation to PaCO2 25 mmHg or less.
Refractory ICP: evacuate mass lesions; if decompressive craniectomy is chosen, a large frontotemporoparietal flap (at least 12 x 15 cm) is recommended.
Do not give corticosteroids (higher mortality) and do not use early prophylactic hypothermia.
# Seizure prophylaxis
Severe TBI: give prophylaxis for 7 days to reduce early post-traumatic seizures. Phenytoin is the agent with guideline support; levetiracetam is widely used, though evidence to prefer it is insufficient.
Prophylaxis beyond 7 days does not prevent late seizures; stop at day 7 unless seizures have occurred.
Treat any seizure promptly as status epilepticus is a potent cause of secondary injury.
# Observation
Neuro observations (GCS, pupils, limb movements, vital signs, SpO2) half-hourly until GCS 15; then half-hourly for 2 hours, hourly for 4 hours, then 2-hourly (NICE).
Urgent senior review for agitation, a sustained drop of 1 GCS point (especially motor), a drop of 3 or more in eye or verbal or 2 or more in motor score, worsening headache or vomiting, or new pupil or limb asymmetry.
Monitor sodium, glucose and temperature. Start mechanical VTE prophylaxis early; add LMWH or low-dose heparin once the neurosurgical team judges the haematoma stable.
# Neurosurgical referral
Discuss every new surgically significant CT abnormality with neurosurgery.
Discuss regardless of imaging: GCS 8 or less after resuscitation, confusion persisting over 4 hours, falling GCS, progressive focal signs, seizure without full recovery, penetrating injury, or CSF leak.
Extradural or subdural haematoma with mass effect or a falling GCS needs emergency evacuation; transfer intubated and ventilated with arterial monitoring and an experienced escort, keeping MAP 80 mmHg or more.
# Pitfalls
A single episode of hypoxia or hypotension, especially during intubation or transfer.
Blaming alcohol for a low GCS in an intoxicated patient.
Routine hyperventilation, steroids, or hypotonic fluids such as Ringer lactate or dextrose in severe TBI.
Missing anticoagulant use, cervical spine injury, or a slowly expanding subdural haematoma in older adults.
# Drugs
Tranexamic acid
# Dose
1 g IV over 10 minutes, then 1 g IV over 8 hours, within 3 hours of injury
NICE 2023 alternative for GCS 12 or less without extracranial bleeding: 2 g IV bolus within 2 hours.
Mannitol 20%, 0.25 to 1 g/kg IV bolus for raised ICP, Causes osmotic diuresis; avoid in hypotension; hold if serum osmolality is above 320 mOsm/kg.
Hypertonic saline 3%, 250 mL IV bolus, repeat guided by ICP and serum sodium, Useful when hypotensive; monitor sodium at least every 4 to 6 hours.
Phenytoin
# Dose
Loading 20 mg/kg IV at no more than 50 mg/min, then 100 mg IV or oral every 8 hours for 7 days
ECG and BP monitoring during loading; check levels; many interactions.
Levetiracetam, 500 to 1000 mg IV or oral every 12 hours for 7 days, Common alternative to phenytoin; reduce dose in renal impairment.
Prothrombin complex concentrate (4-factor) with vitamin K
# Dose
PCC 25 to 50 units/kg IV by INR and weight, plus vitamin K (phytomenadione) 5 to 10 mg IV
Warfarin reversal; PCC 25 to 50 units/kg also used for factor Xa inhibitors when andexanet is unavailable.
Idarucizumab, 5 g IV, Dabigatran reversal in life-threatening bleeding.
