Exam grounding notes for reduction mammaplasty and mastopexy, written for grounding, not for display. Paraphrased from Plastic Surgery 5E Volume 5 on breast reduction and lift.

Regnault grades ptosis against the inframammary fold. Grade I parks the nipple at the fold level. Grade II drops it below the fold yet above the lowest breast contour. Grade III sinks it to the lowest contour point. Pseudoptosis keeps the nipple at or above the fold while glandular tissue bottoms out below.

The inferior pedicle Wise pattern inverted-T remains the workhorse for massive macromastia. A broad inferior dermoglandular pedicle carries the nipple-areola complex with reliable vascularity and preserved sensation. Length is its strength; projection is its weakness.

The superomedial pedicle draws its supply from anterior perforators of the internal thoracic vessels with a superior contribution. It delivers excellent upper pole projection and fullness, suiting moderate reductions where shape matters more than maximal resection weight.

The Lejour vertical scar technique drops the transverse inframammary limb entirely. Extensive parenchymal remodelling with pillar sutures plus elastic skin retraction does the shaping. Less scar trades against a learning curve and temporary puckering.

Gigantomastia beyond a suprasternal notch to nipple distance of 35 to 40 cm defeats pedicle perfusion. A free nipple graft, lifted and replaced as a full-thickness graft, eliminates any risk of pedicle torsion or necrosis at these lengths.

Nipple sensation travels mainly in the lateral cutaneous branch of the fourth intercostal nerve, which pierces deep fascia at the mid-axillary line and runs forward. Wide lateral dissection endangers it, so sensation-preserving plans respect this course.

Nipple-areola perfusion is multi-sourced: anterior intercostal perforators from the internal mammary artery, branches of the lateral thoracic artery, and thoracoacromial contributions. No single named vessel owns the complex, which is why most pedicles survive.

The Benelli round-block periareolar mastopexy tightens mild Grade I ptosis through a purse-string permanent suture around the areola. Scarring hides at the areolar border, though projection gain stays modest and pleating may linger.
