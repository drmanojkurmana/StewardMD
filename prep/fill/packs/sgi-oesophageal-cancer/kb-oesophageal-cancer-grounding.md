# Oesophageal cancer: histology, staging, trials and therapeutics

Squamous cell carcinoma dominates the proximal and mid-thoracic esophagus associated with tobacco, alcohol, hot beverages, and nutritional deficiencies, whereas adenocarcinoma predominates in the distal esophagus arising from Barrett metaplasia.
CROSS trial established neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (carboplatin, paclitaxel, and 41.4 Gy radiation over 23 fractions or 4 to 5 weeks) followed by surgery as standard of care for locally advanced resectable esophageal and GEJ cancers.
CheckMate 577 trial demonstrated that adjuvant nivolumab (anti-PD-1) for 1 year significantly doubles disease-free survival (from 11.0 months to 22.4 months) in patients with residual pathological disease (non-pCR) following neoadjuvant chemoradiation and resection.
Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) is indicated for early esophageal superficial neoplastic lesions confined to mucosa (T1a) without lymphovascular invasion, achieving en bloc resection rates > 90%.
Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) is the most accurate imaging modality for T-staging (90% accuracy) and regional N-staging of esophageal cancer, resolving layers 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 of the gut wall and allowing fine-needle aspiration of suspicious peri-esophageal lymph nodes.
Self-expanding metal stents (SEMS), particularly covered or partially covered designs, provide rapid and effective palliation of dysphagia in unresectable or metastatic obstructing esophageal carcinoma.
Tylosis with esophageal cancer (Howel-Evans syndrome) is an autosomal dominant syndrome caused by RHBDF2 mutations on chromosome 17q25, characterized by focal palmoplantar keratoderma, requiring surveillance from age 20, and carrying up to 95% lifetime risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma.
Plummer-Vinson syndrome (Paterson-Kelly syndrome) features cervical esophageal webs, microcytic iron deficiency anemia, and koilonychia, carrying an increased risk of postcricoid and upper esophageal squamous cancer in up to 10% of cases.

## Additional high-yield clinical details from Sleisenger & Fordtran 11e Chapter 47
Epidemiologically, oesophageal cancer is the sixth leading cause of cancer death worldwide, displaying dramatic geographic variation with high-incidence clusters across Central Asia, Northern China, and Eastern Africa termed the Asian oesophageal cancer belt.
Histologically, squamous cell cancers exhibit nests of polygonal cells, keratin formation, and dyskeratotic cells, whereas adenocarcinomas form mucin-secreting glandular structures with nuclear pseudostratification.
Primary clinical presentation typically involves progressive dysphagia, initially for solids and later for liquids, accompanied by unintended weight loss, odynophagia, hoarseness from recurrent laryngeal nerve involvement, and iron deficiency.
Multimodal pre-treatment staging requires high-resolution contrast-enhanced computed tomography of the chest and abdomen combined with whole-body 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (18F-FDG PET-CT) to rule out occult distant metastatic disease in liver, lungs, or distant retroperitoneal nodes.
Endoscopic ultrasound accurately delineates the five-layer acoustic anatomy: superficial mucosa, deep mucosa, submucosa, muscularis propria, and adventitia.
When curative resection is contemplated, surgical options include transthoracic approaches (Ivor Lewis or McKeown three-hole esophagectomy) and transhiatal resection, paired with en bloc two-field or three-field lymphadenectomy.
