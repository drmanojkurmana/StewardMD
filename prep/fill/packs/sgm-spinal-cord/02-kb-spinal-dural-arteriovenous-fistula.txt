# Spinal dural arteriovenous fistula
Spinal dural arteriovenous fistula, Nervous system / Spinal cord (vascular), Neurology / Interventional neuroradiology.
Aliases: SDAVF; type I spinal arteriovenous malformation; dural AV fistula of the spine.
# Pathophysiology
A spinal dural arteriovenous fistula is an acquired direct shunt situated in the dura near a spinal nerve root, where a dural branch of a radiculomeningeal artery connects to a radicular (medullary) vein without an intervening capillary bed. Arterial pressure is transmitted into the normally low-pressure, valveless coronal venous plexus that drains the cord, raising spinal venous pressure and reducing the arteriovenous pressure gradient. The consequent failure of venous drainage causes chronic venous congestion, interstitial oedema and cord hypoxia, producing a progressive myelopathy; unlike intramedullary arteriovenous malformations, the low-flow dural shunt rarely bleeds, and superimposed venous thrombosis can precipitate abrupt, often irreversible deterioration.
# Prognosis
Gait and motor function commonly improve after treatment by endovascular embolisation or surgical ligation, provided intervention occurs before fixed deficits develop, whereas sphincter recovery is less reliable. Delayed diagnosis allows irreversible cord injury and can result in permanent paraplegia.
# Clinical pearls
This is the commonest spinal vascular malformation and characteristically affects middle-aged and older men, most often at thoracolumbar levels
The lesion is an acquired arteriovenous shunt within the dura of a nerve root sleeve, where a radiculomeningeal artery drains directly into a radicular vein and arterialises the coronal venous plexus
The resulting venous hypertension impairs cord drainage and produces a progressive congestive myelopathy rather than haemorrhage
Patients present with insidious, often stepwise, ascending paraparesis, sensory disturbance and sphincter dysfunction that can be mistaken for a degenerative or inflammatory process
Symptoms characteristically worsen with exertion, prolonged standing, heat or the Valsalva manoeuvre, reflecting the underlying venous congestion
MRI typically shows central cord T2 hyperintensity with cord swelling and serpentine flow voids on the dorsal cord surface, and often patchy enhancement
