# Chronic pancreatitis: genetics, exocrine and endocrine failure, and surgical therapy
# Source: StewardMD gastroenterology knowledge base / Sleisenger 11e Ch. 59, 60

## Pathogenesis, definitions and genetics
Chronic pancreatitis is characterized by progressive irreversible inflammatory fibro-inflammatory injury of the pancreatic parenchyma, ductal stricturing, stone formation, and eventual loss of exocrine and endocrine functions.
Genetic mutations in PRSS1 (cationic trypsinogen, gain of function causing hereditary pancreatitis), SPINK1 (pancreatic secretory trypsin inhibitor), and CFTR (cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator) drive genetic susceptibility (SPINK1 mutations).

## Exocrine insufficiency and enzyme replacement
Fecal elastase-1 testing is the premier non-invasive test of pancreatic exocrine insufficiency, where a fecal elastase concentration < 200 ug/g indicates exocrine deficiency (< 100 ug/g indicates severe insufficiency).
Pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT) delivers enteric-coated pancrelipase containing >= 40,000 to 50,000 USP units of lipase per meal taken with food (lipase per meal, half dose 20,000 to 25,000 units for snacks, when output drops below 10%), abolishing steatorrhoea and restoring fat-soluble vitamin absorption.

## Endocrine complications and lithotripsy
Type 3c diabetes mellitus (pancreatogenic diabetes) results from destruction of both beta cells (insulin) and alpha cells (glucagon), differing from type 2 diabetes and creating brittle glycemic control with high susceptibility to severe hypoglycaemia.
Extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy (ESWL) fragments large radio-opaque pancreatic duct stones (> 5 mm, fragments under 3 mm) in the main pancreatic duct prior to ERCP basket extraction and ductal stent placement.

## Surgical procedures and autoimmune pancreatitis
Surgical drainage and resection procedures for chronic pancreatitis include the Frey procedure (local resection of the pancreatic head with longitudinal pancreaticojejunostomy) and the Beger procedure (duodenum-preserving pancreatic head resection, requiring 2 anastomoses).
Type 1 autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP) is a systemic IgG4-related fibro-inflammatory disease featuring diffuse 'sausage-like' pancreatic enlargement, elevated serum IgG4 (often > 2 times normal), and dramatic response to corticosteroid therapy (sausage-like enlargement).

## Additional clinical details from Sleisenger
CT imaging demonstrates irregular main duct dilatation (chain of lakes) and intraductal calcifications.
Celiac plexus neurolysis provides transient pain palliation.
Secretin stimulation testing represents the historical invasive gold standard.
