Familial adenomatous polyposis from APC mutation on chromosome 5q21 shows hundreds of adenomas by the teens with 100 percent cancer risk, needing prophylactic colectomy by age 25. Fundic gland polyps, desmoids and CHRPE spots mark the syndrome outside the colon.

Attenuated FAP with under 100 adenomas and MAP from biallelic MUTYH suit ileorectal anastomosis with rectal surveillance, while dense polyposis needs proctocolectomy with pouch. Recessive inheritance changes family counselling.

Amsterdam II criteria of 3 relatives with Lynch cancers across 2 generations with 1 under age 50 select families for mismatch repair testing. Bethesda guidelines widen testing to early-onset and synchronous tumours.

Lynch colorectal cancer gets extended colectomy with ileorectal anastomosis rather than segmental resection, because metachronous cancer risk reaches 30 percent at 10 years. Annual endometrial sampling and urinary surveillance cover extracolonic risk.

Peutz-Jeghers STK11 mutation causes hamartomas with mucocutaneous pigmentation and intussusception needing enteroscopic polypectomy, plus breast and pancreatic surveillance from early adulthood.

Juvenile polyposis from SMAD4 or BMPR1A and Cowden PTEN hamartomas need upper and lower surveillance from age 15, because of gastric and colorectal cancer risk. SMAD4 carriers also need arteriovenous malformation screening.

Desmoid tumours in FAP cluster after prophylactic surgery and are managed with sulindac, anti-oestrogens or sorafenib first, reserving surgery for complications because recurrence is high and operations provoke new desmoids.

Duodenal adenomatosis needs Spigelman staging with side-viewing endoscopy, and stage IV disease needs pancreas-sparing duodenectomy or Whipple, because duodenal cancer is the leading killer after colectomy removes the colon risk. CHRPE retinal spots and jaw osteomas often precede bowel symptoms by years, so an eye examination belongs in family screening. Genetic counselling covers dominant APC and STK11 against recessive MUTYH, with predictive testing offered to first-degree relatives in childhood. Women with Lynch begin endometrial sampling and transvaginal ultrasound in the fourth decade, while men follow urinary cytology schedules. Small-bowel enteroscopy with polypectomy prevents emergency laparotomy for intussusception in Peutz-Jeghers teens.
