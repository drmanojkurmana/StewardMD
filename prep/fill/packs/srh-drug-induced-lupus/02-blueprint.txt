# Drug-induced and cutaneous lupus (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison)
DILE starts months to years after continuous drug exposure and fades after prompt withdrawal of the trigger.
Definite high-risk drugs are procainamide with up to 20 percent incidence and hydralazine with 5 percent to 8 percent; moderate-risk drugs are minocycline, diltiazem, quinidine, and penicillamine.
Slow hepatic NAT2 acetylators clear procainamide and hydralazine poorly, accumulating reactive hydroxylamine metabolites that drive autoimmunity.
Anti-histone antibodies are positive in above 95 percent to 100 percent of classic systemic DILE, aimed at the H2A-H2B dimer, but occur in 50 percent of idiopathic SLE so are non-specific.
Anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, and severe hypocomplementemia are absent in classic DILE, except in anti TNF inhibitor-induced lupus which copies idiopathic disease.
Systemic DILE gives fever, fatigue, weight loss with symmetric polyarthralgias and serositis such as pleurisy and pericarditis; severe renal and central nervous disease are characteristically rare.
Drug-induced SCLE gives widespread annular or papulosquamous sun-exposed lesions with anti-Ro SSA positivity, triggered by terbinafine, thiazides, calcium channel blockers, proton pump inhibitors, and checkpoint inhibitors.
Cure is immediate permanent drug stoppage with symptoms gone over weeks to months; temporary low-dose oral glucocorticoids bridge serositis or arthritis during clearance.
Minocycline-induced lupus favours young women on prolonged acne therapy with arthralgias, autoimmune hepatitis features, and anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic positivity; quinidine adds thrombocytopenia to the lupus picture.
Rechallenge with the same drug reproduces disease within days and is never attempted; class switches need a structurally unrelated agent with documented tolerance, such as replacing hydralazine with a non-lupus vasodilator.
