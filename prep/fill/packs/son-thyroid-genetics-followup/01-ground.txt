Thyroid cancer: genetic drivers, TSH targets and follow-up (grounding notes)

Genetic drivers by subtype
- Papillary carcinoma is mostly driven by changes that switch on the MAPK pathway, a chain of growth signals inside the cell. Such changes are present in about 70% of tumors.
- The BRAF V600E change swaps a single amino acid in the BRAF kinase gene. It acts as the initiating trigger in up to 50% of papillary cancers. When lymph nodes are involved, it may point to a poorer course.
- RET/PTC rearrangements join the RET kinase gene to a partner gene. They appear in about 40% of sporadic adult papillary cancers and in about 60% of cases in children. After external radiation the rate is about 80%.
- Follicular carcinoma seldom involves BRAF or RET. RAS point mutations occur in 30% to 50% of follicular tumors. NRAS is the most common RAS family member, ahead of HRAS and KRAS.
- The PAX8::PPARG fusion links the PAX8 transcription factor to the PPARG nuclear receptor. It is found in 26% to 56% of follicular tumors and usually arises without RAS changes. Only 3% carry both.
- Medullary carcinoma arises from calcitonin-producing C cells. Inherited RET mutations drive the familial forms, including multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN 2). Many sporadic cases also carry somatic RET changes.
- Anaplastic carcinoma shares some early changes with differentiated tumors, namely BRAF V600E (about 26%) and RAS (about 22%). Loss-of-function TP53 changes occur in about 48%. TERT promoter mutations occur in about 43%. CTNNB1 mutations, which affect beta-catenin, are found in up to 66% and are not seen in papillary or follicular tumors.
- Oncocytic (Hurthle cell) tumors are packed with mitochondria. Their driver changes lie in mitochondrial genes, mostly those encoding electron transport chain enzymes. In one large genomic series, about 71% had a nonsilent mitochondrial DNA mutation. TERT promoter mutations occurred in about 22%. TP53 changes were uncommon.

TERT promoter and BRAF
- TERT encodes telomerase reverse transcriptase, which keeps chromosome ends intact.
- TERT promoter mutations can accompany BRAF V600E in papillary tumors. Together they act synergistically on aggressiveness, raising recurrence and mortality. Synergy means the joint effect exceeds what each change would produce alone.

TSH targets after treatment
- The TSH goal depends on recurrence risk. Low risk: aim for a TSH sitting in the bottom half of normal, roughly 0.5 to 2.0 mIU/L.
- Intermediate risk: 0.1 to 0.5 mIU/L.
- High risk: 0.1 mIU/L or lower. Known metastatic disease: below 0.1 mIU/L.
- Suppression must be weighed against heart rhythm and bone (osteoporosis) harm.
- Levothyroxine (LT4) is the hormone used to reach these levels.
- Medullary carcinoma: C cells do not take up iodine, so radioiodine is not used. Replacement aims for euthyroidism (normal thyroid function) rather than suppressed TSH.

Surveillance
- Serum thyroglobulin (Tg) is a sensitive marker of residual or recurrent disease. Current assays detect it down to about 0.1 ng/mL.
- Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies (TgAb) occur in about 25% of thyroid cancer patients. They can falsely lower the measured Tg, so they are measured alongside it.
- Neck ultrasound is done at 6 and 12 months after surgery, then yearly for 3 to 5 years according to risk. Most recurrences are in cervical lymph nodes.
- TSH, Tg and TgAb are typically checked yearly for at least 5 years, since most recurrences fall in that window.
- Whole-body radioiodine scanning is reserved for iodine-avid disease, or for raised Tg with negative imaging.
