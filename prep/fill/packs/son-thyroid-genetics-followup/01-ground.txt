Thyroid cancer: genetic drivers, TSH targets and follow-up (grounding notes)

Genetic drivers by subtype
- Papillary carcinoma: roughly 70 percent carry an activating change in the MAPK signalling pathway.
- BRAF V600E is a single amino acid substitution in the BRAF kinase. It is the initiating event in up to 50 percent of papillary tumors. In nodal disease it may point to a worse course.
- RET/PTC rearrangements are fusions of the RET kinase gene with a partner gene. Roughly 40 percent of sporadic adult papillary cases carry one. Children show about 60 percent. After external radiation the figure reaches about 80 percent.
- Follicular carcinoma: BRAF and RET changes are uncommon. RAS point mutations appear in 30 to 50 percent. NRAS is the commonest RAS isoform, ahead of HRAS and KRAS.
- PAX8::PPARG fusion joins the PAX8 transcription factor to the PPARG nuclear receptor. It shows up in 26 to 56 percent of follicular tumors and is largely independent of RAS. Only 3 percent of tumors carry both RAS and PAX8::PPARG changes.
- Medullary carcinoma: arises from calcitonin-producing C cells. Germline RET mutations drive the familial forms, including MEN 2 syndromes.
- Anaplastic carcinoma: many changes are shared with differentiated tumors, especially BRAF V600E (about 26 percent) and RAS (about 22 percent). Loss-of-function TP53 changes occur in about 48 percent. TERT promoter mutations occur in about 43 percent. CTNNB1 (beta-catenin) mutations reach up to 66 percent and are absent from papillary and follicular tumors.
- Oncocytic (Hurthle cell) tumors: cells are packed with mitochondria. Their driver changes sit in mitochondrial genes, mostly enzymes of the electron transport chain.

TERT promoter and BRAF
- TERT encodes telomerase reverse transcriptase, which maintains chromosome ends.
- TERT promoter mutations can coexist with BRAF V600E in papillary tumors. The combination has a synergistic effect on aggressiveness, including recurrence and patient mortality.
- Synergistic means the joint effect is stronger than the two changes would give separately.

TSH targets after treatment
- Target depends on recurrence risk. Keep TSH in the lower half of the normal range for low-risk disease, roughly 0.5 to 2.0 mIU/L.
- Intermediate risk: 0.1 to 0.5 mIU/L.
- High risk, or known metastases: below 0.1 mIU/L.
- Suppression is balanced against cardiovascular and bone (osteoporosis) risk.
- Levothyroxine (LT4) is the replacement hormone used to achieve these levels.

Surveillance
- Serum thyroglobulin (Tg) is a sensitive marker of residual or recurrent disease. Current assays measure down to about 0.1 ng/mL.
- Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies (TgAb) occur in about 25 percent of thyroid cancer patients. They can falsely lower the measured Tg, so they are measured with it.
- Neck ultrasound: most recurrences are in cervical lymph nodes. Scan at 6 and 12 months after surgery, then yearly for 3 to 5 years depending on risk.
- Many centers measure TSH, Tg and TgAb yearly for at least 5 years, since most recurrences fall in that window.
- Whole-body radioiodine scanning is reserved for iodine-avid disease or raised Tg with negative imaging.
