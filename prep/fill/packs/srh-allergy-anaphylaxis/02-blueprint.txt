# Anaphylaxis, urticaria and hypersensitivity (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison; WAO and AAAAI guidance)
Gell and Coombs types are type 1 IgE immediate, type 2 cytotoxic antibody, type 3 immune complex, and type 4 delayed T cell hypersensitivity.
Anaphylaxis requires intramuscular adrenaline, also called epinephrine, into the anterolateral mid-thigh at 0.3 mg to 0.5 mg in adults and 0.01 mg per kg in children, using 1:1,000 strength (1 mg per mL), repeated every 5 to 15 minutes.
Tryptase peaks 1 to 2 hours after onset and normalizes within 24 hours; a rise above 1.2 times baseline plus 2 ng per mL confirms mast cell degranulation.
Chronic spontaneous urticaria lasts 6 weeks or longer; refractory disease responds to omalizumab 300 mg subcutaneously every 4 weeks.
HAE type 1 (85 percent, low protein) and type 2 (15 percent, normal protein with low function) arise from SERPING1 mutations; C4 is low with above 95 percent sensitivity, and C1q is normal unlike acquired deficiency.
HAE attacks ignore adrenaline, antihistamines, and steroids, and require C1-INH concentrate, icatibant, or ecallantide.
ACE inhibitor angioedema causes up to 30 percent of emergency angioedema visits, strikes tongue and pharynx months after starting, and requires lifelong ACE avoidance with ARB caution.
