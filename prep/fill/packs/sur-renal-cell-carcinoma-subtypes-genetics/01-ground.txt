Clear cell renal cell carcinoma dominates adult kidney cancer, causing 70% to 80% of renal malignancies. Its founding lesion is 3p loss, deletion of the short arm of chromosome 3, which removes one copy of the VHL gene, with somatic mutation silencing the second. Biallelic VHL inactivation stabilises hypoxia inducible factors, and the resulting HIF-1alpha and HIF-2alpha excess transactivates vascular endothelial growth factor, VEGF, alongside platelet derived growth factor, producing the hypervascular golden tumours recognised at nephrectomy and the rationale for VEGF directed systemic therapy.

Papillary renal cell carcinoma, at 10% to 15%, divides by genetics and temper. Type 1 tumours carry activating changes of the MET proto-oncogene on chromosome 7 and display basophilic cytoplasm with a generally favourable course. Type 2 tumours arise through fumarate hydratase loss, display eosinophilic cytoplasm, and behave aggressively with early dissemination. Grading and subtyping therefore steer surveillance intensity.

Chromophobe carcinoma, roughly 5%, originates in the intercalated cells of the collecting duct. Diagnosis leans on Hale colloidal iron positivity, a reticular blue cytoplasmic stain, and on hypodiploidy, the loss of entire chromosomes across the genome. The bland biology yields the best disease specific survival among common subtypes, and surgery alone usually suffices.

Birt-Hogg-Dube syndrome follows germline FLCN gene mutation affecting folliculin. Dermatology sees fibrofolliculomas of the face and upper trunk, pulmonology sees basilar cysts rupturing into spontaneous pneumothorax, and urology sees multifocal hybrid chromophobe and oncocytic neoplasms. Management favours repeated nephron sparing resections, accepting recurrence as the price of renal preservation.

Hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell cancer, HLRCC, follows fumarate hydratase germline mutation. Cutaneous piloleiomyomas and early onset uterine leiomyomas precede a solitary, unilateral, Type 2 papillary renal cancer of striking aggression. Unlike von Hippel-Lindau or Birt-Hogg-Dube disease, active surveillance has no role: excision at first recognition with generous margins is the standard.

Renal medullary carcinoma afflicts adolescents and young adults carrying sickle cell trait, arising in the calyceal medulla and progressing to widespread metastasis within months. Immunohistochemistry demonstrates SMARCB1, INI1, loss, confirming the entity and excluding collecting duct carcinoma mimics.

Cystic lesions are triaged by the Bosniak classification, version 2019. Bosniak I denotes the benign simple cyst. Bosniak III lesions warrant surgery or disciplined surveillance given a 50% malignancy risk, while Bosniak IV lesions, with an 85% malignancy risk, proceed to operative management, since enhancing mural nodules within cyst walls signify clear cell biology.
