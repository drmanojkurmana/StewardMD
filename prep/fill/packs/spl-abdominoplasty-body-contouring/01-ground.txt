## Abdominoplasty: Rectus Diastasis Plication and Massive Weight Loss
Citations and Chapter Sources: Plastic Surgery 5E Vol 2 Ch 14 (Abdominoplasty and body contouring)

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Abdominoplasty addresses the triad of lower abdominal skin laxity, subcutaneous adipose excess, and rectus abdominis muscle diastasis.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: three
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: abdominoplasty, skin laxity, rectus diastasis

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Full abdominoplasty involves low transverse suprapubic incision, umbilical transposition, extensive undermining to costal margins, and two-layer midline rectus fascia plication.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: two
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: umbilical transposition, rectus fascia plication

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Mini-abdominoplasty is indicated for mild infraumbilical skin laxity without supraumbilical excess, avoiding umbilical transposition.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: mini-abdominoplasty, infraumbilical

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: High-superior tension abdominoplasty Lockwood anchors the superficial fascial system SFS to the deep iliac fascia, reducing scar migration and seroma rates.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Lockwood, superficial fascial system, SFS

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Progressive tension sutures Baroudi quilting sutures anchor the abdominal flap to underlying rectus sheath, obliterating dead space and reducing seroma rates under 2%.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 2%
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: progressive tension sutures, Baroudi, seroma

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Fleur-de-lis abdominoplasty incorporates a vertical midline resection paired with low transverse excision for massive weight loss patients with circumferential laxity.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Fleur-de-lis, massive weight loss, vertical resection

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Circumferential lower body lift belt lipectomy extends excision 360 degrees around the waist to elevate the thighs, buttocks, and lower abdomen.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 360
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: body lift, belt lipectomy

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Postoperative venous thromboembolism VTE is the leading cause of mortality in abdominoplasty, requiring Caprini risk assessment, pneumatic compression, and enoxaparin prophylaxis.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: venous thromboembolism, Caprini, enoxaparin


Comprehensive Textbook Context:
Surgical planning in Abdominoplasty and Body Contouring requires meticulous anatomical grounding, precise knowledge of blood supply, innervation, fascial planes, and tissue biomechanics.
Operative interventions balance functional restoration of speech, swallowing, mastication, sensory discrimination, and locomotion with aesthetic symmetry and minimal donor site morbidity.
Preoperative evaluation integrates acoustic Doppler, computed tomography angiography, indocyanine green fluorescence imaging, and patient-specific risk factor optimization including smoking cessation, strict glycemic control, and blood pressure management.
Postoperative care prioritizes early mobilization, monitoring of capillary refill, tissue turgor, pinprick bleeding, active rehabilitation, and vigilant surveillance for hematoma, seroma, infection, or microvascular thrombosis.
