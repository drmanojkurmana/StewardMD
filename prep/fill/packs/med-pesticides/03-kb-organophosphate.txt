# Organophosphate / cholinergic poisoning
Organophosphate / cholinergic poisoning, Toxicology.
Red flags: Atropine + pralidoxime; airway/secretion control.
# Investigations
Clinical cholinergic toxidrome
Plasma/RBC cholinesterase
# Pathophysiology
Organophosphate (and carbamate) insecticides, and nerve agents such as sarin, soman, tabun and VX, inhibit acetylcholinesterase, leading to accumulation of synaptic acetylcholine at both muscarinic and nicotinic cholinergic receptor sites; the resulting overstimulation produces the combined muscarinic, nicotinic and CNS toxidrome. Organophosphates are 'toxic time-bombs' in that they may require metabolic activation, so onset of toxicity can be delayed. Separately, organophosphates bind and inhibit neuropathy target esterase, with axonal degeneration of the gracile fasciculus and corticospinal tracts producing a delayed sensorimotor polyneuropathy.
# Prognosis
Death is usually due to respiratory failure. Outcome and reversibility of any poisoning depend on the functional reserve of the individual or target organ, influenced by age and preexisting disease.
# Clinical pearls
Belongs to the depressed physiologic state / cholinergic toxidrome; miosis is common and is most pronounced in opioid and cholinergic poisoning, distinguished from other depressant syndromes by muscarinic and nicotinic signs.
Muscarinic features: seizures, excessive secretions (lacrimation, salivation, bronchorrhoea and wheezing, diaphoresis), and increased bowel/bladder activity with nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal cramps, and incontinence of faeces and urine.
Nicotinic features: hypertension, tachycardia, muscle cramps, fasciculations, weakness, and paralysis — note these oppose the muscarinic picture, so vital signs can be mixed.
Death is usually due to respiratory failure.
Organophosphates and carbamates can produce bradycardia and atrioventricular block on ECG.
Organophosphate (and amanita/mushroom) poisoning can present as acute, often systemic-compromising diarrhoea.
Organophosphates can cause a delayed organophosphate-induced neuropathy: early generalised weakness from neuromuscular blockade, then an axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy ensues.
# Additional differentials
Carbamate insecticide poisoning (aldicarb, carbaryl, propoxur) — same cholinergic mechanism
Other acetylcholinesterase inhibitors: medicinal agents neostigmine, physostigmine, tacrine
Muscarinic agonists: bethanechol, pilocarpine, and muscarine-containing mushrooms (Boletus, Clitocybe, Inocybe spp.)
Nicotine / tobacco and lobeline (nicotinic agonists)
Opioid poisoning (also causes miosis and physiologic depression)
# Non infectious mimics
Guillain-Barré syndrome / acute flaccid paralysis — organophosphate, thallium and arsenic poisonings are listed in the GBS differential
Botulism and myasthenia gravis (neuromuscular junction disorders in the acute paralysis differential)
Paralytic shellfish poisoning, ciguatera, type E botulism and tetrodotoxin poisoning — organophosphate poisoning is in their shared differential (ciguatera chapter)
# Additional investigations
Cholinesterase activity in plasma and red cells is <50% of normal in acetylcholinesterase-inhibitor poisoning
ECG — may show bradycardia and atrioventricular block
EMG/nerve conduction studies in delayed neuropathy — early repetitive firing of CMAPs and decrement on repetitive nerve stimulation; later, axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy
# Red flags
Bronchorrhoea, wheezing and excessive secretions with progression to respiratory failure — the usual cause of death
Nicotinic weakness, fasciculations and paralysis
Seizures
Bradycardia / atrioventricular block
# Pitfalls
Vital signs may be discordant: muscarinic effects cause bradycardia while nicotinic stimulation can cause hypertension and tachycardia, so a 'normal' or mixed heart rate does not exclude poisoning.
Organophosphates are 'toxic time-bombs' requiring metabolic activation, so a patient may be initially well or minimally symptomatic and deteriorate later.
Cholinesterase activity is <50% of normal in acetylcholinesterase-inhibitor poisoning — a key confirmatory threshold not to overlook.
# Organophosphate / cholinergic poisoning: treatment
Cholinergic toxidrome — miosis, salivation/lacrimation, bronchorrhoea, bradycardia, fasciculations — after organophosphate/carbamate exposure, ICU.
Atropine titration + oxime + supportive care
# Steps
Protect staff (PPE) and decontaminate (remove clothing, wash skin).
Secure airway; suction copious secretions — bronchorrhoea/respiratory failure is the killer.
ATROPINE in escalating/doubling doses titrated to drying of secretions and adequate oxygenation (not pupils/HR).
PRALIDOXIME (oxime) for organophosphates with nicotinic features/weakness.
Benzodiazepines for seizures; ICU monitoring (intermediate syndrome, rebound).
Atropine titrated to secretions + oxime
# Investigations
Clinical; plasma/RBC cholinesterase
ABG, ECG
Observe for delayed effects
