Old clots rebleed from membranes. Chronic subdural follows minor trauma weeks earlier in elderly or anticoagulated patients, when a liquefied clot with inner and outer membranes rebleeds.

Fluctuation is the signature. Consciousness drifts, hemiparesis comes and goes, and bilateral collections can quadripare with a good coma score.

Density dates the clot. Hypodense means old, isodense at 2 to 3 weeks is easiest to miss, and mixed septated density means membranes plus fresh blood.

Washout plus drain cures most. One or two burr holes with saline irrigation and a closed drain for 24 to 48 hours halves recurrence.

Match tool to clot. Twist-drill suits the frail, while craniotomy with membranectomy is reserved for organised, calcified or recurrent collections.

Embolise the membrane supply. Middle meningeal artery embolisation is the standard adjunct for recurrent, bilateral or high-risk collections after EMBOLISE and STEM.

Skip routine steroids. Dex-CSDH showed worse function with dexamethasone, and statins or tranexamic acid stay trial-only.

Recover flat then moving. One flat day clears pneumocephalus, early mobilisation prevents clots, and thinners restart within days.
