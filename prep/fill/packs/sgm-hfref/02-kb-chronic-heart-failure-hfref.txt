# Chronic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF)
Adults
Outpatient clinic, ward before discharge
Start all four foundational drug classes early (ARNI or ACE inhibitor or ARB, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2 inhibitor), up-titrate every 1 to 2 weeks to target or highest tolerated doses with potassium and creatinine checks, use diuretics only for congestion, and add iron, devices or other agents where indicated.
Aliases: HFrEF; heart failure; CHF; LV systolic dysfunction; GDMT; foundational medical therapy; four pillars; ARNI; sacubitril valsartan; SGLT2 inhibitor; MRA; HFmrEF.
# Diagnose and classify
Symptoms (breathlessness, orthopnoea, fatigue, ankle swelling) and signs (raised JVP, third heart sound, displaced apex, crackles, oedema) with objective evidence of cardiac dysfunction on echocardiography.
Non-acute natriuretic peptide: NT-proBNP 125 pg/mL or more or BNP 35 pg/mL or more supports HF; lower values make HF unlikely.
ESC 2026 defines HF with reduced EF as LVEF below 50% (the mildly reduced category was removed). ACC/AHA 2022 uses LVEF 40% or less for HFrEF and 41 to 49% for HFmrEF. Trial evidence for all four drug classes is strongest at LVEF 40% or less.
Stage the disease (A at risk, B structural disease without symptoms, C symptomatic, D advanced) and record NYHA class to guide treatment intensity and referral.
# Investigations
12-lead ECG, echocardiography (LVEF, valves, RV function, filling pressures), chest X-ray.
CBC, creatinine and eGFR, sodium, potassium, liver function, TSH, HbA1c, lipids, urine albumin-creatinine ratio, ferritin and transferrin saturation.
Establish the cause: coronary angiography or CT coronary angiography for suspected ischaemic heart disease; cardiac MRI for cardiomyopathy, myocarditis or infiltration.
In India also consider rheumatic valve disease, alcohol, peripartum cardiomyopathy, severe anaemia, thyroid disease and tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy.
# Foundational therapy (four pillars)
Give all four classes to every patient with HFrEF unless contraindicated, to reduce death and HF hospitalisation: ARNI (or ACE inhibitor, or ARB if neither is tolerated), an evidence-based beta-blocker, an MRA and an SGLT2 inhibitor.
Start all four within weeks at low doses rather than maximising one drug before adding the next. The order is flexible: SGLT2 inhibitors and MRAs have little effect on BP.
Sacubitril/valsartan is preferred where tolerated and affordable (ACC/AHA 2022 class 1). When switching from an ACE inhibitor, stop the ACE inhibitor at least 36 hours before the first dose.
Up-titrate at least every 1 to 2 weeks, guided by symptoms, heart rate, BP, potassium and creatinine, towards trial target doses; if a target is not tolerated, continue the highest tolerated dose.
Start beta-blockers once the patient is clinically stable and euvolaemic. Use only evidence-based agents: bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol succinate or nebivolol.
Loop diuretics relieve congestion but do not improve survival; use the lowest dose that keeps the patient euvolaemic and teach weight-guided dose adjustment.
# Additional therapy and devices
Iron deficiency (ferritin below 100 mcg/L, or 100 to 299 mcg/L with TSAT below 20%): IV ferric carboxymaltose or ferric derisomaltose to improve symptoms and reduce HF hospitalisation. Oral iron does not replete stores effectively.
Sinus rhythm, heart rate 70/min or more despite the maximally tolerated beta-blocker, LVEF 35% or less: add ivabradine.
Worsening HF despite foundational therapy: consider vericiguat. Persistent symptoms: digoxin (serum level 0.5 to 0.9 ng/mL). Intolerant of ACE inhibitor, ARB and ARNI, or self-identified Black patients: hydralazine plus isosorbide dinitrate.
ICD for LVEF 35% or less after at least 3 months of optimised therapy (strongest evidence in ischaemic aetiology) with expected survival over 1 year. CRT for LVEF 35% or less in sinus rhythm with LBBB and QRS 150 ms or more.
Treat comorbidities: revascularisation where indicated, AF (rate or rhythm control and anticoagulation), hypertension, diabetes, sleep apnoea. Influenza and pneumococcal vaccination; supervised exercise training.
# Monitoring and safety thresholds
Check creatinine and potassium 1 to 2 weeks after starting or increasing an ACE inhibitor, ARB, ARNI or MRA, and after the final dose step, then about every 4 months when stable.
A creatinine rise of up to 50% above baseline (or to 3.0 mg/dL, 266 micromol/L) and potassium up to 5.5 mmol/L are acceptable; do not stop therapy for these.
Potassium above 5.5 mmol/L: halve the MRA dose. Above 6.0 mmol/L: stop the MRA and review the RAAS inhibitor; a potassium binder may allow therapy to continue.
SGLT2 inhibitors cause an early expected eGFR dip: continue. Withhold during acute illness with poor oral intake or before major surgery (ketoacidosis risk).
Symptomatic hypotension: first reduce diuretics (if not congested) and non-HF vasodilators such as nitrates and calcium channel blockers before reducing foundational drugs.
# Special groups
Pregnancy: ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNI, MRAs and SGLT2 inhibitors are contraindicated; beta-1 selective beta-blockers can continue. Plan pregnancy with a cardiologist.
CKD: SGLT2 inhibitors can be started down to eGFR 20 (empagliflozin) or 25 mL/min/1.73 m2 (dapagliflozin). Avoid starting an MRA if eGFR is below 30 or potassium above 5.0 mmol/L.
Elderly and frail: lower starting doses, slower titration, check orthostatic BP and falls risk.
Advanced HF (stage D): early referral to an advanced HF centre (transplant, durable LVAD) and palliative care input.
# Pitfalls
Avoid NSAIDs, diltiazem, verapamil, thiazolidinediones and saxagliptin in HFrEF.
Never give an ACE inhibitor together with an ARNI (angioedema), and avoid ACE inhibitor plus ARB plus MRA.
Therapeutic inertia: patients left on starting doses or missing one of the four classes.
Stopping foundational therapy during an admission without a clear reason such as shock, severe bradycardia or hyperkalaemia.
# Drugs
Sacubitril/valsartan (ARNI)
# Dose
Start 49/51 mg orally twice daily (24/26 mg twice daily if ACE inhibitor or ARB naive, low BP, eGFR below 30 or elderly); target 97/103 mg twice daily
36-hour washout after an ACE inhibitor. Contraindicated with previous angioedema.
ACE inhibitors
# Dose
Enalapril 2.5 mg twice daily to target 10 to 20 mg twice daily; ramipril 2.5 mg twice daily to target 5 mg twice daily; lisinopril 2.5 to 5 mg once daily to target 20 to 35 mg once daily
If ARNI is unavailable or unaffordable.
ARBs
# Dose
Candesartan 4 mg once daily to target 32 mg once daily; losartan 50 mg once daily to target 150 mg once daily; valsartan 40 mg twice daily to target 160 mg twice daily
If ACE inhibitor and ARNI are not tolerated.
# Dose
Bisoprolol 1.25 mg once daily to target 10 mg once daily; carvedilol 3.125 mg twice daily to target 25 mg twice daily (50 mg twice daily if over 85 kg); metoprolol succinate 12.5 to 25 mg once daily to target 200 mg once daily; nebivolol 1.25 mg once daily to target 10 mg once daily
Double the dose every 2 weeks or more slowly as tolerated.
Spironolactone, Start 12.5 to 25 mg orally once daily; target 50 mg once daily, Gynaecomastia: switch to eplerenone. Check potassium and creatinine.
Eplerenone, Start 25 mg orally once daily; target 50 mg once daily.
Dapagliflozin or empagliflozin, 10 mg orally once daily (no titration), Genital mycotic infection, volume depletion; hold during acute illness or fasting.
Ivabradine
# Dose
Start 5 mg orally twice daily (2.5 mg twice daily if 75 years or older); target 7.5 mg twice daily
Sinus rhythm only. Aim resting heart rate around 50 to 60/min.
Ferric carboxymaltose
IV, total dose by weight and haemoglobin; maximum single infusion 1000 mg (or 20 mg/kg)
For iron deficiency as defined; recheck iron studies after about 3 months.
Other agents
# Dose
Vericiguat 2.5 mg once daily, doubled every 2 weeks to 10 mg once daily; digoxin 62.5 to 125 mcg once daily; hydralazine 37.5 mg with isosorbide dinitrate 20 mg three times daily, to target 75 mg with 40 mg three times daily
Digoxin: lower doses in elderly or CKD; level 0.5 to 0.9 ng/mL.
