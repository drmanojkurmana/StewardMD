# Malaria: uncomplicated and severe (India)
Adults and children
Outpatient, emergency department, ward, ICU
Confirm every case by microscopy or RDT. Vivax: chloroquine 3 days plus primaquine 0.25 mg/kg for 14 days. Falciparum: ACT-AL in the North-East, ACT-SP elsewhere, plus single-dose primaquine. Severe malaria: IV artesunate 2.4 mg/kg (3 mg/kg under 20 kg) at 0, 12 and 24 hours, then daily.
Aliases: P. vivax; P. falciparum; Pv; Pf; mixed malaria; severe malaria; cerebral malaria; ACT; artemether lumefantrine; NVBDCP; NCVBDC drug policy.
# Recognise and confirm
Test every fever in an endemic area or after travel with microscopy or a bivalent RDT before treating; treat by species. If no test is available, give full-dose chloroquine and complete species-specific treatment when the result arrives.
Pf HRP-2 RDTs can stay positive for up to 3 weeks after successful treatment; confirm suspected recrudescence with microscopy.
Severe malaria (any one, with parasitaemia): impaired consciousness or coma, repeated generalised convulsions, creatinine above 3 mg/dL, bilirubin above 3 mg/dL, Hb below 5 g/dL, pulmonary oedema or ARDS, glucose below 40 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L).
Also severe: metabolic acidosis, shock (SBP below 80 mmHg in adults, below 50 mmHg in children), abnormal bleeding or DIC, haemoglobinuria, temperature above 42°C, parasitaemia above 5% of red cells.
Severe malaria can be smear-negative because of sequestration or partial treatment: repeat smear or RDT, and treat as severe malaria if the picture fits and there is no other explanation.
