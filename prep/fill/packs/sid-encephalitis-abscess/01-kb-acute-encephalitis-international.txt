# Acute encephalitis (IDSA and BIA/ABN)
Adults and children
Emergency department, ward, ICU, PICU
Altered mental status for 24 hours or more with fever, seizures, focal signs, CSF pleocytosis or imaging change. Start IV aciclovir 10 mg/kg 8-hourly (500 mg/m2 in children 3 months to 12 years, 20 mg/kg in neonates) at once, add antibacterials if meningitis is possible, do LP and MRI promptly, and treat confirmed HSV for 14 to 21 days.
Aliases: HSV encephalitis; herpes simplex encephalitis; viral encephalitis; autoimmune encephalitis; anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis; ADEM; IDSA encephalitis; ABN BIA encephalitis.
# Recognise (IEC case definition)
Major criterion: altered mental status (reduced or altered consciousness, lethargy or personality change) lasting 24 hours or more with no alternative cause.
Minor criteria: fever 38°C or more within 72 hours of presentation; new generalised or focal seizures; new focal neurological signs; CSF white cells 5/mm3 or more; parenchymal change on imaging; EEG change consistent with encephalitis. Two minor criteria = possible, three or more = probable encephalitis.
HSV-1 is the commonest identified sporadic cause in many countries; others are VZV, enteroviruses, regional arboviruses (West Nile, Japanese encephalitis, tick-borne), rabies, and in the immunocompromised CMV, HHV-6 and JC virus. Autoimmune encephalitis (anti-NMDA receptor, LGI1) is as common as many infections.
Mimics to exclude: bacterial or tuberculous meningitis, cerebral malaria in travellers, septic, metabolic or toxic encephalopathy, hypoglycaemia, and non-convulsive status epilepticus.
Clues: temporal lobe features (behaviour change, aphasia, focal seizures) in HSV; rash or zoster in VZV; tick exposure or season (rickettsial or ehrlichial disease); animal bites; travel; immunosuppression.
# First hours
Airway, breathing and circulation; check capillary glucose; treat seizures with a benzodiazepine then a second-line agent. Intubate for GCS 8 or less, loss of airway reflexes or status epilepticus.
Start IV aciclovir in every patient with suspected encephalitis pending results (IDSA), within 6 hours of admission (BIA/ABN), without waiting for LP or imaging: adults and children over 12 years 10 mg/kg 8-hourly; children 3 months to 12 years 500 mg/m2 8-hourly; neonates 20 mg/kg 8-hourly.
If bacterial meningitis is possible, give ceftriaxone 2 g IV 12-hourly (children 50 mg/kg 12-hourly, maximum 2 g per dose), adding amoxicillin 2 g IV 4-hourly for Listeria risk in older or immunocompromised adults; see the bacterial meningitis protocol.
Add doxycycline (adults 100 mg 12-hourly) when rickettsial or ehrlichial infection is suspected from season, exposure or rash (IDSA). Test for malaria in any traveller from an endemic area.
Hydrate well and adjust aciclovir for renal function; aciclovir crystal nephropathy is common, so check creatinine regularly.
# Investigations
Lumbar puncture as soon as it is safe, aiming within 6 hours (BIA/ABN). Image first if consciousness is reduced or fluctuating, there are focal signs, papilloedema, continuous or uncontrolled seizures, or immunocompromise; correct coagulopathy or thrombocytopenia before LP.
CSF: opening pressure, cells with differential, protein, glucose with paired plasma glucose, Gram stain and culture, PCR for HSV-1, HSV-2, VZV and enterovirus; cryptococcal antigen if immunocompromised. Store extra CSF for later tests.
An early HSV PCR (first 72 hours) can be falsely negative: if suspicion remains, repeat CSF PCR 3 to 7 days later (IDSA).
MRI brain is preferred to CT (IEC, IDSA), ideally within 24 to 48 hours. EEG to detect non-convulsive seizures and support diagnosis.
Blood cultures, HIV test in all patients, syphilis serology, acute and convalescent sera for regional arboviruses, and serum and CSF neuronal antibodies (NMDA receptor, LGI1, CASPR2) when no infective cause is found.
# Specific treatment
Confirmed HSV encephalitis: aciclovir 10 mg/kg IV 8-hourly for 14 to 21 days (IDSA); BIA/ABN advise at least 14 days in immunocompetent adults and 21 days if immunocompromised, repeating LP at the end and continuing if HSV PCR is still positive. Children 3 months to 12 years: 21 days; neonates 21 days.
Stopping empirical aciclovir: when an alternative diagnosis is confirmed, or (BIA/ABN) when HSV PCR is negative on two CSF samples 24 to 48 hours apart with an MRI not suggesting HSV, or on one sample taken 72 hours or more after onset with normal consciousness, normal MRI and CSF white cells below 5/mm3.
VZV encephalitis: aciclovir 10 to 15 mg/kg IV 8-hourly for 10 to 14 days (IDSA).
CMV encephalitis: ganciclovir 5 mg/kg IV 12-hourly plus foscarnet 60 mg/kg IV 8-hourly or 90 mg/kg IV 12-hourly for 3 weeks, then maintenance (IDSA).
ADEM: methylprednisolone 1 g IV daily for 3 to 5 days (IDSA), with plasma exchange or IVIG if the response is inadequate. Suspected autoimmune encephalitis: early neurology-led immunotherapy and a search for tumour (ovarian teratoma with anti-NMDA receptor antibodies).
No proven specific therapy for most arboviral encephalitides; IDSA advises against ribavirin for West Nile and interferon alfa for Japanese encephalitis. Routine corticosteroids are not recommended for HSV or VZV encephalitis.
# Supportive care and monitoring
Neurological observations with GCS; continuous EEG where available if consciousness is unexplained or fluctuating.
Creatinine and urine output during aciclovir; reduce the dose for creatinine clearance and ensure hydration.
Watch sodium (SIADH), temperature, glucose and signs of raised intracranial pressure; head up 30 degrees and treat raised ICP with osmotherapy in ICU.
VTE prophylaxis, nutrition, pressure area care and early rehabilitation.
# Neonates, immunocompromise, pregnancy and national policy
Neonates: aciclovir 20 mg/kg IV 8-hourly for 21 days (IDSA), with repeat CSF PCR before stopping.
Immunocompromised: broader CSF testing (CMV, VZV, HHV-6, JC virus, Toxoplasma, Cryptococcus, TB) and longer HSV courses.
Pregnancy: aciclovir is used at standard doses; obstetric input.
India: the national AES programme (NCVBDC and ICMR) adds empirical cover for scrub typhus, malaria and bacterial meningitis and focuses on Japanese encephalitis and litchi-associated hypoglycaemia; use the India protocol where that epidemiology applies.
# Escalation and follow-up
ICU for GCS 8 or less, status epilepticus, raised intracranial pressure, respiratory failure or autonomic instability.
Involve infectious diseases and neurology early; notify public health for notifiable causes (for example arboviral encephalitis or rabies).
Arrange neuropsychology, rehabilitation and epilepsy follow-up; memory and behavioural problems are common after HSV encephalitis.
# Pitfalls
Delaying aciclovir for CT, LP or MRI.
Stopping aciclovir on a single early negative HSV PCR.
Missing autoimmune encephalitis, non-convulsive status epilepticus or cerebral malaria.
Aciclovir nephrotoxicity from inadequate hydration or unadjusted doses.
# Drugs
Aciclovir
# Dose
Adults and children over 12 years 10 mg/kg IV over 1 hour every 8 hours; children 3 months to 12 years 500 mg/m2 every 8 hours; neonates 20 mg/kg every 8 hours
HSV: 14 to 21 days (21 days in neonates, children and the immunocompromised). Hydrate; reduce for renal impairment.
Aciclovir (VZV encephalitis), 10 to 15 mg/kg IV every 8 hours for 10 to 14 days, IDSA.
Ceftriaxone
# Dose
Adults 2 g IV every 12 hours; children 50 mg/kg every 12 hours (maximum 2 g per dose)
Until bacterial meningitis is excluded.
Amoxicillin, 2 g IV every 4 hours, Listeria cover in older or immunocompromised adults when meningitis is possible.
Doxycycline, Adults 100 mg every 12 hours, Suspected rickettsial or ehrlichial infection.
Ganciclovir plus foscarnet
# Dose
Ganciclovir 5 mg/kg IV every 12 hours plus foscarnet 60 mg/kg IV every 8 hours or 90 mg/kg IV every 12 hours, for 3 weeks
CMV encephalitis, then maintenance. Renal dosing.
Methylprednisolone, 1 g IV once daily for 3 to 5 days, ADEM; also first-line immunotherapy in autoimmune encephalitis under neurology.
