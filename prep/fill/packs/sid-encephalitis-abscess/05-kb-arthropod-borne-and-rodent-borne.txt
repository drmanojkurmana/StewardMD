# Arthropod-Borne and Rodent-Borne Virus Infections
Arthropod-Borne and Rodent-Borne Virus Infections, Infectious / Multisystem.
Aliases: Arboviral infections; Arbovirus infections; Zoonotic arthropod-borne and rodent-borne viral diseases.
# Pathophysiology
These taxonomically diverse zoonotic RNA viruses (Togaviridae/Alphavirus; Flaviviridae/Orthoflavivirus; the bunyaviricete families Arenaviridae, Hantaviridae, Nairoviridae, Peribunyaviridae, Phenuiviridae; Orthomyxoviridae; Bornaviridae and Rhabdoviridae; and the reovirus families) circulate in nature between arthropod vectors (mosquitoes, ticks, sandflies, midges) or chronically infected rodents and vertebrate reservoirs. Arboviruses establish chronic systemic infection of the vector after a blood meal and reach the salivary glands during extrinsic incubation (1-3 weeks in mosquitoes); vertical transovarial transmission also maintains some viruses. In arboviral encephalitis, an initial lymphoid-origin viremia seeds multifocal CNS entry, presumably via olfactory neuroepithelium/cribriform plate or brain capillary endothelium; CNS injury results partly from direct neuronal infection and partly from edema, inflammation, and host immune response, with focal neuronal necrosis, glial nodules, and perivascular lymphoid cuffing. LCM meningitis is T-cell-mediated immunopathology. VHF agents share a core pathophysiology of disrupted vascular function, stability, and integrity producing multisystem organ dysfunction and capillary leak; in HPS, widespread pulmonary capillary leak with depressed cardiac output produces noncardiogenic pulmonary edema and shock.
# Prognosis
Outcome depends heavily on syndrome and specific virus. The F&M syndrome generally resolves completely within 2-5 days, though prolonged asthenia may follow LCMV or dengue infection. A&R illness resolves over days, but joint pain/arthritis may persist for months to years without deformity. Encephalitis ranges from full recovery (often slow, over weeks to months) to permanent deficits or death. HPS carries a case-fatality rate of approximately 30-40% even with appropriate care, though most patients surviving the first 48 hours of hospitalization recover without long-term sequelae. Argentinian and Bolivian hemorrhagic fevers can reach case-fatality rates over 50% in individual outbreaks. About 20% of Lassa virus infections become Lassa fever; survivors have roughly a 30% rate of (usually bilateral) sensorineural deafness, and pregnancy markedly worsens prognosis.
# Severity classification
No single unified severity scale; severity is framed by syndrome. The five-syndrome heuristic (F&M, arthritis and rash, encephalitis, pulmonary disease, viral hemorrhagic fever) stratifies expected severity, with F&M most benign and encephalitis/pulmonary/VHF most severe. Encephalitis severity grades clinically from asymptomatic through febrile headache and aseptic meningitis to full encephalitis with coma. HPS is staged by progression from nonspecific prodrome to the cardiopulmonary phase with respiratory failure and shock. Lassa fever severity/fatality is predicted by high viral load, high serum creatinine and AST, leukocytosis, thrombocytopenia, CNS features, face/neck swelling, hypotension, hemorrhage, jaundice, and pregnancy.
# Clinical pearls
Most arthropod-borne (arbovirus) and rodent-borne zoonotic viral infections in humans are subclinical or unrecognized; humans are usually accidental, dead-end hosts and are not important amplifiers of these viruses.
