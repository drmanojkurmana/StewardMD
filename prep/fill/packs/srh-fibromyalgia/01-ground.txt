# Fibromyalgia and chronic widespread pain (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-fibromyalgia: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

2016 revised ACR diagnostic criteria define Fibromyalgia by Widespread Pain Index (WPI) >= 7 and Symptom Severity Scale (SSS) >= 5, OR WPI 4-6 and SSS >= 9, with generalized pain in >= 4 of 5 defined body regions present >= 3 months.

Central sensitization in fibromyalgia is neurobiologically characterized by decreased descending serotonergic/noradrenergic inhibitory pain pathways and elevated excitatory CSF substance P, glutamate, and nerve growth factor (NGF).

Functional MRI during pain processing demonstrates hyperactivation of the insula, anterior cingulate cortex, and somatosensory cortex at low stimulus pressures, confirming tactile and pressure allodynia.

Pregabalin (alpha-2-delta ligand decreasing presynaptic calcium-dependent excitatory neurotransmitter release) is approved at target doses of 300-450 mg/day divided twice daily for fibromyalgia symptom relief.

Duloxetine (dual serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor, SNRI) administered at 60 mg/day significantly reduces central chronic widespread pain and depressive affect independently of baseline depression scores.

Milnacipran (potent selective SNRI with higher relative norepinephrine selectivity) dosed at 100-200 mg/day improves patient global impression of change, fatigue, and widespread pain scores.

Non-pharmacologic management carries highest EULAR grade of recommendation, specifically graded low-impact aerobic exercise (walking, swimming, cycling >= 150 minutes/week) and cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT).

Opioids and systemic corticosteroids show zero clinical efficacy in central pain processing in fibromyalgia and are strongly contraindicated due to hyperalgesia and dependence risks.
