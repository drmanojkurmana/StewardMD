Pressure defines the disease. Hepatic venous pressure gradient over 10 mmHg marks clinically significant portal hypertension and over 12 mmHg predicts variceal bleeding, measured by transjugular catheterisation of the hepatic vein.

Bleeding is fought on three fronts at once. Acute variceal bleed gets vasoactive octreotide or terlipressin plus antibiotics and band ligation within 12 hours, with balloon tamponade as a bridge for refractory bleeding while definitive therapy is arranged.

Early shunting saves lives. Pre-emptive TIPS within 72 hours suits Child-Pugh B with active bleeding or Child-Pugh C under 14 points, cutting rebleeding and mortality against endoscopy alone in randomised trials.

Prevention pairs drugs with bands. Secondary prophylaxis combines nonselective beta-blockers titrated to heart rate 55 to 60 with repeat banding every 2 to 4 weeks until variceal eradication is complete and red signs disappear.

Selective shunts spare the liver. Distal splenorenal Warren shunt decompresses varices while preserving portal flow to the liver, suiting Child-Pugh A patients with refractory bleeding and good hepatic reserve.

Total shunts trade bleeding for confusion. Portocaval side-to-side shunt fully diverts portal flow with high encephalopathy risk, now reserved for transplant-ineligible Child-Pugh A patients with failed TIPS and recurrent bleeding.

Noncirrhotic block needs devascularisation. Sugiura devascularisation with oesophageal transection and splenectomy suits noncirrhotic portal fibrosis and extrahepatic portal vein obstruction where shunts fail or clot.

Fundal varices glue shut. Gastric fundal varices with red signs get cyanoacrylate glue injection, and balloon-occluded retrograde transvenous obliteration suits gastrorenal shunts when TIPS is contraindicated by encephalopathy or anatomy.
