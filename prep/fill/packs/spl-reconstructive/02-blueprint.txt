# Neligan Plastic Surgery 5th Edition (2024): Comprehensive Reference for Reconstructive Surgery and Flaps

The clinical discipline of Reconstructive Surgery and Flaps addresses complex reconstructive and aesthetic surgical problems across craniofacial, microsurgical, breast, trunk, hand, and integumentary domains.

## Principles of Flap Physiology and Classifications
Citations and Chapter Sources: Plastic Surgery 5E Vol 1 Ch 24 (Flap physiology, classification, and applications)

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Reconstructive ladder progresses from primary closure, healing by secondary intention, skin graft, local flap, regional flap, to distant free tissue transfer.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: reconstructive ladder, free tissue transfer

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Random pattern flaps rely on the subdermal plexus without a named axial vessel, with a traditional length-to-width ratio rarely exceeding 2:1 on the trunk or extremities.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 2:1
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: subdermal plexus, random pattern

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Axial pattern flaps are supplied by a named anatomically recognized arteriovenous vascular axis running along the long axis of the flap.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: axial pattern, arteriovenous

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Mathes and Nahai classify muscle flaps into 5 types based on their vascular pedicle anatomy.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 5
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Mathes and Nahai, muscle flaps

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Type I muscle flap has a single vascular pedicle, exemplified by the tensor fasciae latae and gastrocnemius muscles.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Type I, tensor fasciae latae, gastrocnemius

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Type II muscle flap has a dominant vascular pedicle and minor pedicles, exemplified by the gracilis, trapezius, and soleus muscles.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Type II, gracilis, soleus

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Type III muscle flap has two dominant pedicles of equal size, exemplified by the gluteus maximus and rectus abdominis muscles.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: two
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Type III, gluteus maximus, rectus abdominis

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Type IV muscle flap has segmental pedicles without a dominant supply, exemplified by the sartorius and tibialis anterior muscles.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Type IV, segmental pedicles, sartorius

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Type V muscle flap has one dominant pedicle and secondary segmental pedicles, exemplified by the latissimus dorsi and pectoralis major muscles.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Type V, latissimus dorsi, pectoralis major

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Surgical delay phenomenon enhances flap survival by causing dilation of choke vessels and longitudinal reorientation of blood flow over 10 to 14 days.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 10, 14
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: delay phenomenon, choke vessels
