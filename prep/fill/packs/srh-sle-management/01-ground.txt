# Management of SLE (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-sle-management: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) is strongly recommended for all patients with SLE unless contraindicated, maintaining daily dosing at or below 5.0 mg/kg actual body weight to minimize long-term retinal toxicity, halving overall mortality and preventing disease flares.

The therapeutic goal in SLE is achieving the Lupus Low Disease Activity State (LLDAS) or clinical remission: LLDAS requires SLEDAI-2K <=4, no new organ activity, physician global assessment <=1.0, and maintenance prednisone <=7.5 mg daily.

Systemic glucocorticoids should be rapidly tapered aiming for a maintenance dose of prednisone <=5.0 mg/day or complete withdrawal whenever clinically feasible, to prevent irreversible cardiovascular, cataract, and osteoporotic organ damage accrual.

Methotrexate (15 to 25 mg weekly) or azathioprine (2 mg/kg daily) is recommended as early steroid-sparing therapy for active musculoskeletal and cutaneous manifestations failing hydroxychloroquine monotherapy.

Mycophenolate mofetil (2 to 3 g daily) is the preferred immunosuppressive agent for renal and severe extra-articular manifestations, while being strictly contraindicated during pregnancy due to embryofetal teratogenicity.

Belimumab (anti-BAFF/BLyS monoclonal antibody, 10 mg/kg IV monthly or 200 mg SC weekly) is indicated for active SLE failing first-line csDMARDs and in active lupus nephritis, significantly reducing flares and glucocorticoid requirements.

Anifrolumab (anti-IFNAR1 monoclonal antibody, 300 mg IV every 4 weeks) is recommended for moderate-to-severe SLE with refractory cutaneous or musculoskeletal activity, with herpes zoster vaccination prioritized before initiation.

Rituximab (anti-CD20) is recommended off-label for severe, refractory, organ-threatening disease manifestations (severe thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia, refractory proliferative lupus nephritis, or refractory CNS lupus) failing standard therapies.
