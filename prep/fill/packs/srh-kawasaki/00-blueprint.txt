# Kawasaki disease dosing and thresholds (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison; AHA Kawasaki guidance)
Kawasaki is febrile medium-vessel vasculitis below 5 years in above 85 percent, the top acquired pediatric heart disease cause in developed countries.
Fever for 5 days or more with at least 4 of 5 features defines complete disease: bilateral non-exudative bulbar injection, strawberry tongue with red fissured lips, polymorphous non-vesicular rash, extremity erythema with edema and periungual peeling, and cervical nodes of 1.5 cm or more, usually single-sided.
Untreated coronary aneurysms hit up to 25 percent; lumens are normalized to body surface area as echocardiographic Z-scores.
Aneurysm bands are dilation only (Z 2 to below 2.5), small (Z 2.5 to below 5), medium (Z 5 to below 10 with absolute below 8 mm), and giant (Z 10 or higher or absolute 8 mm or higher).
Primary therapy is single IVIG 2 g per kg over 10 to 12 hours inside the first 10 days of fever, cutting aneurysms from 25 percent to below 4 percent to 5 percent.
Aspirin runs at 30 mg to 50 mg per kg daily or 80 mg to 100 mg per kg daily until afebrile for 48 to 72 hours, then 3 mg to 5 mg per kg daily for 6 to 8 weeks with normal coronaries.
IVIG resistance with fever at or beyond 36 hours after infusion hits 10 percent to 20 percent; Kobayashi and Egami scores pick high-risk children for upfront adjuvant steroids.
Refractory disease takes second IVIG 2 g per kg, IV methylprednisolone pulse 30 mg per kg for 3 days, or infliximab 5 mg per kg to quench vasculitis.
