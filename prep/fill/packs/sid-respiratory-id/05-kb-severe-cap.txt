# Severe Community Acquired Pneumonia
Severe Community Acquired Pneumonia, Respiratory.
# Pathogens
Severe CAP carries a broader and more virulent pathogen spectrum than non-severe CAP. Legionella and S. aureus are disproportionately represented in ICU-level disease. Gram-negatives become relevant with comorbidity, aspiration risk, or recent hospitalization.
Very likely: Streptococcus pneumoniae; Legionella pneumophila.
Likely: Staphylococcus aureus (including post-influenza); Gram-negative enteric bacilli; Haemophilus influenzae.
Possible: Pseudomonas aeruginosa (if structural lung disease).
Red flags: Immediate empiric antibiotics required.
# Investigations
Blood cultures (x2), Bacteremia rate is higher in severe CAP; identifies organism and susceptibility for de-escalation.
Sputum culture and Gram stain, Helps target and de-escalate empiric therapy.
Urinary antigen (pneumococcal and Legionella), Rapid, useful for severe presentations to guide targeted therapy.
ABG, Quantifies hypoxia/hypercapnia and guides ventilatory support decisions.
Chest X-ray / CT chest, Defines extent (multilobar disease, effusion, cavitation) which affects prognosis and management.
Lactate, Screens for tissue hypoperfusion and helps gauge severity/sepsis overlap.
Immediate empiric antibiotics required
