# Psoriatic arthritis (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-psa: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) occurs in up to 30% of patients with cutaneous psoriasis, typically developing 5 to 10 years after skin lesions, although arthritis precedes psoriasis in 15% of cases; nail dystrophy (pitting, onycholysis, subungual hyperkeratosis, oil-drop sign) strongly correlates with enthesitis of the distal phalanx extensor tendon insertion and DIP joint arthritis.

The CASPAR (Classification Criteria for Psoriatic Arthritis) criteria require established inflammatory articular disease (joint, spine, or entheseal) plus at least 3 points from five categories: current psoriasis (2 points) or personal/family history (1 point), typical psoriatic nail dystrophy (1 point), negative rheumatoid factor (1 point), current or historical dactylitis (1 point), and radiographic evidence of juxta-articular new bone formation (1 point).

Moll and Wright recognized five clinical patterns of psoriatic arthritis: asymmetric oligoarthritis (most common initially), symmetric polyarthritis (resembling RA but RF-negative), distal interphalangeal (DIP) joint predominant arthritis, axial spondyloarthritis/sacroiliitis, and arthritis mutilans with severe digital telescoping ('opera-glass hand').

Dactylitis ('sausage digit') represents diffuse, uniform, painful swelling of an entire finger or toe caused by concurrent flexor tenosynovitis and adjacent soft tissue enthesitis, serving as a hallmark diagnostic and poor prognostic indicator in psoriatic arthritis.

Radiographic hallmarks of psoriatic arthritis reflect a unique coexistence of destructive erosive bone changes and proliferative periosteal new bone formation in the same joint: marginal erosions with adjacent bone whittling, pencil-in-cup deformity, periostitis ('whiskering'), acro-osteolysis of distal phalanges, and asymmetric paraspinal coarse syndesmophytes.

In peripheral psoriatic arthritis, methotrexate is the most common first-line conventional synthetic DMARD due to dual cutaneous and joint efficacy; leflunomide and sulfasalazine represent alternatives, while systemic glucocorticoids are used with extreme caution because rapid steroid taper can precipitate life-threatening generalized pustular psoriasis (von Zumbusch).

Biologic and targeted synthetic therapies for psoriatic arthritis span multiple validated cytokine pathways: TNF inhibitors, IL-17A inhibitors (secukinumab, ixekizumab), IL-17A/F dual inhibitor (bimekizumab), IL-12/23 inhibitor (ustekinumab), IL-23p19 specific inhibitors (guselkumab, risankizumab), oral phosphodiesterase-4 (PDE4) inhibitor (apremilast), and JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, upadacitinib).

Deucravacitinib is an oral, first-in-class selective allosteric TYK2 (tyrosine kinase 2) inhibitor that binds to the regulatory pseudokinase domain rather than the conserved ATP-catalytic site, providing selective inhibition of IL-23, IL-12, and type I interferon signaling without off-target JAK1/2/3 hematologic or thrombotic toxicities, approved for plaque psoriasis and active in PsA.
