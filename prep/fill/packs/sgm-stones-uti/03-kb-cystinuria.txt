# Cystinuria
Cystinuria, Renal / Genetics, Nephrology / Clinical Genetics.
Aliases: SLC3A1; SLC7A9; cystine stone disease; cystine nephrolithiasis.
# Pathophysiology
Cystinuria results from defective transport of the dibasic amino acids cystine, ornithine, lysine and arginine across the apical membrane of proximal renal tubular cells and intestinal epithelium. Biallelic mutations in SLC3A1 (encoding the rBAT subunit) or SLC7A9 (encoding the b0,+AT subunit) impair reabsorption, so large amounts of cystine reach the urine. Because cystine is relatively insoluble at normal urinary pH, it precipitates and aggregates into recurrent calculi, with solubility improving as pH rises. Stone burden and obstruction can produce recurrent colic, infection and, over time, chronic kidney damage.
# Prognosis
With lifelong high fluid intake, urinary alkalinisation and monitoring, stone recurrence can be markedly reduced, but adherence is demanding and recurrent stones or interventions may lead to chronic kidney disease over decades.
# Clinical pearls
An inherited disorder of dibasic amino acid transport in the proximal tubule and gut, causing recurrent cystine stones from childhood or early adulthood
Suspect it in any young patient with recurrent radio-opaque stones, a family history, or bilateral or staghorn calculi
