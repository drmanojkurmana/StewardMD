# Acute upper GI bleeding (non-variceal)
Adults
Emergency department, ward, ICU, endoscopy unit
Resuscitate, risk-stratify with the Glasgow-Blatchford score (discharge if 0 to 1), transfuse restrictively (Hb below 7 g/dL, or below 8 g/dL with cardiovascular disease), endoscopy within 24 hours, then high-dose PPI for 72 hours after endoscopic haemostasis.
Aliases: UGIB; UGI bleed; NVUGIB; NVUGIH; peptic ulcer bleeding; bleeding ulcer; Glasgow-Blatchford score; GBS; Forrest classification.
# Recognise and risk-stratify
Haematemesis, coffee-ground vomitus or melaena. Brisk haematochezia with shock may also come from an upper GI source.
Calculate the Glasgow-Blatchford score (GBS: blood urea, haemoglobin, systolic BP, pulse, melaena, syncope, hepatic disease, cardiac failure) before endoscopy.
GBS 0 to 1: very low risk of intervention or death. Discharge with early outpatient endoscopy if there is no other reason to admit (ACG 2021, ESGE 2021).
Known or suspected cirrhosis or portal hypertension (jaundice, ascites, splenomegaly, spider naevi): treat as variceal bleeding with a vasoactive drug and antibiotics until endoscopy (see Acute variceal bleeding protocol).
Common causes: peptic ulcer (NSAIDs, aspirin, H. pylori), erosive oesophagitis or gastroduodenitis, Mallory-Weiss tear, malignancy, Dieulafoy lesion.
# Resuscitation
Assess airway, breathing and circulation; two large-bore IV cannulae; group and crossmatch; give IV crystalloid promptly if haemodynamically unstable.
Restrictive transfusion: transfuse red cells at Hb 7 g/dL or below, aiming for 7 to 9 g/dL after transfusion (ESGE 2021).
Pre-existing cardiovascular disease: transfuse at Hb 8 g/dL or below (ESGE target 10 g/dL or more after transfusion). Hypotensive or massively bleeding patients may need blood before Hb reaches the threshold.
Intubate before endoscopy only for ongoing haematemesis, agitation or encephalopathy with inability to protect the airway. Nasogastric aspiration or lavage is not routinely recommended.
Do not give tranexamic acid (ESGE strong recommendation against) or somatostatin/octreotide for non-variceal bleeding.
Warfarin with haemodynamic instability: hold warfarin, give low-dose vitamin K plus prothrombin complex concentrate (FFP only if PCC unavailable). DOAC: hold; consider a specific reversal agent or PCC only for severe ongoing bleeding. Do not delay endoscopy for INR.
Aspirin for secondary prevention: continue, or restart within 3 to 5 days if stopped. Aspirin for primary prevention: stop. Dual antiplatelet therapy: continue aspirin, withhold the P2Y12 inhibitor and restart within 5 days with cardiology input. No routine platelet transfusion.
# Before endoscopy and timing
High-dose IV PPI before endoscopy may be considered to downstage ulcer stigmata (ESGE); it must not delay endoscopy. ACG 2021 made no recommendation for or against pre-endoscopic PPI.
Erythromycin 250 mg IV given 30 to 120 minutes before endoscopy improves visualisation in severe or ongoing bleeding. Avoid with QT prolongation or interacting drugs. IV erythromycin is not available everywhere; do not delay endoscopy to obtain it.
Endoscopy within 24 hours of presentation after resuscitation (ACG 2021, ESGE 2021). ESGE does not recommend urgent endoscopy within 12 hours over early endoscopy, and endoscopy within 6 hours may worsen outcomes: resuscitate first.
Exsanguinating bleeding not responding to resuscitation: involve the endoscopist, interventional radiology and surgery immediately.
# Endoscopic therapy and after endoscopy
Treat active spurting or oozing (Forrest Ia, Ib) and non-bleeding visible vessel (IIa) endoscopically. Adrenaline injection must be combined with a second modality (thermal contact, clips); consider clot removal for adherent clot (IIb).
After endoscopic haemostasis (or untreated adherent clot): high-dose PPI for 72 hours, either 80 mg IV bolus then 8 mg/h infusion, or intermittent 40 mg IV or oral 2 to 4 times daily (ACG 2021).
High-risk ulcers treated endoscopically: continue oral PPI twice daily until 2 weeks after the index endoscopy, then once daily for a duration set by the ulcer cause (ACG 2021).
Low-risk stigmata (clean base, flat pigmented spot): no endoscopic therapy, oral PPI, early feeding and early discharge if otherwise stable.
Test for H. pylori at the index endoscopy and treat if positive; retest later if the acute test is negative; confirm eradication.
Routine second-look endoscopy is not recommended.
# Monitoring for rebleeding
Close monitoring of pulse, BP, urine output and mental state in a monitored bed for high-risk patients; repeat Hb after resuscitation and whenever rebleeding is suspected.
Rebleeding: fresh haematemesis, recurrent melaena or haematochezia after stool colour normalised, new shock after stabilisation, or Hb fall of 2 g/dL or more after a stable value (ESGE).
Treat suspected rebleeding as an emergency: resuscitate, inform the endoscopist and arrange repeat endoscopy (see escalation).
# Recurrent or refractory bleeding
Recurrent bleeding: repeat endoscopy with endoscopic therapy (consider an over-the-scope or cap-mounted clip).
Failed second endoscopic attempt: transcatheter arterial embolisation (TAE). Surgery if TAE is unavailable or fails.
Persistent bleeding despite standard modalities: topical haemostatic powder or cap-mounted clip as a bridge while arranging TAE.
# Secondary prevention
Stop NSAIDs. If an NSAID is essential, give with a PPI after discussion of risk.
Give PPI co-therapy to patients who need ongoing dual antiplatelet therapy or anticoagulation after an ulcer bleed.
Resume anticoagulation once bleeding is controlled, preferably within or soon after 7 days, guided by thrombotic risk (ESGE).
# Pitfalls
Missing variceal bleeding in undiagnosed cirrhosis: give vasoactive drug and antibiotics empirically when portal hypertension is suspected.
Over-transfusion to a normal Hb, which increases rebleeding and mortality.
Delaying endoscopy to correct INR, or giving tranexamic acid.
Forgetting to restart secondary-prevention aspirin or to treat H. pylori.
# Drugs
Pantoprazole or esomeprazole (IV)
80 mg IV bolus, then 8 mg/h continuous infusion for 72 hours after endoscopic haemostasis
Alternative: 40 mg IV or oral 2 to 4 times daily (intermittent high-dose). Then oral twice daily until 2 weeks after endoscopy for high-risk ulcers.
Erythromycin (IV), 250 mg IV single dose, 30 to 120 minutes before endoscopy, Prokinetic to clear the stomach. Avoid with prolonged QT, electrolyte disturbance or interacting drugs.
Packed red cells
Transfuse at Hb 7 g/dL or below (8 g/dL or below with cardiovascular disease)
Target 7 to 9 g/dL after transfusion; 10 g/dL or more with cardiovascular disease (ESGE).
Oral PPI (maintenance)
# Dose
e.g. pantoprazole 40 mg orally twice daily until 2 weeks after endoscopy, then 40 mg once daily
Duration after 2 weeks depends on ulcer cause, H. pylori status and ongoing antithrombotic or NSAID use.
