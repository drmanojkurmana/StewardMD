# Enteric fever: typhoid and paratyphoid (international guidance)
Adults and children
Outpatient, emergency department, ward
Take blood cultures before antibiotics and a detailed travel history. Avoid empirical fluoroquinolones. Uncomplicated: oral azithromycin. Complicated: IV ceftriaxone, or a carbapenem if acquired in Pakistan or Iraq (XDR risk). Tailor to susceptibility, give dexamethasone for severe typhoid with encephalopathy or shock, and follow public health clearance rules.
Aliases: enteric fever; Salmonella Typhi; Salmonella Paratyphi A; XDR typhoid; MDR typhoid; returning traveller fever; typhoid perforation; TCV; Typhim Vi; Ty21a.
# Recognise
Incubation 6 to 30 days for typhoid and 1 to 10 days for paratyphoid. Insidious fatigue and fever rising to 39 to 40°C by day 3 or 4, with headache, anorexia, abdominal pain, and constipation or diarrhoea.
Complications affect 10 to 15% of hospitalised patients, usually after 2 to 3 weeks of illness: gastrointestinal bleeding, intestinal perforation and encephalopathy. Mortality is under 1% with prompt treatment and above 10% untreated.
