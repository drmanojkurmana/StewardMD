# Acute variceal bleeding in cirrhosis
Adults
Emergency department, HDU, ICU, endoscopy unit
Suspected variceal bleeding: start a vasoactive drug (terlipressin, octreotide or somatostatin) and IV ceftriaxone immediately, transfuse restrictively (Hb 7 to 8 g/dL), band ligation within 12 hours, pre-emptive TIPS for high-risk patients, then beta-blocker plus EVL.
Aliases: AVB; AVH; variceal haemorrhage; variceal hemorrhage; oesophageal varices; esophageal varices; gastric varices; portal hypertensive bleeding; EVL; band ligation; pre-emptive TIPS; Baveno VII.
# Recognise
Upper GI bleeding in a patient with known or suspected cirrhosis or portal hypertension is variceal until proven otherwise: start vasoactive drug and antibiotics before endoscopy.
Include non-cirrhotic portal hypertension (for example extrahepatic portal vein obstruction), a relatively common cause of variceal bleeding in India.
Score Child-Pugh and MELD at admission: Child-Pugh C, a high MELD and failure to achieve primary haemostasis predict 6-week mortality (Baveno VII).
Manage in an intensive or intermediate care unit.
# Resuscitation and first drugs
Restore and maintain haemodynamic stability with cautious volume replacement; avoid over-expansion, which raises portal pressure.
Transfuse red cells restrictively: start at a threshold of about Hb 7 g/dL (AASLD) and target Hb 7 to 8 g/dL (Baveno VII); allow higher targets for cardiovascular disease, older age, ongoing bleeding or instability.
Start a vasoactive drug as soon as variceal bleeding is suspected (terlipressin, octreotide or somatostatin; doses below) and continue for 2 to 5 days if variceal bleeding is confirmed.
Give antibiotic prophylaxis from admission: ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours, adjusted to local resistance patterns; stop when bleeding is controlled and there is no infection (AASLD 2024: up to 5 days).
Intubate before endoscopy if consciousness is impaired or there is active haematemesis; extubate as soon as possible afterwards.
Do not give fresh frozen plasma to correct INR, platelets to a target count, recombinant factor VIIa or tranexamic acid. Temporarily stop anticoagulants.
Give lactulose (oral or enema) to clear blood from the gut and prevent hepatic encephalopathy. Pass a nasogastric tube only with caution (aspiration risk).
# Endoscopy
Upper endoscopy within 12 hours of presentation after resuscitation; if unstable, as soon as it is safe (Baveno VII, AASLD 2024).
Erythromycin 250 mg IV 30 to 120 minutes before endoscopy, if no QT prolongation, improves visualisation.
Oesophageal varices: endoscopic band ligation (EVL) is the treatment of choice.
Isolated gastric varices (IGV1) and GOV2: tissue adhesive (N-butyl-cyanoacrylate). GOV1: EVL or tissue adhesive.
Stop a PPI started before endoscopy once variceal bleeding is confirmed, unless there is another indication (infection and encephalopathy risk).
After stabilisation, obtain contrast CT or MRI (or Doppler ultrasound) to look for portal vein thrombosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.
# Pre-emptive TIPS and refractory bleeding
Pre-emptive covered (PTFE) TIPS within 72 hours, ideally within 24 hours, for oesophageal or GOV1/GOV2 bleeding with Child-Pugh C below 14 points, or Child-Pugh B above 7 with active bleeding at endoscopy, or HVPG above 20 mmHg (Baveno VII). Transfer to a TIPS centre if not available locally.
Encephalopathy, ACLF or hyperbilirubinaemia at admission are not contraindications to pre-emptive TIPS in eligible patients.
Uncontrolled bleeding despite vasoactive drug and EVL: balloon tamponade (maximum 24 hours) or an oesophageal self-expanding metal stent (up to 7 days; safer) as a bridge to salvage TIPS.
Rebleeding within 5 days: repeat endoscopy; rescue TIPS is often the best option if no contraindication.
TIPS may be futile with Child-Pugh 14 or more, or MELD above 30 with lactate above 12 mmol/L, unless early transplant is possible.
# Monitoring
Treatment failure: failure to control bleeding or rebleeding within the first 5 days.
Terlipressin: monitor serum sodium (hyponatraemia) and for ischaemic complications (chest pain, digital, skin or bowel ischaemia).
Watch for infection, hepatic encephalopathy and AKI; start oral nutrition as soon as bleeding is controlled and mentation allows.
# Prevent rebleeding
Without pre-emptive TIPS: start a non-selective beta-blocker (propranolol or nadolol) or carvedilol plus EVL. Start the beta-blocker as the vasoactive drug is stopped, no later than day 5 (AASLD 2024).
Repeat EVL every 2 to 4 weeks until varices are eradicated.
Reduce or stop the beta-blocker if systolic BP falls below 90 mmHg, MAP below 65 mmHg, or HRS-AKI develops; use EVL alone if intolerant.
TIPS for rebleeding despite beta-blocker plus EVL, and consider it early in recurrent ascites. Refer decompensated patients for transplant evaluation.
# Pitfalls
Waiting for endoscopy before starting the vasoactive drug and antibiotics.
Liberal transfusion or FFP to a normal INR: raises portal pressure and rebleeding risk; INR does not reflect bleeding risk in cirrhosis.
Missing the 72-hour window for pre-emptive TIPS in Child C or Child B with active bleeding.
Forgetting to start secondary prophylaxis before discharge.
# Drugs
Terlipressin
# Dose
2 mg IV every 4 hours until bleeding is controlled (first 24 to 48 hours), then 1 mg IV every 4 hours; total 2 to 5 days
AASLD 2024 table allows every 4 to 6 hours. Baseline ECG; caution in ischaemic heart or peripheral vascular disease; monitor sodium.
Octreotide
# Dose
50 mcg IV bolus (repeat in the first hour if bleeding continues), then 50 mcg/h IV infusion for 2 to 5 days
Use one vasoactive drug only.
Somatostatin
# Dose
250 mcg IV bolus (may repeat in the first hour), then 250 to 500 mcg/h IV infusion for 2 to 5 days
Alternative to terlipressin or octreotide.
Ceftriaxone
1 g IV every 24 hours
Prophylaxis from admission; up to 5 days (AASLD 2024) or maximum 7 days (AASLD 2016). Choose per local resistance.
Erythromycin (IV), 250 mg IV single dose, 30 to 120 minutes before endoscopy, Avoid with QT prolongation.
Lactulose
# Dose
15 to 30 mL (10 to 20 g) orally or by NG tube, titrated to 2 to 3 soft stools a day; or enema if unable to swallow
Clears blood from the gut to prevent hepatic encephalopathy.
Carvedilol (secondary prophylaxis)
# Dose
6.25 mg orally once daily, increase to 6.25 mg twice daily after 3 days; maximum 12.5 mg/day
Keep systolic BP 90 mmHg or above. Combine with EVL.
Propranolol (secondary prophylaxis)
# Dose
20 to 40 mg orally twice daily, titrate every 2 to 3 days to heart rate 55 to 60/min
Maximum 320 mg/day without ascites, 160 mg/day with ascites. Nadolol 20 to 40 mg once daily is an alternative.
