# Ascites, SBP and hepatorenal syndrome in cirrhosis
Adults
Emergency department, ward, day-care paracentesis, clinic
Tap every new or admitted ascites (SAAG, PMN count, protein, culture); treat with 2 g sodium diet and spironolactone plus furosemide (100:40); give albumin 6 to 8 g per litre after paracentesis over 5 L; treat SBP (PMN above 250) with antibiotics plus albumin 1.5 g/kg day 1 and 1 g/kg day 3; treat HRS-AKI with terlipressin plus albumin.
Aliases: cirrhotic ascites; SBP; spontaneous bacterial peritonitis; SAAG; serum ascites albumin gradient; LVP; large-volume paracentesis; HRS; hepatorenal syndrome; refractory ascites; terlipressin albumin; diuretics cirrhosis.
# Diagnostic paracentesis
Tap every patient with new-onset ascites, every patient with ascites admitted to hospital (even without symptoms), and any patient with ascites who develops fever, abdominal pain, encephalopathy, AKI, jaundice or unexplained deterioration.
Send ascitic cell count with differential (neutrophil/PMN count), total protein and albumin with a same-time serum albumin. Inoculate aerobic and anaerobic blood culture bottles at the bedside before antibiotics.
SAAG (serum albumin minus ascitic albumin) 1.1 g/dL or more = portal hypertension (about 97% accurate). Below 1.1 g/dL: look for peritoneal tuberculosis, malignancy, pancreatic ascites or nephrotic syndrome.
High SAAG with ascitic protein above 2.5 g/dL suggests cardiac ascites. Low SAAG, high-protein, lymphocyte-predominant fluid in India: check ascitic ADA for tuberculous peritonitis; send cytology, AFB studies or amylase as indicated.
Paracentesis is safe despite a raised INR or low platelets; routine FFP or platelet transfusion is not needed.
# Uncomplicated ascites (grade 2)
Moderate sodium restriction to 2 g (90 mmol) a day with dietitian advice; avoid malnutrition. No fluid restriction unless serum sodium is 125 mmol/L or less.
Spironolactone 100 mg once daily, with or without furosemide 40 mg once daily. A first episode may respond to spironolactone alone; long-standing ascites responds better to combination therapy.
Increase stepwise, at intervals of at least 72 hours, keeping the 100:40 ratio, to a maximum of spironolactone 400 mg and furosemide 160 mg a day.
Target weight loss: up to 0.5 kg/day without peripheral oedema, up to 1 kg/day with oedema. Spot urine sodium/potassium ratio above 1 with no weight loss suggests dietary sodium excess.
Avoid NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers and aminoglycosides.
Tense (grade 3) ascites: large-volume paracentesis first, then sodium restriction and diuretics. Refer grade 2 or 3 ascites for transplant evaluation.
# Large-volume paracentesis and refractory ascites
Paracentesis of more than 5 L: give 20% albumin 6 to 8 g per litre removed (AASLD 2021; BSG 2021 uses 8 g/L), for example about 40 g after 5 L, to prevent post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction.
Risk of circulatory dysfunction rises when more than 8 L is removed in one session.
Consider albumin even for smaller taps if systolic BP is below 90 mmHg, serum sodium below 130 mmol/L or AKI is present.
