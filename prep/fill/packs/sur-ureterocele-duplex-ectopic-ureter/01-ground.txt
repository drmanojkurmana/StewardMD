The Weigert-Meyer law rules duplex kidneys: the upper pole ureter lands inferomedial and ectopic, prone to upper pole obstruction with ureterocele, while the lower pole ureter lands superolateral and refluxes with lower pole reflux, so both moieties are imaged separately since one twin hides the other. A ureterocele balloons the terminal ureter, either intravesical ureterocele confined to the bladder or ectopic ureterocele spilling to the neck or urethra, with ectopic forms obstructing and infecting more through narrow necks.

The cobra head sign, also called the spring onion sign, shows a dense round filling defect ringed by a pale halo in a full bladder on urography, since half empty bladders hide the halo. Endoscopic puncture by Bugbee electrode, cold knife, or laser gives first ureterocele decompression for obstructing, especially septic neonatal, ureteroceles, punctured low and medial to preserve continence, with de novo reflux watched afterwards. In girls an ectopic ureter female may open beyond the sphincter into urethra, vagina, or vestibule, causing continuous incontinence with dribbling despite normal voiding cycles, as the upper moiety bypasses control. In boys an ectopic ureter male always joins above the sphincter at posterior urethra, seminal vesicle, or ejaculatory duct and never causes incontinence, presenting instead with infection or epididymitis.

Dead moieties under 10% split function on DMSA undergo laparoscopic or robotic heminephroureterectomy, upper pole excision, leaving short stumps against later infection, while functioning systems take ureteroureterostomy, tube to tube join of blocked upper to working lower ureter for moiety preservation without pelvic dissection, stented briefly till drainage is confirmed. Antenatal hydronephrosis with a duplex system is watched by serial ultrasound first, since many neonatal ureteroceles decompress their pressure spontaneously as the child grows. Prophylactic antibiotics bridge high grade obstruction until definitive repair, and febrile breakthrough mandates admission. Duplex and ectopic ureter care matches plumbing to function.
