## Cleft Lip: Embryology, Millard Rotation-Advancement and Fisher Repairs
Citations and Chapter Sources: Plastic Surgery 5E Vol 3 Ch 22 (Cleft lip: principles and primary repair)

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Cleft lip results from failure of fusion between the maxillary prominence and the medial nasal prominence around the 4th to 7th week of embryogenesis.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 4, 7
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: cleft lip, maxillary prominence, medial nasal prominence

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: The classic Rule of Tens for safe timing of elective cleft lip repair mandates age at least 10 weeks, weight 10 pounds, and hemoglobin 10 g/dL.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 10
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Rule of Tens, cleft lip repair, hemoglobin

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Orbicularis oris muscle in a cleft lip runs abnormally along the cleft margin to insert inappropriately into the anterior nasal spine and alar base.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: orbicularis oris, nasal spine, alar base

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Millard rotation-advancement repair rotates the shortened medial lip downward with a back-cut and advances the lateral lip element into the rotation gap.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Millard, rotation-advancement, back-cut

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Fisher anatomical subunit repair plans incisions strictly along aesthetic subunit junctions, avoiding the high cross-lip scar of rotation-advancement.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Fisher, anatomical subunit repair

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Bilateral cleft lip repair preserves the prolabium to construct the philtrum central column while repositioning the premaxilla into normal maxillary arch contour.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: bilateral cleft lip, prolabium, premaxilla

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Primary cleft rhinoplasty simultaneously mobilises and repositions the displaced lower lateral alar cartilage to recreate nasal tip symmetry.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: cleft rhinoplasty, alar cartilage

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Presurgical infant orthopedics such as nasoalveolar molding NAM realigns alveolar segments and expands columellar height prior to surgical repair.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: nasoalveolar molding, NAM


Comprehensive Textbook Context:
Surgical planning in Cleft Lip Reconstruction requires meticulous anatomical grounding, precise knowledge of blood supply, innervation, fascial planes, and tissue biomechanics.
Operative interventions balance functional restoration of speech, swallowing, mastication, sensory discrimination, and locomotion with aesthetic symmetry and minimal donor site morbidity.
Preoperative evaluation integrates acoustic Doppler, computed tomography angiography, indocyanine green fluorescence imaging, and patient-specific risk factor optimization including smoking cessation, strict glycemic control, and blood pressure management.
Postoperative care prioritizes early mobilization, monitoring of capillary refill, tissue turgor, pinprick bleeding, active rehabilitation, and vigilant surveillance for hematoma, seroma, infection, or microvascular thrombosis.
