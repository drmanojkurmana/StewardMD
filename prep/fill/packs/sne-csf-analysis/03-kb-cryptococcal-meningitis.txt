# Cryptococcal meningitis, HIV-associated (adult)
Adults
Ward, HDU, ART clinic
Diagnose by lumbar puncture with opening pressure and CSF cryptococcal antigen; induce with a single 10 mg/kg dose of liposomal amphotericin B plus 14 days of flucytosine and fluconazole, drain raised pressure by repeated therapeutic lumbar puncture, and defer ART for 4 to 6 weeks.
Aliases: cryptococcal meningitis; crypto meningitis; CM; Cryptococcus neoformans; CrAg; cryptococcal antigen; India ink; liposomal amphotericin; AmBisome; therapeutic lumbar puncture; raised intracranial pressure.
# Recognise
Suspect in anyone with HIV and a low CD4 count (usually below 100 cells/mm3) with headache, fever, vomiting, confusion, visual loss, seizures or cranial nerve palsy (especially VI). Neck stiffness is often absent.
Raised intracranial pressure (CSF opening pressure 25 cmH2O or more) occurs in up to 80% and is a major cause of death and blindness.
Headache or neurological symptoms with a positive serum CrAg: treat as meningitis until a lumbar puncture proves otherwise.
Differentials: TB meningitis, bacterial and viral meningitis, toxoplasmosis and other space-occupying lesions.
# Diagnosis
Prompt lumbar puncture with opening pressure and rapid CSF CrAg (lateral flow assay) is the preferred approach. Send CSF fungal culture for a first episode.
If CrAg testing is unavailable, use CSF India ink; it has low sensitivity, so confirm a negative result with CrAg or culture.
Raised pressure does not contraindicate LP in cryptococcal meningitis. Image first only for focal signs (other than VI palsy), recurrent seizures or coagulopathy, and do not let imaging delay the LP.
LP not possible: serum, plasma or whole-blood CrAg supports the diagnosis; start treatment.
Baseline: HIV test if status unknown, CD4, viral load, creatinine, potassium, magnesium, CBC, blood cultures, CSF TB molecular test, and a pregnancy test.
# Induction (first 2 weeks)
Preferred (WHO 2022): liposomal amphotericin B 10 mg/kg IV as a single dose on day 1, plus flucytosine 100 mg/kg/day orally in 4 divided doses for 14 days, plus fluconazole 1200 mg orally once daily for 14 days.
No liposomal amphotericin: amphotericin B deoxycholate 1 mg/kg/day IV + flucytosine 100 mg/kg/day in 4 divided doses for 7 days, then fluconazole 1200 mg daily for 7 days.
No amphotericin at all: fluconazole 1200 mg daily + flucytosine 100 mg/kg/day in 4 divided doses for 14 days (the only recommended all-oral regimen).
No flucytosine: liposomal amphotericin B 3 to 4 mg/kg/day IV + fluconazole 1200 mg daily for 14 days, or amphotericin B deoxycholate 1 mg/kg/day + fluconazole 1200 mg daily for 14 days. Flucytosine-containing regimens are superior.
Do not give routine adjunctive corticosteroids, and do not use mannitol, acetazolamide or furosemide to lower intracranial pressure: no benefit and possible harm.
# Preventing amphotericin toxicity
Before each amphotericin dose (including the single liposomal dose): 1 L of 0.9% saline with 20 mEq potassium chloride over 2 hours, plus oral potassium chloride 8 mEq tablets twice daily (2 tablets per dose with deoxycholate) and magnesium supplements if available.
Omit potassium supplements if there is pre-existing renal impairment or hyperkalaemia. Amphotericin is incompatible with saline: dilute it in 5% glucose.
Monitor potassium and creatinine regularly (several times a week with deoxycholate) and haemoglobin weekly; CBC at least weekly while on flucytosine (marrow suppression).
Potassium below 3.3 mmol/L: increase to 40 mEq potassium chloride IV and/or 1 to 2 oral 8 mEq tablets three times daily; check potassium daily.
Creatinine doubles on deoxycholate: omit one dose and/or increase prehydration to 1 L saline 8-hourly, restart at 0.7 mg/kg/day or on alternate days, or switch to liposomal amphotericin. Adjust flucytosine and fluconazole for renal impairment.
# Raised intracranial pressure and response
Measure opening pressure at the first LP and repeat an early LP to recheck pressure, whether or not there are symptoms.
Therapeutic LP: drain enough CSF to bring the pressure below 20 cmH2O, or to half the baseline if extremely high. Recheck after each 10 mL; usually 20 to 30 mL is enough.
Persistent headache, blurred vision or drowsiness: repeat therapeutic LP daily (two LPs about 12 hours apart may be needed) until symptoms resolve or pressure is normal for at least 2 days. Consider a shunt only if repeated LPs fail and antifungal therapy has been given.
Assess clinical response daily during induction. Routine repeat LP for culture is not needed if improving, and CrAg titres are not used to monitor response.
# Consolidation, maintenance and ART
Consolidation: fluconazole 800 mg orally daily for 8 weeks after induction.
Maintenance (secondary prophylaxis): fluconazole 200 mg daily until immune reconstitution (CD4 above 200 cells/mm3) with suppressed viral load on ART.
Start ART 4 to 6 weeks after starting antifungal treatment (about 4 weeks after an amphotericin-based induction; 4 to 6 weeks after fluconazole plus flucytosine). Earlier ART increases mortality.
People already on ART: check viral load for treatment failure; defer any regimen switch by the same 4 to 6 weeks.
Relapse: restart induction, manage pressure with therapeutic LP, reinforce adherence and request fluconazole susceptibility testing.
# Special situations
CrAg positive on screening without meningitis: LP if feasible. If meningitis is excluded, give fluconazole 800 to 1200 mg daily for 2 weeks, then consolidation and maintenance as above, and start ART after 2 weeks.
Cryptococcal IRIS (usually 3 to 12 weeks after ART): continue ART, treat raised pressure, optimise antifungals and consider re-induction. For life-threatening deterioration only, prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day for at least 1 week, tapered over 2 to 6 weeks.
Pregnancy: amphotericin B can be used when benefit outweighs harm; fluconazole and flucytosine carry teratogenic risk, so decide case by case with obstetric input.
Cryptococcal meningitis without HIV (transplant, corticosteroids, haematological malignancy) usually needs a longer induction; seek infectious diseases advice.
# Pitfalls
Not measuring opening pressure, or not repeating therapeutic LPs, when raised pressure is the main treatable cause of death.
Starting ART immediately after diagnosis.
Using corticosteroids, mannitol or acetazolamide for raised pressure.
Excluding the diagnosis on a negative India ink.
Giving amphotericin without prehydration and potassium, or diluting it in saline.
Stopping fluconazole maintenance before CD4 recovery and viral suppression.
# Drugs
Liposomal amphotericin B
# Dose
10 mg/kg IV single dose on day 1 (preferred induction); or 3 to 4 mg/kg/day IV for 14 days if flucytosine unavailable
Prehydrate with saline and potassium; dilute in 5% glucose.
Flucytosine
100 mg/kg/day orally in 4 divided doses for 14 days (7 days with amphotericin deoxycholate)
CBC weekly; reduce dose in renal impairment.
Fluconazole
Induction 1200 mg daily; consolidation 800 mg daily for 8 weeks; maintenance 200 mg daily
Adjust for renal impairment; teratogenic risk in pregnancy.
Amphotericin B deoxycholate, 1 mg/kg/day IV for 7 days (with flucytosine) or 14 days (with fluconazole), Nephrotoxicity, hypokalaemia, anaemia; needs the full monitoring package.
Potassium chloride
# Dose
20 mEq in 1 L 0.9% saline over 2 hours before each amphotericin dose, plus 8 mEq tablets orally twice daily (2 tablets per dose with deoxycholate)
