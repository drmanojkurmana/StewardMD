# Lumbar puncture in neonates
Neonates
SNCU, NICU, emergency department
Sample CSF for suspected neonatal meningitis once the baby is stable. Sit the baby or lie it on its side with the spine flexed, enter below L2 to L3 (L3 to L4 or L4 to L5), use a 22 G or 25 G needle, advance in small steps checking for CSF, and fill 4 tubes with about 1 to 2 mL each.
Aliases: LP; neonatal LP; spinal tap; CSF sampling; lumbar tap; meningitis workup newborn; CSF analysis neonate; NeoCLEAR.
# Indications and contraindications
Indications: suspected CNS infection (meningitis, encephalitis, congenital infection) and neonatal fever; also checking response to antimicrobial treatment, metabolic disease, intracranial haemorrhage, intrathecal therapy and CSF drainage in non-obstructive hydrocephalus.
Contraindications: raised intracranial pressure from a mass lesion, coagulopathy, severe thrombocytopenia, and infection at the puncture site.
Consider head imaging first if there is prior CNS disease or seizures, abnormal immunity, an abnormal level of consciousness or neurological examination, or papilloedema.
# Preparation and position
Needle: 22 G, 3.5 cm is standard; a 25 G needle gives comparable success with fewer traumatic taps.
Gather antiseptic, sterile gloves and drapes, the LP needle and CSF tubes. Give analgesia: topical lidocaine cream, local lidocaine infiltration or oral sucrose.
Start cardiorespiratory and oxygen saturation monitoring. Desaturation risk is higher at low gestation and in ventilated babies.
Sitting: flex the lower back with the spine perpendicular to the bed. Lateral: flex hips and knees to the chest, spine parallel to the bed, shoulders and hips vertical. Use a firm surface.
NeoCLEAR (21 UK units, 27 to 44 weeks corrected, 1 kg or more) found the sitting position more successful and better tolerated than lying. A meta-analysis found sitting at least equal to lateral; prone looked better for preterm and low birth weight babies in one study.
# Technique
The cord ends at L3 in neonates: puncture below L2 to L3, at L3 to L4 or L4 to L5. The line joining the iliac crests crosses the midline just above L4. Some protocols use L4 to L5 or L5 to S1.
Clean the skin and set up a sterile field. Insert in the midline, perpendicular to the spine, aiming cephalad toward the umbilicus when lateral and slightly caudal when sitting; bevel up when lateral, to the side when sitting.
Advance needle and stylet together in small steps, withdrawing the stylet to check for CSF; or remove the stylet after the skin and advance until CSF appears. If you hit bone, withdraw partly and redirect cephalad or caudad.
Estimated depth to avoid going too deep: 2 x weight (kg) + 7 mm, or 2.5 x weight (kg) + 6 mm (see the procedure calculator).
Fill 4 tubes with about 1 to 2 mL each, then replace the stylet, withdraw, press on the site and apply a dressing.
# Samples
Send cell count with differential (use the least bloody tube), protein and glucose, and Gram stain and culture. Label every tube.
# Pitfalls and complications
Traumatic or failed taps occur in 30 to 50%; overall success is about 60%, and first attempt success varies from 50 to 80%.
Going too deep causes traumatic taps: advance in small steps.
LP-related bradycardia is self-resolving; IVH risk is higher in babies with bleeding risk factors.
Rare: lumbar epidural abscess and vertebral osteomyelitis after repeated LP in preterm infants, cerebellar herniation, spinal epidural haematoma, conus medullaris injury, and late epidermoid tumour.
