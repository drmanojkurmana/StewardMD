# Rheumatologic emergencies (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-emergencies: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome (CAPS) is an acute medical emergency presenting with multiorgan microvascular thrombosis involving >=3 organs over <1 week; immediate treatment requires emergency triple therapy: intravenous pulse methylprednisolone (1 g daily for 3 days), therapeutic parenteral anticoagulation (heparin), and plasma exchange or IVIG.

Scleroderma renal crisis (SRC) is an acute hypertensive emergency characterized by malignant hypertension, rapid oliguric acute renal failure, and microangiopathic hemolytic anemia; emergency therapy requires prompt initiation and upward titration of short-acting ACE inhibitors (oral captopril) with blood pressure gradually reduced over 48 to 72 hours.

Diffuse alveolar hemorrhage (DAH) in pulmonary-renal syndrome manifests with hemoptysis, acute hypoxemic respiratory failure, bilateral diffuse alveolar infiltrates, and an abrupt drop in hemoglobin with increased diffusing capacity (DLCO); urgent bronchoscopy with sequential bronchoalveolar lavage shows progressively bloodier aliquots and hemosiderin-laden macrophages.

Atlantoaxial subluxation with cervical myelopathy in rheumatoid arthritis presents with occipital pain radiating to the vertex, Lhermitte sign, spastic quadriparesis, and hyperreflexia; an anterior atlanto-dens interval (AADI) >3 mm on flexion lateral cervical radiographs confirms instability, and posterior-atlantodental interval (PADI) <=14 mm indicates imminent spinal cord compression requiring urgent neurosurgical stabilization.

Giant cell arteritis with acute visual loss or transient amaurosis fugax represents an ophthalmic emergency; intravenous pulse methylprednisolone (500 to 1,000 mg daily for 3 consecutive days) must be administered immediately prior to temporal artery biopsy to prevent bilateral irreversible anterior ischemic optic neuropathy.

Macrophage activation syndrome (MAS) is a secondary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (sHLH) complicating systemic JIA or adult-onset Still disease; clinical hallmarks include unremitting fever, falling ESR with extreme hyperferritinemia (>10,000 ng/mL), consumptive hypofibrinogenemia, thrombocytopenia, and hemophagocytosis on bone marrow biopsy, treated urgently with pulse steroids, anakinra, or cyclosporine.

Native joint bacterial septic arthritis is an orthopedic emergency causing enzymatic chondrolysis and irreversible joint destruction within 24 to 48 hours; urgent needle arthrocentesis must be performed for synovial analysis and culture, followed immediately by empiric intravenous bactericidal antibiotics and complete joint decompression.

Acute lupus myocarditis manifests with unexplained resting tachycardia, gallop rhythm, acute congestive heart failure, and cardiogenic shock, with cardiac MRI demonstrating myocardial edema on T2 and diffuse late gadolinium enhancement; urgent management requires intravenous pulse methylprednisolone combined with cyclophosphamide or IVIG.
