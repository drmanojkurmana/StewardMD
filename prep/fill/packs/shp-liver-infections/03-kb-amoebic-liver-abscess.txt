# Amoebic Liver Abscess
Amoebic Liver Abscess, Hepatobiliary / Tropical Fever.
# Pathogens
Entamoeba histolytica is the specific cause of amoebic liver abscess, reaching the liver via the portal circulation after invading the colonic mucosa; the classic presentation is a single right-lobe abscess, which helps but does not definitively distinguish it from pyogenic abscess without aspirate/serology.
Very likely: Entamoeba histolytica.
# Possible
Concurrent or preceding intestinal amoebiasis (only a minority have concurrent diarrhea at presentation)
# Investigations
Abdominal ultrasound or CT
Characterizes the abscess (typically single, right lobe, in amoebic disease) and guides the decision on aspiration.
Amoebic serology (IgG)
Highly sensitive for invasive amoebiasis and useful when aspiration is not performed or is inconclusive.
Stool microscopy for E. histolytica (trophozoites/cysts)
