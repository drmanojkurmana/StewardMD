# Desmoplakin Cardiomyopathy (DSP-related arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy)
Desmoplakin Cardiomyopathy (DSP-related arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy), Cardiovascular, Cardiology (inherited cardiac conditions).
Aliases: DSP cardiomyopathy; Arrhythmogenic left dominant cardiomyopathy; Desmoplakin arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.
# Pathophysiology
Desmoplakin is the most abundant protein of the desmosomal plaque and provides the structural bridge between the desmosomal cadherins (via plakoglobin and plakophilin) and the desmin intermediate filament network of the cardiomyocyte. Loss-of-function or dominant-negative variants weaken cell-cell adhesion at the intercalated disc, so mechanical stress — amplified by vigorous exercise — provokes cardiomyocyte detachment, death and an inflammatory, fibro-fatty repair process. Unlike the fatty replacement classically seen with plakophilin-2 disease, desmoplakin variants produce prominent fibrosis with a predilection for the subepicardial and mid-myocardial left ventricle, generating the ring-like scar pattern seen on imaging. Desmosomal disruption also destabilises gap junctions and sodium channel complexes at the intercalated disc, slowing conduction and creating the substrate for re-entrant ventricular arrhythmia. Recurrent 'hot phases' of acute myocyte necrosis with troponin release and inflammatory infiltrate can clinically and histologically resemble viral myocarditis, and repeated injury drives progressive systolic dysfunction. In biallelic disease the same adhesion defect affects epidermal desmosomes, producing woolly hair and palmoplantar keratoderma.
# Prognosis
Prognosis is variable and driven more by arrhythmic burden and fibrosis than by systolic function, with meaningful risk of ventricular arrhythmia and sudden death even when ejection fraction is preserved. Progressive biventricular failure develops in a subset, and outcomes improve with early risk stratification, exercise modification and appropriate defibrillator therapy.
# Clinical pearls
Caused by pathogenic variants in DSP encoding desmoplakin, an obligate desmosomal plaque protein linking the cadherin complex to the intermediate filament cytoskeleton; usually autosomal dominant, with recessive cardiocutaneous syndromes (Carvajal) also described.
Distinct from classic plakophilin-2 arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy: DSP disease is typically left-dominant or biventricular, with early left ventricular involvement often preceding overt right ventricular dilatation.
Presents with a characteristic 'hot phase' pattern of episodic myocardial injury — chest pain with raised troponin and normal coronary arteries mimicking myocarditis, often recurrent and sometimes triggered by exertion or viral illness.
Cardiac MRI frequently shows subepicardial or mid-wall late gadolinium enhancement in a ring-like circumferential left ventricular distribution, which can appear before any fall in ejection fraction.
Cutaneous and adnexal clues include woolly or wiry hair and palmoplantar keratoderma, most prominent in biallelic Carvajal syndrome but sometimes subtle in dominant disease.
Ventricular arrhythmia and sudden death risk can be disproportionate to the degree of systolic impairment, so risk stratification should not rely on ejection fraction alone.
Exercise restriction from competitive and high-intensity endurance sport is advised because vigorous activity accelerates disease penetrance and arrhythmic events in desmosomal cardiomyopathy.
Cascade genetic testing and serial imaging of first-degree relatives is essential, as gene carriers may be phenotype-negative yet still at arrhythmic risk.
# Additional differentials
Acute viral or lymphocytic myocarditis
Plakophilin-2 arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy
Cardiac sarcoidosis
Idiopathic or other genetic dilated cardiomyopathy (e.g. lamin A/C, filamin C)
Chagas cardiomyopathy in endemic regions
Giant cell myocarditis
# Additional investigations
Cardiac MRI with late gadolinium enhancement to detect subepicardial/mid-wall ring-like left ventricular fibrosis
Serial high-sensitivity troponin during chest-pain episodes to document 'hot phase' myocardial injury
Signal-averaged ECG and ambulatory rhythm monitoring for ventricular ectopy and non-sustained ventricular tachycardia
Targeted cardiomyopathy gene panel including DSP with segregation analysis in relatives
Dermatological examination and, where relevant, skin/hair assessment for keratoderma and woolly hair
