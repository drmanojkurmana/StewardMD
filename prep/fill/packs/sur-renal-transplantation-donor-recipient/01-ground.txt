Laparoscopic donor nephrectomy takes the left kidney for its longer left renal vein, since the short right vein drains directly into the IVC, sparing adrenal with gonadal branches and prizing donor safety. Heterotopic placement seats the allograft extraperitoneally in the iliac fossa with external iliac anastomosis of artery to external or internal iliac vessels, leaving natives behind. Extravesical Lich-Gregoir reimplantation with double-J stent gives lowest leak and obstruction rates, removed cystoscopically in weeks with drainage watched.

Transplant ureteral stenosis in 2 to 5 percent follows distal ureteral ischemia from lower pole devascularization, suspected with rising creatinine and hydronephrosis for stenting then reconstruction. Urine leak in 1 to 4 percent shows creatinine rise with pain and wound drainage, proven when drain fluid creatinine tops serum, stented or reimplanted with wide drainage. Lymphocele in 1 to 15 percent from unligated lymphatics compresses vein or ureter for laparoscopic peritoneal fenestration marsupialization, prevented by routine ligation.

Artery stenosis with refractory hypertension and dysfunction takes percutaneous angioplasty and stenting after duplex confirmation, with restenosis watched. Renal vein thrombosis under 1 to 2 percent within 48 to 72 hours brings oliguria with graft swelling to rapid loss, explored emergently with thrombectomy.
