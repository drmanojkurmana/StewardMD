Khorana score, notes

What it estimates. A points total for the chance of symptomatic clotting in an outpatient with cancer who is about to begin chemotherapy. Five inputs can each add points.

Tumour site. Stomach and pancreas give 2 points. Lung, lymphoma, gynaecologic, and genitourinary tumours give 1 point each, with prostate left out of the genitourinary group. Other sites give nothing.

Measurements taken before chemotherapy. A platelet count of 350,000 per microlitre or higher gives 1 point. Haemoglobin under 10 g/dL, or treatment with red cell growth factors, including erythropoiesis-stimulating agents, gives 1 point. A leukocyte count strictly above 11,000 per microlitre gives 1 point, so exactly 11,000 does not count. A body mass index of 35 kg/m2 or more gives 1 point.

Risk groups by total. A total of 0 is low. A total of 1 or 2 is intermediate. A total of 3 or more is high.

Symptomatic VTE by group. Low group roughly 0.3 to 0.8 percent. Intermediate group roughly 1.8 to 2.0 percent. High group roughly 6.7 to 7.1 percent. Symptomatic means the clot produces signs or symptoms.

Prophylaxis evidence. In the high-risk group, rivaroxaban or apixaban trials showed about a 50 percent cut in clotting, with bleeding near 5 percent. Giving prophylaxis routinely during chemotherapy is still debated. Low molecular weight heparin, or LMWH, is an injected fragment of heparin. Guidance from ASCO does not endorse prophylaxis as a routine step.

Treatment duration. For cancer-linked clots, LMWH or a DOAC is continued for at least 6 months. One regimen: apixaban 10 mg twice daily for 7 days, then 5 mg twice daily. Apixaban proved no worse than dalteparin. Continue until the patient is declared cancer-free. LMWH alone is preferred when the cancer is gastrointestinal. Edoxaban is an accepted DOAC option.

Platelets and anticoagulation. Standard references name a count under 50,000 per microlitre as a reason to hold back prophylaxis in medically ill patients with cancer. Bleeding from a low count alone is uncommon above 50,000 per microlitre. Bleeding tends to appear only when counts drop under 10,000 to 20,000 per microlitre.

Pulmonary embolism. Massive PE: low blood pressure and clot involving at least half the pulmonary vessels, about 5 to 10 percent of cases. Submassive PE: normal blood pressure with right ventricular dysfunction, about 20 to 25 percent of cases. Fibrinolysis with alteplase for massive PE is a 100 mg infusion over 2 hours. For submassive PE, fibrinolysis is debated and considered when the patient worsens.

Catheters. Long-term central venous catheters may be followed by major vessel thrombosis. Usual response: take the catheter out and start anticoagulation. Superior vena cava syndrome caught early can be cleared with fibrinolysis while the catheter stays in. An indwelling pleural drain may need intermittent thrombolysis to keep its patency.

Guideline additions.

ASCO 2019 edition, Khorana recommendation. Outpatients scoring 2 or more on the Khorana score who are starting systemic therapy may be given prophylaxis with apixaban, rivaroxaban, or low molecular weight heparin. Arithmetic: stomach or pancreatic primary gives 2 points from site alone, so these patients reach a score of 2 without any blood count or body mass input.

ISTH 2018 guidance, Samuelson Bannow and colleagues, on cancer thrombosis with low platelets. Platelet level of 50,000 per microlitre marks the switch point: full-dose treatment above it, while under it clinicians lower the dose or give platelet transfusions aiming for 50,000 or higher.

Infusion Nurses Society standards, 2021 edition. For a blocked catheter lumen, alteplase is instilled into the lumen to restore function.
