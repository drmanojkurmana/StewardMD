# Branchial cyst
Branchial cyst, Paediatric surgery / Thoracic, Paediatric Surgery.
Aliases: branchial cleft cyst; branchial cleft anomaly; second branchial cleft cyst.
# Pathophysiology
The branchial apparatus of clefts, arches, and pouches contributes to head and neck development and normally regresses. Failure of obliteration of a cleft or its associated structures leaves an epithelial-lined remnant that can form a cyst, a blind-ending sinus, or a complete fistula connecting skin to pharynx. Second cleft anomalies characteristically pass between the internal and external carotid arteries toward the tonsillar fossa. Retained secretions distend the cyst, and communication with the pharynx or skin predisposes to recurrent infection.
# Prognosis
Benign branchial anomalies have an excellent prognosis after complete excision, with recurrence mainly seen when a tract is incompletely removed or when surgery is undertaken during active infection. Long-term outlook is worse only when a presumed cyst proves to be a cystic malignancy.
# Clinical pearls
A lateral neck swelling arising from incomplete regression of the embryological branchial (pharyngeal) apparatus, most commonly from the second cleft
The typical location is along the anterior border of the sternocleidomastoid muscle in the upper to mid neck
It often becomes clinically apparent in older children or young adults, sometimes enlarging after an upper respiratory infection
Anomalies may take the form of a cyst, sinus, or fistula; a fistulous opening at the lower neck that discharges may indicate a tract to the pharynx
Infected cysts present as tender, inflamed, sometimes fluctuant masses and should have infection controlled before elective excision
Ultrasound is a useful first-line study, with cross-sectional imaging helping to define the tract before surgery
Definitive management is complete surgical excision of the cyst and any associated tract to prevent recurrence
In adults, a persistently cystic lateral neck mass should raise suspicion of a cystic nodal metastasis rather than a benign branchial cyst
# Additional differentials
Cervical lymphadenopathy or reactive node
Cystic hygroma (lymphatic malformation)
Cystic metastatic lymph node (in older patients)
Dermoid or epidermoid cyst
Tuberculous or atypical mycobacterial lymphadenitis
