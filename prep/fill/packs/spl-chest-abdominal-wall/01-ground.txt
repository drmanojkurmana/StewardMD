## Sternal Dehiscence, Chest Wall Defects and Component Separation
Citations and Chapter Sources: Plastic Surgery 5E Vol 4 Ch 8 (Chest wall reconstruction) & Ch 11 (Abdominal wall reconstruction)

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Median sternotomy wound infections and dehiscence require debridement, antibiotic therapy, and coverage with bilateral pectoralis major turnover or advancement flaps.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: sternotomy dehiscence, pectoralis major

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Inferior third sternal defects are reconstructed using a rectus abdominis muscle flap or a greater omentum free or pedicled flap.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: inferior sternal, rectus abdominis, greater omentum

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Full-thickness chest wall resections involving four or more consecutive ribs require skeletal stabilization with titanium mesh or methylmethacrylate sandwich to prevent paradoxical flail chest.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: four
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: chest wall resection, paradoxical flail chest, titanium mesh

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Ramirez anterior components separation technique releases the external oblique aponeurosis lateral to the semilunar line, advancing rectus muscle medially by up to 10 cm on each side.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 10
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Ramirez, components separation, external oblique

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Posterior components separation with transversus abdominis release TAR incises the posterior rectus sheath and transversus abdominis muscle, providing extensive preperitoneal space.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: transversus abdominis release, TAR, posterior rectus sheath

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Synthetic polypropylene mesh provides high tensile strength in clean abdominal repairs but is contraindicated in contaminated fields due to high infection and fistula risks.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: polypropylene mesh, fistula

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Biologic meshes derived from human or porcine acellular dermal matrix are utilized in contaminated or infected hernia fields to promote neovascularisation and resist infection.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: biologic meshes, acellular dermal matrix

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Loss of abdominal domain exceeding 20% to 30% of total peritoneal cavity requires preoperative progressive pneumoperitoneum or botulinum toxin A to prevent abdominal compartment syndrome.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 20%, 30%
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: loss of domain, pneumoperitoneum, botulinum toxin


Comprehensive Textbook Context:
Surgical planning in Chest and Abdominal Wall Reconstruction requires meticulous anatomical grounding, precise knowledge of blood supply, innervation, fascial planes, and tissue biomechanics.
Operative interventions balance functional restoration of speech, swallowing, mastication, sensory discrimination, and locomotion with aesthetic symmetry and minimal donor site morbidity.
Preoperative evaluation integrates acoustic Doppler, computed tomography angiography, indocyanine green fluorescence imaging, and patient-specific risk factor optimization including smoking cessation, strict glycemic control, and blood pressure management.
Postoperative care prioritizes early mobilization, monitoring of capillary refill, tissue turgor, pinprick bleeding, active rehabilitation, and vigilant surveillance for hematoma, seroma, infection, or microvascular thrombosis.
Examination standards for NEET-SS MCh Plastic Surgery emphasize surgical classifications, anatomical landmarks, technical incision designs, load-bearing versus load-sharing fixation principles, and clinical indications for operative versus non-operative management.
