Monopolar transurethral resection, monopolar TURP, irrigates with hypoosmolar non-conductive fluid, 1.5% glycine or sorbitol and mannitol blends, because the single circuit current requires an ion free medium. Resection proceeds lobe by lobe, but every minute adds absorbed irrigant to the circulation, setting the stage for dilutional collapse.

TUR syndrome follows excessive hypoosmolar glycine uptake through divided prostatic veins: acute hypervolemic hyponatremia with encephalopathy, visual disturbances, and bradycardia, sometimes with pulmonary oedema. Severe symptomatic cases receive controlled correction with 3% hypertonic saline alongside fluid restriction and diuresis, while mild confusion with modest sodium falls is managed conservatively.

Bipolar TURP confines current between loop electrodes, generating a plasma corona that cuts in isotonic saline, 0.9% NaCl. Since absorbed saline matches plasma tonicity, dilutional hyponatremia cannot develop, and TUR syndrome risk is eliminated entirely, permitting longer resections in larger glands and safer surgery in cardiac patients.

Holmium laser enucleation, HoLEP, exploits the avascular plane of the surgical capsule, dissecting the complete adenoma free before mechanical tissue morcellation evacuates fragments through the sheath. Enucleation succeeds independent of gland volume, yields generous histology, and bleeds little, making it the reference operation where expertise exists.

Thulium enucleation, ThuLEP, uses continuous-wave energy with a coagulation depth of merely 0.1 to 0.2 mm, cutting crisply while sealing microvessels. The negligible thermal halo preserves surrounding structures and permits uninterrupted oral anticoagulation, a decisive advantage in comorbid elderly men.

Prostatic urethral lift, UroLift, places permanent transprostatic suture implants that retract the lateral lobes anterolaterally. With no thermal injury and no tissue removal, ejaculatory preservation is absolute, trading lesser flow improvement for an office compatible profile suited to sexually active men with moderate obstruction.

Water vapor thermal therapy, Rezum, delivers radiofrequency-created convective steam at 103 deg C through transurethral needles into the transition zone and any median lobe. Condensation releases latent heat, producing coagulative necrosis that resorbs over weeks, with the procedure completed under local anaesthesia.

Simple prostatectomy endures for glands of 80 cc to 100 cc and beyond where lasers are unavailable: Freyer transvesical enucleation through the open bladder, or Millin retropubic enucleation behind the pubis, each replicated laparoscopically and robotically, removing the adenoma whole at the price of open surgical recovery.
