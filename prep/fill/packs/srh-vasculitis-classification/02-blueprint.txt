# Classification and assessment of vasculitis (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-vasculitis-classification: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

The 2012 Revised Chapel Hill Consensus Conference (CHCC) establishes nomenclature for systemic vasculitides based on predominant vessel caliber: large vessel (Takayasu, GCA), medium vessel (PAN, Kawasaki), and small vessel (ANCA-associated and immune complex).

Small-vessel vasculitis is divided into ANCA-associated vasculitides (pauci-immune: microscopic polyangiitis, granulomatosis with polyangiitis, eosinophilic GPA) and immune complex vasculitides (anti-GBM disease, cryoglobulinemic vasculitis, IgA vasculitis, hypocomplementemic urticarial vasculitis).

Variable-vessel vasculitis is defined as systemic vasculitis capable of affecting vessels of any size (arteries, veins, and capillaries), classic examples being Behcet disease and Cogan syndrome.

Single-organ vasculitis (SOV) affects arteries or veins in a single organ without systemic involvement, including primary angiitis of the CNS, cutaneous leukocytoclastic angiitis, and isolated testicular vasculitis.

Vasculitis associated with systemic disease includes lupus vasculitis, rheumatoid vasculitis, and sarcoid vasculitis; secondary vasculitis associated with probable etiology includes hepatitis B virus-associated PAN and hepatitis C virus-associated cryoglobulinemia.

The 2022 ACR/EULAR classification criteria provide separate, validated, scoring-based criteria sets for GPA, MPA, EGPA, Takayasu arteritis, and GCA, reflecting modern imaging, ANCA serology, and clinical features.

The Birmingham Vasculitis Activity Score (BVAS v3) evaluates 64 items across nine organ systems to distinguish active ongoing vasculitis from permanent chronic irreversible damage captured by the Vasculitis Damage Index (VDI).

The Five-Factor Score (FFS, revised 2009) predicts 5-year mortality in necrotizing vasculitides based on age >65, cardiac involvement, gastrointestinal involvement, renal insufficiency (creatinine >=150 micromol/L), and absence of ENT signs.
