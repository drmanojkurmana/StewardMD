# Helicobacter pylori infection (adult)
Adults
Outpatient, ward
Test with a urea breath test, stool antigen or biopsy (off PPI for 2 weeks), treat every confirmed infection with optimised bismuth quadruple therapy for 14 days (or rifabutin triple or vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual), avoid empiric clarithromycin, and confirm eradication at least 4 weeks after treatment.
Aliases: H. pylori; H pylori; HP eradication; bismuth quadruple therapy; BQT; vonoprazan dual therapy; rifabutin triple; urea breath test; stool antigen; test of cure; Maastricht VI.
# Who to test
Current or past peptic ulcer disease, gastric MALT lymphoma, and after endoscopic resection of early gastric cancer.
Dyspepsia without alarm features in patients under 60 (test and treat). Alarm features (GI bleeding, persistent vomiting, unexplained weight loss, iron deficiency, dysphagia) need endoscopy with gastric biopsies instead.
Unexplained iron deficiency anaemia, immune thrombocytopenia, and before long-term NSAIDs or daily aspirin.
Gastric premalignant conditions (atrophy, intestinal metaplasia, autoimmune gastritis), first-degree relatives of gastric cancer patients, migrants from high-incidence regions, and adult household members of an infected person.
WHO classes H. pylori as a group 1 carcinogen. Every patient who tests positive should be offered treatment (ACG 2024, Maastricht VI).
# Testing
Non-invasive: urea breath test or stool antigen test. At endoscopy: histology or rapid urease test from antrum and body biopsies.
Do not use serology to diagnose active infection or to confirm cure: antibodies persist after eradication.
Stop PPIs (and potassium-competitive acid blockers) for at least 2 weeks, and antibiotics and bismuth for at least 4 weeks, before any test, to avoid false negatives. H2 receptor antagonists and antacids may be used meanwhile.
After failed treatment, consider antibiotic susceptibility testing (culture or molecular clarithromycin and levofloxacin resistance testing) where available.
# First-line (treatment-naive)
Preferred: optimised bismuth quadruple therapy (BQT) for 14 days: standard-dose PPI twice daily + bismuth four times daily + tetracycline 500 mg four times daily + metronidazole 500 mg three or four times daily.
Alternative: rifabutin triple therapy for 14 days (omeprazole 40 mg + amoxicillin 1 g + rifabutin 50 mg, all three times daily).
Alternative: vonoprazan 20 mg twice daily + amoxicillin 1 g three times daily for 14 days.
Do not use PPI-clarithromycin triple therapy empirically; use a clarithromycin regimen (preferably vonoprazan-based) only when clarithromycin susceptibility is proven. Where clarithromycin resistance exceeds 15%, Maastricht VI also recommends BQT first line.
Doxycycline is not an acceptable substitute for tetracycline.
True penicillin allergy: use BQT, and consider formal allergy testing if further amoxicillin-based options are needed.
# Persistent infection (salvage)
Ask about every previous regimen and antibiotic exposure; do not repeat clarithromycin or levofloxacin unless susceptibility is proven.
No previous optimised BQT: give optimised BQT for 14 days.
Previous optimised BQT failed: rifabutin triple therapy for 14 days is suggested. Vonoprazan or high-dose PPI with amoxicillin dual therapy is an alternative.
Levofloxacin triple therapy (PPI twice daily + amoxicillin 1 g twice daily + levofloxacin 500 mg once daily) only when levofloxacin susceptibility is shown or no other option exists.
After two failures, refer to gastroenterology for endoscopy with culture or molecular susceptibility testing.
# Test of cure
Confirm eradication in every treated patient with a urea breath test, stool antigen or biopsy test at least 4 weeks after finishing antibiotics, and at least 2 weeks off PPI.
Recurrence after confirmed eradication is uncommon (about 1% per year); new symptoms after cure deserve fresh assessment rather than automatic re-treatment.
Counsel on adherence before starting: missed doses are a common cause of failure of these 14-day, multi-dose regimens.
# Special situations
Pregnancy: defer eradication until after delivery where possible; tetracycline is contraindicated in pregnancy.
Rapid CYP2C19 metabolisers: prefer esomeprazole or rabeprazole (or vonoprazan) over omeprazole, lansoprazole or pantoprazole.
India: clarithromycin and metronidazole resistance are reported to be high in many Indian studies, so empiric clarithromycin triple therapy is not advised; bismuth and tetracycline supply varies locally.
# Pitfalls
Testing while the patient is still on a PPI or soon after antibiotics or bismuth (false negative).
Using serology for diagnosis or as a test of cure.
Re-using clarithromycin after a previous failed clarithromycin regimen.
Skipping the test of cure: treatment failure is common and untreated infection still carries ulcer and cancer risk.
Not warning about black stools (bismuth) and the disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol (metronidazole).
# Drugs
Standard-dose PPI (for BQT or levofloxacin triple)
# Dose
Omeprazole 20 mg, lansoprazole 30 mg, pantoprazole 40 mg, rabeprazole 20 mg or esomeprazole 20 mg orally twice daily for 14 days
Take 30 minutes before meals.
Bismuth
# Dose
Bismuth subcitrate 120 to 300 mg or bismuth subsalicylate 300 to 524 mg orally four times daily for 14 days
Part of optimised BQT. Causes black stools.
Tetracycline, 500 mg orally four times daily for 14 days, Contraindicated in pregnancy. Do not substitute doxycycline.
Metronidazole, 500 mg orally three or four times daily for 14 days, No alcohol during treatment and for 48 hours after.
Rifabutin triple (fixed combination)
# Dose
Omeprazole 40 mg + amoxicillin 1 g + rifabutin 50 mg orally three times daily for 14 days
If rifabutin 50 mg is unavailable, rifabutin 150 mg twice daily has been substituted. Orange discolouration of body fluids; check interactions.
Vonoprazan-amoxicillin dual
# Dose
Vonoprazan 20 mg orally twice daily + amoxicillin 1 g orally three times daily for 14 days
First-line alternative or salvage; not for penicillin allergy.
Levofloxacin triple
# Dose
PPI twice daily + amoxicillin 1 g twice daily + levofloxacin 500 mg once daily, orally
Only with proven levofloxacin susceptibility or no alternative.
