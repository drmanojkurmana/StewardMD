# HIV: starting and monitoring antiretroviral therapy (adult)
Adults
ART clinic, outpatient, ward
Offer ART to everyone with confirmed HIV within 7 days of diagnosis (same day if ready) using TLD (tenofovir DF, lamivudine, dolutegravir) once daily, screen for TB and cryptococcal antigen when CD4 is low, give co-trimoxazole prophylaxis, and monitor viral load, switching only after confirmed failure.
Aliases: ART; HAART; antiretroviral therapy; PLHIV; TLD; HIV treatment; advanced HIV disease; AHD; viral load; treatment failure; IRIS; co-trimoxazole prophylaxis; CPT; rapid ART.
# Rapid ART start
Offer ART to every adult with confirmed HIV, whatever the CD4 count. WHO: start within 7 days of diagnosis and offer same-day start to people who are ready; give priority to people with advanced HIV disease.
Confirm the diagnosis by the national testing algorithm before the first dose. Send CD4, viral load and safety bloods, but results need not be back before starting unless the history suggests TB or cryptococcal meningitis.
Before a rapid start, ask about cough, fever, weight loss, night sweats, headache and confusion. Investigate these first, because ART timing differs with TB meningitis and cryptococcal meningitis (see below).
Counsel on lifelong daily adherence, disclosure, condoms and partner testing. A sustained undetectable viral load means no sexual transmission.
# Baseline tests
CD4 count (defines advanced HIV disease and prophylaxis needs), HIV viral load, CBC, creatinine with eGFR, ALT, glucose, lipids and urinalysis.
HBsAg and anti-HCV. If HBsAg is positive or not yet known, the regimen must contain tenofovir plus lamivudine or emtricitabine.
Pregnancy test in women of childbearing potential, syphilis serology and STI screen, and cervical cancer screening.
TB screen at every visit (symptoms, chest X-ray, CRP or molecular test). Urine LF-LAM if CD4 200 or below (inpatient), 100 or below (outpatient), or any CD4 if seriously ill.
Serum or plasma cryptococcal antigen (CrAg) if CD4 is below 100 cells/mm3 (consider below 200). A positive CrAg needs a lumbar puncture to exclude meningitis.
DHHS: genotypic resistance testing (reverse transcriptase and protease) where available, plus integrase genotype after long-acting cabotegravir PrEP; do not delay ART for the result. HLA-B*5701 before any abacavir.
# First-line regimen
Preferred (WHO): TLD, tenofovir disoproxil fumarate 300 mg + lamivudine 300 mg + dolutegravir 50 mg as one tablet orally once daily. This includes women who are pregnant or may become pregnant.
DHHS regimens for most people: bictegravir/TAF/emtricitabine, or dolutegravir with TAF or TDF plus emtricitabine or lamivudine. Dolutegravir/lamivudine dual therapy only if viral load is 500,000 copies/mL or less, there is no hepatitis B, and genotype and HBV results are known.
If dolutegravir is unsuitable: TDF + lamivudine + efavirenz 400 mg once daily (WHO alternative; avoid where pretreatment NNRTI resistance is 10% or more or after previous ARV exposure), or a boosted protease inhibitor, with darunavir/ritonavir preferred (WHO 2025).
After long-acting cabotegravir PrEP, start boosted darunavir with tenofovir and lamivudine or emtricitabine until the integrase genotype is known (DHHS).
Do not start TDF if eGFR is below 50 mL/min, with uncontrolled hypertension or untreated diabetes (WHO); use abacavir (HLA-B*5701 negative) or TAF with lamivudine and dolutegravir.
Dolutegravir with rifampicin: increase to 50 mg twice daily (with TLD, add a separate dolutegravir 50 mg tablet 12 hours later). No change is needed with rifapentine (3HP).
Give dolutegravir 2 hours before or 6 hours after antacids and iron, calcium, magnesium or zinc supplements. Avoid high-dose metformin, and avoid carbamazepine, phenytoin and phenobarbital (use valproate or another alternative).
# Advanced HIV disease (CD4 below 200 or WHO stage 3 or 4)
Give the WHO package: rapid ART, TB screening with LF-LAM and molecular testing, CrAg screening, co-trimoxazole prophylaxis, TB preventive treatment once active TB is excluded, and pre-emptive fluconazole for CrAg-positive people.
Co-trimoxazole 960 mg (160/800 mg) orally once daily. WHO: CD4 350 or below, WHO stage 3 or 4, or active TB, and everyone where malaria or severe bacterial infections are common. US threshold: CD4 below 200 or oral candidiasis.
Stop co-trimoxazole (low malaria and bacterial infection settings) when clinically stable on ART with immune recovery and viral suppression (WHO); US criterion: CD4 above 200 for more than 3 months on ART.
CrAg positive, meningitis excluded: fluconazole 800 to 1200 mg daily for 2 weeks, then 800 mg daily for 8 weeks, then 200 mg daily maintenance. Start ART after 2 weeks of fluconazole.
TB preventive treatment once active TB is excluded: 3HP (weekly isoniazid 900 mg + rifapentine 900 mg for 12 weeks) is preferred (WHO 2025); 6 or 9 months of daily isoniazid is the alternative. Add pyridoxine.
# TB, meningitis and IRIS
Active TB (not meningitis): start TB treatment first, then ART as soon as possible within 2 weeks, whatever the CD4 count (WHO).
TB meningitis: delay ART for at least 4 weeks and start within 8 weeks of starting TB treatment (WHO). Give adjunctive corticosteroids for TB meningitis.
Cryptococcal meningitis: defer ART for 4 to 6 weeks from the start of antifungal therapy (see Cryptococcal meningitis protocol). Other OIs such as PCP: start ART within 2 weeks.
