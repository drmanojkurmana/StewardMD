# Pulmonary tuberculosis, drug-susceptible (WHO)
Adults
Outpatient, TB clinic, ward
Confirm TB with a rapid molecular test that also detects rifampicin resistance, then treat drug-susceptible pulmonary TB with daily 2HRZE/4HR for 6 months, or the 4-month 2HPMZ/2HPM regimen in eligible patients aged 12 or more weighing over 40 kg, with ART within 2 weeks for people with HIV and monthly review.
Aliases: TB; PTB; HRZE; RIPE; 2HRZE/4HR; HPMZ; 2HPMZ/2HPM; 4-month TB regimen; rifapentine moxifloxacin; Study 31; ATT; anti-tubercular treatment.
# Suspect, confirm and assess
Suspect TB with persistent cough, fever, night sweats, weight loss, haemoptysis or an abnormal chest X-ray. Screen high-risk groups actively: people with HIV, household contacts, diabetes, silicosis, undernutrition and immunosuppressive therapy.
Use a WHO-recommended rapid molecular test (for example Xpert MTB/RIF Ultra or Truenat) on sputum as the initial test, with simultaneous rifampicin resistance detection; add testing for isoniazid and fluoroquinolone resistance, and culture, where available.
Baseline: HIV test, glucose or HbA1c, full blood count, liver function tests (hepatitis B and C serology if at risk), creatinine, weight and BMI, and visual acuity with colour vision before ethambutol (ATS/CDC/IDSA).
Rifampicin resistance detected: do not start HRZE; refer for an MDR/RR-TB regimen. Isoniazid-resistant, rifampicin-susceptible TB: 6 months of rifampicin, ethambutol, pyrazinamide and levofloxacin (WHO).
Previously treated patients need rapid drug susceptibility testing before a regimen is chosen; WHO has abandoned the old 8-month streptomycin-containing retreatment regimen.
# 6-month regimen (all patients)
2HRZE/4HR: isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide and ethambutol daily for 2 months, then isoniazid and rifampicin daily for 4 months. Suitable for any age, HIV status and pregnancy, and for extrapulmonary TB except CNS, bone and joint TB (which need longer treatment).
Give treatment daily (7 days a week) in both phases; WHO recommends against thrice-weekly dosing. Prefer fixed-dose combination (FDC) tablets.
WHO doses: isoniazid 5 mg/kg (4 to 6), rifampicin 10 mg/kg (8 to 12), pyrazinamide 25 mg/kg (20 to 30, maximum 2 g), ethambutol 15 mg/kg (15 to 25). HRZE 75/150/400/275 mg FDC (WHO 2025 bands): 25 to 29 kg 2 tablets; 30 to 34 kg 3; 35 to 64 kg 4; 65 kg and above 5.
Weigh monthly and adjust the dose when weight changes. Give pyridoxine with isoniazid to those at risk of neuropathy (HIV, diabetes, alcohol use, undernutrition, CKD, pregnancy, breastfeeding).
WHO does not extend treatment for a positive smear at 2 months. ATS/CDC/IDSA extend the continuation phase to 7 months (9 months in total) when there is cavitation on the initial chest X-ray and the 2-month culture is positive.
Support adherence with patient-centred care: a treatment supporter, video-observed or digital adherence tools, and a record of every dose; restart or extend according to the length and timing of any interruption.
# 4-month regimen (2HPMZ/2HPM)
WHO 2025 option for people aged 12 years or more weighing over 40 kg with drug-susceptible pulmonary TB, whatever the severity, including people with HIV with CD4 100 cells/mm3 or more and people with diabetes.
8 weeks of daily isoniazid, rifapentine, moxifloxacin and pyrazinamide, then 9 weeks of daily isoniazid, rifapentine and moxifloxacin (119 doses), 7 days a week, taken with food.
Doses: rifapentine 1200 mg, moxifloxacin 400 mg and isoniazid 300 mg once daily; pyrazinamide 1000 mg (40 to 54 kg), 1500 mg (55 to 75 kg) or 2000 mg (above 75 kg) daily for the first 8 weeks (CDC). Do not substitute another fluoroquinolone for moxifloxacin.
Not for: weight below 40 kg, age under 12, pregnancy, breastfeeding or postpartum, CD4 below 100 cells/mm3, severe extrapulmonary TB (meningitis, miliary, bone and joint, abdominal), known resistance to any component, or prolonged QT or other QT-prolonging drugs.
Rifapentine has rifamycin interactions: CDC allows it with efavirenz-based ART only when there are no other interacting drugs. Test for fluoroquinolone resistance at baseline where fluoroquinolones are widely used.
# Monitoring and adverse effects
Clinical review weekly in the first month, then monthly: symptoms, weight, adherence and adverse effects. Chest X-ray at baseline, end of month 2 and end of treatment (WHO).
Sputum smear (and culture where available) at the end of month 2 and at the end of treatment. A positive 2-month result without clinical improvement needs culture and rapid drug susceptibility testing to look for resistance.
Stop isoniazid, rifampicin and pyrazinamide if ALT or AST is 5 or more times the upper limit of normal, or 3 or more times with symptoms or jaundice (bilirubin above 3 mg/dL).
Reintroduce one drug at a time once transaminases are below 2 times normal: rifampicin with ethambutol first, then isoniazid 3 to 7 days later. If pyrazinamide is permanently stopped, extend treatment to 9 months.
Ethambutol: ask about blurred vision or red-green colour change at every visit and stop it if they occur. Established isoniazid neuropathy: raise pyridoxine to 100 mg daily.
Moxifloxacin: baseline ECG only if clinically indicated (WHO); watch for tendinopathy, dysglycaemia and C. difficile diarrhoea.
# HIV and special situations
HIV: start ART as soon as possible within 2 weeks of starting TB treatment, regardless of CD4 count, except TB meningitis (delay ART 4 to 8 weeks). With rifampicin give dolutegravir 50 mg twice daily; avoid nevirapine and protease inhibitors. Give co-trimoxazole prophylaxis.
Pregnancy and breastfeeding: use 2HRZE/4HR with pyridoxine; the 4-month HPMZ regimen is not recommended. Continue breastfeeding; the infant need not be separated once the mother has had 2 weeks of treatment.
Creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min or dialysis: pyrazinamide 25 to 35 mg/kg and ethambutol 15 to 25 mg/kg three times a week after dialysis; isoniazid, rifampicin, rifapentine and moxifloxacin need no renal adjustment.
Chronic liver disease (Child-Pugh score): 7 or less, 2 months of HRE then 7 months of HR (no pyrazinamide); 8 to 10, only one hepatotoxic drug, preferably rifampicin; 11 or more, no hepatotoxic drugs. Manage with a specialist.
TB meningitis: adjuvant dexamethasone or prednisolone tapered over 6 to 8 weeks; TB pericarditis: adjuvant corticosteroids may be used (WHO).
Rifampicin lowers levels of hormonal contraceptives, warfarin, direct oral anticoagulants, many antiretrovirals, antiepileptics, sulfonylureas, statins and corticosteroids: review every drug.
India: the national programme (NTEP) uses 2HRZE/4HRE (ethambutol continued throughout), different weight bands and FDC tablet counts, and has not adopted the 4-month HPMZ regimen; follow NTEP where it applies.
# Contacts and infection control
Evaluate all household and close contacts: treat active TB and offer TB preventive treatment to eligible contacts once TB disease is excluded (see TB preventive treatment protocol).
Advise cough hygiene, ventilation and a mask while infectious; in hospital use airborne precautions until the patient is on effective treatment and improving.
Screen for and treat diabetes, HIV, undernutrition, smoking and alcohol use, which worsen outcomes.
# Pitfalls
Starting HRZE without rifampicin resistance testing, or treating presumed pneumonia with fluoroquinolones before sputum TB testing (masks TB and selects resistance).
Using the 4-month regimen outside its eligibility (under 40 kg, pregnancy, CD4 below 100, extrapulmonary disease) or replacing moxifloxacin with levofloxacin.
Intermittent dosing, not re-weighing, or stopping treatment when symptoms settle.
Missing rifamycin interactions with contraceptives, antiretrovirals and anticoagulants.
# Drugs
HRZE FDC (isoniazid 75 mg, rifampicin 150 mg, pyrazinamide 400 mg, ethambutol 275 mg)
# Dose
Orally once daily for 2 months (WHO 2025 bands): 25 to 29 kg 2 tablets; 30 to 34 kg 3; 35 to 64 kg 4; 65 kg and above 5
Then HR 75/150 mg FDC with the same number of tablets for 4 months.
Isoniazid, 5 mg/kg (4 to 6 mg/kg) orally once daily, usually 300 mg, Hepatitis, peripheral neuropathy; give pyridoxine if at risk.
Rifampicin, 10 mg/kg (8 to 12 mg/kg) orally once daily, usually 600 mg, Empty stomach. Orange body fluids; strong enzyme inducer with many interactions.
Pyrazinamide
25 mg/kg (20 to 30 mg/kg) orally once daily, maximum 2 g
Creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min: 25 to 35 mg/kg three times weekly after dialysis. Hepatotoxic; raises urate.
Ethambutol
15 mg/kg (15 to 25 mg/kg) orally once daily
Creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min: 15 to 25 mg/kg three times weekly. Optic neuritis: check vision.
Rifapentine (4-month regimen)
1200 mg orally once daily with food for 17 weeks
With isoniazid 300 mg and moxifloxacin 400 mg daily, plus weight-banded pyrazinamide for the first 8 weeks.
Moxifloxacin (4-month regimen), 400 mg orally once daily for 17 weeks, QT prolongation; avoid with other QT-prolonging drugs.

# Dose
