# Ascites, SBP and hepatorenal syndrome in cirrhosis
Adults
Emergency department, ward, day-care paracentesis, clinic
Tap every new or admitted ascites (SAAG, PMN count, protein, culture); treat with 2 g sodium diet and spironolactone plus furosemide (100:40); give albumin 6 to 8 g per litre after paracentesis over 5 L; treat SBP (PMN above 250) with antibiotics plus albumin 1.5 g/kg day 1 and 1 g/kg day 3; treat HRS-AKI with terlipressin plus albumin.
Aliases: cirrhotic ascites; SBP; spontaneous bacterial peritonitis; SAAG; serum ascites albumin gradient; LVP; large-volume paracentesis; HRS; hepatorenal syndrome; refractory ascites; terlipressin albumin; diuretics cirrhosis.
# Diagnostic paracentesis
Tap every patient with new-onset ascites, every patient with ascites admitted to hospital (even without symptoms), and any patient with ascites who develops fever, abdominal pain, encephalopathy, AKI, jaundice or unexplained deterioration.
Send ascitic cell count with differential (neutrophil/PMN count), total protein and albumin with a same-time serum albumin. Inoculate aerobic and anaerobic blood culture bottles at the bedside before antibiotics.
SAAG (serum albumin minus ascitic albumin) 1.1 g/dL or more = portal hypertension (about 97% accurate). Below 1.1 g/dL: look for peritoneal tuberculosis, malignancy, pancreatic ascites or nephrotic syndrome.
High SAAG with ascitic protein above 2.5 g/dL suggests cardiac ascites. Low SAAG, high-protein, lymphocyte-predominant fluid in India: check ascitic ADA for tuberculous peritonitis; send cytology, AFB studies or amylase as indicated.
Paracentesis is safe despite a raised INR or low platelets; routine FFP or platelet transfusion is not needed.
# Uncomplicated ascites (grade 2)
Moderate sodium restriction to 2 g (90 mmol) a day with dietitian advice; avoid malnutrition. No fluid restriction unless serum sodium is 125 mmol/L or less.
Spironolactone 100 mg once daily, with or without furosemide 40 mg once daily. A first episode may respond to spironolactone alone; long-standing ascites responds better to combination therapy.
Increase stepwise, at intervals of at least 72 hours, keeping the 100:40 ratio, to a maximum of spironolactone 400 mg and furosemide 160 mg a day.
Target weight loss: up to 0.5 kg/day without peripheral oedema, up to 1 kg/day with oedema. Spot urine sodium/potassium ratio above 1 with no weight loss suggests dietary sodium excess.
Avoid NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers and aminoglycosides.
Tense (grade 3) ascites: large-volume paracentesis first, then sodium restriction and diuretics. Refer grade 2 or 3 ascites for transplant evaluation.
# Large-volume paracentesis and refractory ascites
Paracentesis of more than 5 L: give 20% albumin 6 to 8 g per litre removed (AASLD 2021; BSG 2021 uses 8 g/L), for example about 40 g after 5 L, to prevent post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction.
Risk of circulatory dysfunction rises when more than 8 L is removed in one session.
Consider albumin even for smaller taps if systolic BP is below 90 mmHg, serum sodium below 130 mmol/L or AKI is present.
Refractory ascites (no response to maximal diuretics or diuretic-limiting complications, or early recurrence within 4 weeks): continue sodium restriction, serial LVP with albumin, consider covered TIPS (below 10 mm) in selected patients, and refer for transplant.
In refractory ascites use beta-blockers cautiously and reduce or stop them with systolic BP below 90 mmHg, sodium below 130 mmol/L or creatinine above 1.5 mg/dL.
# Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis
Ascitic PMN count above 250 cells/mm3 = SBP (or spontaneous bacterial empyema in pleural fluid). Start IV antibiotics immediately, before culture results.
Community-acquired SBP: third-generation cephalosporin (cefotaxime 2 g IV every 12 hours or ceftriaxone). Nosocomial or healthcare-associated infection, recent antibiotics or septic shock: broader cover (piperacillin-tazobactam; meropenem if multidrug-resistant Gram-negative colonisation). Follow the local antibiogram.
IV albumin 1.5 g/kg within 6 hours of diagnosis (day 1) and 1 g/kg on day 3; benefit is greatest with AKI or jaundice.
Repeat paracentesis at 48 hours: PMN fall of less than 25% = treatment failure; broaden antibiotics and exclude secondary peritonitis (CT, surgical opinion).
Treat for 5 to 7 days. Hold beta-blockers if MAP falls below 65 mmHg or AKI develops.
Suspect secondary peritonitis with polymicrobial culture, very high PMN count, or a focal source (perforation, abscess, cholecystitis).
# SBP prophylaxis
After recovery from SBP: long-term oral norfloxacin 400 mg once daily; alternatives ciprofloxacin 500 mg once daily or co-trimoxazole 960 mg once daily (BSG 2021).
Cirrhosis with upper GI bleeding: ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours for a maximum of 7 days (AASLD 2024 portal hypertension guidance: up to 5 days).
Primary prophylaxis can be considered with ascitic protein below 1.5 g/dL plus renal dysfunction (creatinine above 1.2 mg/dL, BUN above 25 mg/dL or sodium below 130 mmol/L) or liver failure (Child-Pugh score above 9 with bilirubin above 3 mg/dL).
# AKI and hepatorenal syndrome (HRS-AKI)
AKI in cirrhosis: creatinine rise of 0.3 mg/dL or more within 48 hours, or 50% or more within 7 days.
First steps: stop diuretics, nephrotoxins and NSAIDs; hold beta-blockers if hypotensive; treat infection and bleeding; give albumin 1 g/kg/day (maximum 100 g/day) for 2 days.
HRS-AKI: cirrhosis with ascites, AKI not responding to 2 days of diuretic withdrawal and albumin, no shock, no current or recent nephrotoxins, and no structural kidney injury (proteinuria above 500 mg/day, more than 50 red cells per high-power field, abnormal kidney ultrasound).
Treat HRS-AKI with terlipressin plus albumin (terlipressin preferred). Albumin 1 g/kg (maximum 100 g) on day 1, then 40 to 50 g/day during therapy.
Terlipressin: do not start if SpO2 is below 90% or the patient is volume overloaded; monitor continuous pulse oximetry and stop if SpO2 falls below 90%. Watch for pulmonary oedema and ischaemia.
If terlipressin is unavailable or contraindicated: noradrenaline infusion in ICU. Midodrine plus octreotide is much less effective.
Response: creatinine below 1.5 mg/dL or within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline, treating up to 14 days. Stop if creatinine has not fallen after 4 days at the maximum tolerated dose. Refer for transplant assessment.
# Monitoring and hyponatraemia
On diuretics: daily weight; sodium, potassium and creatinine regularly, especially in the first weeks. Stop or reduce for AKI, sodium below 125 mmol/L, potassium below 3 or above 6 mmol/L, or encephalopathy.
Hyponatraemia 120 to 125 mmol/L: stop diuretics and restrict water to 1,000 mL/day. Below 120 mmol/L: stricter water restriction plus albumin.
Correct chronic hyponatraemia by 4 to 6 mmol/L per 24 hours and never more than 8 mmol/L per 24 hours (osmotic demyelination risk). Hypertonic saline only for severe symptoms or imminent transplant.
Muscle cramps: correct potassium and magnesium; albumin 20 to 40 g/week or baclofen 10 mg/day increased weekly by 10 mg to 30 mg/day.
# Pitfalls
Not tapping ascites on admission and missing asymptomatic SBP.
Delaying antibiotics for culture results, or giving antibiotics without albumin in SBP.
Forgetting albumin after large-volume paracentesis, or over-diuresis causing AKI and hyponatraemia.
Labelling AKI as HRS without the 2-day albumin challenge and exclusion of shock, nephrotoxins and structural disease.
# Drugs
Spironolactone
# Dose
100 mg orally once daily, increase every 3 or more days to a maximum of 400 mg/day
Watch potassium and creatinine; eplerenone or amiloride if painful gynaecomastia.
Furosemide
# Dose
40 mg orally once daily, increase in step with spironolactone (100:40 ratio) to a maximum of 160 mg/day
Hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia and AKI are more common with loop diuretics.
Albumin 20% (after LVP)
# Dose
6 to 8 g per litre of ascites removed when more than 5 L is drained
Consider for smaller volumes with hypotension, hyponatraemia or AKI.

1.5 g/kg IV within 6 hours of diagnosis (day 1), then 1 g/kg IV on day 3
Given in addition to antibiotics.

2 g IV every 12 hours for 5 to 7 days

# Dose
1 mg IV bolus every 6 hours; if creatinine has fallen by less than 30% on day 4, increase to 2 mg every 6 hours. Alternative: continuous infusion 2 mg/day, increased every 24 to 48 hours up to 12 mg/day
With albumin. Maximum 14 days. Continuous infusion has fewer ischaemic side effects. Do not use if SpO2 below 90%.
