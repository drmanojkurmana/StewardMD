# Acute (decompensated) heart failure and pulmonary oedema
Treat the precipitant, give oxygen or NIV for hypoxaemia and respiratory distress, IV loop diuretic at 1 to 2 times the oral dose (20 to 40 mg furosemide if naive) with natriuresis-guided escalation, IV nitrate if SBP is above 110 mmHg, and reserve inotropes for hypotension with hypoperfusion.
recognise
# Recognise
Presentations: decompensated chronic HF (gradual congestion), acute pulmonary oedema (rapid onset, often hypertensive), isolated right ventricular failure, and cardiogenic shock. ESC 2026 calls acute HF decompensated heart failure.
Profile at the bedside: congestion, wet or dry (orthopnoea, raised JVP, crackles, oedema, hepatomegaly), and perfusion, warm or cold (cold peripheries, confusion, oliguria, raised lactate, narrow pulse pressure).
Four profiles guide treatment. Warm-wet (commonest): diuretic, plus vasodilator if SBP is high. Cold-wet with SBP below 90 mmHg: cardiogenic shock (inotrope, vasopressor if refractory, diuretic once perfusion improves, ICU care); cold-wet with SBP 90 mmHg or more: vasodilator and diuretic, inotrope only if refractory. Cold-dry: hypoperfused without congestion, consider a cautious fluid challenge. Warm-dry: compensated, adjust oral therapy.
Look for the precipitant immediately: acute coronary syndrome, hypertensive emergency, arrhythmia, acute mechanical cause (valve rupture, VSR), pulmonary embolism, infection, non-adherence to drugs or salt restriction, NSAIDs, anaemia, thyroid disease.
India: consider rheumatic valve disease (especially mitral stenosis), severe anaemia, peripartum cardiomyopathy and alcohol.
immediate
# First hour
Sit upright. Oxygen if SpO2 is below 90% or PaO2 below 60 mmHg; avoid hyperoxia.
Non-invasive ventilation (CPAP or BiPAP) for respiratory distress (respiratory rate above 25/min, SpO2 below 90%) to relieve dyspnoea and reduce intubation.
IV loop diuretic without delay for congestion: diuretic-naive, furosemide 20 to 40 mg IV; already on an oral loop diuretic, 1 to 2 times the total daily oral dose IV.
IV nitrate if SBP is above 110 mmHg (particularly hypertensive pulmonary oedema); start low and titrate, stop if SBP falls below 90 mmHg.
12-lead ECG at once; STEMI or very high-risk NSTE-ACS goes for immediate angiography. Treat fast AF, bradyarrhythmia and hypertensive emergency specifically.
Do not give routine opioids; reserve for severe pain or distress with caution (respiratory depression).
investigations
# Investigations
Natriuretic peptide: BNP below 100 pg/mL, NT-proBNP below 300 pg/mL or MR-proANP below 120 pmol/L make acute HF unlikely. Obesity lowers and AF, age and CKD raise values.
ECG, troponin, creatinine, urea, sodium, potassium, glucose, CBC, liver function, TSH; blood gas with lactate if hypoperfused or in respiratory distress.
Chest X-ray and lung ultrasound (bilateral B-lines, pleural effusion). Echocardiography urgently in shock or suspected mechanical cause, otherwise within 48 hours if structure and function are unknown.
Iron studies (ferritin and transferrin saturation) before discharge; treat iron deficiency (ferritin below 100 mcg/L, or 100 to 299 mcg/L with TSAT below 20%) with IV iron.
treatment
# Decongestion: diuretic escalation
Check response early: spot urine sodium at 2 hours below 50 to 70 mmol/L, or urine output below 100 to 150 mL/h over the first 6 hours, means an inadequate response.
Inadequate response: double the IV loop diuretic dose (give as 2 to 3 boluses per day or as an infusion), usually to a maximum of 400 to 600 mg furosemide per day.
Persistent congestion despite adequate loop diuretic: add acetazolamide or a thiazide (sequential nephron blockade), with close monitoring of potassium, sodium and creatinine.
Continue until euvolaemic. A modest creatinine rise during effective decongestion is acceptable and is not a reason to stop diuretics if congestion persists.
Start an SGLT2 inhibitor in hospital once stabilised. Continue beta-blockers and other foundational therapy unless shock, severe bradycardia or hyperkalaemia; reduce rather than stop where possible.
treatment
# Hypoperfusion and cardiogenic shock
Cardiogenic shock: SBP below 90 mmHg (or needing vasopressors) with signs of hypoperfusion. Echo, ECG and lactate at once; immediate angiography and PCI if ACS is the cause.
Inotrope (dobutamine, levosimendan or milrinone) only for hypotension with hypoperfusion, at the lowest effective dose for the shortest time; not for normotensive patients.
Add a vasopressor, preferably noradrenaline, for persistent hypotension; arterial line and ICU care.
Refer early to a shock team or centre offering temporary mechanical circulatory support; do not use routine intra-aortic balloon pump.
Give a fluid challenge (250 mL over 15 to 30 minutes) only if there is no overt congestion or in RV infarction.
monitoring
# Monitoring
SpO2, heart rate and rhythm, BP, respiratory rate continuously at first; urine output hourly initially (catheter only if needed).
Daily weight and fluid balance; daily creatinine, sodium and potassium while on IV diuretics. Keep potassium 4.0 to 5.0 mmol/L.
Signs of congestion daily (JVP, oedema, orthopnoea, lung ultrasound) to judge when decongestion is complete.
Thromboprophylaxis with LMWH unless contraindicated or already anticoagulated.
escalate
# Escalate
Intubation for respiratory failure with hypoxaemia (PaO2 below 60 mmHg), hypercapnia (PaCO2 above 50 mmHg) and acidosis (pH below 7.35) not responding to NIV, or reduced consciousness.
ICU or CCU for shock, NIV need, persistent SpO2 below 90% despite oxygen, heart rate below 40 or above 130/min, or refractory arrhythmia.
Diuretic resistance despite combination therapy: consider ultrafiltration or renal replacement therapy with nephrology input.
disposition
# Before discharge
Discharge only when euvolaemic on a stable oral diuretic dose for at least 24 hours, with a plan for weight-guided diuretic adjustment.
Start and rapidly up-titrate foundational therapy before and after discharge (ARNI or ACE inhibitor or ARB, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2 inhibitor for reduced LVEF), with review within 1 to 2 weeks of discharge.
Educate on salt restriction, fluid guidance, daily weights and early warning symptoms; refer to cardiac rehabilitation.
pitfalls
# Pitfalls
Under-dosing IV diuretic in patients already on oral loop diuretics, or stopping early because of a small creatinine rise.
Giving IV fluid boluses to a congested patient with a low BP and low output state instead of treating the cause.
Stopping beta-blockers and RAAS inhibitors routinely on admission.
Missing ACS, rheumatic mitral stenosis or a mechanical complication as the precipitant.
