Small bowel GIST follows size and mitoses. Small bowel GIST over 5 cm or over 5 mitoses per 5 square mm is high risk per modified NIH, needing 3 years of adjuvant imatinib after R0 segmental resection with clear margins.

Shrinking first saves organs. Neoadjuvant imatinib for 6 to 12 months shrinks borderline duodenal and rectal GIST to allow organ-preserving surgery, with response judged by Choi density criteria on contrast scans.

Mesenteric lumps bleed and block. Small bowel neuroendocrine tumours with mesenteric desmoplastic mass and flushing-diarrhoea syndrome need bowel resection with mesenteric nodal clearance plus octreotide for hormonal control.

Retroperitoneum needs compartments. Retroperitoneal sarcoma over 10 cm needs compartmental resection with adjacent organs at specialist centres, with well-differentiated liposarcoma recurring locally and dedifferentiated metastasising distantly.

Desmoid watches first. Desmoid fibromatosis in FAP or after surgery is watched first with 20 percent spontaneous regression, and sorafenib or low-dose chemotherapy suits progressive disease over surgery because recurrence is high.

Ampullary adenomas threaten. Duodenal adenoma with high-grade dysplasia or ampullary adenoma over 3 cm needs transduodenal ampullectomy or Whipple because of invasive cancer risk in the ampullary complex.

Polyps telescope bowel. Peutz-Jeghers small bowel polyps over 1.5 cm get enteroscopic polypectomy to prevent intussusception, with clean sweep laparotomy and intraoperative enteroscopy for heavy burden.

Small bowel cancer resects wide. Primary small bowel adenocarcinoma of the duodenum and proximal jejunum presents with obstruction and needs segmental resection with nodal clearance, with adjuvant capecitabine-oxaliplatin for node-positive disease.
