# Connective tissue disease associated ILD (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison; SLS II, SENSCIS, INBUILD trials)
CTD-ILD drives mortality most in SSc (up to 80 percent), RA (10 percent to 30 percent), and myositis with antisynthetase syndrome (up to 70 percent).
HRCT is the diagnostic gold standard: NSIP with basilar ground glass, reticulation, and traction bronchiectasis leads in SSc, myositis, and SLE, while UIP with subpleural honeycombing leads in RA.
Lung function shows restrictive defect with reduced forced vital capacity (FVC) and reduced carbon monoxide diffusing capacity (DLCO).
SLS II proved oral mycophenolate (MMF target 3 g daily for 2 years) equals 1 year of oral cyclophosphamide for FVC preservation with superior safety, making MMF first-line for CTD-ILD.
SENSCIS in SSc-ILD and INBUILD across progressive fibrosing CTD-ILDs proved nintedanib 150 mg twice daily, a tyrosine kinase blocker of PDGF, FGF, and VEGF receptors, cutting annual FVC decline by 44 percent to 57 percent.
FocuSSced and phase 3 data showed the IL-6 receptor blocker tocilizumab stabilizing FVC in early active diffuse SSc with raised acute-phase reactants, gaining SSc-ILD approval.
Antisynthetase ILD progresses rapidly with anti-Jo-1, anti-PL-7, or anti-PL-12; therapy pairs high-dose glucocorticoids with mycophenolate, tacrolimus, or rituximab.
Progressive pulmonary fibrosis (PPF) needs at least 2 of 3 within the past year: worse symptoms, FVC fall of 5 percent or more or DLCO fall of 10 percent or more, and HRCT progression, qualifying for add-on antifibrotic therapy.
Pirfenidone is avoided when creatinine clearance falls below 30 mL per minute; nintedanib needs liver function checks before starting and monthly for 3 months, with dose reduction for transaminases above 3 times normal.
Acute exacerbations with new widespread ground glass over weeks carry inpatient mortality above 50 percent; high-dose glucocorticoids with broad antibiotics and early intensive care review are required.
Baseline DLCO below 40 percent of predicted with pulmonary hypertension on echocardiography marks high-risk SSc-ILD; oxygen assessment on exertion plus early transplant referral improves long-term survival.
