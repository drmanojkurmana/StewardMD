Glasgow sums eye, voice, movement. The Glasgow Coma Scale scores eye opening 1 to 4, verbal response 1 to 5 and motor response 1 to 6, for a total of 3 to 15 recorded as E, V and M.

Bands decide the airway. Severe injury at 8 or less needs intubation, moderate injury at 9 to 12 needs admission, and mild injury at 13 to 15 needs a scan plus a responsible adult.

NICE lists the hour rule. CT within 1 hour covers GCS below 13 on arrival, below 15 at 2 hours, open or basal fracture, seizure, focal deficit, or repeated vomiting.

Thinners change everything. Anticoagulated patients with blackout or amnesia need CT within 8 hours on warfarin or direct oral anticoagulants even when alert.

Marshall grades the scan. Diffuse injury I to IV plus evacuated V and non-evacuated VI predict mortality from a tenth to over half.

Rotterdam refines the grade. Subarachnoid and ventricular blood add risk, an epidural mass subtracts it, and the score predicts six month outcome.

Monitors go in early. GCS 3 to 8 with an abnormal CT, or a normal CT with two of age above 40, posturing, or systolic below 90, earns a pressure bolt.

Pupils warn of herniation. A fixed dilated Hutchinson pupil marks uncal third nerve compression, while same-side weakness is a Kernohan notch of the opposite peduncle.
