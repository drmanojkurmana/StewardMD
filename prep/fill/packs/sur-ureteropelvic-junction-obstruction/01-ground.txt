Adult pelviureteric junction obstruction comes from an intrinsic aperistaltic segment that cannot propel urine, or from extrinsic compression by crossing lower pole vessels found in 30 to 50 percent of cases. An accessory renal artery or vein pressing on the junction is mapped with CT angiography before repair, because an unseen vessel dooms an endoscopic incision and a dismembered pyeloplasty is chosen to transpose the junction away from the vessels.

Intermittent obstruction produces Dietl crisis, episodic severe flank pain with nausea and vomiting as the pelvis overdistends and then floods out. Walking or standing with nephroptosis, a mobile dropping kidney, kinks the junction with posture, and a palpable loin lump between attacks is the distended pelvis.

A dilated system on ultrasound may be a harmless extrarenal pelvis, so diuretic renography with 99mTc-MAG3 settles the question. After tracer injection, furosemide, called lasix, washes the system, and a clearance half time over 20 minutes proves obstruction, 10 to 20 minutes reads equivocal, and under 10 minutes is normal drainage. The Whitaker pressure perfusion test is reserved for equivocal scans: fluid is infused antegrade at 10 mL per min through a nephrostomy while pelvic and bladder pressures are recorded, and a gradient above 22 cmH2O diagnoses obstruction.

Anderson-Hynes dismembered pyeloplasty excises the narrow segment and reattaches ureter to the most dependent pelvis, with success above 95 percent by open, laparoscopic, or robotic routes, and a stent with pelvic drain protects healing. Endopyelotomy cuts full thickness posterolateral through the stenotic segment to dodge anterior vessels, and suits primary strictures under 1.5 cm with minimal hydronephrosis, splinted by a single double J stent for weeks. Fenger plasty closes a longitudinal cut transversely and Foley Y-V pyeloplasty swings a pelvic flap into the ureter, both reserved for secondary strictures or high ureteral insertions without crossing vessels.

Prompt correction is warranted for symptoms, a blocked solitary functioning kidney, or differential function falling below 40 percent on serial renography, with urgent stent or nephrostomy when infection supervenes, while silent well drained systems are watched.
