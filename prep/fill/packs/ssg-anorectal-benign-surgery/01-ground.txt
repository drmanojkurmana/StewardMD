Internal haemorrhoids grade by prolapse from grade I with bleeding only through grade II prolapsing on straining, grade III needing manual return and grade IV with irreducible prolapse. Grades I and II suit rubber band ligation above the dentate line, while grades III and IV need stapled haemorrhoidopexy or excisional haemorrhoidectomy.

Fistula direction follows rules. The Parks classification maps tracts as intersphincteric, transsphincteric, suprasphincteric and extrasphincteric, and the Goodsall rule predicts a curved posterior tract from a midline posterior opening but a straight radial tract from an anterior one.

Sphincter decides the operation. Low simple fistulae involving little muscle get fistulotomy laying the tract open, while high complex tracts involving over 30 percent of the sphincter need continence-saving LIFT ligation of the intersphincteric tract or an advancement flap covering the internal opening.

Fissures heal with relaxation. A chronic posterior midline fissure with sentinel pile and hypertrophied papilla failing 6 weeks of diltiazem or nitrate ointment needs lateral internal sphincterotomy divided up to the dentate line, with lasting relief in most patients.

Natal cleft disease needs the midline moved. Pilonidal disease with midline pits and abscess gets drainage first, then a Bascom cleft-lift or Karykakis flap with off-midline closure, because wounds closed in the midline break down and recur most.

Prolapse repair matches the patient. Full-thickness rectal prolapse over 5 cm in the fit young suits laparoscopic ventral mesh rectopexy preserving pelvic nerves, while frail elderly under spinal anaesthesia suit perineal Delorme mucosectomy or Altemeier rectosigmoidectomy.

Anal cancer is treated without a knife first. Anal squamous carcinoma under 5 cm gets the Nigro protocol of 5-fluorouracil with mitomycin plus radiotherapy as primary therapy, reserving abdominoperineal resection with permanent colostomy for salvage after failed chemoradiotherapy.

Sweat-gland sepsis needs excision. Hidradenitis suppurativa of the perineum with recurrent boils and sinus tracts needs wide excision with secondary healing or flap cover, because antibiotics alone never cure the established tunnels.
