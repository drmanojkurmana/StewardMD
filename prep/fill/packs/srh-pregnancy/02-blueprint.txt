# Pregnancy and reproductive health in rheumatic disease (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-pregnancy: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Preconception counseling in systemic lupus erythematosus mandates maintaining quiescent disease or low disease activity for at least 6 months prior to conception, which significantly reduces the risks of maternal disease flares, lupus nephritis relapses, preeclampsia, and preterm delivery.

Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) is safe and strongly recommended to be continued throughout pregnancy and breastfeeding in all women with SLE; HCQ reduces maternal flare rates, lowers preeclampsia risk, and halves the recurrence risk of congenital heart block in anti-Ro/SSA-positive mothers.

Transplacental crossing of maternal anti-Ro/SSA (anti-Ro52/Ro60) and anti-La/SSB antibodies causes neonatal lupus erythematosus; the most severe permanent manifestation is congenital complete (third-degree) heart block, developing in 2% of primigravid anti-Ro positive mothers and recurring in up to 15% to 20% in subsequent pregnancies.

Serial fetal echocardiographic surveillance (pulsed Doppler monitoring of mechanical PR interval) is recommended weekly or biweekly from gestational weeks 16 to 26 in anti-Ro/SSA-positive pregnancies to detect early, potentially reversible first- or second-degree heart block.

Absolute teratogenic contraindications in pregnancy include methotrexate (fetal aminopterin-methotrexate syndrome with craniofacial and skeletal defects), mycophenolate mofetil (microtia, cleft palate, cardiac anomalies), cyclophosphamide, and leflunomide (requires cholestyramine washout before conception).

Immunosuppressive medications compatible with pregnancy and lactation include azathioprine (dose <=2 mg/kg/day), calcineurin inhibitors (cyclosporine, tacrolimus), and systemic glucocorticoids (prednisone <=20 mg/day, metabolized by placental 11-beta-HSD2 so minimal drug reaches the fetus).

Certolizumab pegol is a pegylated Fab fragment of an anti-TNF antibody that lacks an Fc region and cannot bind the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn); prospective pharmacokinetic trials demonstrate negligible placental transfer throughout all three trimesters, making it the preferred anti-TNF biologic in pregnancy.

Low-dose aspirin (75 to 150 mg daily) initiated before 16 weeks of gestation is recommended for all pregnant women with SLE or antiphospholipid antibodies to reduce the risk of maternal preeclampsia, placental insufficiency, and fetal growth restriction.
