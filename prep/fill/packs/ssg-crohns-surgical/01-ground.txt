Crohn disease earns an operation when its complications outgrow medicines. A fibrostenotic stricture that will not open, fistulising disease burrowing into bladder or skin, an undrained abscess, toxic megacolon, or growth failure in a child are surgical indications, while purely inflammatory luminal disease without these features stays on medical therapy.

Narrowed bowel need not always be removed. Short strictures under 10 cm in quiet, non-inflamed bowel are opened lengthwise and sewn crosswise as a Heineke-Mikulicz stricturoplasty, while longer diseased segments take a Finney side-to-side or isoperistaltic stricturoplasty. The bowel stays in circuit and short bowel is avoided.

The commonest resection removes the diseased terminal ileum with the caecum. Ileocolic resection takes the segment with its mesentery and joins healthy ends, and the Kono-S antimesenteric functional end-to-end configuration places the anastomosis away from the mesentery to lower surgical recurrence.

Disease around the anus follows its own rules. Complex perianal Crohn with branching tracts needs examination under anaesthesia to map every opening, with loose seton drainage keeping sepsis controlled while anti-TNF therapy heals the bowel. Only a destroyed, incontinent rectum is removed by proctectomy.

Fistulae declare themselves by their drainage. An enterovesical fistula between ileum and bladder gives pneumaturia with recurrent urinary infections that never clear, confirmed on contrast imaging, and needs resection of the bowel segment with repair of the bladder cuff rather than bladder removal.

Dilation changes everything. Toxic megacolon with a transverse colon over 6 cm plus fever, tachycardia and systemic toxicity is a surgical emergency; the standard is urgent subtotal colectomy with ileostomy, because primary anastomosis in this setting leaks and kills.

Recurrence is the lifelong enemy. Smoking doubles postoperative Crohn recurrence, so cessation is prescribed as firmly as any drug, and colonoscopy at 6 months grades the join with the Rutgeerts score, directing timely prophylaxis with thiopurines or anti-TNF before symptoms return.

Sepsis is drained before it is cut. An intra-abdominal abscess over 3 cm gets percutaneous drainage with antibiotics first, and definitive resection follows after 6 to 8 weeks once fever has settled, drains are dry and nutrition is rebuilt.
