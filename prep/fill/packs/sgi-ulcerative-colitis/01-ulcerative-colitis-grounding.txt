# Ulcerative colitis: classification, severe flares, complications and surveillance
# Source: StewardMD gastroenterology knowledge base / Sleisenger 11e Ch. 115

## Disease extent and definition
Ulcerative colitis is characterized by continuous, confluent mucosal inflammation starting in the rectum (proctitis, present in 95% of patients) and extending proximally to involve varying lengths of the colon without skip lesions or transmural involvement.
Montreal classification categorizes UC disease extent into E1 (proctitis, limited to rectum), E2 (left-sided colitis, distal to splenic flexure), and E3 (extensive colitis, proximal to splenic flexure).

## Acute severe flare and predictive scores
Truelove and Witts criteria identify acute severe ulcerative colitis (ASUC) by at least 6 bloody bowel motions each day plus at least one marker of systemic toxicity: pulse > 90 bpm, oral temperature > 37.8 C, haemoglobin < 10.5 g/dL, or ESR > 30 mm/h.
Oxford criteria on day 3 of IV corticosteroid therapy predict 85% colectomy risk in ASUC if stool frequency > 8 per day, or stool frequency 3 to 8 per day accompanied by CRP > 45 mg/L.

## Rescue therapy and emergency complications
Rescue medical therapy for steroid-refractory ASUC comprises either infliximab (5 to 10 mg/kg) or intravenous ciclosporin (target trough level 150 to 250 ng/mL), demonstrating equivalent 90-day colectomy-free survival in the CYCLO trial.
Toxic megacolon is defined by total or segmental non-obstructive colonic dilatation > 6 cm on abdominal radiography alongside systemic toxicity (fever > 38.6 C, tachycardia > 120 beats per minute), requiring emergent surgical consultation and decompressive therapy.

## Histopathology and dysplasia surveillance
Histopathologic hallmarks of ulcerative colitis include diffuse crypt architectural distortion, basal plasmacytosis, mucin depletion, and crypt abscesses confined to the mucosal and submucosal layers.
Surveillance colonoscopy for colorectal cancer detection is initiated 8 years after symptom onset in extensive (E3) or left-sided (E2) colitis, but immediately upon diagnosis in patients with concomitant primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC).

## Additional clinical details
Initial management of ASUC requires intravenous hydrocortisone (100 mg four times daily) or methylprednisolone (60 mg once daily).
Restorative proctocolectomy with ileal pouch-anal anastomosis (IPAA) is the surgical gold standard for medically refractory disease or dysplasia.
Pouchitis represents the most common long-term postoperative complication following IPAA, treated with ciprofloxacin or metronidazole.
