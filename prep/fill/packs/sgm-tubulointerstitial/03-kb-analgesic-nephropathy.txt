# Analgesic nephropathy
Analgesic nephropathy, Renal / Nephrology, Nephrology / Urology.
Aliases: phenacetin nephropathy; analgesic abuse nephropathy; analgesic-induced chronic interstitial nephritis; analgesic kidney.
# Pathophysiology
Long-term ingestion of analgesic mixtures damages the renal medulla by two complementary mechanisms. Phenacetin and its metabolite paracetamol accumulate and generate oxidative injury in the papillary tissue, while aspirin depletes protective prostaglandins, reducing medullary blood flow, and depletes glutathione, potentiating the toxicity. The medulla, already functioning at low oxygen tension, sustains combined ischaemic and oxidative damage leading to papillary necrosis and a surrounding chronic tubulointerstitial nephritis with fibrosis and tubular atrophy. Over years this produces shrunken, scarred kidneys, and chronic urothelial injury predisposes to transitional cell carcinoma.
