# Stevens-Johnson syndrome / TEN
Stevens-Johnson syndrome / TEN, Dermatology / Emergency.
# Red flags
Skin detachment — manage like a burn; high mortality
# Investigations
Stop the culprit drug
Dermatology review
SCORTEN severity
# Pathophysiology
A delayed, T-cell-mediated (type IVa, cytotoxic) severe cutaneous adverse reaction. A drug or metabolite-altered antigen is presented in association with a risk HLA class I allele by keratinocytes or other antigen-presenting cells; a restricted T-cell receptor on skin-enriched CD8+ T cells recognizes this drug-HLA complex, triggering activation and release of cytolytic mediators (notably granulysin) that cause full-thickness epidermal keratinocyte death and detachment. Proposed antigen-presentation models include hapten/prohapten covalent binding, the pharmacologic-interaction (p-i) noncovalent model, and the altered-peptide-repertoire model (as proven for abacavir-HLA-B*57:01). Carbamazepine SJS/TEN shows a highly restricted V-alpha/V-beta TCR repertoire shared among HLA-B*15:02 patients. A risk HLA allele alone is insufficient; non-MHC factors such as drug metabolism (e.g., CYP2C9*3 slow metabolism with phenytoin) contribute.
# Prognosis
Mortality is substantial and rises with the extent of epidermal detachment: at least ~10% of SJS and ~30% of TEN patients die from the disease (overall reported mortality up to ~30%). Older age, greater BSA detachment, malignancy, and renal insufficiency are independent poor-prognostic factors. Best outcomes follow early diagnosis and immediate withdrawal of the culprit drug.
# Severity classification
Classified by percent body surface area (BSA) of epidermal detachment: SJS = <10% BSA; SJS/TEN overlap = 10-30% BSA; TEN = >30% BSA (maximal form).. SCORTEN (severity-of-illness score for TEN) is the standard prognostic tool; note the source text does not enumerate its parameters.
# Clinical pearls
SJS/TEN is a single disease spectrum of full-thickness epidermal necrosis with blistering and mucosal/epidermal detachment, separated only by the extent of body surface area (BSA) detachment.
Drugs cause roughly 80% of cases; the most frequently implicated agents are sulfonamide antibiotics, allopurinol, antiepileptics (lamotrigine, phenytoin, carbamazepine), oxicam NSAIDs, beta-lactam and other antibiotics, and nevirapine.
Onset is typically delayed, around 5-28 days after starting the culprit drug.
Prodrome: fever >39 C (102.2 F), sore throat, and conjunctivitis precede or accompany the rash; lesions are painful, dusky, atypical and target-like.
It is uncommon in children and over-represented among people with HIV, SLE, certain HLA types, and slow acetylators.
Mucosal surfaces are characteristically involved, and Nikolsky's sign is positive.
SJS/TEN is mediated by CD8+ cytotoxic T cells recognizing a drug-HLA class I complex; granulysin released by these cells is thought to be the principal mediator of keratinocyte death.
Strong, drug- and phenotype-specific HLA associations exist: HLA-B*15:02 with carbamazepine SJS/TEN (Han Chinese), HLA-B*58:01 with allopurinol, HLA-B*13:01 with dapsone/sulfonamide.
Frozen-section skin biopsy can aid rapid diagnosis and distinguish SJS/TEN (full-thickness epidermal necrosis) from mimics.
Loss of skin barrier integrity leads to dehydration and secondary sepsis, which are major causes of death.
# Additional differentials
Acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) - may resemble early SJS but erosions are superficial and prominent mucosal involvement is lacking
Drug-induced hypersensitivity syndrome / DRESS (DIHS) - can have TEN-like overlap features
Overlap hypersensitivity syndromes (DIHS-TEN-like, AGEP-TEN-like) - up to ~20% of severe drug eruptions show overlap, supporting a clinical spectrum
# Infection mimics
Reactive infectious mucocutaneous eruption (RIME) - Mycoplasma and other respiratory infections in children cause prominent mucositis with limited cutaneous involvement
Erythema multiforme associated with herpes simplex virus - painful mucosal erosions and target lesions, typically acral, with limited skin detachment
# Non infectious mimics
Staphylococcal scalded-skin syndrome (SSSS) - diffuse tender erythema with bullae/desquamation and positive Nikolsky's sign; cleavage is superficial (subcorneal), spares mucosa
DRESS / drug-induced hypersensitivity syndrome - maculopapular eruption, facial edema, eosinophilia, multiorgan (especially hepatic) involvement
Erythema multiforme major - target lesions with mucous-membrane involvement
Acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP) - sterile pustules on erythema, flexural predominance
Exfoliative erythroderma syndrome - diffuse erythema/scaling, may be drug-induced
Autoimmune blistering disease (e.g., bullous pemphigoid, which can also be drug-induced/exacerbated)
# Additional investigations
Frozen-section skin biopsy for rapid diagnosis; histology shows full-thickness epidermal necrosis (distinguishes from AGEP's superficial/subcorneal neutrophilic pustules)
Calculate BSA of epidermal detachment to classify SJS vs overlap vs TEN
Assess for renal insufficiency and consider underlying malignancy (both poor-prognosis markers)
Patch testing with the responsible drug (after recovery) and use of the ALDEN algorithm to rank likelihood of drug causality
# Red flags
Extensive/rapidly progressing epidermal detachment (BSA toward TEN range) - maximal-severity form
Older age, malignancy, or renal insufficiency - independently associated with poor prognosis
Signs of sepsis or dehydration from loss of skin barrier integrity
Mucosal (ocular, oral, urogenital, respiratory) involvement - risk of acute and long-term sequelae
# Pitfalls
Early SJS may be hard to distinguish from AGEP; in AGEP erosions are superficial and prominent mucosal involvement is NOT present.
Drugs may be implicated even when the eruption appears shortly AFTER the drug has been discontinued (delayed-type reactions).
A risk HLA allele does NOT by itself cause SJS/TEN - most carriers remain drug-tolerant; other factors (e.g., drug metabolism) are required.
Designation of a single diagnosis is NOT always possible in overlap hypersensitivity syndromes; manage according to the dominant evolving clinical features.
Skin biopsy helps characterize the reaction but does NOT by itself establish drug causality.
# Confirm
Painful mucocutaneous rash with blistering/skin detachment and mucosal erosions, usually drug-induced; SJS <10%, TEN >30% body-surface detachment.
Admit (burns/ICU); urgent dermatology.
Stop culprit drug + burns-type supportive care
# Steps
STOP the culprit drug immediately (single most important step affecting outcome).
Manage like a burn — fluid/electrolyte balance, temperature control, meticulous wound/eye/mucosal care; ideally a burns/specialist unit.
Analgesia, nutrition, infection surveillance (avoid prophylactic antibiotics).
Ophthalmology and urology/gynae input for mucosal involvement; calculate SCORTEN.
Immunomodulation (ciclosporin/IVIG/etanercept) per specialist — evidence evolving.
