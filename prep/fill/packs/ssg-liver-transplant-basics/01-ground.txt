King College criteria list paracetamol acidosis with pH under 7.3, INR over 6.5 with grade 3 to 4 encephalopathy, and creatinine over 300 micromol per L as triggers for super-urgent listing in acute liver failure. Non-paracetamol causes use separate thresholds.

Model for End-Stage Liver Disease over 15 prioritises transplant listing, with exception points for HCC within Milan and for portopulmonary or hepatopulmonary syndromes. Higher MELD means shorter expected survival without graft.

Piggyback caval preservation avoids venovenous bypass and keeps haemodynamics stable, while the duct-to-duct biliary anastomosis over a T-tube suits intact recipient ducts. Roux-en-Y biliary drainage serves diseased ducts.

Hepatic artery thrombosis within 2 weeks shows rising transaminases with absent Doppler flow and needs urgent revascularisation or retransplantation to prevent graft loss. Late thrombosis presents with biliary strictures.

Acute cellular rejection with fever, graft tenderness and transaminitis in weeks 2 to 6 responds to pulse methylprednisolone 1 g daily for 3 days. Steroid-resistant rejection gets antithymocyte globulin.

Tacrolimus troughs of 8 to 12 ng per mL early and 5 to 8 later balance rejection against nephrotoxicity, with mycophenolate and steroids tapered by 3 months. Tacrolimus nephrotoxicity, tremor and diabetes need monitoring.

Living-donor right-lobe grafts need graft-to-recipient weight ratio over 0.8 percent and remnant over 30 percent in the donor, with middle hepatic vein decision by volumetry. Smaller grafts risk small-for-size syndrome.

Biliary strictures months after transplant get ERCP dilation with stenting first, and recurrent cholangitis with diffuse ischaemic cholangiopathy needs retransplant evaluation. Hepatic artery patency is checked first. Donor workup excludes steatosis over 30 percent with biopsy, and antibody incompatibility is crossed by desensitisation. Protocol biopsies catch subclinical rejection, while donor-specific antibodies herald chronic ductopenic rejection needing relisting. Recurrence of primary sclerosing cholangitis and alcohol relapse surveillance continue lifelong, with yearly colonoscopy in colitis patients. Cytomegalovirus prophylaxis with valganciclovir covers the first 3 months in high-risk pairs.
