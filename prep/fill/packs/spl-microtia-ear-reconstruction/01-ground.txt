Exam grounding notes for microtia and ear reconstruction, written for grounding, not for display. Paraphrased from Plastic Surgery 5E Volume 3 on auricular reconstruction.

Microtia grades run from I to IV. Grade I is a mildly dysmorphic but complete pinna. Grade II keeps partial development with a stenotic ear canal. Grade III is the classic peanut ear with a lobule remnant. Grade IV is anotia, total absence of the auricle.

Autologous cartilage reconstruction waits until 6 to 10 years of age, when chest circumference exceeds 60 cm and the opposite ear nears adult size for a template. Earlier harvest yields too little cartilage; later repair still works but the child endures more years of deformity.

Brent staging builds the ear in four operations. First the surgeon carves and implants the cartilage framework. Next the lobule is transposed into position. Then the framework is elevated off the head with a skin graft behind it. Last the tragus and concha are sculpted for detail.

Nagata compresses the work into two stages. Stage 1 fabricates the full framework including the tragus and transposes the lobule in the same sitting. Stage 2 elevates the construct with a temporoparietal fascia flap under the skin for a crisp outline. Fewer sittings suit families travelling far.

The framework is carved from the ipsilateral synchondrosis of the 6th, 7th, and 8th costal cartilages. Base block comes from the fused sixth and seventh, while the slender 8th cartilage forms the helical rim curled around the edge. Donor chest deformity stays minimal with careful technique.

Porous polyethylene, known as Medpor, offers a one stage alloplastic alternative that avoids any rib harvest. The implant adjusts to size at once, but it carries a lifelong risk of framework extrusion through thin cover, especially after trauma or infection.

A prominent ear is corrected by otoplasty, not by microtia techniques. Absence of the antihelical fold is recreated with Mustard mattress cartilage sutures, while conchal hypertrophy is pinned back with Furnas concho-mastoid sutures. Both manoeuvres set the ear closer to the head.

Proportions guide every reconstruction. A normal auricle measures 5.5 to 6.5 cm in length, tilts posteriorly off the vertical by approximately 15 to 20 degrees, and projects 1.5 to 2.0 cm from the mastoid surface. The rebuilt ear is matched to these numbers and to the opposite side.
