# Innate and adaptive immunity in rheumatic disease (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-immune-basics: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Pattern recognition receptors include Toll-like receptors (TLRs) and NOD-like receptors. TLR4 recognises bacterial lipopolysaccharide and endogenous danger molecules such as HMGB1, whereas endosomal TLR7 and TLR9 recognise viral or self single-stranded RNA and unmethylated CpG DNA, driving type I interferon production in systemic lupus erythematosus.

The NLRP3 inflammasome consists of NLRP3, ASC adapter protein, and procaspase-1. Cleavage of procaspase-1 generates active caspase-1, which processes pro-IL-1beta and pro-IL-18 into their mature active secretory forms, a pivotal mechanism in gout, pseudogout, and cryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes.

Naive CD4+ T helper differentiation is directed by lineage-specifying cytokines: IL-12 and IFN-gamma induce Th1 via T-bet, IL-4 induces Th2 via GATA3, whereas IL-6 and TGF-beta drive Th17 differentiation through transcription factor ROR-gamma-t, producing IL-17A, IL-17F, and IL-22 which drive neutrophilic tissue inflammation in spondyloarthritis.

Regulatory T cells express CD4, CD25, and transcription factor FOXP3. Defective FOXP3 function leads to the IPEX syndrome (immune dysregulation, polyendocrinopathy, enteropathy, X-linked), highlighting the non-redundant role of Tregs in suppressing autoreactive effector T cells via IL-10, TGF-beta, and contact-dependent CTLA-4.

Central B-cell tolerance in the bone marrow eliminates self-reactive immature B cells via clonal deletion or receptor editing of immunoglobulin light chains. Peripheral tolerance mechanisms include clonal anergy, functional suppression by regulatory B cells, and dependency on survival factor BAFF, excess of which promotes lupus autoimmunity.

Proinflammatory cytokines orchestrate articular damage: TNF-alpha and IL-6 promote synovial proliferation and osteoclastogenesis via RANKL induction. IL-23 stabilizes the Th17 phenotype, establishing the IL-23/IL-17 axis that underpins enthesitis and pathological new bone formation in spondyloarthropathies.

Genetic susceptibility across rheumatic diseases maps strongly to MHC loci: HLA-B27 confers a relative risk of over 40 for ankylosing spondylitis via peptide presentation, misfolding, or homodimer formation. Conversely, the shared epitope, a conserved pentapeptide sequence QKRAA or QRRAA in the HLA-DRB1 third hypervariable region, strongly predisposes to anti-CCP-positive rheumatoid arthritis.

Molecular mimicry occurs when microbial antigens share structural homology with self-peptides, triggering cross-reactive autoimmunity. The classic paradigm is group A streptococcal M protein evoking cross-reactive antibodies and cellular responses against cardiac myosin and basal ganglia in acute rheumatic fever.
