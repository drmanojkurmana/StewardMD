# Anti-CV2/CRMP5 Paraneoplastic Syndrome
Anti-CV2/CRMP5 Paraneoplastic Syndrome, Neurology / Paraneoplastic, Neurology / Oncology.
Aliases: Anti-CRMP5 syndrome; CV2 antibody syndrome.
# Pathophysiology
CRMP5 is a cytoplasmic phosphoprotein of the collapsin response mediator family involved in axon guidance and neurite outgrowth during development and re-expressed in a restricted pattern in the adult nervous system, including oligodendrocytes and neurons of the basal ganglia, retina and other regions. Ectopic expression by small cell lung carcinoma or thymoma triggers an onconeural immune response in which the antibody serves as a marker while cytotoxic T-cell mechanisms contribute to tissue injury. The wide anatomical distribution of the antigen explains the multifocal disease: basal-ganglia involvement produces chorea, optic nerve and retinal involvement produce visual loss, oligodendrocyte involvement contributes to demyelination, and cerebellar, spinal and peripheral-nerve involvement complete a broad clinical spectrum, accompanied by inflammatory infiltrates and neuronal and glial loss.
# Prognosis
The outcome is variable and generally guarded, but a subset of patients stabilises or partially improves with early tumour treatment and immunotherapy, more often than in anti-Hu or anti-Yo disease. Prognosis is heavily influenced by the underlying malignancy and any coexisting onconeural antibody.
# Clinical pearls
The CV2 antibody recognises CRMP5 (collapsin response mediator protein 5), which is expressed in adult oligodendrocytes and a subset of neurons.
It is most often associated with small cell lung carcinoma and thymoma.
The phenotype is strikingly heterogeneous, spanning encephalomyelitis, cerebellar ataxia, chorea, cranial neuropathy, myelopathy and a mixed sensorimotor peripheral neuropathy.
Distinctive features that point to CRMP5 include chorea and painless visual loss from optic neuritis or retinitis and uveitis.
The peripheral neuropathy may be demyelinating or axonal and can closely mimic an inflammatory neuropathy.
Anti-CV2/CRMP5 frequently coexists with anti-Hu or anti-amphiphysin, which broadens the clinical picture and the tumour association.
Compared with anti-Hu and anti-Yo disease, a subset of patients shows modest stabilisation or improvement with tumour treatment and immunotherapy.
Olfactory and taste disturbance and subtle visual symptoms can be early, easily overlooked clues.
# Additional differentials
Anti-Hu paraneoplastic encephalomyelitis, which may coexist
Huntington disease and other genetic or acquired chorea
Optic neuritis from multiple sclerosis or neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder
Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy or vasculitic neuropathy
Other causes of paraneoplastic or autoimmune retinopathy
