# CNS Tuberculosis (TB Meningitis)
CNS Tuberculosis (TB Meningitis), Central Nervous System / Tuberculosis.
# Pathogens
TB meningitis is caused by M. tuberculosis and is distinguished from bacterial meningitis by its subacute (days-to-weeks) evolution, basal meningeal involvement (often causing cranial nerve palsies and hydrocephalus), and characteristic CSF profile (lymphocytic pleocytosis, very low glucose, markedly elevated protein).
Very likely: Mycobacterium tuberculosis.
Red flags: Immediate empiric antibiotics required.
# Investigations
Lumbar puncture with CSF analysis, AFB smear, CBNAAT/Xpert, and culture
CSF in TB meningitis classically shows lymphocytic pleocytosis, very low glucose, and high protein; CBNAAT provides faster confirmation and rifampicin resistance status than culture.
MRI brain with contrast
Identifies basal meningeal enhancement, tuberculomas, infarcts, and hydrocephalus — all characteristic complications requiring specific management.
Chest X-ray, Concurrent or prior pulmonary TB is common and supports the diagnosis.
HIV testing, TB meningitis is disproportionately seen in HIV co-infection, which changes overall management and prognosis.
Immediate empiric antibiotics required
# Reason
Subacute fever with neck stiffness, altered sensorium or focal deficits, evolving over days to weeks rather than hours (distinguishing it from acute bacterial meningitis), raises strong suspicion for tuberculous meningitis. Unlike pulmonary TB, empiric antitubercular therapy should be started promptly on strong clinical suspicion WITHOUT waiting for microbiological confirmation, since the morbidity and mortality of delayed treatment in TB meningitis is severe and confirmatory tests (culture) take weeks.
