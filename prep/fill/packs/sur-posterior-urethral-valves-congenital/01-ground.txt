Posterior urethral valves, PUV, form the commonest severe congenital obstruction of the male lower tract, affecting 1 in 4000 to 8000 live male births. Redundant urethral mucosa valves the lumen, transmitting voiding pressures to the developing kidneys and bladder, so recognition begins at the first weak neonatal stream.

Antenatal ultrasound announces the disease through bilateral hydroureteronephrosis, a thick trabeculated bladder wall, oligohydramnios from curtailed fetal urine output, and the keyhole sign of a dilated posterior urethra ballooning beneath the bladder neck. The constellation prompts delivery planning near paediatric urology and nephrology services.

Young Type I valves, 95% of cases, comprise mucosal folds sweeping distally from the verumontanum toward the bulbar urethra, while Young Type III presents a transverse disc diaphragm with central perforation. Cystoscopic anatomy dictates technique: folds are incised along their length, discs are punctured centrally.

Neonatal decompression uses a soft 5F or 6F feeding tube passed gently per urethra, deliberately avoiding balloon catheters that traumatise the diminutive channel. Simultaneous correction of fluid, electrolytes, and acid-base derangement stabilises the infant for definitive surgery.

Definitive valve ablation proceeds transurethrally with cold knife, Bugbee electrocautery, or Holmium laser, dividing leaflets at the 5, 7, and 12 o'clock positions while preserving the sphincteric mechanism at six o'clock. Successful ablation restores gravity drainage within days.

VURD syndrome, valves with unilateral reflux into a dysplastic kidney, functions as a pressure pop-off: the sacrificed moiety vents intravesical hypertension, sparing the contralateral kidney superior long-term function compared with bilateral high-pressure exposure.

Valve bladder syndrome follows chronic obstruction with poor compliance, detrusor overactivity, and eventual myogenic failure, perpetuating upper tract dilatation despite an open outlet. Management spans urodynamic surveillance, pharmacotherapy, and clean intermittent catheterization through adolescence.

Prognosis condenses to nadir serum creatinine: values under 0.8 mg/dL, equivalent to 70 micromol/L, achieved within infancy predict favourable renal survival, while higher nadirs forecast progressive chronic kidney disease demanding transplant planning.
