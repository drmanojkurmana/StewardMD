# Osteoporosis and metabolic bone disease (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-metabolic-bone: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Osteoporosis is defined by the World Health Organization as a bone mineral density (BMD) T-score of -2.5 or lower at the lumbar spine, femoral neck, or total hip on dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA), or the presence of a low-trauma fragility fracture of the hip or vertebra regardless of BMD.

Glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis (GIOP) is the most frequent secondary cause of osteoporosis; bone loss occurs rapidly within the first 3 to 6 months of glucocorticoid therapy (prednisone >=2.5 to 5 mg/day) primarily through increased osteoclast survival and profound suppression of osteoblast function and osteocalcin synthesis.

The FRAX tool calculates the 10-year probability of a major osteoporotic fracture and hip fracture; the FRAX glucocorticoid adjustment increases fracture risk by approximately 15% to 20% for prednisone doses >7.5 mg daily.

Oral bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate) and intravenous zoledronic acid (5 mg annually) inhibit farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP) synthase in the mevalonate pathway, inducing osteoclast apoptosis and reducing vertebral and non-vertebral fracture rates; rare adverse events include atypical subtrochanteric femoral fractures and osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ).

Denosumab is a fully human IgG2 monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity to RANKL, preventing osteoclast formation and bone resorption; abrupt discontinuation triggers a rebound surge in osteoclastogenesis and rapid bone loss, with high risk of multiple rebound vertebral fractures unless immediately followed by bisphosphonate consolidation.

Anabolic osteoanabolic agents are prioritized for very high fracture risk or severe GIOP: teriparatide (recombinant human PTH 1-34) and abaloparatide (PTHrP analog) stimulate osteoblastic bone formation when administered intermittently daily, whereas romosozumab is a dual-action sclerostin inhibitor that simultaneously increases bone formation and decreases resorption.

Osteomalacia in adults results from defective bone matrix mineralization due to severe vitamin D deficiency, renal phosphate wasting (oncogenic osteomalacia mediated by FGF23), or hypophosphatasia; hallmarks include widespread bone pain, proximal muscle weakness, waddling gait, elevated alkaline phosphatase, and pseudofractures (Looser zones) on plain radiographs.

Paget disease of bone (osteitis deformans) is a focal disorder of accelerated, disorganized bone remodeling driven by hyperactive osteoclasts linked to SQSTM1 (p62) mutations; manifestations include localized bone pain, skeletal deformity (sabre shin, skull enlargement causing leontiasis ossea), hearing loss from cranial nerve compression, markedy elevated serum alkaline phosphatase, and a risk of secondary osteosarcoma in <1%.
