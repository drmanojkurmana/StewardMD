# Thin basement membrane nephropathy
Thin basement membrane nephropathy, Renal / Genetics, Nephrology / Clinical Genetics.
Aliases: COL4A3; COL4A4; TBMN; autosomal dominant Alport-spectrum carrier; benign familial haematuria; thin basement membrane; thin basement membrane disease.
# Pathophysiology
Thin basement membrane nephropathy is a disorder of the type IV collagen network that forms the glomerular basement membrane. Heterozygous variants in COL4A3 or COL4A4, which encode alpha chains of type IV collagen, produce a structurally thinner but largely functional basement membrane. The attenuated membrane is more permeable to red cells, giving rise to persistent glomerular (dysmorphic) microscopic haematuria, but in most people the filtration barrier otherwise functions normally so proteinuria, hypertension and renal impairment are absent or minimal. Because the same genes, when affected biallelically or with certain variants, cause autosomal recessive or dominant Alport syndrome, the condition represents the mild end of a continuous type IV collagen disease spectrum, and a minority of carriers show slow progression over decades.
# Prognosis
The great majority of individuals maintain normal renal function lifelong with isolated haematuria, but because COL4A3/COL4A4 carriers occasionally progress, ongoing surveillance for proteinuria, hypertension and renal decline is warranted.
# Clinical pearls
Thin basement membrane nephropathy typically causes persistent or intermittent isolated microscopic haematuria, often discovered incidentally and frequently familial
