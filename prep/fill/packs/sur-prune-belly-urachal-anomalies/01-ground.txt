Prune belly syndrome, Eagle-Barrett syndrome, unites the classic triad of deficient abdominal wall musculature with lax wrinkled skin, bilateral cryptorchidism with intra-abdominal gonads, and a dilated tortuous urinary tract in megacystis-megaureter configuration. The triad predominates in boys and heralds variable renal dysplasia requiring lifelong nephrologic follow-up.

Prostatic hypoplasia with a dilated posterior urethra typifies the syndrome, producing functional urinary stasis through poor propulsion rather than mechanical posterior urethral valves. Endoscopy confirms the absence of obstructing leaflets, redirecting care toward drainage and surveillance instead of ablation.

Definitive reconstruction combines Monfort plication abdominoplasty pleating the redundant wall, bilateral orchiopexy performed transabdominally to position both testes for examination and fertility, and selective ureteral tapering with reimplantation for aperistaltic segments. Single-stage infancy reconstruction is favoured where expertise allows.

Patent urachus represents 50% of congenital urachal anomalies: the allantoic duct fails entirely to obliterate, leaving umbilical urinary drainage of clear urine that wets the navel continuously. Imaging traces the channel to the bladder dome, and complete excision prevents recurrent infection.

Urachal cyst comprises 30%, both ends of the canal sealing while the midsegment stays patent, subsequently presenting as an infected infraumbilical mass below the navel. Management pairs antibiotics with total excision of the secretory lining.

Urachal sinus drains intermittently as purulent discharge from a blind umbilical tract aimed at but disconnected from the dome. Sinography maps the dead end, and full tract excision ends the cycle of flare and remission.

Urachal carcinoma, an aggressive adenocarcinoma of intestinal metaplasia at the bladder dome, accounts for under 0.5% of bladder cancers. Midline dome masses with mucoid urine in adults demand oncologic staging that excludes enteric and ovarian primaries.

Operative cure requires en bloc excision through partial cystectomy of the dome together with the urachus, median umbilical ligament, and umbilectomy, since retained tract epithelium harbours skip disease and recurrence.
