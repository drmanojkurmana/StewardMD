Genitourinary tuberculosis follows hematogenous spread of Mycobacterium tuberculosis from a healed or active pulmonary focus to the renal cortex, seeding glomerular and peritubular capillaries. Latency spans years while granulomas caseate silently, and reactivation ulcerates into collecting systems to seed the ureter and bladder downhill.

Sterile pyuria, leukocytes without routine bacterial growth, heralds urinary tuberculosis and mandates mycobacterial workup with smears, cultures, and nucleic acid testing across three morning specimens. Persistent pyuria despite antibacterials is tuberculosis until proven otherwise in endemic regions.

Ureteral disease carves the Kerr kink: transmural caseating granulomas stricture successive segments into a stepladder or corkscrew ureter, the rigid pipe-stem ureter of chronic urinary TB. Fibrotic fixation resists stenting long term, so definitive reconstruction awaits microbiologic cure.

Thimble bladder, a contracted bladder scarred below 50 mL to 100 mL capacity, ends medical management and begins surgical planning: enterocystoplasty augmentation with detubularized bowel restores storage once cure is confirmed, since grafting diseased unsterilized tissue fails.

Putty kidney, the cement kidney of complete autonephrectomy, describes total caseous replacement with calcification that devitalizes the organ while retaining bacilli. Symptomatic dead kidneys with pain, hypertension, or persistent infection undergo nephrectomy; silent calcified units are observed.

Standard cure uses a 6-month regimen of antitubercular therapy: 2 months of isoniazid, rifampin, pyrazinamide, and ethambutol, then 4 months of isoniazid with rifampin under direct observation. Shorter courses relapse, and drug resistance testing guides retreatment.

Fungal balls, Candida bezoars of Candida albicans, felt within the renal pelvis of diabetics and immunocompromised hosts, obstructing like soft calculi. Fungal urine with filling defects distinguishes bezoars from stones before instrumentation.

Obstructing bezoars require debulking by endourologic or open means, continuous amphotericin B irrigation at 50 mg/L in sterile water through nephrostomy, and systemic fluconazole for tissue-phase organisms, alongside correction of hyperglycaemia and immunosuppression. Follow-up imaging confirms cavity clearance before tubes are removed, and recurrent fungal colonization prompts prolonged azole suppression.
