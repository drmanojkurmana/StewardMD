# Giant cell (temporal) arteritis
Giant cell (temporal) arteritis, Rheumatology.
# Red flags
Visual loss is an emergency — do not delay steroids
# Investigations
ESR / CRP (markedly raised)
Temporal artery biopsy
Start high-dose steroids if suspected
# Pathophysiology
A panarteritis of medium- and large-sized arteries, classically branches of the carotid (especially the temporal artery) but systemically involving the aorta and its branches. Histology shows mononuclear inflammatory infiltrate within the vessel wall with frequent giant cell formation, intimal proliferation, and fragmentation of the internal elastic lamina; organ damage results from ischemia of involved vessels. It is considered an antigen-driven process: CD4+ T cells enter via the adventitial vasa vasorum, become activated, and drive macrophage differentiation, with restricted clonal T-cell expansion suggesting an arterial-wall antigen. Lesional T cells produce mainly IL-2 and IFN-gamma; both IFN-gamma-producing TH1 and IL-17-producing TH17 lineages contribute and may differ in glucocorticoid responsiveness. Associated with HLA-DR4 and HLA-DRB1*04 variants.
# Prognosis
Acute mortality directly from GCA is uncommon; when fatal it is usually due to cerebrovascular events or myocardial infarction. The main late mortality risk is rupture or dissection of an aortic aneurysm (18-fold increased thoracic aortic aneurysm risk). Disease is highly glucocorticoid-responsive but relapsing: symptom recurrence during steroid tapering occurs in 60-85% of patients, most require at least 2 years of treatment, and glucocorticoid toxicity affects 35-65% and is a major source of morbidity.
# Clinical pearls
Occurs almost exclusively in people over 50; mean age of onset around 70, with women making up roughly two-thirds of cases. Rare in Black patients; higher incidence in Scandinavian populations.
Four phenotypic patterns can occur together, in isolation, or at different times: cranial arteritis, polymyalgia rheumatica, large-vessel disease, and systemic inflammation.
Headache is the dominant cranial symptom; it is usually dull/boring rather than throbbing, may be unilateral or bilateral, is temporally located in only about half of patients, often worse at night, and aggravated by cold.
Scalp tenderness can be marked - brushing hair or resting the head on a pillow may be intolerable; the temporal (or less often occipital) artery may be tender, thickened, nodular, reddened or have overlying red skin streaks.
The temporal artery may pulsate early in disease but can become occluded later.
Jaw and tongue claudication and scalp pain are recognized cranial-ischemic features; rarer cranial ischemic events include stroke and scalp or tongue infarction.
ESR and/or CRP are elevated in most patients, but a normal ESR does NOT exclude giant cell arteritis.
Other supportive labs: normochromic or slightly hypochromic anemia, raised alkaline phosphatase (liver function abnormalities common), and increased IgG and complement.
Most patients report head or eye complaints before any visual loss, so attention to early cranial symptoms with prompt therapy reduces the risk of blindness.
Polymyalgia rheumatica overlaps strongly: PMR is seen in 40-50% of GCA patients, and 10-20% who present with isolated PMR later develop GCA - GCA and PMR are regarded as a spectrum of one disease process.
In PMR, creatine kinase is not elevated and rheumatoid factor / anti-CCP should be negative; a worsening peripheral arthritis pattern should prompt reconsideration of seronegative RA or other inflammatory arthropathy.
# Additional differentials
Migraine - common in the elderly (more prevalent than GCA) and may improve with prednisone, so a steroid response does not by itself confirm GCA.
Nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy - associated with a crowded 'disc-at-risk' / small optic cup, in which glucocorticoids should NOT be prescribed, contrasting with the arteritic form.
Isolated polymyalgia rheumatica without arteritis.
Other inflammatory causes of secondary headache such as sarcoidosis and Behcet's syndrome.
# Non infectious mimics
Seronegative rheumatoid arthritis or other inflammatory arthropathy mimicking the peripheral arthritis of PMR.
