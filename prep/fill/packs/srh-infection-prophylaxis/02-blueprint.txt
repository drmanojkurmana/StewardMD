Infection screening and vaccination in immunosuppression: TB screening with preventive regimens, hepatitis B screening with antivirals, PJP prophylaxis, live-vaccine bans, zoster and pneumococcal schedules, rituximab timing.

Screening for latent tuberculosis infection, called LTBI, with an interferon-gamma release assay, IGRA in the form of QuantiFERON-TB Gold Plus or T-SPOT.TB, plus a baseline chest radiograph, is mandatory prior to initiating any biologic DMARD or targeted synthetic DMARD such as a JAK inhibitor. A positive IGRA with a clean film means latent disease, while symptoms or shadows mean active tuberculosis needing full treatment first.

When latent tuberculosis infection is identified, targeted preventive therapy in one of three forms, oral rifampin 600 mg daily for 4 months, or once-weekly isoniazid 900 mg plus rifapentine 900 mg for 12 weeks, or isoniazid 300 mg daily for 9 months with pyridoxine cover against neuropathy, must be initiated, with biologic initiation delayed for at least 1 month after starting therapy. Liver tests run through every regimen.

Serological screening for Hepatitis B virus with HBsAg, anti-HBc total, and anti-HBs is mandatory prior to immunosuppressive therapy; in patients who are HBsAg-positive or anti-HBc-positive receiving B-cell depleting therapy with rituximab, prophylactic antiviral therapy with entecavir or tenofovir must be started before rituximab and continued for at least 12 to 18 months post-depletion, because reactivation kills. Hepatitis C and HIV screens ride along.

Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia, called PJP chemoprophylaxis, with oral trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole as one single-strength tablet daily or one double-strength tablet three times weekly is indicated for patients receiving high-dose glucocorticoids with prednisone at or above 20 mg daily for at least 4 to 8 weeks combined with another immunosuppressant, or cyclophosphamide therapy. Dapsone or atovaquone covers sulfa allergy.

Live-attenuated vaccines including measles-mumps-rubella (MMR), varicella, yellow fever, oral polio, and live attenuated zoster are strictly contraindicated in patients receiving biologic agents, targeted synthetic DMARDs, or high-dose systemic glucocorticoids due to the risk of uncontrolled vaccine-strain viral replication. Household contacts take inactivated polio instead of oral drops.

Recombinant zoster vaccine, called RZV and sold as Shingrix, is an adjuvanted non-live subunit vaccine that is strongly recommended for all patients aged at or above 50 years, and for immunocompromised patients aged at or above 18 years, administered as a two-dose intramuscular series 2 to 6 months apart, safe to administer during immunosuppressive therapy. Post-herpetic neuralgia prevention is the prize.

Pneumococcal vaccination is strongly recommended for all patients on immunosuppression using the 20-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine (PCV20) alone, or the 15-valent conjugate vaccine (PCV15) followed at least 8 weeks later by the 23-valent polysaccharide vaccine (PPSV23), administered prior to immunosuppression whenever feasible. One PCV20 shot simplifies the schedule.

B-cell depleting therapy with rituximab markedly blunts humoral immune responses to vaccines; non-live vaccines should ideally be completed at least 2 to 4 weeks prior to rituximab infusion, or delayed until at least 5 to 6 months after the last rituximab dose when peripheral B cells begin reconstituting. Vaccination calendars are therefore drawn around infusion dates.
