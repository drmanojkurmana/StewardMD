Gallstones announce themselves differently by location. Asymptomatic stones need no treatment with under 2 percent yearly biliary pain risk, while biliary colic with wall thickening over 3 mm and a Murphy sign marks acute cholecystitis needing admission.

Tokyo grades decide urgency. Tokyo grading spans mild localised disease through moderate with palpable mass or symptom duration over 72 hours to severe with organ dysfunction, deciding between early surgery and drainage.

Speed protects the ducts. Early laparoscopic cholecystectomy within 72 hours of cholecystitis onset beats delayed surgery for complications and stay, with the critical view of safety exposing two structures entering the gallbladder before any clip is applied.

Ductal stones need clearance. Common bile duct stones with cholangitis, jaundice or dilated duct over 6 mm need preoperative ERCP clearance, while single-stage laparoscopic bile duct exploration suits fit patients with large stones and a roomy duct.

Mirizzi squeezes from outside. Mirizzi syndrome compresses the common hepatic duct from an impacted cystic duct stone, classified Csendes I to V up to cholecystobiliary fistula needing hepaticojejunostomy rather than simple cholecystectomy.

Bouveret blocks the outlet. Bouveret syndrome with gastric outlet obstruction from a choledochoduodenal fistula stone over 2.5 cm needs enterolithotomy first, with cholecystectomy deferred to a second stage once inflammation settles.

Polyps have size rules. Gallbladder polyps over 10 mm, sessile morphology or primary sclerosing cholangitis background need cholecystectomy because of malignant potential, while small mobile polyps suit ultrasound surveillance.

Drainage bridges the unfit. Percutaneous cholecystostomy bridges severe cholecystitis in unfit patients, with interval cholecystectomy at 6 to 8 weeks once sepsis resolves and fitness improves for anaesthesia.
