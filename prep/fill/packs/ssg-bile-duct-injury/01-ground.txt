Strasberg grades the injury. Strasberg grades injury A to E from cystic stump leak through lateral injury to transection with loss of confluence, and type E with excised segment length decides the repair level.

Bismuth maps stricture height. Bismuth-Corlette types I to V locate strictures from distal stump over 2 cm to hilar separation, with type V adding cystic duct injury, guiding the height of hepaticojejunostomy.

Prevention beats repair. Critical view of safety with two structures, Rouviere sulcus landmark and intraoperative cholangiogram in unclear anatomy prevents most major injuries.

Hepp-Couinaud drains high strictures. Hepp-Couinaud technique opens the left hepatic duct along the hilar plate for a wide side-to-side hepaticojejunostomy with 60 to 70 cm Roux limb to prevent reflux cholangitis.

Timing follows physiology. Early repair within 72 hours by a hepatobiliary surgeon matches delayed repair results, while sepsis and inflammation favour drainage first with definitive repair at 6 weeks.

Leaks grade by bilirubin. Postoperative bile leak with drain bilirubin over 3 times serum on day 3 is ISGLS grade A when self-limiting and grade B or C when needing ERCP stenting or reoperation.

Arteries bleed with ducts. Vascular injury with right hepatic artery damage accompanies 30 percent of major bile duct injuries and needs CT angiography because ischaemia threatens any repair.

Watch repairs for life. Long-term stricture surveillance with liver function and MRCP detects secondary biliary cirrhosis, and redo hepaticojejunostomy suits failed repairs before transplantation.
