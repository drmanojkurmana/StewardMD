# Mediastinal teratoma
Mediastinal teratoma, Respiratory / Thoracic, Respiratory / Thoracic Surgery.
Aliases: mature mediastinal teratoma; mediastinal dermoid cyst; benign mediastinal germ cell tumour.
# Pathophysiology
Mediastinal teratomas are thought to arise from primordial germ cells that fail to complete their migration and become mislocated within the mediastinum during embryogenesis. These pluripotent cells give rise to tissues derived from all three germ layers, so the resulting mass typically contains a mixture of ectodermal skin and hair, mesodermal fat and cartilage, and endodermal glandular elements, accounting for the characteristic heterogeneous imaging appearance with fat and calcification. Mature teratomas are well differentiated and benign, but tumours containing immature tissue or other malignant germ cell components behave more aggressively and may secrete tumour markers.
# Prognosis
Completely resected mature teratomas have an excellent prognosis and are effectively cured, whereas immature or malignant germ cell tumours require multimodality treatment and carry a more guarded outlook.
# Clinical pearls
The commonest germ cell tumour of the anterior mediastinum, usually a mature (benign) lesion containing tissues from more than one germ layer
Sits within the classic anterior-mediastinal 'four Ts' differential alongside thymoma, thyroid masses, and 'terrible' lymphoma
Often asymptomatic and found incidentally, but may cause chest pain, cough, or breathlessness through mass effect
Cross-sectional imaging typically shows a heterogeneous mass containing fat, fluid, soft tissue, and calcification, sometimes with a recognisable tooth
Coughing up hair or sebaceous material (trichoptysis) is a rare but essentially diagnostic feature when a teratoma erodes into an airway
Mature teratomas are benign, whereas immature or malignant germ cell elements alter management and prognosis
Complete surgical resection is both diagnostic and curative for mature lesions
# Additional differentials
Thymoma or thymic carcinoma
Malignant mediastinal germ cell tumour (seminomatous or non-seminomatous)
Lymphoma involving the anterior mediastinum
Substernal (retrosternal) thyroid goitre
Thymic cyst or other foregut cyst
Bronchogenic cyst
# Additional investigations
Contrast-enhanced CT of the thorax showing fat, fluid, and calcific components
MRI to further characterise tissue components and delineate adjacent structures
Serum alpha-fetoprotein and beta-human chorionic gonadotrophin to help exclude malignant germ cell elements
