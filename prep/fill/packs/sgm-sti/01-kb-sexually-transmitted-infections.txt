# Sexually transmitted infections: treatment and partner management (adult)
Adults
Outpatient, STI clinic, emergency department
Test and treat at the same visit: gonorrhoea with ceftriaxone 500 mg IM (1 g if 150 kg or more), chlamydia with doxycycline for 7 days, syphilis with benzathine penicillin by stage, trichomonas with metronidazole, and PID with a 14-day triple regimen, plus HIV testing and partner treatment.
Aliases: STI; STD; PID; pelvic inflammatory disease; urethral discharge; vaginal discharge; genital ulcer; syndromic management; EPT; NGU; bacterial vaginosis; LGV.
# Syndromes (WHO syndromic approach)
Urethral discharge or dysuria in men: treat for gonorrhoea and chlamydia at the first visit, testing by NAAT where available.
Vaginal discharge: usually bacterial vaginosis, candida or trichomonas; consider cervicitis (gonorrhoea, chlamydia) when risk factors or mucopurulent cervical discharge are present.
Genital ulcer: syphilis and herpes are the commonest causes; chancroid and lymphogranuloma venereum (LGV) occur in some regions. When same-day testing is not available, treat syphilis and herpes presumptively.
Lower abdominal pain in a sexually active woman: PID if there is cervical motion, uterine or adnexal tenderness. Exclude ectopic pregnancy and surgical causes.
Anorectal pain, discharge or bleeding after receptive anal sex: gonorrhoea, chlamydia (including LGV), herpes or syphilis.
# Investigations
NAAT for gonorrhoea and chlamydia: first-void urine (men), vaginal swab (women), plus rectal and pharyngeal swabs by exposure. Gonococcal culture with susceptibility if treatment fails.
Urethritis is confirmed by visible discharge, 2 or more WBC per oil immersion field on Gram stain, or a positive leucocyte esterase or 10 or more WBC per high-power field in first-void urine.
Syphilis: treponemal test plus a non-treponemal test (RPR or VDRL); record the RPR titre on the day of treatment. Dual treponemal and non-treponemal rapid tests allow same-day treatment.
Trichomonas NAAT (wet mount is less sensitive); bacterial vaginosis by Amsel criteria or Gram stain; herpes PCR of any ulcer.
HIV test for everyone; hepatitis B and C by risk; pregnancy test in women.
# Gonorrhoea, chlamydia and urethritis
Uncomplicated gonorrhoea (urethra, cervix, rectum, pharynx): ceftriaxone 500 mg IM single dose, or 1 g if weight is 150 kg or more (CDC 2021). WHO (2024) recommends ceftriaxone 1 g IM for all.
Add doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 7 days if chlamydia has not been excluded.
Cephalosporin allergy: gentamicin 240 mg IM + azithromycin 2 g orally, single doses. Ceftriaxone unavailable: cefixime 800 mg orally single dose with a test of cure. Neither is reliable for pharyngeal gonorrhoea.
Chlamydia: doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 7 days. Alternatives: azithromycin 1 g orally single dose, or levofloxacin 500 mg orally once daily for 7 days.
Non-gonococcal urethritis or cervicitis: doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 7 days. Persistent urethritis (Mycoplasma genitalium, no resistance testing): doxycycline for 7 days then moxifloxacin 400 mg once daily for 7 days.
LGV: doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 21 days. Chancroid: azithromycin 1 g orally single dose or ceftriaxone 250 mg IM single dose.
# Syphilis
Primary, secondary or early latent (acquired within 1 year): benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM single dose.
Late latent or unknown duration: benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM weekly for 3 weeks (7.2 million units). Dose interval 7 to 9 days preferred; 10 to 14 days may be acceptable outside pregnancy, otherwise restart the course.
Neurosyphilis, ocular or otosyphilis: aqueous crystalline penicillin G 18 to 24 million units/day (3 to 4 million units IV 4-hourly or continuous infusion) for 10 to 14 days. Alternative: procaine penicillin 2.4 million units IM daily + probenecid 500 mg orally four times daily for 10 to 14 days.
Any neurological, eye or hearing symptoms: examine CSF and arrange urgent ophthalmology or ENT review.
Penicillin allergy (not pregnant): early syphilis doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 14 days (or ceftriaxone 1 g IM or IV daily for 10 days); late latent doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 28 days. Azithromycin is not recommended (resistance).
Warn about the Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction (fever, headache, myalgia within 24 hours of treatment); treat with antipyretics. It is not penicillin allergy.
Repeat the non-treponemal titre at 6 and 12 months. Failure: a sustained fourfold rise, or failure to fall fourfold within 12 months; re-evaluate (including HIV and CSF) and retreat.
# Vaginal infections and PID
Trichomonas: women metronidazole 500 mg orally twice daily for 7 days; men metronidazole 2 g orally single dose. Alternative: tinidazole 2 g single dose (avoid in pregnancy). Treat all current partners.
Bacterial vaginosis: metronidazole 500 mg orally twice daily for 7 days, or metronidazole gel 0.75% 5 g intravaginally daily for 5 days, or clindamycin 2% cream 5 g intravaginally at bedtime for 7 days. Partner treatment is not recommended; alcohol avoidance is unnecessary (CDC).
PID outpatient: ceftriaxone 500 mg IM single dose + doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 14 days + metronidazole 500 mg orally twice daily for 14 days.
PID inpatient: ceftriaxone 1 g IV every 24 hours + doxycycline 100 mg orally or IV every 12 hours + metronidazole 500 mg orally or IV every 12 hours; switch to oral after 24 to 48 hours of improvement to complete 14 days.
PID alternative: clindamycin 900 mg IV 8-hourly + gentamicin 2 mg/kg IV load then 1.5 mg/kg 8-hourly (or 3 to 5 mg/kg once daily), then oral doxycycline or clindamycin to complete 14 days.
Admit PID if a surgical emergency cannot be excluded, tubo-ovarian abscess, pregnancy, severe illness, vomiting, temperature above 38.5°C, or no response to oral therapy within 72 hours. An IUD need not be removed.
# Partners, follow-up and prevention
Gonorrhoea, chlamydia and PID: evaluate, test and treat all partners from the 60 days before symptoms or diagnosis, and the most recent partner even if earlier.
Syphilis: partners exposed within 90 days before a diagnosis of primary, secondary or early latent syphilis should be treated presumptively even if seronegative.
Expedited partner therapy where legally allowed: gonorrhoea cefixime 800 mg orally single dose (add doxycycline if chlamydia is not excluded); chlamydia doxycycline.
Abstain from sex for 7 days after single-dose treatment or until a 7-day course is finished and symptoms have resolved, and until partners are treated.
Retest for gonorrhoea, chlamydia and trichomonas (women) about 3 months after treatment. Test of cure 7 to 14 days after treatment for pharyngeal gonorrhoea, and about 4 weeks after chlamydia treatment in pregnancy.
Offer HIV PrEP where indicated, hepatitis B and HPV vaccination, and condoms.
# Pregnancy, HIV and India
Pregnancy: chlamydia azithromycin 1 g single dose (amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily for 7 days as alternative); avoid doxycycline, fluoroquinolones and tinidazole.
Syphilis in pregnancy: penicillin only; desensitise if allergic. For early syphilis a second 2.4 million unit dose 1 week later may be given. In late latent syphilis a gap of more than 9 days between doses means restarting the course.
People with HIV receive the same regimens, except that women with HIV and trichomonas need the 7-day metronidazole course.
India: NACO designated STI/RTI clinics use colour-coded, pre-packed syndromic treatment kits whose regimens differ in places from CDC and WHO; follow the current national kit when working within the programme.
# Pitfalls
Treating gonorrhoea with azithromycin alone or with ciprofloxacin: resistance is widespread.
Missing pharyngeal and rectal sites by testing urine only.
Treating syphilis without recording the baseline RPR titre, making follow-up uninterpretable.
Using non-penicillin syphilis regimens in pregnancy.
Diagnosing PID without a pregnancy test and missing ectopic pregnancy.
Treating the index patient but not the partner, leading to reinfection.
# Drugs
Ceftriaxone
# Dose
Gonorrhoea 500 mg IM single dose (1 g if 150 kg or more); PID inpatient 1 g IV every 24 hours
WHO 2024 uses 1 g IM for gonorrhoea. Chancroid 250 mg IM single dose.
Doxycycline
# Dose
100 mg orally twice daily: 7 days (chlamydia, NGU), 14 days (PID, early syphilis if penicillin allergic), 21 days (LGV), 28 days (late syphilis if allergic)

Azithromycin
# Dose
1 g orally single dose (chlamydia in pregnancy, chancroid); 2 g single dose with gentamicin for gonorrhoea if cephalosporin allergic
Not for gonorrhoea monotherapy or syphilis.

# Dose
2.4 million units IM single dose (early syphilis); weekly for 3 weeks (late latent or unknown duration)

# Dose

Neurosyphilis, ocular and otosyphilis.

# Dose
500 mg orally twice daily for 7 days (trichomonas in women, BV) or 14 days (PID); 2 g single dose (trichomonas in men)
