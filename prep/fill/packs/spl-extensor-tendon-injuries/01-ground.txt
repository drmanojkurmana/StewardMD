Extensor tendon injuries are mapped by nine anatomical zones running from fingertip to forearm, and each zone predicts both the deformity and the repair. Zone I lies over the DIP joint, Zone II over the middle phalanx, Zone III over the PIP joint, Zone IV over the proximal phalanx, Zone V over the MCP joint, Zone VI over the metacarpal dorsum, Zone VII under the carpal retinaculum, Zone VIII over the distal forearm, and Zone IX within the muscle belly itself.

Zone I failure gives the classic drooping fingertip. Mallet finger means the extensor tendon has ruptured or avulsed a bone flake from the distal phalanx base, so the DIP joint falls into involuntary flexion that the patient cannot actively straighten.

Closed mallet injuries heal without surgery if extension is never lost. Continuous uninterrupted DIP joint extension splinting for 6 to 8 weeks lets the tendon reattach, followed by 4 weeks of night splinting while daytime motion resumes. A single episode of flexion during the day splinting period restarts the clock.

Zone III holds the central slip, and its rupture unbalances the whole extensor hood. Boutonniere deformity follows, with the PIP joint sinking into flexion while the DIP joint compensates into hyperextension as the lateral bands slide volar to the PIP axis.

The Elson test proves central slip integrity at the bedside. The patient flexes the PIP joint 90 degrees over a table edge and tries to extend the DIP joint against resistance; weak PIP extension with a rigid DIP confirms the central slip has ruptured and the lateral bands are doing all the work.

Swan-neck deformity is the mirror image, pairing PIP joint hyperextension with DIP joint flexion. It arises when the lateral bands sublux dorsally, when the volar plate turns lax, or when the terminal extensor tendon ruptures and frees the DIP joint.

At the knuckle, the sagittal band centers the extensor over the metacarpal head. Zone V rupture classically strikes the middle finger extensor digitorum communis tendon, letting it sublux into the ulnar intermetacarpal groove so the knuckle cannot extend and the tendon visibly snaps sideways on flexion.

Zone VII passes beneath the extensor retinaculum, which divides the dorsum into six fibro-osseous compartments walled by vertical septa.
