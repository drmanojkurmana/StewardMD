# Rheumatoid arthritis pathogenesis and diagnosis (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-ra-pathogenesis: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Genetic susceptibility to rheumatoid arthritis maps predominantly to the shared epitope, a conserved pentapeptide motif (QKRAA, QRRAA, or RRRAA) at positions 70 to 74 in the third hypervariable region of the HLA-DRB1 beta chain; gene-environment interaction with cigarette smoking dramatically multiplies the risk of developing anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA).

Peptidylarginine deiminase enzymes (PAD2 and PAD4), upregulated in inflamed airways and periodontitis (associated with Porphyromonas gingivalis), catalyze the calcium-dependent post-translational conversion of positively charged peptidylarginine into neutral citrulline, generating novel neoepitopes recognized by high-affinity ACPA.

The 2010 ACR/EULAR classification criteria for rheumatoid arthritis apply to patients with at least 1 joint with definite clinical synovitis not explained by another disease; a score of 6 or more out of 10 points classifies definite RA based on joint distribution (up to 5 points), serology (RF and ACPA, up to 3 points), symptom duration at least 6 weeks (1 point), and acute-phase reactants (1 point).

The rheumatoid synovial pannus is an invasive, hyperplastic, macrophage- and fibroblast-rich tissue that attaches to articular cartilage and bone; fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS) secrete high levels of matrix metalloproteinases (MMP-1, MMP-3, MMP-13) and cathepsins that degrade type II collagen and aggrecan.

Osteoclastogenesis at the cartilage-pannus junction is driven by receptor activator of nuclear factor-kappa B ligand (RANKL), abundantly expressed by synovial T cells and activated FLS; binding to RANK on myeloid precursor cells stimulates osteoclast differentiation and drives subchondral marginal bone erosions.

Clinical hallmarks of early rheumatoid arthritis include symmetrical inflammatory polyarthritis predominantly involving the metacarpophalangeal (MCP), proximal interphalangeal (PIP), and metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joints, accompanied by prominent inflammatory morning stiffness lasting at least 30 to 60 minutes, with characteristic sparing of distal interphalangeal (DIP) joints and the thoracolumbar spine.

Advanced untreated rheumatoid arthritis produces characteristic tendon and joint deformities: ulnar deviation of the fingers at MCP joints, swan-neck deformity (hyperextension of PIP with flexion of DIP), boutonniere deformity (flexion of PIP with hyperextension of DIP), and hitchhiker thumb (Z-deformity).

Anti-carbamylated protein (anti-CarP) antibodies and anti-acetylated protein antibodies represent emerging post-translational modification autoantibodies in rheumatoid arthritis that identify severe erosive disease and poor radiographic outcomes, including in seronegative patients who lack RF and ACPA.
