# Neurological manifestations of rheumatic disease (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-neuro-rheum: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Neuropsychiatric systemic lupus erythematosus (NPSLE) classification encompasses 19 case definitions by ACR; diffuse psychiatric/cognitive manifestations are linked with anti-ribosomal P antibodies, while lupus psychosis correlates with CSF pleocytosis/elevated IgG index.

Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) co-occurs with SLE and Sjögren syndrome; mediated by pathogenic IgG antibodies targeting aquaporin-4 (AQP4) water channels on astrocyte foot processes, causing longitudinally extensive transverse myelitis (LETM >= 3 vertebral segments).

Primary angiitis of the central nervous system (PACNS) is restricted to brain/spinal leptomeningeal parenchymal arteries <500 µm, diagnosed definitively by high-resolution vessel wall MRI and stereotactic brain-leptomeningeal biopsy showing granulomatous or lymphocytic vasculitis.

Mononeuritis multiplex (asymmetric axonal neuropathy of >= 2 non-contiguous nerve trunks) is the neurologic hallmark of necrotizing systemic vasculitis (PAN, EGPA, GPA) caused by ischemic necrosis from epineurial arteriole necrotizing vasculitis.

Neuro-Behçet disease manifests parenchymally in 80% (brainstem-diencephalic syndrome with pyramidal/cerebellar signs) and non-parenchymally in 20% (cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, CVST); parenchymal disease is treated with high-dose steroids and anti-TNF or azathioprine.

Neurosarcoidosis exhibits predilection for basal meninges, producing cranial neuropathy VII (unilateral or bilateral Bell palsy in 50%), diabetes insipidus from hypothalamic-pituitary granulomas, and leptomeningeal enhancement on post-gadolinium MRI.

Central nervous system pachymeningitis (diffuse dural thickening with contrast enhancement sparing leptomeninges) is a characteristic complication of IgG4-related disease and ANCA-associated vasculitis (predominantly PR3-positive GPA).

Cervical myelopathy from atlantoaxial subluxation (AAS) in rheumatoid arthritis develops when pannus erodes the transverse odontoid ligament, producing anterior atlanto-dens interval (ADI) >3 mm in adults (>9 mm signals impending medullary compression).
