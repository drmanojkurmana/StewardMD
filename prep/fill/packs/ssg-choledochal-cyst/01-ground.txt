Todani types I to V span fusiform extrahepatic dilation, saccular diverticulum, choledochocele, intrahepatic Caroli disease and combined disease, with type I commonest. Classification guides excision against endoscopic therapy.

Anomalous pancreaticobiliary duct junction with a long common channel over 15 mm lets pancreatic juice reflux into bile, driving cyst formation and gallbladder cancer. The reflux theory explains cancer without stones.

Complete cyst excision with Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy is the standard for types I and IV, because retained cyst carries up to 30 percent lifetime cholangiocarcinoma risk. Mucosectomy at the pancreatic end prevents remnant cancer.

Type III choledochocele gets endoscopic sphincterotomy rather than excision, while Caroli disease confined to one lobe gets hepatectomy and diffuse disease needs transplantation. Drainage procedures are abandoned for cancer risk.

Childhood presentation with the triad of pain, jaundice and a palpable mass is classic but seen in under 20 percent, and most children present with pancreatitis or cholangitis. Antenatal ultrasound increasingly detects cysts.

Intraoperative cholangiogram and biopsy of suspicious areas detect synchronous cancer in 10 percent of adult cysts, mandating frozen section before reconstruction. Thickened walls and nodules are sampled.

Post-excision surveillance with annual liver function and MRCP detects anastomotic stricture and remnant intrapancreatic cyst malignancy lifelong. Intrahepatic stones form above strictures.

Gallbladder cancer coexists with maljunction without stones, so prophylactic cholecystectomy is advised when the junction is anomalous even without a cyst. Family screening follows paediatric cases. MRCP maps the whole biliary tree without invasion, replacing diagnostic ERCP in children. Pregnancy with a known cyst favours planned delivery with obstetrics before elective excision. Adults with type IVa disease need bilateral ductoplasty to both lobes, since isolated hilar excision stenoses the contralateral ducts. Laparoscopic excision with Roux reconstruction matches open results in experienced centres. Percutaneous drainage temporises cholangitis before definitive excision in frail adults. Robotic excision offers wristed suturing for the hepaticojejunostomy in deep hila.
