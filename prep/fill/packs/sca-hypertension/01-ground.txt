Exam notes for systemic hypertension, written for grounding, not for display.
Primary aldosteronism, including Conn syndrome from an aldosterone producing adenoma, is a common secondary cause and is found in about 5% to 10% of people with hypertension. Screening divides aldosterone by renin. A result over 20, or over 30, in the assay's own units, together with a frankly high aldosterone, is only a screen. Confirm with a salt loading test or a fludrocortisone suppression test before labelling the disease. The adenoma sits in the adrenal cortex and drives sodium retention with potassium loss.
Fibromuscular dysplasia causes renal artery stenosis in younger women and the classic angiogram is a string of beads. Atherosclerotic renal artery stenosis is the other pattern, usually in older patients with widespread atheroma. Either can drive renovascular hypertension.
Office targets differ by risk. In high cardiovascular risk, a systolic pressure near 120 mmHg is used when the patient tolerates it. In diabetes the usual target is below 130/80 mmHg. Frail or elderly patients need slower lowering and a check for orthostatic hypotension. These three situations are the point of the target figure.
Hypertensive emergency means severe hypertension, above 180/120 mmHg, plus acute organ damage such as encephalopathy, pulmonary oedema, acute coronary ischaemia or acute kidney injury. Intravenous labetalol, nicardipine or nitroprusside is used in a monitored bed. The usual first hour target is a fall in mean arterial pressure, MAP, of no more than 20% to 25%. Aortic dissection, acute ischaemic stroke and phaeochromocytoma use different targets, so they are exceptions to that 20% to 25% rule.
Resistant hypertension means the pressure stays above target on 3 antihypertensive drugs of different classes at full tolerated doses, and one of those drugs is a diuretic. Standard triple therapy pairs a RAS inhibitor with a dihydropyridine calcium blocker and a thiazide-like diuretic. Spironolactone 25 to 50 mg daily is the preferred fourth line agent. PATHWAY-2 showed that this mineralocorticoid antagonist beat other add on drugs. Call it drug 4 in the sequence.
Catheter based renal sympathetic denervation delivers radiofrequency energy, or focused ultrasound, to afferent and efferent nerves along the main renal artery and its branches. It is considered when blood pressure stays high after a proper tablet regimen, including spironolactone when it is safe.
Phaeochromocytoma and paraganglioma release catecholamines. The picture is paroxysmal hypertension with headache, sweating and tachycardia. Diagnose with plasma free metanephrines or a 24 hour urine collection for fractionated metanephrines. Prepare with alpha blockade before any beta blocker.
Drug names: labetalol nicardipine nitroprusside spironolactone fludrocortisone.
