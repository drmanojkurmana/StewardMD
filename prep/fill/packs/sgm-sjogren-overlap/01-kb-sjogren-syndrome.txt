# Sjögren syndrome
Sjögren syndrome, Rheumatology / MSK, Rheumatology.
Aliases: SS; sicca syndrome; Sjogren's syndrome; autoimmune epithelitis.
# Pathophysiology
Sjögren syndrome is a chronic autoimmune exocrinopathy in which lymphocytic (mainly CD4 T-cell and B-cell) infiltration of the exocrine glands destroys secretory epithelium, sometimes termed autoimmune epithelitis. B-cell hyperactivity produces autoantibodies (anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB) and hypergammaglobulinaemia, and sustained clonal B-cell proliferation underlies the elevated lymphoma risk. Type I interferon signalling and genetic HLA associations contribute to disease susceptibility. Extraglandular disease reflects immune complex deposition and lymphocytic infiltration of organs including skin, joints, lungs, kidneys, and nervous system.
# Prognosis
Most patients have a good prognosis with a slowly progressive course dominated by sicca symptoms, but systemic involvement and, importantly, lymphoma development can worsen outcomes. Lifelong monitoring for lymphoproliferative disease is warranted.
# Clinical pearls
Characterised by dry eyes (keratoconjunctivitis sicca) and dry mouth (xerostomia) from immune-mediated destruction of lacrimal and salivary glands
Predominantly affects middle-aged women and may be primary or secondary to another connective tissue disease such as rheumatoid arthritis or lupus
Systemic features include fatigue, arthralgia, Raynaud phenomenon, parotid gland swelling, and cutaneous vasculitis
Anti-Ro/SSA and anti-La/SSB antibodies are characteristic, along with positive antinuclear antibody and rheumatoid factor
Objective sicca can be confirmed with Schirmer test, ocular surface staining, and unstimulated salivary flow; labial minor salivary gland biopsy shows focal lymphocytic sialadenitis
There is a markedly increased lifetime risk of B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma, particularly MALT lymphoma
Anti-Ro antibodies in pregnancy carry a risk of neonatal lupus and congenital heart block
Management centres on symptomatic relief of dryness (tear and saliva substitutes, secretagogues) with immunosuppression reserved for significant systemic involvement
# Additional differentials
Age-related or medication-induced sicca (anticholinergics)
Sarcoidosis and IgG4-related disease affecting salivary glands
Chronic viral infection (hepatitis C, HIV)
Radiation-induced glandular damage
Other connective tissue diseases with secondary sicca
Amyloidosis
# Additional investigations
Anti-Ro/SSA and anti-La/SSB antibodies, ANA, and rheumatoid factor
Schirmer test and ocular surface staining for aqueous tear deficiency
Unstimulated whole salivary flow and salivary gland ultrasound or imaging
Labial minor salivary gland biopsy showing focal lymphocytic sialadenitis
Serum immunoglobulins and protein electrophoresis; monitoring for lymphoma if persistent gland enlargement or new lymphadenopathy
# Red flags
Persistent unilateral parotid enlargement, lymphadenopathy, or new cytopenias suggesting lymphoma transformation
Signs of systemic vasculitis, mononeuritis multiplex, or interstitial lung disease
Renal involvement such as renal tubular acidosis or interstitial nephritis
Pregnancy with anti-Ro antibodies given the risk of fetal congenital heart block
Severe corneal complications from untreated dry eye
# Pitfalls
Dismissing sicca symptoms as trivial and missing systemic or lymphomatous complications
Failing to consider mimics such as IgG4-related disease, sarcoidosis, or viral infection
Not counselling and monitoring anti-Ro-positive women in pregnancy for fetal heart block
Overlooking increased dental caries and ocular surface damage from chronic dryness
Attributing new persistent gland swelling to disease flare rather than investigating for lymphoma
# Management
Treat keratoconjunctivitis sicca with artificial tears, topical ciclosporin or lifitegrast and punctal occlusion, and xerostomia with saliva substitutes and secretagogues such as pilocarpine or cevimeline
Provide preventive dental and oral care with topical fluoride and antifungal treatment of oral candidiasis
Use hydroxychloroquine for arthralgia, fatigue and cutaneous features, adding corticosteroids and immunosuppressants such as methotrexate, azathioprine or mycophenolate, or rituximab, for significant extraglandular organ disease
Maintain lifelong surveillance for B-cell non-Hodgkin and MALT lymphoma, evaluating persistent gland enlargement, new lymphadenopathy, cryoglobulinaemia or falling complement
Advise environmental humidification and avoidance of anticholinergic and drying medications
Coordinate multidisciplinary care with rheumatology, ophthalmology and dentistry, and in pregnancy monitor anti-Ro and anti-La antibodies for neonatal lupus and congenital heart block
