# Malaria: uncomplicated and severe (India)
Adults and children
Outpatient, emergency department, ward, ICU
Confirm every case by microscopy or RDT. Vivax: chloroquine 3 days plus primaquine 0.25 mg/kg for 14 days. Falciparum: ACT-AL in the North-East, ACT-SP elsewhere, plus single-dose primaquine. Severe malaria: IV artesunate 2.4 mg/kg (3 mg/kg under 20 kg) at 0, 12 and 24 hours, then daily.
Aliases: P. vivax; P. falciparum; Pv; Pf; mixed malaria; severe malaria; cerebral malaria; ACT; artemether lumefantrine; NVBDCP; NCVBDC drug policy.
# Recognise and confirm
Test every fever in an endemic area or after travel with microscopy or a bivalent RDT before treating; treat by species. If no test is available, give full-dose chloroquine and complete species-specific treatment when the result arrives.
Pf HRP-2 RDTs can stay positive for up to 3 weeks after successful treatment; confirm suspected recrudescence with microscopy.
Severe malaria (any one, with parasitaemia): impaired consciousness or coma, repeated generalised convulsions, creatinine above 3 mg/dL, bilirubin above 3 mg/dL, Hb below 5 g/dL, pulmonary oedema or ARDS, glucose below 40 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L).
Also severe: metabolic acidosis, shock (SBP below 80 mmHg in adults, below 50 mmHg in children), abnormal bleeding or DIC, haemoglobinuria, temperature above 42°C, parasitaemia above 5% of red cells.
Severe malaria can be smear-negative because of sequestration or partial treatment: repeat smear or RDT, and treat as severe malaria if the picture fits and there is no other explanation.
Severe P. vivax malaria occurs in India and is managed exactly like severe falciparum malaria.
# Uncomplicated P. vivax (and P. ovale)
Chloroquine 25 mg base/kg over 3 days: 10 mg/kg on day 1, 10 mg/kg on day 2, 5 mg/kg on day 3 (150 mg base tablets; age-wise dosing in Drugs).
Radical cure: primaquine 0.25 mg/kg daily for 14 days, supervised. Not in pregnancy, lactation, infants under 1 year or known G6PD deficiency.
Test G6PD before primaquine where available. Stop primaquine and review urgently for dark urine, yellow eyes, bluish lips, abdominal pain, vomiting or breathlessness.
Known G6PD deficiency: WHO suggests primaquine 0.75 mg/kg once weekly for 8 weeks under close supervision instead of the daily course.
P. ovale is treated as vivax; the rare P. malariae infection is treated as falciparum.
# Uncomplicated P. falciparum and mixed infection
North-Eastern states (Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura): artemether-lumefantrine (ACT-AL) for 3 days plus primaquine 0.75 mg/kg single dose on day 2.
Rest of India: ACT-SP, artesunate 4 mg/kg daily for 3 days plus sulfadoxine 25 mg/kg with pyrimethamine 1.25 mg/kg on day 1, plus primaquine 0.75 mg/kg single dose on day 2.
Mixed Pf plus Pv: area-specific ACT plus primaquine 0.25 mg/kg daily for 14 days. The national guideline notes SP has no vivax activity, so a non-SP ACT such as AL is preferable where available.
Other fixed-dose ACTs licensed in India (artesunate-amodiaquine, artesunate-mefloquine, arterolane-piperaquine for adults) may be used. Oral artemisinin monotherapy is banned.
Give the first dose under observation after food. Repeat the dose if the patient vomits within 30 minutes; if vomiting recurs, treat as severe (parenteral) and refer.
No clinical or parasitological response by 72 hours despite a full course: treat with an alternative ACT, or quinine plus doxycycline, and report to the district malaria officer.
# Severe malaria: antimalarial
IV artesunate 2.4 mg/kg (3 mg/kg in children under 20 kg) at 0, 12 and 24 hours, then once daily. Reconstitute with the 5% sodium bicarbonate supplied. IV is preferred; IM is acceptable if IV access fails.
Give parenteral treatment for at least 24 hours, then as soon as oral intake is possible give a full 3-day course of area-specific oral ACT plus primaquine. Avoid mefloquine-containing ACT after cerebral malaria.
Before referral from a peripheral facility: take a smear or RDT and give the first parenteral dose (IM artesunate or IM artemether 3.2 mg/kg); send the slide and treatment details with the patient.
If artesunate is unavailable: IV quinine 20 mg salt/kg in 5% dextrose over 4 hours, then 10 mg/kg 8-hourly (no loading dose if quinine already given; never as a bolus). Alternatives: IM artemether, or IM arteether 150 mg daily for 3 days in adults only.
Severe vivax malaria: same regimen, then primaquine for 14 days once the patient recovers and can tolerate it.
# Supportive care and monitoring
Check blood glucose frequently and on any deterioration; hypoglycaemia is common with quinine and in pregnancy. Treat values below 40 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L) with IV dextrose.
Do not give corticosteroids, mannitol, heparin or adrenaline, and avoid overhydration (national drug policy).
Manage organ failure: transfusion for severe anaemia or bleeding, early dialysis for AKI, ventilation for ARDS, anticonvulsants for seizures, and antibiotics for suspected bacterial co-infection or shock.
Check haemoglobin over the 4 weeks after IV artesunate to detect delayed haemolysis.
Cure means no fever or parasitaemia to day 28; ask the patient to return if fever recurs.
# Pregnancy and children
Uncomplicated falciparum, first trimester: national policy is oral quinine salt 10 mg/kg 3 times daily for 7 days, taken with food. WHO (2022 onwards) recommends artemether-lumefantrine in the first trimester.
