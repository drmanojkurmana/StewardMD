# Neligan Plastic Surgery 5th Edition (2024): Comprehensive Reference for Peripheral Nerve Injury and Repair

The clinical discipline of Peripheral Nerve Injury and Repair addresses complex reconstructive and aesthetic surgical problems across craniofacial, microsurgical, breast, trunk, hand, and integumentary domains.

## Seddons & Sunderland Classifications, Nerve Grafts and Conduits
Citations and Chapter Sources: Plastic Surgery 5E Vol 1 Ch 21 (Repair and grafting of peripheral nerve)

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Seddon classification grades nerve injury into neurapraxia local myelin conduction block, axonotmesis axonal disruption with intact endoneurium, and neurotmesis complete structural transection.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: three
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Seddon, neurapraxia, axonotmesis, neurotmesis

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Sunderland expanded nerve injury classification into five degrees: 1st neurapraxia, 2nd axonotmesis, 3rd endoneurial disruption, 4th perineurial disruption, and 5th complete epineurial transection.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: five, 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Sunderland, five degrees, endoneurium, perineurium

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Wallerian degeneration begins within 24 to 48 hours distal to nerve transection, with Schwann cell proliferation forming bands of Bungner to guide regenerating axons.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 24, 48
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Wallerian degeneration, Schwann cell, bands of Bungner

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Axonal regeneration proceeds at a biological rate of approximately 1 mm per day or 1 inch per month following a latency period.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 1
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: axonal regeneration

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Tinel sign tracks advancing regenerative axonal growth, where gentle percussion over the nerve trunk elicits distal tingling paresthesias.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Tinel sign, regenerative axonal growth

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Direct end-to-end epineurial neurorrhaphy using 8-0 to 10-0 nylon under magnification is the preferred gold standard when repair is completely tension-free.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 8-0, 10-0
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: epineurial neurorrhaphy, tension-free

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Nerve gaps exceeding 2 cm cannot be closed without harmful tension and require autologous nerve grafting, with the sural nerve serving as the primary donor.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 2
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: nerve gaps, sural nerve, autologous nerve grafting

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Processed nerve allografts Avance or bioabsorbable conduits provide effective alternatives for sensory digital nerve gaps under 2 to 3 cm.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 2, 3
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: nerve allografts, conduits


Comprehensive Textbook Context:
Surgical planning in Peripheral Nerve Injury and Repair requires meticulous anatomical grounding, precise knowledge of blood supply, innervation, fascial planes, and tissue biomechanics.
Operative interventions balance functional restoration of speech, swallowing, mastication, sensory discrimination, and locomotion with aesthetic symmetry and minimal donor site morbidity.
Preoperative evaluation integrates acoustic Doppler, computed tomography angiography, indocyanine green fluorescence imaging, and patient-specific risk factor optimization including smoking cessation, strict glycemic control, and blood pressure management.
Postoperative care prioritizes early mobilization, monitoring of capillary refill, tissue turgor, pinprick bleeding, active rehabilitation, and vigilant surveillance for hematoma, seroma, infection, or microvascular thrombosis.
