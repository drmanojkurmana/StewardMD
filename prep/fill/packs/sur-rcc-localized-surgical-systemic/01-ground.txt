Partial nephrectomy is preferred for T1a masses of 4 cm or less and feasible T1b tumours, matching radical surgery for cancer control while sparing long term function, and it is mandatory for solitary kidneys, bilateral tumours, or prior renal impairment. Warm ischaemia is kept under 20 to 25 minutes before acute tubular necrosis risk climbs, while cold ischaemia with ice slush allows under 35 minutes, and early unclamping after the deep layer saves minutes.

Tumour thrombus reaches renal vein and IVC in 4 to 10 percent of cancers and is staged by Neves-Zincke levels from I in the renal vein to IV above the diaphragm in the atrium, with height setting the operation. Level IV atrial disease needs cardiopulmonary bypass with deep hypothermic circulatory arrest and transoesophageal echo monitoring by a joint cardiac vascular urology team.

Metastatic clear cell disease is grouped by 6 IMDC factors: Karnofsky below 80 percent, diagnosis to drugs under 1 year, anaemia, hypercalcaemia, neutrophilia, and thrombocytosis, giving favourable, intermediate, and poor bands that guide combinations. Intermediate and poor risk start with nivolumab plus ipilimumab dual checkpoint blockade or a VEGFR-TKI with anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 such as pembrolizumab plus axitinib or lenvatinib, beating solo TKI. After nephrectomy for high risk clear cell cancer, adjuvant pembrolizumab for 1 year prolongs disease free survival as proven in KEYNOTE-564, with thyroid, bowel, lung, and liver watched for immune toxicity. Small masses under 3 cm in frail comorbid patients may be watched or treated by cryoablation freezing or radiofrequency heating, converting to surgery on growth.

Large tumours are staged with contrast CT of chest abdomen and pelvis, and MRI defines caval wall invasion when thrombus is suspected. Paraneoplastic clues such as new anaemia, raised calcium, polycythaemia from erythropoietin, and Stauffer liver dysfunction are recorded at diagnosis, because clear cell tumours secrete erythropoietin and cytokines. Cytoreductive nephrectomy is still offered to selected intermediate risk patients with limited metastatic burden who need local control, while poor risk widespread disease starts with systemic therapy first.
