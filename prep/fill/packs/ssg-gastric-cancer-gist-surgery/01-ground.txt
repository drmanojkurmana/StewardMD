Gastric cancer divides into two biological worlds. The intestinal type grows as a discrete distal mass on a background of Helicobacter infection, salty preserved foods and smoking. The diffuse type infiltrates the whole wall as signet ring cells, stiffening the stomach into the classic linitis plastica leather bottle, and a germline CDH1 mutation drives this pattern in families.

Spread beyond the wall is often invisible on scans. Staging laparoscopy with peritoneal cytology precedes curative surgery for T3 and T4 tumours or node-positive disease, because free cancer cells in washings upstage the patient to metastatic M1 disease and cancel a futile gastrectomy.

The operation matches the site. Distal tumours take a subtotal gastrectomy with a 5 cm proximal margin, while proximal and junctional tumours need total gastrectomy with Roux-en-Y oesophagojejunostomy. Nodal clearance follows the Japanese D2 template, and harvesting at least 16 nodes stages the disease accurately and improves survival.

Chemotherapy now brackets surgery. Perioperative FLOT, a four-drug regimen, improves cure rates for resectable T2 or higher disease, and in Asian practice adjuvant S-1 or capecitabine with oxaliplatin follows a D2 resection. Response on restaging decides whether the planned operation still stands.

A different tumour, GIST, arises from the pacemaker cells of the gut wall. Small gastric lesions under 2 cm without high-risk features need only a wedge resection with 1 to 2 cm cuffs of normal wall, and lymphadenectomy is skipped because nodal spread is vanishingly rare.

Mutation decides drug therapy. KIT exon 11 mutation predicts a strong imatinib response, and high-risk disease over 5 cm or over 5 mitoses per 5 square millimetres takes adjuvant imatinib 400 mg daily for 3 years. The PDGFRA D842V variant resists imatinib but answers to avapritinib, so genotyping precedes every prescription.

Lymphoma of the stomach behaves medically. Gastric MALT lymphoma with Helicobacter positivity melts away with eradication alone in 80 percent of cases, while deep invasion or the t(11;18) translocation needs radiotherapy or rituximab-based therapy instead of the knife.

Advanced disease announces itself at distant sites. Krukenberg tumours fill both ovaries, the Sister Mary Joseph nodule hardens the umbilicus, and a Blumer shelf is felt on rectal examination when transcoelomic seedlings pool in the pelvis. At this stage surgery is palliative, bypassing or stenting the obstruction.
