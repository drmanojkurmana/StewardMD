Germinal matrix haemorrhage affects preterm infants because the subependymal germinal matrix at the caudothalamic groove regresses by 32 to 34 weeks and its fragile capillaries lack autoregulation. Papile Grade I bleeding stays confined to the subependymal matrix. Grade II ruptures into the lateral ventricle filling less than 50 percent of the lumen without ventricular dilatation. Grade III fills more than 50 percent with acute ventricular dilatation. Grade IV is periventricular venous haemorrhagic infarction from terminal medullary vein occlusion, later forming porencephalic cysts. Screening cranial ultrasound is required under 1500 g birth weight or before 30 weeks gestation, timed at day 3 to 7 of life because over 90 percent of bleeds occur within 72 hours. Post-haemorrhagic hydrocephalus follows aqueduct blockage or arachnoiditis, tracked with the Levene ventricular index against the 97th percentile. The mastoid fontanelle window shows the fourth ventricle, cerebellum and cisterna magna. Spinal dysraphism includes thickened filum terminale over 2.0 mm at L5-S1, low conus, lipomyelomeningocele with the neural lipoma junction outside the canal, myelomeningocele with Chiari II malformation and raised maternal alpha-fetoprotein in open lesions.
 Periventricular leukomalacia injures periventricular white matter in preterms, showing echogenicity brighter than choroid plexus in days 1 to 7, then cystic change and irregular ventricular enlargement with selective corticospinal tract injury causing spastic diplegia. Term hypoxic ischaemic encephalopathy after acute profound asphyxia damages basal ganglia, thalamus and brainstem, while partial prolonged asphyxia injures watershed cortex. Diffusion restriction appears early, the posterior limb of the internal capsule loses its normal T1 myelin signal, and therapeutic hypothermia at 33.5 degrees for 72 hours improves outcome. Craniosynostosis includes sagittal scaphocephaly, unilateral coronal plagiocephaly with harlequin orbit, metopic trigonocephaly, positional plagiocephaly with parallelogram head shape, cloverleaf Kleeblattschadel skull, beaten copper calvarial markings from raised pressure, and Apert, Crouzon and Pfeiffer syndromes from FGFR mutations, best defined on 3D CT.
 Grade I has 0 percent intraventricular blood, and the conus normally ends at L1 to L2 after term, with spinal ultrasound easiest under 3 to 4 months and 100 percent of open myelomeningoceles linking to Chiari II.
 Caudal regression ranges from partial sacral agenesis to complete absence below T11 or T12 in severe diabetic embryopathy.
