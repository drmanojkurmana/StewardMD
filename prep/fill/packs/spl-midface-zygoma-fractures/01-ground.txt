Exam grounding notes for maxillary and zygomatic fractures, written for grounding, not for display. Paraphrased from Plastic Surgery 5E Volume 3 on midface trauma.

The Le Fort I line, called the Guerin fracture, runs above the dental roots and detaches the alveolar process with the palate from the midface skeleton. The tooth-bearing segment drops as one block, and the upper face stays intact. Malocclusion with a mobile maxilla marks this low transverse pattern.

Le Fort II draws a pyramidal break: it crosses the nasal bones, climbs the frontal process of the maxilla, passes the lacrimal bones, floors the orbit, and exits through the zygomaticomaxillary suture. The pyramid apex sits at the nasal root and the base spans the molar region.

Le Fort III is a craniofacial dysjunction that separates the whole facial skeleton from the cranial base. The line traverses the frontonasal, frontomaxillary, and frontozygomatic sutures, then continues back along both zygomatic arches. The entire face floats free of the skull.

Every classic Le Fort pattern, whether I, II, or III, breaks the pterygoid plates. A fractured pterygoid plate is therefore pathognomonic of a Le Fort injury and is uniformly present across all three levels. CT readers check the pterygoids first.

The zygomaticomaxillary complex breaks as a tetrapod at four articulations: the frontozygomatic suture, the inferior orbital rim, the zygomaticomaxillary buttress, and the zygomatic arch. All four struts give way together when the cheek takes a direct blow.

Bedside clues form a classic triad of three signs: malar flattening where the cheek prominence collapses, trismus as the displaced arch impinges on the coronoid process, and numbness in infraorbital nerve territory over the cheek and upper lip. Three findings together mean ZMC until proven otherwise.

An isolated arch fracture without displacement elsewhere is lifted through the Gilles approach: a small temporal hairline incision carried deep to the deep temporal fascia, then an elevator passed beneath the depressed arch. No plate is needed when the arch springs back to contour.

Displaced ZMC fractures are plated at the load-bearing points, prioritising the frontozygomatic suture and the zygomaticomaxillary buttress. Low-profile titanium miniplates hold the reduction without palpable bulk under thin cheek skin.
