Revised Atlanta separates interstitial oedematous from necrotising disease, with organ failure under 48 hours marking moderately severe and persistent failure marking severe pancreatitis with intensive care need.

Gallstones and alcohol cause 80 percent of attacks, with triglycerides over 1000 mg per dL and calcium over 10 mg per dL as metabolic triggers needing same-admission cholecystectomy for biliary cases once the attack settles.

Contrast CT at 72 to 96 hours stages necrosis and detects gas in infected necrosis, while early CT underestimates necrosis and is reserved for diagnostic doubt when the cause is unclear.

Infected necrosis with gas, positive culture or persistent sepsis beyond 4 weeks follows the PANTER step-up from percutaneous drainage through endoscopic necrosectomy to minimally invasive retroperitoneal debridement as needed.

Walled-off necrosis after 4 weeks with obstruction, pain or infection needs endoscopic cystgastrostomy with lumen-apposing metal stents rather than open surgery, draining debris into the stomach.

Abdominal compartment syndrome with bladder pressure over 20 mmHg plus organ failure needs decompressive laparotomy, and percutaneous drainage is tried first when collections dominate the picture.

Enteral feeding within 48 hours through nasojejunal tube preserves gut barrier and cuts infection, while prophylactic antibiotics and probiotics do not prevent infected necrosis and are not routinely given.

Cholangitis with biliary pancreatitis needs ERCP within 24 hours, while mere biliary obstruction without cholangitis waits for the attack to settle before elective clearance of the duct.
