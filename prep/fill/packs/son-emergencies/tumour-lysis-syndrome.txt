# Tumour lysis syndrome (adult)
Risk-stratify every patient before anti-cancer therapy; hydrate and give allopurinol for low or intermediate risk, rasburicase for high risk or established TLS (after excluding G6PD deficiency), monitor electrolytes closely and involve renal and ICU teams early for dialysis.
recognise
# Recognise (Cairo-Bishop)
Laboratory TLS: 2 or more of the following from 3 days before to 7 days after starting therapy: uric acid 476 micromol/L (8 mg/dL) or more, potassium 6.0 mmol/L or more, phosphate 1.45 mmol/L (4.5 mg/dL) or more, calcium 1.75 mmol/L (7 mg/dL) or less, or a 25% change from baseline.
Clinical TLS: laboratory TLS plus creatinine 1.5 times the upper limit of normal or more, cardiac arrhythmia or sudden death, or seizure.
Can occur spontaneously before treatment in bulky, rapidly proliferating tumours, and after steroids alone, radiotherapy or BCL-2 inhibitors (venetoclax).
Symptoms are non-specific: nausea, vomiting, lethargy, oliguria, cramps, tetany, palpitations, confusion or seizures. Metabolic acidosis and raised lactate are common.
prevention
# Risk stratification and prophylaxis
High risk: Burkitt lymphoma or leukaemia, lymphoblastic lymphoma, ALL, AML or CML blast crisis with WBC 100 x 10^9/L or more (or WBC 25 to 100 x 10^9/L with LDH above 2 times normal), high-grade NHL with LDH above 4 times normal or bulky disease above 10 cm with raised LDH.
Pre-existing renal impairment, dehydration, older age and venetoclax (even in CLL) push patients into a higher risk category; follow the regimen-specific venetoclax ramp-up protocol.
Low or intermediate risk: oral or IV fluids 2 to 3 L per day on the day before, the day of and the day after starting therapy, plus allopurinol 300 mg once daily for 7 days started 12 to 24 hours before treatment.
Allopurinol intolerant: febuxostat 120 mg once daily from 2 days before therapy for 7 days (up to 9 days).
High risk: IV hydration from 24 hours before treatment to a urine output of 100 mL/m2/hour, plus rasburicase 3 mg IV single dose 30 to 60 minutes before therapy or high-dose steroids (BSH 2025); give a second 3 mg dose if urate or clinical status requires.
Do not add potassium or phosphate to fluids. Avoid nephrotoxins, NSAIDs, thiazide and potassium-sparing diuretics and, where possible, IV contrast.
immediate
# Established TLS: first hours
Liaise with renal and ICU teams early. Place a urinary catheter, keep strict hourly fluid balance and attach cardiac monitoring if potassium is high or there is any arrhythmia.
IV fluids above 3 L/m2/day (about twice maintenance) with no added potassium, aiming for urine output above 100 mL/m2/hour. If fluid overloaded (weight gain above 3 kg) give IV furosemide 0.5 to 1 mg/kg once hypovolaemia is excluded.
Rasburicase 0.2 mg/kg IV over 30 minutes once daily for 3 to 5 days (up to 7 days) guided by uric acid and clinical course. Treat as time-critical: give within 1 hour of prescription.
Stop allopurinol while rasburicase is given; restart it 24 hours after the last rasburicase dose if still needed.
If rasburicase is contraindicated: allopurinol up to 800 mg/day in 2 to 3 divided doses, and discuss renal replacement therapy early because allopurinol does not remove urate already formed.
Do not alkalinise the urine with sodium bicarbonate; it promotes calcium phosphate and xanthine precipitation in the tubules.
treatment
# Electrolyte management
Hyperkalaemia: treat promptly per the hyperkalaemia protocol: 10% calcium gluconate 30 mL IV over 10 minutes if ECG changes, soluble insulin 10 units with glucose 25 g IV, and nebulised salbutamol 10 to 20 mg. Monitor capillary glucose for 6 hours after insulin.
Hyperphosphataemia: rely on hydration and timely rasburicase. Oral phosphate binders reduce dietary phosphate only; a persistent rise usually needs renal replacement therapy.
Hypocalcaemia: do not treat if asymptomatic, because calcium given with high phosphate precipitates in tissues.
Symptomatic hypocalcaemia (tetany, seizures, arrhythmia, prolonged QT): 10% calcium gluconate 10 to 20 mL IV over 10 minutes with ECG monitoring, aiming to relieve symptoms rather than normalise calcium.
Check magnesium periodically and replace if low.
monitoring
# Monitoring
High risk or established TLS: potassium, phosphate, calcium, urate, urea, creatinine and LDH at least 6-hourly initially, with venous blood gas for rapid potassium, bicarbonate and lactate. Reduce frequency once stable.
Hourly urine output, formal fluid balance every 4 hours, vital signs 4 to 6-hourly, weight at least daily and baseline ECG.
After rasburicase, urate keeps falling in the sample tube: send samples on ice for rapid assay, otherwise results are falsely low.
Low or intermediate risk: daily weight, fluid balance and renal profile with electrolytes, urate and LDH during the first days of therapy.
escalate
# Renal replacement therapy and ICU
Potassium 6.5 mmol/L or more persisting 4 hours after treatment, or hyperkalaemia with ECG changes.
Rising creatinine, urea or phosphate despite hydration and rasburicase, or oliguria or anuria.
Fluid overload unresponsive to diuretics, severe metabolic acidosis, uraemic encephalopathy, or symptomatic hypocalcaemia with severe hyperphosphataemia.
special
# G6PD deficiency and India context
G6PD deficiency is common in India and in people of African, Mediterranean, Middle Eastern and Asian heritage. Request G6PD during the diagnostic work-up so the result is ready before rasburicase is needed.
Rasburicase is contraindicated in G6PD deficiency, prior haemolytic anaemia and methaemoglobinaemia (risk of severe haemolysis and methaemoglobinaemia). In a very unwell patient the consultant may give it before the result after weighing risk and benefit.
Rasburicase is expensive: the single fixed 3 mg prophylactic dose with close monitoring (BSH 2025) is effective and reduces cost.
pitfalls
# Pitfalls
Missing spontaneous TLS at presentation in bulky high-grade lymphoma or acute leukaemia with high WBC.
Delayed rasburicase in established TLS; NHS England issued a 2025 patient safety alert about harm from delays.
Giving calcium for asymptomatic hypocalcaemia, adding potassium to maintenance fluids, or alkalinising urine.
Continuing allopurinol with azathioprine or mercaptopurine at full dose (toxicity); febuxostat should not be combined with them.
