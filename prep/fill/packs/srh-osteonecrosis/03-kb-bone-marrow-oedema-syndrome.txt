# Bone marrow oedema syndrome
Bone marrow oedema syndrome, Musculoskeletal, Orthopaedics / Rheumatology.
Aliases: transient bone marrow oedema syndrome; regional migratory osteoporosis.
# Pathophysiology
Bone marrow oedema syndrome denotes reversible accumulation of interstitial fluid within bone marrow producing pain and characteristic MRI signal change, without an underlying tumour, infection, established osteonecrosis or fracture. The mechanism is uncertain and probably multifactorial, involving transient alterations in local perfusion, increased intraosseous pressure and a reparative or neurovascular response. Its migratory and self-limiting nature and the absence of necrosis distinguish it from avascular necrosis, though the two may overlap on a spectrum.
# Prognosis
The syndrome is benign and self-limiting, generally resolving over several months, though symptoms may migrate to other joints before settling.
# Clinical pearls
A self-limiting syndrome of joint pain with MRI bone marrow oedema and no identifiable structural cause
It is an umbrella that includes transient osteoporosis of the hip and regional migratory osteoporosis
It typically affects weight-bearing joints of the lower limb and can migrate between sites over time
MRI shows marrow oedema without the subchondral necrosis of avascular necrosis or a fracture line
The main differentials are early avascular necrosis, subchondral insufficiency fracture and infection
It resolves spontaneously over months, and management is supportive with protected weight bearing and analgesia
Additional differentials: Avascular necrosis (early); Subchondral insufficiency fracture; Osteomyelitis; Inflammatory arthritis; Complex regional pain syndrome; Bone neoplasm.
# Additional investigations
MRI demonstrating marrow oedema without necrosis or fracture
Radiographs and, where needed, follow-up imaging
