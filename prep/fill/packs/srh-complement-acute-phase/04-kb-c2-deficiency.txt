# C2 deficiency
C2 deficiency, Immune system, Immunology.
Aliases: C2 deficiency; Complement C2 deficiency; Hereditary C2 deficiency.
# Pathophysiology
C2 is a component shared by the classical and lectin complement pathways; it is cleaved to form the C3 convertase that amplifies complement activation. In C2 deficiency these pathways cannot efficiently generate C3 convertase, impairing opsonisation and clearance of encapsulated bacteria and predisposing to invasive pneumococcal disease. Reduced classical-pathway function also compromises the clearance of immune complexes and apoptotic material, promoting autoantibody formation and a lupus-like syndrome. Because the alternative pathway is preserved and can still be triggered directly on microbial surfaces, residual complement activity persists, which is why many individuals remain asymptomatic and infections are usually less severe than in complete terminal-pathway deficiency.
