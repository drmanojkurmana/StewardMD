Exam grounding notes for pressure injury staging and reconstruction, written for grounding, not for display. Paraphrased from Plastic Surgery 5E Volume 4 on pressure sores.

Staging follows NPUAP and EPUAP. Stage 1 is intact skin with non-blanchable erythema. Stage 2 is a partial-thickness break with visible dermis. Stage 3 passes the full dermis into subcutaneous fat without exposing deeper planes.

Stage 4 destroys full-thickness skin and underlying tissue until fascia, muscle, tendon, ligament, cartilage, or bone lies exposed or directly palpable in the wound. Any visible bone, tendon, or muscle makes the ulcer Stage 4 regardless of size.

An ulcer hidden by slough or eschar cannot be staged and is labelled unstageable until debrided. Deep tissue pressure injury shows intact or broken skin with persistent deep red, maroon, or purple discoloration from deep damage, and it often declares a deep wound within days.

Occlusion begins when external pressure tops the capillary closing pressure of 32 mmHg. Sustained occlusion starves tissue, and necrosis follows within 2 hours over a bony prominence. Relief, turning, and pressure-mapping mattresses prevent this cascade.

Sacral defects are closed most reliably with a superior gluteal artery perforator flap or with a gluteus maximus V-Y advancement flap. Both import well-vascularised muscle or perforator skin that resists pressure and fills dead space.

Ischial ulcers over the sitting bone take an inferior gluteal artery perforator flap, a gluteus maximus rotation flap, or a hamstring advancement flap. The sitting surface demands durable padded cover that tolerates shear.

Trochanteric ulcers over the greater trochanter are covered with a tensor fasciae latae myofascial flap, rotated or advanced into the defect. The TFL brings fascia lata strength and a robust pedicle to a lateral wound.

Poor nutrition predicts failure: preoperative serum albumin below 3.0 g per dL and haemoglobin below 10 g per dL correlate strongly with dehiscence and recurrent ulceration. Both are corrected before flap surgery, alongside spasm control and pressure relief.
