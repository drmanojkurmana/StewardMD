# Exertional heat stroke
Exertional heat stroke, Multisystem, Sports medicine / Emergency medicine.
Aliases: exercise-induced heat stroke.
# Pathophysiology
Exertional heat stroke occurs when intense muscular heat production during exercise overwhelms the body's capacity to dissipate heat, causing core temperature to rise to a level that injures tissues. Excessive heat denatures proteins and triggers a systemic inflammatory response and endothelial injury, which can progress to multi-organ dysfunction. Muscle breakdown releases myoglobin and potassium, and the combination of hyperthermia, inflammation and hypoperfusion drives rhabdomyolysis, coagulopathy and acute kidney and liver injury. The speed of cooling strongly influences outcome.
# Prognosis
With immediate aggressive cooling most patients recover fully, but delayed cooling or established multi-organ failure carries a high risk of death or lasting organ damage.
# Clinical pearls
A life-threatening emergency defined by a very high core temperature with central nervous system dysfunction arising during strenuous exertion
It affects young, fit individuals such as athletes and military recruits, often in hot humid conditions
Central nervous system features such as confusion, agitation, seizures or collapse distinguish it from milder heat illness
Unlike classic heat stroke, patients may still be sweating
Rapid whole-body cooling, ideally by cold-water immersion, is the priority and should not await transfer
Complications include rhabdomyolysis, acute kidney injury, disseminated intravascular coagulation and liver injury
