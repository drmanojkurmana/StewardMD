Buerger disease (thromboangiitis obliterans) is a non-atherosclerotic segmental inflammation of small and medium limb arteries and veins, striking almost only heavy smokers younger than forty five. Smoke inflames young limb vessels while sparing the elastic walls of atherosclerosis-free youth.

Its thrombi stay hypercellular and angry with microabscesses and giant cells, yet the elastic lamina and wall architecture survive intact, unlike necrotizing arteritis that shreds walls. Angry clots spare the scaffolding that necrotizing arteritis destroys.

Fingers whiten, ache at rest, ulcerate, and blacken; superficial veins clot and migrate; distal pulses vanish. Only total permanent quitting of every tobacco and nicotine product halts the march; no drug or bypass substitutes for cessation. Quitting alone arrests Buerger.
