# Cobb syndrome
Cobb syndrome, Genetics, Clinical Genetics.
Aliases: cutaneomeningospinal angiomatosis; spinal arteriovenous metameric syndrome; cutaneous-spinal angiomatosis.
# Pathophysiology
Cobb syndrome is a metameric neurocutaneous vascular disorder in which a vascular malformation of the spinal cord and its coverings coexists with a cutaneous vascular lesion in the matching body segment. The shared segmental distribution is thought to arise from a developmental event affecting cells destined for a single metamere, so that skin, vertebral, meningeal, and cord tissues derived from that segment carry the vascular anomaly. The neurological consequences stem from the spinal arteriovenous malformation, which can injure the cord by direct compression, by diverting blood and raising venous pressure with resulting cord ischaemia, or by haemorrhage. The condition is characteristically sporadic.
# Prognosis
Outcome depends on the size, location, and haemodynamics of the spinal malformation and on the degree of cord injury at presentation. Timely diagnosis and treatment can stabilise or improve neurological function, whereas late presentation with established cord damage or haemorrhage carries a risk of permanent disability.
# Clinical pearls
The syndrome links a spinal cord vascular malformation with a cutaneous vascular lesion, both localised to the same body segment (metamere)
The skin lesion is typically a vascular stain or angioma overlying the trunk or back in the dermatome corresponding to the affected spinal level
Patients commonly present with a progressive myelopathy: limb weakness, sensory disturbance, and bladder or bowel dysfunction, sometimes with acute deterioration
