# Hypoparathyroidism
Hypoparathyroidism, Endocrine / Metabolic, Endocrinology.
Aliases: PTH deficiency; hypoparathyroid state; postsurgical hypoparathyroidism; idiopathic hypoparathyroidism.
# Pathophysiology
Hypoparathyroidism results from deficient secretion or action of parathyroid hormone, most often after surgical injury to the glands but also from autoimmune destruction, genetic syndromes, infiltration, or severe magnesium disturbance. Lack of PTH reduces renal calcium reabsorption and phosphate excretion, decreases bone resorption, and impairs conversion of vitamin D to its active form, together producing hypocalcaemia and hyperphosphataemia. Low ionised calcium increases neuromuscular excitability, causing the characteristic clinical features.
# Prognosis
With appropriate calcium and active vitamin D therapy most patients maintain adequate calcium and lead normal lives, though lifelong treatment and monitoring are needed and chronic therapy carries risks of hypercalciuria, nephrocalcinosis, and renal impairment.
# Clinical pearls
The hallmark is hypocalcaemia with an inappropriately low or undetectable parathyroid hormone level, usually accompanied by hyperphosphataemia
Neck surgery is the most common cause, from inadvertent removal of or damage to the parathyroid glands
Symptoms of hypocalcaemia range from perioral and acral paraesthesiae and muscle cramps to tetany, laryngospasm, and seizures
Chvostek and Trousseau signs are useful bedside indicators of neuromuscular irritability from low calcium
Severe symptomatic or acute hypocalcaemia is a medical emergency requiring intravenous calcium and cardiac monitoring
Long-term management uses calcium and active vitamin D (calcitriol) analogues, aiming to relieve symptoms while avoiding hypercalciuria and renal complications
Autoimmune, genetic, and magnesium-related causes should be considered when there is no surgical history, as hypomagnesaemia impairs PTH secretion and action
# Additional differentials
Vitamin D deficiency causing hypocalcaemia
Chronic kidney disease with mineral bone disorder
Hypomagnesaemia impairing PTH secretion
Pseudohypoparathyroidism (PTH resistance)
Acute pancreatitis or massive transfusion causing hypocalcaemia
Hungry bone syndrome after parathyroidectomy
# Additional investigations
Serum calcium (ideally ionised or albumin-corrected) and phosphate
Intact parathyroid hormone level
Serum magnesium
25-hydroxyvitamin D and renal function
ECG to assess for QT prolongation
24-hour urinary calcium during treatment to monitor for hypercalciuria
# Red flags
Tetany, laryngospasm, seizures, or carpopedal spasm
Prolonged QT interval or cardiac arrhythmia from acute hypocalcaemia
Rapidly falling calcium in the early postoperative period after neck surgery
Altered mental status or stridor accompanying severe hypocalcaemia
# Pitfalls
Overlooking coexisting hypomagnesaemia, which must be corrected before hypocalcaemia will respond
Interpreting calcium without correcting for albumin or measuring the ionised fraction
Aiming for fully normal serum calcium and inducing hypercalciuria and renal damage
Failing to anticipate and monitor for hypocalcaemia after thyroid or parathyroid surgery
# Management
Manage chronic disease with oral calcium and an active vitamin D analogue such as calcitriol or alfacalcidol to maintain calcium and relieve symptoms
Correct coexisting hypomagnesaemia, as magnesium disturbance both causes and perpetuates the hypoparathyroid state
