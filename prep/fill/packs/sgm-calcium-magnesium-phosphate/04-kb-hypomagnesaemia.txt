# Hypomagnesaemia (adult)
Adults
Emergency department, ward, ICU
Give IV magnesium sulphate 1 to 2 g for arrhythmia, torsades or seizures followed by 4 to 8 g over 12 to 24 hours, use oral magnesium for mild deficiency, correct linked hypokalaemia and hypocalcaemia, and stop culprit drugs such as PPIs and diuretics.
Aliases: low magnesium; low Mg; magnesium sulphate; MgSO4; torsades de pointes; PPI hypomagnesaemia; refractory hypokalaemia.
# Recognise
Serum magnesium below 0.7 mmol/L (1.7 mg/dL). Symptoms are usual below 0.5 mmol/L (1.2 mg/dL). Serum levels underestimate the total body deficit.
Features: tremor, tetany, Chvostek and Trousseau signs, seizures, weakness, confusion, arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation, ventricular tachycardia, torsades de pointes) and prolonged QT.
Often coexists with hypokalaemia and hypocalcaemia, which stay refractory until magnesium is corrected.
Causes: GI losses (diarrhoea, malabsorption, PPIs), renal losses (loop and thiazide diuretics, alcohol, aminoglycosides, amphotericin B, cisplatin, calcineurin inhibitors, EGFR antibodies), refeeding, pancreatitis, and after parathyroidectomy.
# Investigations
Check potassium, calcium, phosphate, creatinine and a 12-lead ECG for QT prolongation.
Cause unclear: fractional excretion of magnesium (FEMg) = (urine Mg x plasma creatinine) / (0.7 x plasma Mg x urine creatinine) x 100. Above 2% during hypomagnesaemia suggests renal wasting.
Low FEMg or a 24-hour urine magnesium below 12 mg suggests GI loss or poor intake.
# Severe or symptomatic
Torsades de pointes, unstable arrhythmia or seizures: magnesium sulphate 1 to 2 g (4 to 8 mmol) IV over 15 minutes; give as a rapid bolus in pulseless torsades during resuscitation.
Symptomatic but stable severe hypomagnesaemia: magnesium sulphate 1 to 2 g IV over 1 hour.
Then magnesium sulphate 4 to 8 g (16 to 32 mmol) IV over 12 to 24 hours, repeated daily as needed, continuing for 1 to 2 days after serum magnesium normalises to replete stores.
50% magnesium sulphate contains 1 g (about 4 mmol) in 2 mL; dilute it (to 20% or weaker) for IV use.
Continuous cardiac monitoring for arrhythmia, and correct potassium and calcium in parallel.
# Mild or asymptomatic
Oral magnesium salts: about 5 to 14 mmol elemental magnesium daily for mild deficiency, up to 15 to 28 mmol daily in divided doses for larger deficits. Diarrhoea limits the dose; slow-release forms are better tolerated.
Continue for 1 to 2 weeks after normalisation for a transient cause, or long term for ongoing losses.
Stop or switch culprit drugs: change a PPI to an H2 blocker where possible and review diuretics. Amiloride can reduce renal magnesium wasting.
Refeeding risk: replace magnesium, phosphate and potassium alongside thiamine and cautious feeding.
# Monitoring
In severe cases, check magnesium every 4 to 6 hours during IV replacement with cardiac monitoring.
Watch for hypermagnesaemia: loss of deep tendon reflexes, hypotension, bradycardia and respiratory depression, especially in renal impairment.
Recheck potassium and calcium after magnesium is corrected.
# Renal impairment and pregnancy
eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73 m2: reduce the IV dose by 25 to 50% and monitor levels closely.
Magnesium sulphate for eclampsia or pre-eclampsia follows the obstetric regimen (see the eclampsia protocol), not this replacement schedule.
Patients on cisplatin or anti-EGFR antibodies need regular magnesium monitoring and often supplementation.
# Pitfalls
Treating refractory hypokalaemia or hypocalcaemia without checking magnesium.
Stopping replacement as soon as the serum level is normal, before intracellular stores recover.
Missing long-term PPI use, especially with diuretics in older adults.
Fast IV infusion in a conscious patient without arrhythmia, causing flushing and hypotension.

Magnesium sulphate IV (bolus)
# Dose
1 to 2 g (4 to 8 mmol) IV over 15 minutes for arrhythmia, torsades or seizures; over 1 hour if stable
Rapid bolus in pulseless torsades. Dilute 50% solution before IV use.
Magnesium sulphate IV (infusion)
4 to 8 g (16 to 32 mmol) IV over 12 to 24 hours
Continue 1 to 2 days after normalisation. Reduce by 25 to 50% if eGFR below 30.
Oral magnesium salts
# Dose
