Acute embolic ischaemia from atrial fibrillation lodges at the superior mesenteric artery branch points with pain out of proportion, lactic acidosis and a 50 to 90 percent mortality when revascularisation is delayed. Embolectomy with bowel assessment is the emergency operation.

Biphasic CT angiography is the test of choice, showing SMA cutoff, pneumatosis and portal venous gas, and must precede laparotomy to plan embolectomy against bypass. Lactate above 2 with a cutoff mandates immediate operation.

Nonocclusive mesenteric ischaemia follows low-flow states on vasopressors and improves with resuscitation, prostaglandin infusion and stopping vasoconstrictors rather than surgery. Digoxin and ergot triggers are withdrawn.

Mesenteric venous thrombosis with hypercoagulable states or portal hypertension gets therapeutic heparin then 6 months of anticoagulation, with surgery only for infarction or peritonitis. Warfarin or a direct oral agent continues after heparin.

Second-look laparotomy at 24 to 48 hours reassesses bowel viability after embolectomy, using fluorescein or Doppler to separate viable from gangrenous segments. Planned re-exploration avoids both short bowel and missed necrosis.

Chronic mesenteric ischaemia shows food fear with weight loss and 2 of 3 vessel stenoses, treated by endovascular stenting of the celiac and SMA or open bypass. Endovascular first suits high-risk patients.

Median arcuate ligament syndrome compresses the celiac axis in young thin women with postprandial pain relieved by expiration on duplex, cured by laparoscopic ligament release with ganglionectomy.

Ischaemic colitis at the watershed Griffith point and Sudeck point presents with bloody diarrhoea within 48 hours of hypotension, and gangrene or stricture needs segmental resection. Most cases settle with fluids and antibiotics. Postoperative heparin bridging resumes within 24 hours when drains run clear, and lifelong antiplatelet therapy follows stenting to protect patency. Ergot alkaloids and digoxin constrict mesenteric vessels and are stopped at once. Warfarin starts once heparin is therapeutic, overlapping for 5 days before heparin stops.
