Renovascular hypertension follows the Goldblatt model, the two-kidney one-clip experiment: clamping one renal artery fires renin-angiotensin-aldosterone release from the starved kidney, holding systemic pressure high, so flash pulmonary oedema or a small asymmetric kidney should prompt a renovascular hunt. Atherosclerotic stenosis causes over 90%, crusting the ostium and proximal third of the artery in older smokers with diffuse vascular disease. Fibromuscular dysplasia, FMD, causes about 10%, striking young women with medial fibroplasia, the string-of-beads alternation of stenoses and dilatations; among FMD subtypes, medial fibroplasia dominates at 85%, with perimedial and intimal forms trailing.

FMD without complex branch disease takes PTRA, percutaneous transluminal renal angioplasty without stenting, since balloons alone cure over 80%, while atherosclerotic ostial plaques need stenting in atherosclerosis to pin back elastic recoil, after trials tempered routine intervention. Renal artery aneurysm, RAA, threatens rupture in pregnancy and beyond, so repair is advised over 2 cm or at any size in childbearing age, usually by endovascular coiling or covered stenting. Congenital arteriovenous malformations, cirsoid AVM tangles, present with haematuria and a flank bruit and are closed by transcatheter embolization, while acquired arteriovenous fistulae, especially post-biopsy AVF after percutaneous renal biopsy, often close on their own under watch. Duplex ultrasound screens resistant hypertension for asymmetric kidneys and tardus parvus waveforms before angiography, and captopril renography has faded with better cross sectional imaging. Restenosis after PTRA is watched by duplex surveillance, with repeat ballooning for recurrent beading. Nephrectomy for renovascular hypertension is reserved for unreconstructable kidneys with uncontrollable pressure, closing the chapter when plumbing fails. Smoking cessation is counselled at every visit, since tobacco accelerates both atherosclerotic and dysplastic arterial injury. Aortitis and Takayasu arteritis enter the differential in young patients with pulseless limbs and raised inflammatory markers. Statins with blood pressure control continue lifelong regardless of plumbing, since stents never replace medicines. Renovascular disease rewards pressure, pictures, and selective plumbing.
