## ISSVA Classification: Infantile Hemangiomas and Vascular Malformations
Citations and Chapter Sources: Plastic Surgery 5E Vol 3 Ch 31 (Vascular anomalies)

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: ISSVA classification separates vascular anomalies into vascular tumours endothelial hyperplasia, e.g., infantile hemangioma and vascular malformations abnormal structural dysmorphogenesis.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: ISSVA, vascular tumours, vascular malformations

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Infantile hemangiomas stain strongly positive for glucose transporter-1 GLUT-1, whereas congenital hemangiomas and vascular malformations are consistently GLUT-1 negative.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: GLUT-1, infantile hemangioma

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Infantile hemangiomas display a predictable natural life history: rapid proliferative phase during the first 6 to 9 months, followed by slow involution resolving by 5 to 9 years.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 6, 9, 5
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: proliferative phase, involution

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: First-line pharmacotherapy for problematic proliferating infantile hemangiomas is oral propranolol dosed at 2 to 3 mg per kg per day, inducing pericyte apoptosis and vasoconstriction.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 2, 3
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: propranolol, infantile hemangioma

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Kasabach-Merritt phenomenon life-threatening consumptive thrombocytopenia and coagulopathy is caused by kaposiform hemangioendothelioma KHE or tufted angioma, never common infantile hemangioma.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Kasabach-Merritt, thrombocytopenia, kaposiform hemangioendothelioma

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Vascular malformations are present at birth, grow proportionately with child growth, and never exhibit spontaneous involution.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: vascular malformations, proportionate growth

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Low-flow vascular malformations comprise venous malformations blue soft compressible, lymphatic malformations microcystic or macrocystic, and capillary malformations port-wine stain.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: low-flow, venous malformations, lymphatic malformations, port-wine stain

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: High-flow arteriovenous malformations AVM feature direct arterial-venous nidi without intervening capillaries, staged by the Schobinger clinical classification from Stage I to IV.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: I, IV
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: arteriovenous malformations, AVM, Schobinger, nidus


Comprehensive Textbook Context:
Surgical planning in Vascular Anomalies and Malformations requires meticulous anatomical grounding, precise knowledge of blood supply, innervation, fascial planes, and tissue biomechanics.
Operative interventions balance functional restoration of speech, swallowing, mastication, sensory discrimination, and locomotion with aesthetic symmetry and minimal donor site morbidity.
Preoperative evaluation integrates acoustic Doppler, computed tomography angiography, indocyanine green fluorescence imaging, and patient-specific risk factor optimization including smoking cessation, strict glycemic control, and blood pressure management.
Postoperative care prioritizes early mobilization, monitoring of capillary refill, tissue turgor, pinprick bleeding, active rehabilitation, and vigilant surveillance for hematoma, seroma, infection, or microvascular thrombosis.
