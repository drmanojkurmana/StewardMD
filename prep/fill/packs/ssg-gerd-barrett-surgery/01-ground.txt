Gastroesophageal reflux disease with a large hiatus hernia, refractory oesophagitis or troublesome regurgitation despite medicines is an indication for laparoscopic fundoplication. A Nissen 360-degree wrap suits a normally contracting oesophagus, while a Toupet 270-degree posterior partial wrap suits weak peristalsis because a full wrap would cause severe dysphagia. Preoperative manometry and pH-impedance testing confirm reflux and grade motility before any wrap is planned.

Barrett oesophagus is diagnosed when biopsy shows intestinal metaplasia within at least 1 cm of columnar lining above the gastroesophageal junction. The dysplasia grade decides management. Nondysplastic Barrett undergoes surveillance endoscopy every 3 to 5 years. Low-grade dysplasia is surveyed every 6 to 12 months. High-grade dysplasia is treated by endoscopic eradication. Nodules within Barrett tissue are removed by endoscopic mucosal resection, and the remaining flat Barrett segment is ablated by radiofrequency ablation.

Magnetic sphincter augmentation with the LINX device places magnetic beads around the lower sphincter and suits small hernias with normal motility. It is avoided with a large hiatus hernia, severe oesophagitis or nickel allergy, where fundoplication remains the choice.

A failed fundoplication presents with recurrent reflux, new dysphagia or gas-bloat with inability to belch. Redo surgery needs repeat manometry and pH-impedance studies to separate a disrupted or slipped wrap from a wrong initial indication such as achalasia mistaken for reflux.

Paraesophageal hernia with organoaxial gastric volvulus is an emergency. Borchardt triad of epigastric pain, retching without vomiting and inability to pass a nasogastric tube signals complete obstruction needing urgent reduction and fixation.

A short oesophagus is recognised when 2 to 3 cm of tension-free intra-abdominal oesophagus cannot be achieved. A Collis gastroplasty lengthens the oesophagus with a gastric tube so the fundoplication sits without tension.

Mesh cruroplasty is reserved for hiatal defects over 5 cm or redo repairs. Biologic or absorbable mesh is preferred because permanent synthetic mesh at the hiatus can erode into the oesophagus.
