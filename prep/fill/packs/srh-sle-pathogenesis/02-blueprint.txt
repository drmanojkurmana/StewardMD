# SLE pathogenesis and classification (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-sle-pathogenesis: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is a prototypic multiorgan autoimmune disease characterized by defective clearance of apoptotic cell debris, leading to secondary necrosis and exposure of intracellular nuclear autoantigens (chromatin, snRNPs, DNA) to the immune system.

Endosomal Toll-like receptors (TLR7 and TLR9) in plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) recognize internalized RNA- and DNA-containing immune complexes via Fc-gamma-RIIa (CD32), driving continuous, massive production of type I interferons (IFN-alpha and IFN-beta), establishing the characteristic pathogenic 'interferon gene signature'.

B-cell hyperreactivity and breach of central and peripheral tolerance in SLE are driven by excess B-cell activating factor (BAFF, also known as BLyS) and a proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL), promoting the survival, expansion, and maturation of autoreactive plasma cells producing high-affinity pathogenic autoantibodies.

Genetic susceptibility to SLE includes homozygous loss-of-function mutations in early classical complement components (C1q, C1r, C1s, C4A/B), where C1q deficiency confers a >90% penetrance of early-onset, severe cutaneous and neuropsychiatric lupus.

Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) generated during exaggerated NETosis expose citrullinated histones, LL-37, and modified double-stranded DNA; impaired NET degradation by serum DNase I promotes persistent immune complex formation and induces endothelial injury in lupus nephritis.

Immune complex deposition in tissues activates the classical complement cascade, generating anaphylatoxins (C3a and C5a) that recruit neutrophils, monocytes, and mac-1 expressing inflammatory cells, producing tissue damage and hypocomplementemia (depressed C3 and C4) during active disease flares.

T-cell abnormalities in SLE include defective IL-2 production, altered T-cell receptor (TCR) zeta chain signaling (replaced by Fc-epsilon-RI-gamma), hyperactive calcium signaling, and impaired suppression by CD4+ CD25+ FOXP3+ regulatory T cells, promoting follicular helper T (Tfh) cell support of autoantibody synthesis.

Environmental triggers of lupus flares include ultraviolet (UV) radiation (inducing keratinocyte apoptosis and translocation of Ro/SSA and nuclear antigens to the cell surface), Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) infection with molecular mimicry between EBNA-1 and Sm antigens, and sex hormones (estrogen promoting B-cell survival).
