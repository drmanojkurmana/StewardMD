Silk Road sheet. Figures referenced: 2014, 2, 1, 4, 94, 51, 5101, 50, 80, 5, 6, 20, 22, 24, 48, 70, 40, 10. Agents referenced: glucocorticoids, cyclophosphamide, infliximab, adalimumab.

Behcet disease, the Silk Road vasculitis, attacks vessels of every caliber with recurrent mouth and genital ulcers, inflamed eyes, and clots in arteries and veins alike. Ulcers with eyes and clots mark the road.

The twenty fourteen international criteria grant two points each to oral ulcers, genital ulcers, and eye lesions, plus one point each to skin lesions, vessel lesions, brain disease, and positive pathergy; four or more points confirm diagnosis with sensitivity above ninety four percent. Points pile toward four.

Risk rides with HLA-B fifty one (B fifty one oh one subtype), carried by fifty to eighty percent of Silk Road patients from the Middle East through Turkey and Central Asia to Japan, multiplying risk five to six fold. Genes cluster along the caravan route.

Pathergy proves twitchy skin: a sterile twenty to twenty two gauge needle prick in the forearm raises an erythematous papule or sterile pustule of two millimeters or more with a red halo within twenty four to forty eight hours. Needles raise pustules in a day or two.

Eyes suffer in fifty to seventy percent: recurrent non-granulomatous panuveitis with retinal vasculitis and sterile hypopyon (white cells layering in the front chamber) that shifts with gravity, blinding permanently when untreated. Pus layers shift; vision fades.

Vessels suffer as angio-Behcet in up to forty percent, veins far more than arteries: superficial thrombophlebitis, deep vein thrombosis, Budd-Chiari liver blockade, and superior vena cava syndrome; pulmonary artery aneurysms stand as the classic arterial hallmark, killing by hemoptysis on rupture. Veins clot while aneurysms burst.

Brains suffer as neuro-Behcet in five to ten percent, split into parenchymal disease (brainstem-diencephalic encephalitis and meningoencephalitis with grim prognosis) and non-parenchymal disease (venous sinus thrombosis with isolated raised pressure and papilledema). Parenchyma inflames; sinuses clot.

Severe disease (posterior uveitis, pulmonary aneurysms, neuro-Behcet) demands high-dose steroids with intravenous cyclophosphamide or anti-TNF monoclonals (infliximab, adalimumab); lone anticoagulation kills, since active pulmonary aneurysms rupture into bronchi under blood thinners. Immune drugs save; thinners alone betray.
