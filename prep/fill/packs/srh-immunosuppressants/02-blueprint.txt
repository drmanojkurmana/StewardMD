Cytotoxic and immunosuppressive agents: cyclophosphamide activation with bladder and gonadal toxicity, Euro-Lupus dosing, calcineurin inhibitors with voclosporin, plasma exchange, IVIG.

Cyclophosphamide is an oxazaphosphorine alkylating prodrug converted by hepatic CYP2B6 and CYP3A4 enzymes into 4-hydroxycyclophosphamide, which spontaneously breaks down into active phosphoramide mustard, which cross-links DNA strands at guanine N-7 positions and kills dividing lymphocytes, plus toxic acrolein as the byproduct. Oral daily and intravenous pulse schedules both work, with pulses sparing the bladder somewhat.

Acrolein concentrates in the bladder lumen, causing chemical mucosal ulceration and acute hemorrhagic cystitis with gross hematuria, and multiplies the long-term risk of transitional cell carcinoma of the bladder decades later. Co-administration of sodium 2-mercaptoethanesulfonate, universally called mesna, with vigorous hydration neutralizes acrolein into a non-toxic thioether adduct excreted in urine. Morning dosing with high fluid intake and frequent voiding adds protection.

Cyclophosphamide induces dose-dependent premature ovarian failure with amenorrhea and male azoospermia, with risk escalating dramatically above a cumulative lifetime dose of 10 to 15 g combined with advancing female age above 30 years. Co-administration of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonists such as leuprolide during treatment significantly preserves ovarian reserve by quieting the ovaries. Sperm banking before the first pulse and oocyte or embryo cryopreservation for females are offered in every young patient.

The Euro-Lupus regimen, low-dose IV cyclophosphamide at 500 mg every 2 weeks for 6 doses with a total of 3 g, demonstrated equivalent long-term renal remission and significantly lower rates of severe infections and gonadal toxicity compared to the high-dose NIH regimen at 0.5 to 1 g per square meter monthly for 6 months. Euro-Lupus suits most lupus nephritis, reserving NIH doses for the worst crescentic disease.

Tacrolimus binds intracellular FK-binding protein 12, called FKBP12, and cyclosporine binds cyclophilin; both drug-protein complexes inhibit the phosphatase activity of calcineurin, preventing nuclear translocation of NFAT, the nuclear factor of activated T cells, and blocking IL-2 gene transcription with clonal T-cell shutdown. Nephrotoxicity with hypertension, tremor, gum hypertrophy, and hirsutism with cyclosporine, plus diabetes and hypomagnesemia with tacrolimus, require drug level and creatinine watches.

Voclosporin is a next-generation semi-synthetic calcineurin inhibitor featuring an altered carbon-1 carbon-single-bond side chain that increases calcineurin-binding potency 3- to 4-fold without altering elimination; it demonstrates a predictable pharmacokinetic profile that eliminates the need for routine therapeutic drug monitoring, winning approval added to mycophenolate for lupus nephritis. No level checks and fewer metabolic effects separate it from older siblings.

Therapeutic plasma exchange, called TPE, removes pathogenic circulating autoantibodies, immune complexes, and cryoglobulins from the plasma volume across daily sessions; recognized ASFA Category I indications in rheumatology include catastrophic antiphospholipid syndrome, Goodpasture anti-GBM disease with alveolar hemorrhage, and thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP). Calcium replacement during albumin swaps prevents citrate tingling.

High-dose intravenous immunoglobulin, IVIG at 2 g per kg total dose administered over 2 to 5 days, exerts multimodal immunomodulatory effects: blockade of Fc-gamma receptors on macrophages, neutralization of autoantibodies by anti-idiotypic networks, enhanced clearance of pathogenic IgG via saturation of FcRn, the neonatal Fc receptor, and inhibition of complement activation. Headache with aseptic meningitis, thrombosis, and renal injury in sucrose preparations are watched.
