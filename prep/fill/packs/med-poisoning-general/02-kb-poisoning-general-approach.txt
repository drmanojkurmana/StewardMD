# Acute poisoning: general approach (adult, India)
Adults
Emergency department, ICU
Resuscitate with an ABCDE approach and a bedside glucose, identify the toxidrome, give activated charcoal 50 g only within 1 hour to a patient with a protected airway after a significant adsorbable ingestion, give specific antidotes early, and consider enhanced elimination for selected poisons.
Aliases: unknown ingestion; activated charcoal; gut decontamination; gastric lavage; poison information centre; ISCCM poisoning.
# History and toxidromes
Get the history from the patient, relatives and bystanders: agent, amount, time, route, intent, alcohol or other co-ingestants, and any empty strips, bottles or containers. Interpret with caution and assume the worst credible ingestion.
Cholinergic (organophosphates, carbamates): miosis, salivation, lacrimation, sweating, bronchorrhoea, bradycardia, vomiting, diarrhoea, fasciculations and weakness.
Anticholinergic (antihistamines, tricyclics, antispasmodics, datura): agitated delirium, mydriasis, hyperthermia, tachycardia, dry flushed skin, urinary retention, reduced bowel sounds.
Opioid: coma, pinpoint pupils, slow or absent breathing. Sedative-hypnotic (benzodiazepines, alcohol, barbiturates): CNS depression with hypoventilation, often without specific signs.
Sympathomimetic (cocaine, amphetamines, theophylline): agitation, mydriasis, tachycardia, hypertension, hyperthermia, sweating, seizures. Serotonin toxicity: clonus, hyperreflexia, tremor, agitation, hyperthermia.
In India pesticides (organophosphates, aluminium phosphide, paraquat) are the leading lethal poisons; also consider snakebite, scorpion sting, methanol and drug overdose.
# Resuscitation (first minutes)
Use an ABCDE approach as for a trauma patient. In cardiac arrest start CPR with compressions first and continue resuscitation longer than usual, as poisoned patients can recover well.
Attach pulse oximetry and continuous ECG, check capillary glucose and a blood gas in every patient. Give oxygen if SpO2 is 92% or less; avoid liberal oxygen in paraquat poisoning.
Hypoglycaemia (glucose below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L)): give IV dextrose. Give thiamine 100 mg IV with it in malnourished or alcohol-dependent patients.
Intubate for airway compromise, loss of protective airway reflexes or ventilatory failure. Do not intubate solely to allow gastric lavage or charcoal.
Seizures: benzodiazepines first line (lorazepam 4 mg IV or diazepam 10 mg IV, repeat once). Avoid phenytoin as the first drug. Pyridoxine for isoniazid and dextrose for hypoglycaemic seizures.
Agitation with hypertension: benzodiazepine first, then labetalol if needed. Hypotension: fluid boluses, then vasopressor, and look for a specific antidote.
Skin or eye exposure (pesticides, corrosives): remove contaminated clothing and irrigate with plenty of water; staff wear gloves.
# Investigations
12-lead ECG: QRS above 100 ms (sodium channel blockade, for example tricyclics), QTc prolongation, heart block, bradycardia or tachyarrhythmia.
Renal and liver function, electrolytes including calcium and magnesium, blood gas with lactate, anion gap, measured osmolality (osmolar gap for toxic alcohols), CK, and a pregnancy test in women of childbearing age.
Paracetamol level at 4 hours or later after any intentional overdose; specific levels (salicylate, lithium, digoxin, iron, valproate) only when relevant.
Qualitative urine drug screens are not recommended routinely: a positive result may reflect past use and a negative one does not exclude poisoning.
Chest X-ray for aspiration or pulmonary oedema; CT head if coma persists after correcting glucose and giving thiamine, or if there is head injury.
# Gut decontamination
Activated charcoal 50 g orally or by nasogastric tube (1 g/kg, range 25 to 100 g) within 1 hour of a potentially toxic ingestion of an adsorbable poison, only if the airway is protected or the patient is alert and cooperative.
Beyond 1 hour, consider charcoal only for sustained-release or delayed-release preparations or large ingestions of slowly absorbed drugs.
Charcoal does not bind alcohols (methanol, ethanol, ethylene glycol), metals (iron, lithium, lead, arsenic, mercury), acids, alkalis or hydrocarbons. Do not give with bowel obstruction or if endoscopy is needed.
Gastric lavage is not routine (aspiration risk 5 to 7%). Consider only within 1 hour of a potentially lethal ingestion, after stabilisation, with the airway protected. No lavage after corrosives or hydrocarbons.
Do not use carriers such as water, saline, oils or potassium permanganate for decontamination, and do not induce vomiting or give cathartics.
Whole bowel irrigation (polyethylene glycol 1.5 to 2 L/h until the effluent is clear) only for sustained-release tablets, iron or drug packets.
Multiple-dose activated charcoal for life-threatening carbamazepine, dapsone, phenobarbital, quinine or theophylline poisoning.
# Antidotes (give early when indicated)
Paracetamol: acetylcysteine. Opioids: naloxone. Organophosphates: atropine with or without pralidoxime. Methanol: fomepizole or ethanol. Snakebite: polyvalent ASV. Indian red scorpion: prazosin and scorpion antivenom. See the specific protocols.
Tricyclics and other sodium channel blockers with QRS above 100 ms, arrhythmia or hypotension: sodium bicarbonate 8.4% 1 to 2 mEq/kg IV bolus.
Calcium channel blocker or beta blocker shock: IV calcium (especially calcium channel blockers), vasopressors, glucagon (beta blockers) and high-dose insulin euglycaemia therapy, regular insulin 1 unit/kg IV bolus then 1 unit/kg/h titrated up to 10 units/kg/h, with dextrose to keep glucose in range; check glucose every 15 to 30 minutes and potassium hourly at first (AHA 2023).
Symptomatic methaemoglobinaemia (dapsone, nitrites, aniline dyes, aluminium phosphide): methylene blue 1 to 2 mg/kg IV over 5 minutes; avoid in G6PD deficiency.
Isoniazid seizures: pyridoxine 1 g per g of isoniazid ingested (5 g IV if the amount is unknown). Cyanide: hydroxocobalamin 5 g IV over 15 minutes.
Digoxin toxicity with potassium above 5 to 5.5 mmol/L, life-threatening arrhythmia or end-organ hypoperfusion: digoxin-specific Fab fragments.
Flumazenil is not for undifferentiated coma: it can precipitate seizures with tricyclic or other proconvulsant co-ingestion, or in benzodiazepine dependence.
Local anaesthetic systemic toxicity: 20% lipid emulsion 1.5 mL/kg IV bolus, then infusion.
# Enhanced elimination and organ support
Extracorporeal treatment is not routine. Consider for severe poisoning with dialysable toxins (small molecule, volume of distribution below 1 to 2 L/kg, low endogenous clearance): toxic alcohols, lithium, salicylates, metformin-associated lactic acidosis, valproate, theophylline, barbiturates.
Urinary alkalinisation (target urine pH 7.5 to 8.5) for moderate to severe salicylate poisoning.
Paraquat: ISCCM suggests early charcoal haemoperfusion (within 4 hours) and withholding oxygen until SpO2 falls below 90%.
Refractory cardiogenic shock from cardiotoxic poisons (aluminium phosphide, beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers): consider early referral for VA-ECMO where available.
# Disposition and specialist advice
ISCCM recommends observing every poisoned patient for at least 24 hours after symptoms resolve; longer after sustained-release drugs or long-acting opioids.
