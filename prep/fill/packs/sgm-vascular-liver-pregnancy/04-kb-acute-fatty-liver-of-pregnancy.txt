# Acute fatty liver of pregnancy
Acute fatty liver of pregnancy, Reproductive / Obstetrics, Obstetrics and gynaecology.
Aliases: AFLP; acute yellow atrophy of pregnancy.
# Pathophysiology
Acute fatty liver of pregnancy reflects impaired mitochondrial fatty-acid oxidation within hepatocytes, leading to accumulation of microvesicular fat. In many cases the fetus carries a defect of the fatty-acid oxidation pathway, most notably long-chain 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency; the metabolic burden of unmetabolised fatty-acid intermediates from the feto-placental unit is thought to overwhelm the maternal liver, whose oxidative capacity is already stressed in late pregnancy. The resulting hepatocellular dysfunction impairs gluconeogenesis, causing hypoglycaemia; synthesis of clotting factors and fibrinogen, causing coagulopathy; and ureagenesis and clearance, causing hyperammonaemia and encephalopathy. Accumulation of toxic metabolites and endothelial dysfunction contribute to renal impairment and, in severe cases, multiorgan failure. Removal of the fetal and placental metabolic load by delivery allows the maternal liver to recover.
# Prognosis
With early recognition, prompt delivery, and intensive supportive care, maternal survival and hepatic recovery are usual, though maternal and perinatal mortality remain significant when diagnosis is delayed; the liver typically recovers without long-term sequelae, and recurrence is uncommon but recognised, particularly with fatty-acid oxidation defects.
# Clinical pearls
Acute fatty liver of pregnancy is a rare but life-threatening microvesicular fatty infiltration of hepatocytes, typically presenting in the third trimester or early postpartum period
It is associated in a proportion of cases with inherited fetal defects of mitochondrial fatty-acid oxidation, especially long-chain 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency, so testing of the newborn is warranted
