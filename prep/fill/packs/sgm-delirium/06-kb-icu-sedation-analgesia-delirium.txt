# ICU pain, sedation and delirium (PADIS)
Adults
ICU, HDU
Assess pain, sedation and delirium with validated tools, treat pain first (analgesia-first sedation), keep ventilated adults lightly sedated with propofol or dexmedetomidine rather than benzodiazepines, prevent delirium with non-drug bundles, mobilise early and protect sleep.
Aliases: PADIS; ICU sedation; ICU delirium; RASS; CPOT; BPS; ICU liberation; ABCDEF bundle.
# Assess regularly with validated tools
Pain: patient self-report on a 0 to 10 numeric rating scale whenever possible; if unable to self-report, use the Behavioural Pain Scale (BPS, or BPS-NI if not intubated) or the CPOT. Vital signs alone are not valid pain indicators.
Sedation: Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS, -5 to +4) or SAS, each shift and after dose changes.
Delirium: screen each shift with CAM-ICU or ICDSC (4 or more is positive). CAM-ICU is positive with acute change or fluctuation plus inattention, plus either altered level of consciousness or disorganised thinking; assess when RASS is -3 or lighter.
Delirium risk factors: benzodiazepines and blood transfusion (modifiable); older age, dementia, prior coma, emergency surgery or trauma, higher illness severity.
# Analgesia first
Treat pain before sedating, using an assessment-driven, protocol-based stepwise approach. Opioid (fentanyl, morphine) at the lowest effective dose; give analgesia before painful procedures (turning, drains, suctioning, line insertion).
Opioid-sparing adjuncts: paracetamol; low-dose ketamine after surgery; gabapentin, pregabalin or carbamazepine for neuropathic pain; nefopam if available. Avoid routine IV lidocaine and COX-1 selective NSAIDs.
Non-drug measures: massage, music, cold packs for procedures and relaxation techniques.
Renal impairment: avoid morphine infusions (active metabolites accumulate); fentanyl is preferred. Hepatic failure: reduce doses and titrate slowly.
# Light sedation
Target light sedation in ventilated adults (calm, rousable to voice, follows simple commands; for example RASS -1 to 0) unless deep sedation is needed (severe ARDS with neuromuscular blockade, raised intracranial pressure, status epilepticus).
Use daily sedation interruption (spontaneous awakening trial, aiming for eye opening to voice or RASS -1 to +1) paired with a spontaneous breathing trial, or nurse-protocolised targeted sedation.
Choose propofol or dexmedetomidine over benzodiazepines. SCCM 2025 suggests dexmedetomidine over propofol when light sedation or less delirium is the priority; after cardiac surgery, propofol is preferred over benzodiazepines.
Keep benzodiazepines for specific needs: alcohol or benzodiazepine withdrawal, seizures, or when other agents fail or are unavailable.
Bispectral index is most useful during deep sedation or neuromuscular blockade.
Minimise physical restraints; they have not been shown to prevent self-extubation and can increase agitation.
# Delirium prevention and treatment
Multicomponent non-drug bundle: reorientation, glasses and hearing aids, family presence, daytime mobilisation, sleep protection, and fewer deliriogenic drugs (benzodiazepines, anticholinergics).
Do not use haloperidol, atypical antipsychotics, dexmedetomidine, statins or ketamine to prevent delirium.
Search for causes: pain, hypoxia, sepsis, metabolic upset, urinary retention, constipation, withdrawal and drugs.
Antipsychotics: SCCM 2025 makes no recommendation for or against them for delirium. If used for distressing agitation or psychosis, give a short course, monitor QTc and stop before ICU discharge.
Dexmedetomidine is suggested for delirium with agitation that is preventing weaning or extubation.
# Mobility and sleep
Start rehabilitation and mobilisation early; SCCM 2025 suggests enhanced mobilisation (earlier, more frequent, longer or protocolised) over usual care.
Sleep: reduce noise and light, cluster night-time care, use a sleep-promoting protocol; SCCM 2025 suggests melatonin. Do not use propofol to promote sleep.
Daily physiotherapy: once haemodynamically stable, progress ventilated patients from in-bed exercises to sitting, standing and walking.
# ICU Liberation (ABCDEF) bundle and drug safety
A: assess, prevent and manage pain. B: both spontaneous awakening and breathing trials. C: choice of analgesia and sedation. D: delirium assessment, prevention and management. E: early mobility and exercise. F: family engagement.
Propofol: check triglycerides, lactate and CK with prolonged or high-dose use (propofol infusion syndrome); count lipid calories (1.1 kcal/mL for 1% propofol).
Dexmedetomidine: bradycardia and hypotension; avoid a loading dose in unstable patients.
Benzodiazepine and opioid infusions accumulate in renal or hepatic failure and obesity; reassess the need daily.
# Special situations
Elderly: lower doses, high delirium risk; avoid benzodiazepines and anticholinergic drugs.
Alcohol withdrawal: symptom-triggered benzodiazepines plus thiamine; dexmedetomidine only as an adjunct.
Resource-limited ICUs: pain scores, RASS targets, daily SAT/SBT and the non-drug delirium bundle cost little and give most of the benefit.
# Pitfalls
Deep sedation by default, or sedating an agitated patient whose pain is untreated.
