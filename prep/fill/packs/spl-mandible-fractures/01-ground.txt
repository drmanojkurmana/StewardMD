## Mandibular Fractures: Champy Principles, Occlusion and Fixation
Citations and Chapter Sources: Plastic Surgery 5E Vol 3 Ch 12 (Mandible fractures)

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Mandible fractures are anatomically classified into condyle 30%, angle 25%, body 20%, symphysis and parasymphysis 15%, ramus 3%, and coronoid 2%.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 30%, 25%, 20%, 15%, 3%, 2%
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: mandible fractures, condyle, symphysis

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Restoration of pre-traumatic dental occlusion is the cardinal goal of mandible fracture treatment, established intraoperatively via maxillomandibular fixation MMF.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: dental occlusion, maxillomandibular fixation, MMF

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Champy lines of ideal osteosynthesis demonstrate that tensile forces concentrate along the superior border of the mandible while compressive forces concentrate along the inferior border.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Champy lines, osteosynthesis, tensile forces, compressive forces

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: In mandibular angle fractures, a single non-compression monocortical 2.0 mm miniplate placed on the superior border external oblique ridge per Champy technique provides reliable stabilization.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 2.0
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: mandibular angle, Champy technique, miniplate

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Parasymphysis and symphysis fractures experience high torsional torque and require two miniplates: a superior tension band plate and an inferior monocortical or bicortical plate.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: two
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: parasymphysis, torsional torque, tension band

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Favorable angle fractures resist displacement due to masseter and medial pterygoid muscle vectors pulling the distal segment into reduction, whereas unfavorable fractures displace widely.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: favorable fractures, unfavorable fractures, masseter

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Absolute indications for open reduction of condylar fractures include displacement into the middle cranial fossa, impossibility of obtaining occlusion, and lateral extracapsular displacement.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: condylar fractures, open reduction, middle cranial fossa

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Mental nerve sensation paresthesia or hypesthesia occurs commonly in body fractures traversing the mental foramen between premolars.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: mental nerve, paresthesia, mental foramen


Comprehensive Textbook Context:
Surgical planning in Mandibular Fractures and Fixation requires meticulous anatomical grounding, precise knowledge of blood supply, innervation, fascial planes, and tissue biomechanics.
Operative interventions balance functional restoration of speech, swallowing, mastication, sensory discrimination, and locomotion with aesthetic symmetry and minimal donor site morbidity.
Preoperative evaluation integrates acoustic Doppler, computed tomography angiography, indocyanine green fluorescence imaging, and patient-specific risk factor optimization including smoking cessation, strict glycemic control, and blood pressure management.
Postoperative care prioritizes early mobilization, monitoring of capillary refill, tissue turgor, pinprick bleeding, active rehabilitation, and vigilant surveillance for hematoma, seroma, infection, or microvascular thrombosis.
