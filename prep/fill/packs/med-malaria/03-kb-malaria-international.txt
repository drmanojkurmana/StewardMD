# Malaria: uncomplicated and severe (WHO)
Adults and children
Outpatient, emergency department, ward, ICU
Confirm every case by microscopy or RDT. Uncomplicated falciparum: a 3-day ACT (artemether-lumefantrine in the first trimester), plus single-dose primaquine 0.25 mg/kg in low-transmission areas. Vivax and ovale: chloroquine or ACT plus G6PD-guided primaquine or tafenoquine. Severe malaria: IV artesunate 2.4 mg/kg (3 mg/kg under 20 kg) for at least 24 hours, then a full ACT.
Aliases: WHO malaria guidelines 2025; P. falciparum; P. vivax; P. ovale; P. knowlesi; severe malaria; cerebral malaria; ACT; artemether lumefantrine; G6PD; radical cure; imported malaria.
# Recognise and confirm
Suspect malaria in any fever (or recent fever) with residence in or travel to an endemic area; ask about antimalarials taken in the previous 1 to 2 months. Confirm every suspected case by microscopy or RDT before treating.
If the species is not known with certainty, treat as P. falciparum. HRP2-based RDTs can stay positive for weeks after cure, so confirm suspected treatment failure by microscopy.
Severe falciparum malaria (WHO), with asexual parasitaemia and no other cause: GCS below 11 (Blantyre below 3 in children), prostration, more than 2 convulsions in 24 hours, acidosis (base deficit above 8 mEq/L, bicarbonate below 15 mmol/L or venous lactate 5 mmol/L or more), glucose below 40 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L).
Also severe: Hb 5 g/dL or less in children under 12 (below 7 g/dL in adults) with parasites above 10,000/microlitre; creatinine above 3 mg/dL (265 micromol/L) or urea above 20 mmol/L; bilirubin above 3 mg/dL (50 micromol/L) with parasites above 100,000/microlitre.
Also severe: pulmonary oedema (or SpO2 below 92% on air with respiratory rate above 30/min), significant bleeding, shock (systolic BP below 80 mmHg in adults, below 70 mmHg in children, with poor perfusion), or P. falciparum parasitaemia above 10%.
Severe vivax and knowlesi malaria use the same criteria without parasite density thresholds (knowlesi hyperparasitaemia is above 100,000/microlitre). Treat both exactly like severe falciparum malaria.
# Severe malaria: first hours
IV or IM artesunate 2.4 mg/kg per dose (3 mg/kg per dose in children under 20 kg) at 0, 12 and 24 hours, then once daily, for all ages and in all trimesters of pregnancy. Give parenteral treatment for at least 24 hours and until oral intake is tolerated.
Then complete treatment with a full 3-day ACT. Avoid artesunate-mefloquine after a presentation with impaired consciousness (neuropsychiatric reactions).
Artesunate unavailable: IM artemether 3.2 mg/kg (anterior thigh) then 1.6 mg/kg daily, in preference to quinine. Last resort: quinine dihydrochloride 20 mg salt/kg IV over 4 hours, then 10 mg salt/kg 8-hourly as a rate-controlled infusion, never as a bolus.
Pre-referral where full treatment is not possible: single IM artesunate (or IM artemether, then IM quinine). Children under 6 years with no IM option: rectal artesunate 10 mg/kg single dose and refer immediately; rectal artesunate is not for older children or adults.
Secure the airway in coma, weigh the patient, check glucose, Hb, parasite count and renal function at once. Lumbar puncture in unconscious patients to exclude bacterial meningitis once safe.
Start broad-spectrum antibiotics as well in shock, suspected sepsis or aspiration, and in all children with suspected severe malaria in moderate to high transmission areas.
# Uncomplicated P. falciparum
Give one of the WHO-recommended ACTs for 3 days: artemether-lumefantrine (AL), artesunate-amodiaquine, artesunate-mefloquine, dihydroartemisinin-piperaquine, artesunate plus sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine, or artesunate-pyronaridine, as chosen by national policy on local efficacy.
AL twice daily for 3 days (6 doses; first two doses 8 hours apart), after fatty food or milk: under 15 kg 20/120 mg; 15 to under 25 kg 40/240 mg; 25 to under 35 kg 60/360 mg; 35 kg or more 80/480 mg per dose. CDC (2026) extends AL to 5 days for falciparum in US travellers; WHO recommends 3 days.
Low-transmission areas: add primaquine 0.25 mg base/kg as a single dose on day 1 to block transmission; G6PD testing is not required. Not in pregnancy, infants under 1 month, or women breastfeeding infants under 1 month.
Infants under 5 kg: give an ACT at the same mg/kg target as a 5 kg child and monitor response closely. Avoid artesunate-pyronaridine in known liver disease.
HIV: avoid artesunate plus SP with co-trimoxazole, and artesunate-amodiaquine with efavirenz or zidovudine.
Failure within 28 days: use an alternative ACT known to be effective locally. Recurrence after 28 days: treat as a new infection with the first-line ACT, but avoid mefloquine again within 60 days.
# P. vivax and P. ovale (blood stage and radical cure)
Blood stage: chloroquine (25 mg base/kg over 3 days: 10, 10 and 5 mg/kg) where chloroquine-susceptible, or an ACT; use an ACT where chloroquine resistance exists and for mixed or uncertain species. P. malariae and P. knowlesi are treated the same way.
Determine G6PD status before primaquine or tafenoquine. Below 30% activity is deficient; 30 to 70% (heterozygous females) is intermediate and detectable only by quantitative or semi-quantitative tests.
Radical cure (WHO 2024): primaquine total 7 mg/kg, as 0.5 mg/kg daily for 14 days, or 1 mg/kg daily for 7 days only if G6PD activity is 70% or more. In South Asia and the Americas a 3.5 mg/kg total (0.25 mg/kg daily for 14 days or 0.5 mg/kg daily for 7 days) may be used.
Qualitative test normal, or intermediate activity (30 to 70%): primaquine 0.5 mg/kg daily for 14 or 7 days, with precautions. G6PD deficient (below 30%): primaquine 0.75 mg/kg once weekly for 8 weeks under medical supervision.
Tafenoquine (single dose with chloroquine on day 1 or 2) is a WHO option in South America for patients aged 2 years or more with G6PD activity 70% or more on a quantitative or semi-quantitative test. Not with an ACT, and not in pregnancy or lactation.
No G6PD test available: decide on primaquine by weighing relapse risk against haemolysis risk and local G6PD prevalence; warn about dark urine, jaundice or breathlessness and stop at once if they occur.
# Supportive care and monitoring (severe malaria)
Glucose at least 4-hourly, especially if unconscious. Treat below 40 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L), or below 54 mg/dL (3 mmol/L) in children under 5, then maintain with glucose-containing infusion. Hypoglycaemia recurs with quinine and in pregnancy.
Assess fluids individually: adults easily develop pulmonary oedema, children are more often dry. Rapid bolus infusion of crystalloid or colloid is contraindicated.
Transfuse for Hb below 5 g/dL (haematocrit below 15%) in children in high-transmission settings, or below 7 g/dL (haematocrit below 20%) in low-transmission settings, adapted to the patient.
Early haemofiltration or haemodialysis for established AKI or severe metabolic acidosis. Treat seizures with IV or rectal benzodiazepines; do not give prophylactic phenobarbital.
Do not give corticosteroids, heparin, adrenaline, low-molecular-weight dextran, pentoxifylline, aspirin or albumin boluses. WHO makes no recommendation for exchange transfusion and CDC advises against it.
Monitor parasitaemia daily. Check Hb weekly for 4 weeks after IV artesunate (CDC) to detect delayed haemolysis, particularly after hyperparasitaemia.
