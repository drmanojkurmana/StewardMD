# Heritable pulmonary arterial hypertension (BMPR2)
Heritable pulmonary arterial hypertension (BMPR2), Respiratory / Pulmonary vascular, Pulmonary Hypertension.
Aliases: hereditary PAH; familial pulmonary arterial hypertension; HPAH; BMPR2-associated PAH.
# Pathophysiology
Heritable pulmonary arterial hypertension arises when a germline mutation, most commonly a loss-of-function variant in the bone morphogenetic protein receptor type 2 (BMPR2) gene, impairs BMP signalling in the pulmonary vasculature and shifts the balance toward proliferation over apoptosis. Penetrance is incomplete, so a second hit from environmental, hormonal, or additional genetic factors is generally required for disease to manifest, which explains the female preponderance and variable family expression. The resulting pulmonary arteriolar remodelling comprises intimal proliferation, medial hypertrophy, adventitial thickening, and plexiform lesions that progressively narrow and obliterate small vessels. Rising pulmonary vascular resistance imposes a chronic pressure load on the right ventricle, which hypertrophies and ultimately dilates and fails, producing the pre-capillary haemodynamic profile seen at catheterisation.
# Prognosis
The natural history is one of progressive right ventricular failure and premature death, though modern targeted therapy delivered through specialist centres has improved survival and functional status. Heritable BMPR2 disease often behaves more aggressively than idiopathic disease, and lung transplantation remains an option for those who progress despite combination therapy.
# Clinical pearls
A group 1 pre-capillary pulmonary arterial hypertension defined haemodynamically by a raised mean pulmonary artery pressure with an elevated pulmonary vascular resistance and a normal or low pulmonary artery wedge pressure at right heart catheterisation
