# Pulmonary hypertension: recognition, referral and basic management
Adults
Outpatient clinic, ward
PH is mPAP above 20 mmHg at right heart catheterisation. Estimate probability by echo (TR velocity plus RV and PA signs), classify into groups 1 to 5, treat the underlying heart or lung disease, screen for CTEPH with a V/Q scan, and refer suspected PAH or CTEPH to a PH centre (ESC/ERS 2022).
Aliases: PH; PAH; pulmonary arterial hypertension; CTEPH; chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension; cor pulmonale; right heart failure; raised PA pressure; ESC/ERS 2022 PH.
# Recognise
Symptoms are non-specific: progressive exertional breathlessness, fatigue, exertional chest pain, presyncope or syncope; later ankle oedema, ascites, raised JVP, loud P2, RV heave and a TR murmur.
Haemodynamic definition (RHC): mPAP above 20 mmHg at rest. Pre-capillary: PAWP 15 mmHg or less and PVR above 2 Wood units. Isolated post-capillary: PAWP above 15 mmHg and PVR 2 WU or less; combined: PAWP above 15 mmHg and PVR above 2 WU.
Groups: 1 PAH (idiopathic, heritable, drug or toxin, connective tissue disease, HIV, portal hypertension, congenital heart disease); 2 left heart disease; 3 lung disease or hypoxia; 4 CTEPH and other pulmonary artery obstruction; 5 unclear or multifactorial.
Left heart and lung disease (groups 2 and 3) are the commonest causes; PAH and CTEPH are rarer but have specific, effective treatment.
Warning signs needing immediate referral to a PH centre: rapid progression or WHO functional class III to IV, syncope, signs of right heart failure, low cardiac output, poorly tolerated arrhythmias.
# Investigations
First line: ECG, chest X-ray, BNP or NT-proBNP, spirometry and lung volumes with DLCO, arterial blood gas, and transthoracic echocardiography.
Echo probability: peak TR velocity above 3.4 m/s is high; 2.9 to 3.4 m/s is intermediate (high if other PH signs present); 2.8 m/s or less or unmeasurable is low (intermediate if other PH signs present).
Other echo PH signs (need at least two categories): ventricles (RV/LV basal ratio above 1.0, septal flattening, TAPSE/sPAP below 0.55 mm/mmHg); PA (RVOT acceleration time below 105 ms or notching, PR velocity above 2.2 m/s, PA above 25 mm); IVC above 21 mm with poor collapse, RA area above 18 cm2.
V/Q scan in unexplained PH to screen for CTEPH; CT pulmonary angiography complements it but is less sensitive for chronic clot.
Look for causes: HRCT chest, autoimmune serology (ANA, anti-centromere, anti-Scl-70), HIV, liver tests and abdominal ultrasound for portal hypertension, thyroid function, and a sleep study if nocturnal hypoventilation is suspected.
Right heart catheterisation at a PH centre confirms the diagnosis and type before any PAH-specific drug; vasoreactivity testing in idiopathic, heritable or drug-associated PAH.
# General measures (all groups)
Treat the underlying cause: optimise heart failure and valve disease in group 2; treat lung disease, hypoxaemia and sleep-disordered breathing in group 3.
Diuretics for fluid retention and right heart failure, with monitoring of renal function and electrolytes.
Long-term oxygen if arterial PO2 is below 8 kPa (60 mmHg) at rest; in-flight oxygen for patients on oxygen or with PaO2 below 8 kPa at sea level.
Correct iron deficiency; vaccinate against influenza, pneumococcus and SARS-CoV-2; supervised exercise training for stable PAH on medical therapy.
Women with PAH should avoid pregnancy (high maternal mortality): provide effective contraception and counselling; endothelin receptor antagonists are teratogenic.
Do not use PAH-specific drugs in group 2 PH. In PAH, avoid ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNI, SGLT2 inhibitors, beta-blockers and ivabradine unless another condition requires them.
# PAH and CTEPH (PH centre)
Positive vasoreactivity test (idiopathic, heritable or drug-associated PAH): high-dose calcium channel blocker with close reassessment of response.
Low or intermediate risk PAH without cardiopulmonary comorbidity: initial combination of an endothelin receptor antagonist and a PDE5 inhibitor (for example ambrisentan plus tadalafil, or macitentan plus tadalafil).
High-risk PAH: initial combination including an IV or SC prostacyclin analogue; discuss lung transplantation when response is inadequate.
PAH with cardiopulmonary comorbidities (older age, obesity, diabetes, coronary disease, hypertension): consider initial monotherapy with a PDE5 inhibitor or an endothelin receptor antagonist.
Reassess every 3 to 6 months with the 4-strata tool: WHO functional class, 6-minute walk distance (above 440 m low risk, below 165 m high risk) and NT-proBNP (below 300 ng/L low risk, above 1100 ng/L high risk). Escalate therapy if not low risk.
CTEPH: lifelong therapeutic anticoagulation; pulmonary endarterectomy for operable disease; balloon pulmonary angioplasty or riociguat for inoperable or residual PH.
Group 3: inhaled treprostinil may be considered in PH with interstitial lung disease. Ambrisentan is not recommended in PH with IPF, nor riociguat in PH with idiopathic interstitial pneumonia.
# Referral and admission
Refer to a PH centre: intermediate or high echo probability with PAH risk factors (connective tissue disease, especially systemic sclerosis; HIV; portal hypertension; family history; congenital heart disease) or a history of PE.
Refer when PH seems out of proportion to left heart or lung disease (for example PVR above 5 WU or severe RV dysfunction).
Admit for right heart failure with hypotension, syncope, arrhythmia or worsening hypoxaemia. Avoid intubation where possible (RV collapse on induction) and involve the PH centre early.
# Special situations
Systemic sclerosis: yearly assessment of PAH risk (echo, DLCO, NT-proBNP).
After acute PE: persistent breathlessness or exercise limitation after 3 months of anticoagulation should prompt echo and a V/Q scan for CTEPH.
Drugs and toxins linked to PAH include methamphetamine, dasatinib, and anorexigens such as fenfluramine and benfluorex.
Pregnancy in a woman with PAH: urgent joint care with a PH centre and expert obstetric and anaesthetic teams.
In India, rheumatic mitral valve disease (group 2) and post-tuberculosis or COPD-related lung disease (group 3) are frequent causes; treat these before labelling PAH.
# Pitfalls
Diagnosing PH from echo alone: echo only gives a probability; RHC confirms.
Starting sildenafil or other PAH drugs for group 2 or group 3 PH without PH-centre assessment.
Excluding CTEPH on CT pulmonary angiography alone; the V/Q scan is more sensitive.
Combining a PDE5 inhibitor with nitrates or riociguat (severe hypotension).
Aggressive fluid loading or routine intubation in decompensated right heart failure.
# Drugs
Sildenafil, 20 mg orally three times daily, PAH, PH-centre initiated. Contraindicated with nitrates or riociguat.
Tadalafil
40 mg orally once daily
Start 20 mg daily in mild to moderate renal or hepatic impairment; avoid in severe impairment. Not with nitrates or riociguat.
Ambrisentan, 5 mg orally once daily, increase to 10 mg once daily if tolerated, Teratogenic: exclude pregnancy and use contraception. Peripheral oedema, anaemia. Not for PH with IPF.
Macitentan, 10 mg orally once daily, Teratogenic. Check haemoglobin and liver tests.
Riociguat
# Dose
1 mg orally three times daily, increased by 0.5 mg every 2 weeks to a maximum of 2.5 mg three times daily
Inoperable or residual CTEPH, and PAH. Never with PDE5 inhibitors or nitrates. Hold up-titration if SBP is below 95 mmHg.
Furosemide, 20 to 40 mg orally or IV daily, titrated to weight and congestion, Right heart failure with fluid retention; monitor potassium and creatinine.
Anticoagulation for CTEPH, Warfarin to INR 2.0 to 3.0, or a DOAC at full treatment dose, lifelong, Use a VKA, not a DOAC, in antiphospholipid syndrome.
