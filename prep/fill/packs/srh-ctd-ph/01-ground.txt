Pulmonary hypertension in connective tissue disease is a devastating vessel complication. Group one arterial hypertension strikes systemic sclerosis, mixed connective tissue disease, and lupus, killing faster than idiopathic disease. Connective tissue vessels carry worse fates.

Proof demands right heart catheterization under twenty twenty two ESC ERS rules: mean artery pressure above twenty, wedge fifteen or below, resistance above two Wood units. Catheter numbers crown the diagnosis.

Screening runs yearly in every scleroderma patient through the two-step DETECT algorithm (bedside variables, lung function, NT-proBNP blood test, ECG) or resting echocardiography showing tricuspid jet velocity above two point eight meters per second. Yearly DETECT or echo hunts early pressures.

Lung function whispers the clue: isolated disproportionate fall in carbon monoxide diffusing capacity below sixty percent predicted with preserved vital capacity, giving an FVC to DLCO ratio above one point six to one point eight. Low diffusion with kept volumes flags vessels.

Low-to-intermediate risk disease starts with upfront oral pairing: an endothelin receptor antagonist (ambrisentan, macitentan, or bosentan) plus a phosphodiesterase five inhibitor (tadalafil or sildenafil). Two pills block two pathways from day one.

High-risk patients (functional class four, cardiac index below two point zero, failing right ventricle) need upfront combinations built around continuous prostacyclin: intravenous epoprostenol or subcutaneous treprostinil. Failing hearts need drips, not pills.

Group three hypertension from fibrosing lung disease often coexists: hypoxia and destroyed capillary beds raise pressures alongside interstitial disease. Management optimizes the underlying lung disease with supplemental oxygen, since systemic vasodilators risk worsening ventilation-perfusion mismatch and deepening hypoxemia. Fix lungs first; dilators can mismatch.

Inhaled treprostinil is FDA approved for lung-disease-associated pulmonary hypertension on the INCREASE trial: six-minute walk distance improved and NT-proBNP fell without worsening mismatch. Inhaled drug walks further without mismatch.
