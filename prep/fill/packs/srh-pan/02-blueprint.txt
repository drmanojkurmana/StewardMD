# Polyarteritis nodosa (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-pan: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN) is a systemic necrotizing arteritis predominantly affecting medium- and small-sized muscular arteries, characteristically sparing the pulmonary arterial circulation and the microvasculature (arterioles, capillaries, venules), with no association with ANCA.

The 2022 ACR/EULAR classification criteria for polyarteritis nodosa require biopsy confirmation of necrotizing arteritis or angiographic aneurysms/occlusions with accumulated score >=5 points across weighted domains including mononeuritis multiplex, motor neuropathy, testicular pain, livedo reticularis, renal insufficiency, and abdominal symptoms.

Secondary polyarteritis nodosa is historically linked to active Hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection in up to 30% of global cases, where circulating HBsAg-anti-HBs immune complexes deposit in arterial walls within the first 6 months of acute HBV infection.

Renal involvement in PAN is driven by renal artery vasculitis and ischemia causing severe renin-mediated renovascular hypertension and renal infarcts; true glomerulonephritis is absent because glomerular capillaries are small vessels spared by PAN.

Visceral catheter angiography or CT angiography demonstrates microaneurysms (typically 1 to 5 mm in diameter) and stenoses affecting branching points of the renal, mesenteric, and hepatic medium-sized arteries, creating the classic 'beads-on-a-string' appearance.

Gastrointestinal involvement presents with postprandial abdominal angina, bowel ischemia, transmural infarction, and spontaneous visceral perforation, representing a major contributor to vasculitis-associated mortality and scoring on the Five-Factor Score.

First-line induction therapy for severe idiopathic PAN (FFS >=1) combines high-dose systemic glucocorticoids (prednisone 1 mg/kg/day or IV pulse methylprednisolone) with intravenous cyclophosphamide; mild non-organ-threatening disease (FFS = 0) can be initiated on glucocorticoid monotherapy.

Management of HBV-associated PAN fundamentally differs from idiopathic PAN: a short, time-limited course of glucocorticoids and plasma exchange is combined with immediate potent antiviral therapy (entecavir or tenofovir), avoiding long-term cytotoxic immunosuppression which promotes viral replication and hepatic failure.
