# ADA2 deficiency vasculitis (paediatric PAN-like)
ADA2 deficiency vasculitis (paediatric PAN-like), Rheumatology / Paediatrics, Paediatric Rheumatology.
Aliases: DADA2; deficiency of adenosine deaminase 2; ADA2 deficiency; monogenic polyarteritis nodosa-like vasculopathy.
# Pathophysiology
Deficiency of adenosine deaminase 2 results from biallelic mutations in the ADA2 gene, reducing the activity of a secreted enzyme important for endothelial integrity and the differentiation of monocytes and macrophages. Loss of ADA2 skews monocytes towards a proinflammatory phenotype with excess tumour necrosis factor, causing endothelial damage and a systemic vasculopathy affecting small and medium vessels. This produces the polyarteritis-nodosa-like vasculitis, cutaneous livedo and, through injury to small cerebral arteries, the characteristic recurrent lacunar strokes, while impaired haematopoiesis and immune function account for cytopenias and immunodeficiency.
# Prognosis
Untreated disease carries substantial morbidity from recurrent strokes and vasculitis, but TNF inhibition markedly reduces stroke recurrence and controls inflammation. Prognosis is worse when severe haematological or immune involvement dominates, in which case stem cell transplantation may be curative.
# Clinical pearls
A monogenic autoinflammatory vasculopathy caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in the ADA2 gene (formerly CECR1)
Presents in childhood with a polyarteritis-nodosa-like small- and medium-vessel vasculitis, livedo racemosa and recurrent fevers
Early-onset ischaemic and haemorrhagic strokes, often affecting the deep brain (lacunar strokes), are a hallmark and can be recurrent
A wide phenotype also includes immunodeficiency with recurrent infections and low immunoglobulins, and haematological features such as cytopenias, bone marrow failure or features resembling pure red cell aplasia
Diagnosis rests on genetic testing for ADA2 variants together with demonstration of low serum ADA2 enzyme activity
TNF inhibition is highly effective at preventing strokes and controlling vasculitis and is preferred over corticosteroids for long-term control
Antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy is generally avoided because of the risk of haemorrhagic stroke, and haematopoietic stem cell transplantation is considered for severe haematological or immune disease

# Additional investigations
Genetic testing for biallelic ADA2 variants
Serum ADA2 enzyme activity assay
Brain MRI and MR angiography to detect lacunar strokes and vasculopathy
Inflammatory markers and full blood count for cytopenias
Immunoglobulin levels and lymphocyte subsets to assess immunodeficiency
Skin biopsy of vasculitic or livedoid lesions where indicated
Bone marrow examination when marrow failure is suspected
Red flags: Acute focal neurological deficit indicating stroke; Recurrent strokes despite treatment; Severe cytopenias or bone marrow failure; Serious or recurrent infection reflecting immunodeficiency; Progressive systemic vasculitis with organ involvement.
# Pitfalls
Diagnosing idiopathic polyarteritis nodosa without testing for ADA2 deficiency in a child
Using anticoagulation or antiplatelet agents that increase haemorrhagic stroke risk
Relying on corticosteroids alone rather than starting TNF inhibition for durable control
Missing the haematological and immunodeficiency components of the phenotype
Overlooking a family history of early strokes or unexplained vasculitis
# Management
Start tumour necrosis factor inhibition as first-line therapy to prevent strokes and control the polyarteritis-nodosa-like vasculitis
Avoid antiplatelet and anticoagulant drugs for stroke prevention because of the risk of haemorrhagic stroke, prioritising TNF blockade
Use corticosteroids to control acute flares while TNF inhibition takes effect
Provide immunoglobulin replacement and infection prophylaxis for immunodeficiency and hypogammaglobulinaemia
Monitor with brain MRI and MR angiography, blood counts and inflammatory markers for recurrent lacunar strokes and haematological involvement
Consider haematopoietic stem cell transplantation for severe bone marrow failure or refractory disease, which can be curative
Offer genetic counselling and cascade family testing for this autosomal recessive ADA2 disorder, managed through paediatric rheumatology
