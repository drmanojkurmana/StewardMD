Conventional osteosarcoma is the commonest primary malignant bone tumour at 10 to 20 years, arising in the metaphysis of long bones with distal femur 45 percent and proximal tibia 20 percent. Radiographs show cloud-like osteoid matrix, cortical destruction, sunburst periosteal reaction and Codman triangle. Ewing sarcoma carries the EWSR1-FLI1 translocation t(11;22), affects diaphysis or metadiaphysis and pelvis, and shows permeative moth-eaten destruction with onion-skin lamellated periostitis and a large soft tissue mass. Langerhans cell histiocytosis forms punched-out lytic lesions without sclerotic margins, beveled-edge skull defects and vertebra plana. Aneurysmal bone cyst is expansile, eccentric and metaphyseal with septations and multiple fluid-fluid levels on T2 MRI. Non-ossifying fibroma is eccentric and multilocular with scalloped sclerotic border in distal femur or proximal tibia, called fibrous cortical defect under 2 cm. Osteochondroma shows cortical and medullary continuity with parent bone and points away from the joint. Non-accidental trauma, skeletal dysplasias including developmental hip dysplasia, Perthes disease and slipped capital femoral epiphysis complete this paediatric musculoskeletal module for examination purposes.
 Non-accidental trauma shows high specificity injuries including classic metaphyseal corner and bucket handle fractures, posterior rib fractures, scapular and spinous process fractures, and complex skull fractures in infants under 3 years, needing full skeletal survey and retinal examination. Developmental dysplasia of the hip uses Graf alpha angle over 60 degrees as normal on ultrasound before ossification, acetabular index on radiographs, and Ortolani and Barlow manoeuvres clinically. Achondroplasia shows rhizomelic shortening, trident hand, champagne glass pelvis and decreasing interpedicular distance caudally. Legg-Calve-Perthes disease fragments and flattens the femoral head epiphysis in boys of 4 to 8 years, while slipped capital femoral epiphysis displaces the epiphysis medially and posteriorly in obese adolescents, drawn by Klein line missing the lateral head, needing urgent in situ pinning and prophylactic contralateral assessment.
 Skeletal survey timing, repeat films at 2 weeks and ophthalmology review increase detection of occult fractures in suspected abuse cases.
