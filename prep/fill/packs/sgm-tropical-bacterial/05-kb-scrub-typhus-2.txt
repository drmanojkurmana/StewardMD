# Scrub Typhus
Scrub Typhus, Tropical Fever.
# Pathogens
Orientia tsutsugamushi is the sole cause of scrub typhus; the clinical diagnosis (eschar, lymphadenopathy, exposure history in an endemic rural/peri-urban setting) is often sufficiently characteristic to justify empiric treatment before serology returns.

# Investigations
Careful skin examination for eschar
Eschar (a painless, often overlooked necrotic lesion at the chigger bite site) is highly specific for scrub typhus when found — actively look in skin folds, axillae, groin.
Scrub typhus IgM ELISA / Weil-Felix (less specific)
Confirms diagnosis serologically, though treatment should not wait for results when clinical suspicion is high.

# Pathophysiology
Scrub typhus is caused by Orientia tsutsugamushi, an obligate intracellular organism placed in a separate genus within the family Rickettsiaceae. It differs substantially from Rickettsia species both genetically and in cell-wall composition, notably lacking lipopolysaccharide. Disease results from infection of vascular endothelial cells with increased vascular permeability (a mechanism shared across the rickettsioses). The organism is maintained in nature by transovarial transmission within trombiculid mites; infected larval mites (chiggers, the only mite stage that feeds on a vertebrate host) inoculate the organism into the skin during feeding. Severe disease reflects widespread vascular injury, producing the encephalitis and interstitial pneumonia that characterize advanced cases. Closely related Orientia species (O. chuto, Candidatus O. chiloensis) cause scrub typhus-like illness in newly recognized foci.
# Prognosis
Illness ranges from mild and self-limiting to fatal. The case-fatality rate for untreated classic cases is about 6% and would likely be lower if all mild cases were captured; overall reported mortality varies widely by location (cited as 1% to 70%). Risk of death rises with increasing age and with the presence of myocarditis, delirium, pneumonitis, or signs of hemorrhage. Recognized in time, the disease responds well to appropriate antibiotic therapy.
