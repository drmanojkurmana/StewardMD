Rigid telescopes for cystoscopy and laparoscopy gather light through the Hopkins rod lens optical system, in which long glass rods divided by thin air spaces replace classic curved lenses. The design descends from Harold Hopkins and gives brighter, sharper views through slimmer barrels than older optics allowed.

Standard rigid scopes come in four viewing angles. The 0 degree lens looks straight ahead for diagnostic cystoscopy and ureterorenoscopy, the 30 degree lens is the everyday inspector, the 70 degree lens covers the lateral bladder wall and dome, and the 120 degree lens looks backward at the bladder neck. Rotating the light post sweeps the field without shifting the sheath.

Monopolar electrosurgery drives current from the active loop through the patient to a dispersive return electrode pad on the skin, so the whole body joins the circuit. Stray current through cracked insulation or metal to metal contact, called direct coupling, can burn bowel far outside the camera view.

Bipolar instruments mount both electrodes on the tip, so current crosses only gripped tissue and no patient pad is needed. Because the circuit is local, saline replaces glycine as irrigant, which removes the dilutional sodium crash of classic TUR syndrome and makes bipolar the modern standard for prostate resection.

Carbon dioxide is the insufflation gas for pneumoperitoneum because blood dissolves it quickly, the lungs clear it fast, and it cannot ignite near diathermy. Working pressure stays at 12 mmHg to 15 mmHg, since lower pressure collapses the view while higher pressure compresses veins and reduces renal perfusion.

Long gas filled operations acidify blood as the peritoneum absorbs carbon dioxide across hours of surgery. Rising arterial CO2 with falling pH means hypercarbia with respiratory acidosis, and the anaesthetist answers by raising minute ventilation to wash the gas out through the lungs.

The first port is the most dangerous step of laparoscopy. The Hasson open technique cuts down to peritoneum under direct vision and docks a blunt trocar without any blind needle puncture, which makes it the default after prior abdominal surgery where scars plaster bowel to the wall.

Ultrasonic shears vibrate longitudinally at 55.5 kHz to denature protein and weld vessels up to 5 mm shut with lateral thermal spread under 1 mm. The narrow heat footprint spares nearby nerves and ureters while the target seals, which suits dissection near delicate structures.
