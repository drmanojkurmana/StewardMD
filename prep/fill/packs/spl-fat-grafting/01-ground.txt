## Autologous Fat Grafting: Coleman Technique and Adipose Stem Cells
Citations and Chapter Sources: Plastic Surgery 5E Vol 1 Ch 22 (Repair and grafting fat and adipose tissue)

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Coleman technique for autologous fat transfer standardises low-pressure manual aspiration with 10 mL syringes, low-speed centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 3 minutes, and micro-droplet reinjection.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 10, 3000, 3
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Coleman technique, fat transfer, centrifugation

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Centrifugation separates the lipoaspirate into three distinct layers: an oily top layer of free lipids, a middle purified condensed fat graft, and a bottom aqueous and blood layer.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: three
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: lipoaspirate, free lipids, condensed fat

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Adipose-derived stem cells ADSCs within the stromal vascular fraction SVF possess multipotent differentiation capacity and secrete potent pro-angiogenic cytokines VEGF and FGF.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: adipose-derived stem cells, ADSCs, stromal vascular fraction, VEGF

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Fat graft survival follows Eto and Yoshimura tri-zonal model consisting of an outer surviving zone under 300 micrometers, an intermediate regenerating zone, and a necrotic central zone.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 300
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Eto and Yoshimura, surviving zone, regenerating zone, necrotic zone

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Fat parcels must be deposited in multi-planar micro-droplets under 0.1 mL to maximize the recipient surface-to-volume ratio and facilitate vascular ingrowth.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 0.1
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: micro-droplets, vascular ingrowth

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Long-term volumetric retention of autologous fat grafts typically ranges from 40% to 70%, with graft resorption peaking within the first 3 to 6 months.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 40%, 70%, 3, 6
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: volumetric retention, graft resorption

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Clinical applications include facial rejuvenation, correction of contour deformities, post-mastectomy radiation fibrosis, and autologous breast augmentation.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: facial rejuvenation, radiation fibrosis, breast augmentation

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Complications of fat grafting include oil cyst formation, microcalcifications that may mimic malignancy on mammography, fat necrosis, and devastating intravascular fat embolism.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: oil cyst, microcalcifications, fat embolism


Comprehensive Textbook Context:
Surgical planning in Fat Grafting and Regenerative Surgery requires meticulous anatomical grounding, precise knowledge of blood supply, innervation, fascial planes, and tissue biomechanics.
Operative interventions balance functional restoration of speech, swallowing, mastication, sensory discrimination, and locomotion with aesthetic symmetry and minimal donor site morbidity.
Preoperative evaluation integrates acoustic Doppler, computed tomography angiography, indocyanine green fluorescence imaging, and patient-specific risk factor optimization including smoking cessation, strict glycemic control, and blood pressure management.
Postoperative care prioritizes early mobilization, monitoring of capillary refill, tissue turgor, pinprick bleeding, active rehabilitation, and vigilant surveillance for hematoma, seroma, infection, or microvascular thrombosis.
