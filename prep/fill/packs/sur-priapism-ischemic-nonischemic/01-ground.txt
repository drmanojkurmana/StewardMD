Ischemic priapism is a compartment emergency of the penis. Low-flow veno-occlusive erection is painful and rigid, with no cavernosal arterial inflow and progressive cavernosal acidosis and hypoxia. Trapped deoxygenated blood turns the corpora into a closed compartment like a limb under a tight cast, and time directly predicts tissue death.

Blood gas proves the type. Cavernosal sampling in ischemic priapism shows profound acidosis and hypoxia with pH below 7.25, pO2 below 30 mmHg, and pCO2 above 60 mmHg, a profile identical to ischemic venous blood. Non-ischemic high-flow blood looks arterial instead, so the gas result alone separates the emergency from the non-emergency at the bedside.

High-flow priapism is a different disease. Non-ischemic arterial priapism is typically painless and non-rigid, caused by blunt perineal trauma that tears an arteriole and creates an uncontrolled cavernosal arteriolar-sinusoidal fistula. Oxygenated blood flows straight through without trapping, so there is no acidosis, no pain, and no urgency, only a prolonged semi-erection.

First treatment is aspiration plus phenylephrine. The corpora are aspirated and irrigated with saline to remove acidotic blood, then phenylephrine, an alpha-1 agonist, is injected intracavernosally at 100 to 500 mcg per mL every 3 to 5 minutes to constrict the inflow. Blood pressure and pulse are watched throughout because systemic absorption can spike pressure, especially in older men.

When drugs fail, distal shunts drain through the glans. Percutaneous distal shunts such as the Winter and Ebbehoj procedures drive a large-bore biopsy needle or scalpel from the glans into the corpora, creating a channel between glans and cavernosa. Blood escapes through the new window, detumescence follows, and the puncture heals with little morbidity.

Open distal shunts remove a core of tunica. The Al-Ghorab and Burnett procedures excise a 5 mm circular or elliptical core of distal tunica albuginea through the glans, making a larger permanent window for outflow. They are chosen when needle shunts fail to hold, trading a bigger glans wound for reliable drainage.

Proximal shunts reroute blood at the base. The Quackels corporospongiosal anastomosis joins corpus cavernosum to spongiosum at the perineum, while the Grayhack shunt connects saphenous vein to corpus cavernosum. Both move blood out of the rigid corpora into low-pressure systems, and both are kept for cases where distal shunting cannot be done or has failed.

Delay destroys the erectile tissue. Ischemic priapism beyond 36 to 48 hours causes complete cavernosal smooth muscle necrosis and fibrosis, leaving a scarred non-functional penis. Immediate insertion of a penile prosthesis in this window resolves the priapism and holds length, preventing the shortening that fibrosis would otherwise impose.
