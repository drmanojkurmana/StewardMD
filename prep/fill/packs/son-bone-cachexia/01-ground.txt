Bone-modifying agents and cancer cachexia: grounding notes

Bisphosphonate action on bone
- Nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates have a strong attraction for mineral exposed on bone surfaces.
- Osteoclasts, the cells that resorb bone, take the agent up while they dissolve the surface.
- Proposed intracellular step: inhibition of farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase in the mevalonate route, which lowers protein prenylation and pushes the osteoclast toward programmed death.
- Net result: fewer active osteoclasts, slower bone breakdown, fewer bone complications.

Skeletal-related events
- Skeletal-related events (SREs) bundle bone pain needing opioids, radiotherapy or surgery aimed at bone, high blood calcium, pathological fracture, and spinal cord compression.
- Bone-directed therapy does not treat the cancer itself, but it lowers the chance of these events.
- Start a bone-modifying agent when bone spread is diagnosed, then continue alongside systemic treatment.
- Castration-resistant prostate cancer trials: intravenous zoledronic acid given every 3 to 4 weeks cut SREs by 25 percent against comparison.

Kidney limits
- Bisphosphonate doses must follow renal function, since kidney injury can occur.
- Intravenous aminobisphosphonates are contraindicated when glomerular filtration rate falls under 35 mL/min/1.73 m2.
- In advanced kidney disease, lower-dose denosumab is an option for hypercalcemia because aminobisphosphonates are off the table.
- Pamidronate for hypercalcemia: 60 to 90 mg intravenously over roughly 2 to 4 hours.

Denosumab
- A fully human monoclonal antibody against RANKL, the signal that drives osteoclast formation and activity.
- Not a bisphosphonate, and it does not enter bone cells through the same route.
- Cleared by the reticuloendothelial system instead of the kidney.
- Phase 3 comparison with zoledronate: grade 3 or higher hypocalcemia in 5 percent on denosumab, 1 percent on zoledronate.

Cachexia mediators
- Multifactorial: muscle and fat loss, metabolic disturbance, lower intake.
- Calorie replacement alone does not reverse it.
- Tumor necrosis factor alpha: lipolysis, muscle breakdown, higher glucose turnover; most evidence comes from animal models.
- Interferon gamma: more lipolysis, less protein synthesis.
- Interleukin 1: anorexia, early satiety, release of interleukin 6.
- Interleukin 6: severe wasting, measurable in serum.

Leptin
- Adipocyte-derived hormone that controls fat stores.
- Lowers appetite and raises resting energy expenditure through hypothalamic signals.
- Stays persistently low in cachectic patients, read as tumors mimicking its signal and sustaining anorexia.

Anabolic steroids
- Androgens act through the androgen receptor, a steroid hormone nuclear receptor.
- In systematic reviews, testosterone raised lean body mass and muscle strength in men with HIV and weight loss.
- Oxandrolone is an oral androgen with a liver toxicity risk.
- In cancer, the nutrition benefit is unproven, though symptom relief is possible.
