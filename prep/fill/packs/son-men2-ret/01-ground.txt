Grounding notes: RET-driven thyroid cancer and MEN syndromes

Gene and syndromes
Germline changes in the RET gene drive multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN 2). Three forms are recognized: MEN 2A, MEN 2B (also called MEN 3), and familial medullary thyroid cancer with no other tumors. Almost every family with these syndromes carries a RET change, so genetic testing is the route to identifying relatives at risk.

Codon tiers (ATA guideline, tabulated in the 2015 revision)
- Highest tier, scored +++: codon 918 in exon 16, the M918T change.
- High tier, scored ++: codon 634 in exon 11, and codon 883 (A883F) in exon 15.
- Moderate tier, scored +: codons 609, 611, 618 and 620 in exon 10, and others such as 630, 666, 768, 790, 804, 891 and 912.

Timing of prophylactic total thyroidectomy
- Highest tier: as soon as possible, and no later than age 1 year.
- High tier: by age 5.
- Moderate tier: by age 5 at the latest, timed by calcitonin and a joint decision with the family. Later surgery is reasonable if calcitonin and neck ultrasound are normal.
Central neck dissection is usually added to the total thyroidectomy.

Screening before surgery
- Pheochromocytoma must be excluded before any thyroid operation, and also in pregnancy planning.
- Pheochromocytoma screening starts at age 11 for the highest and high tiers, and at age 16 for the moderate tier.
- Hyperparathyroidism screening starts at age 11 for the high tier and age 16 for the moderate tier. It is not required for codon 918 carriers, because the feature is absent in MEN 2B.

MEN 2A features
Medullary thyroid carcinoma in about 90 percent. Pheochromocytoma in about half. Primary hyperparathyroidism in 10 to 25 percent. Cutaneous lichen amyloidosis and Hirschsprung disease can occur. Codon 634 accounts for most cases.

MEN 2B features
Medullary thyroid carcinoma in more than 90 percent. Pheochromocytoma in more than half. Mucosal neuromas of the lips, tongue and eyelids, a marfanoid body build, and intestinal nerve dysfunction causing megacolon. Hyperparathyroidism is not a feature. About 95 percent of cases involve codon 918, and MEN 2B makes up about 5 percent of MEN 2 cases.

Cell of origin and iodine
Medullary thyroid carcinoma arises from parafollicular C cells, which make calcitonin. C cells do not concentrate iodine, so radioactive iodine offers no benefit.

Levothyroxine after thyroidectomy
Levothyroxine is given for life to maintain euthyroidism, which means normal thyroid function. In differentiated thyroid cancer, levothyroxine is used to suppress thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) because those tumors respond to TSH. For medullary carcinoma the target is replacement, not suppression.

Follow-up
Calcitonin and carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) are measured 3 to 6 months after surgery. Undetectable values on both mean that recurrence is unlikely. Neck ultrasound and physical checks continue every 6 months while imaging stays clear. A calcitonin doubling time under 6 months carries the highest rate of persistent or recurrent disease.

Systemic therapy
Tyrosine kinase inhibitors include vandetanib, cabozantinib, selpercatinib and pralsetinib. Selpercatinib and pralsetinib are RET-selective. These drugs are reserved for rapidly progressive or symptomatic metastatic disease because of toxicity. A rising calcitonin with negative imaging is managed by observation, periodic imaging and marker checks, not by starting a kinase inhibitor.
