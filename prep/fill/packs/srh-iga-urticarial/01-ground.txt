IgA vasculitis, the Henoch-Schonlein purpura of childhood, is an immune complex small vessel vasculitis planting galactose-deficient IgA one in skin, joints, gut, and glomeruli, striking children aged three to ten. Faulty IgA one seeds four organs in schoolchildren between preschool and preteens.

Its tetrad reads: non-thrombocytopenic palpable purpura favoring legs and buttocks, colicky abdominal pain threatening ileo-ileal intussusception, aching arthralgias and arthritis, plus glomerulonephritis. Purpura with pain, joints, and kidneys complete the classic four-part picture.

Kidney biopsy shows mesangial proliferation with dominant granular mesangial IgA and C three on immunofluorescence, looking identical to primary IgA nephropathy (Berger disease). Mesangial IgA with C three mirrors Berger disease under every microscope.

Hypocomplementemic urticarial vasculitis syndrome (McDuffie) keeps hives beyond twenty four to forty eight hours that burn or hurt rather than itch, drains complement (low C one q, C three, C four), and raises anti-C one q antibodies. Long painful hives with eaten complement define McDuffie syndrome unmistakably.

Skin biopsy settles urticarial vasculitis with true leukocytoclastic vasculitis: postcapillary venular fibrinoid necrosis, swollen endothelium, and shattered neutrophil nuclei, far beyond simple allergic wheals. Vessels necrose while wheals merely swell and fade within a day.

Lungs kill McDuffie patients first through accelerated panacinar emphysema with severe obstructive disease in young adults, smokers or never-smokers alike. Emphysema outruns age in this syndrome.

Mild pediatric IgA disease needs only fluids and simple analgesics; systemic steroids calm severe colic and joint pain yet never prevent chronic nephritis. Comfort eases pain while kidneys decide themselves over months of watchful urine testing.

Severe crescentic nephritis or severe systemic McDuffie disease (glomerulonephritis, blinding eye or failing lung disease) demands high-dose steroids joined by mycophenolate mofetil, cyclophosphamide, or rituximab. Strong organs demand strong combinations with Pneumocystis vigilance throughout.
