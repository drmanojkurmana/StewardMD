Muscle-invasive bladder cancer is treated with chemotherapy before surgery, not surgery alone. Neoadjuvant cisplatin-based combinations, either accelerated MVAC or gemcitabine plus cisplatin, add an absolute 5 to 8 percent overall survival advantage at 5 years. The gain comes from eradicating micrometastases while the primary is still in place, so fit patients with cT2 to T4a disease receive chemotherapy first and cystectomy after.

The standard operation removes the entire bladder with its neighbours. In men radical cystectomy takes the bladder, prostate, seminal vesicles, distal ureters, and regional lymph nodes. In women anterior pelvic exenteration removes the bladder, urethra, uterus, ovaries, and anterior vaginal wall. The extent reflects how bladder cancer spreads locally, and anything less leaves microscopic disease behind.

Node surgery has a numeric standard. Bilateral pelvic lymph node dissection should clear at least 10 to 15 nodes across the external iliac, internal iliac or hypogastric, and obturator packets. The count matters twice: it stages the patient accurately and it removes occult metastases, with higher yields linked to better survival. A token sampling of a few nodes understages the disease.

The simplest diversion is a piece of ileum. The ileal conduit, or Bricker diversion, isolates 15 to 20 cm of terminal ileum taken 15 cm proximal to the ileocecal valve, implants both ureters into one end, and brings the other end to the skin as a stoma. It is incontinent by design and remains the most reliable diversion, especially for elderly or comorbid patients who cannot manage a continent reservoir.

The continent alternative is built from more bowel. The orthotopic neobladder of Studer or Hautmann detubularizes 50 to 60 cm of ileum, folds it into a low-pressure spherical reservoir, and joins it directly to the native urethral stump so the patient voids per urethra. It demands motivation, manual dexterity for mucus irrigation, and willingness to self-catheterize if hypercontinence develops, so selection is strict.

Bowel in the urinary tract changes electrolytes predictably. Ileal and colonic segments reabsorb urinary ammonium and chloride while secreting bicarbonate and sodium, producing a hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis with chronic bicarbonate loss. Long-term follow-up therefore checks venous bicarbonate and renal function, and maintenance alkali keeps the acidosis from dissolving bone and forming stones.

Selected patients keep their bladders. Trimodal bladder preservation combines maximal TURBT with concurrent chemoradiotherapy and achieves survival comparable to cystectomy in carefully chosen patients: solitary T2 tumours, no hydronephrosis, no extensive carcinoma in situ, and a bladder that empties well. Surveillance is lifelong and unforgiving, with salvage cystectomy held in reserve for incomplete response or invasive recurrence.

Metastatic disease has a new first line. Enfortumab vedotin, an antibody-drug conjugate aimed at Nectin-4, combined with pembrolizumab improves progression-free and overall survival as first-line therapy for locally advanced or metastatic urothelial cancer. The combination has displaced platinum chemotherapy for eligible patients, with skin toxicity and neuropathy as the signature side effects to watch.
