# Sjogren disease (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-sjogren: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Sjogren disease is a chronic systemic autoimmune exocrinopathy characterized by lymphocytic infiltration of salivary and lacrimal glands, resulting in xerostomia (dry mouth) and keratoconjunctivitis sicca (dry eyes), occurring primarily in perimenopausal women.

The 2016 ACR/EULAR classification criteria for primary Sjogren syndrome apply to patients with ocular or oral dryness or suspicion of systemic disease; a score >=4 points confirms classification based on weighted criteria: focal lymphocytic sialadenitis on minor salivary gland biopsy (focus score >=1 per 4 mm2, 3 points), positive anti-SSA/Ro (3 points), ocular staining score >=5 or van Bijsterveld score >=4 (1 point), Schirmer test <=5 mm/5 min (1 point), and unstimulated whole saliva flow <=0.1 mL/min (1 point).

Minor salivary gland labial lip biopsy requires histopathologic demonstration of focal lymphocytic sialadenitis, defined by a focus score of 1 or more (a focus is an aggregate of 50 or more mononuclear inflammatory cells, predominantly CD4+ T cells, adjacent to normal-appearing acinar units) in a 4 square millimeter tissue area.

Anti-Ro/SSA antibodies are positive in 60% to 70% of primary Sjogren patients, whereas anti-La/SSB antibodies (found in 30% to 40%) are almost never present without anti-Ro; isolated anti-La is non-specific and excluded from the 2016 classification criteria.

Systemic extraglandular manifestations occur in over 30% of Sjogren patients: cutaneous purpura, non-erosive polyarthritis, Raynaud phenomenon, interstitial lung disease (LIP and NSIP), interstitial nephritis, distal (type 1) renal tubular acidosis, and small-fiber sensory ganglionopathy.

Patients with primary Sjogren disease face a 15- to 30-fold increased lifetime relative risk of developing non-Hodgkin lymphoma, most commonly mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) marginal zone B-cell lymphoma of the salivary glands; major clinical predictors include persistent parotid enlargement, cutaneous purpura, cryoglobulinemia, hypocomplementemia (low C4), and monoclonal gammopathy.

Symptomatic treatment of glandular dryness includes preservative-free artificial tears and ocular lubricants, oral muscarinic acetylcholine receptor agonists (pilocarpine 5 mg three to four times daily, or cevimeline 30 mg three times daily) which stimulate remaining functional acinar secretions, alongside meticulous dental hygiene.

Systemic immunomodulatory therapy is guided by the EULAR Sjogren's Syndrome Disease Activity Index (ESSDAI); hydroxychloroquine is effective for arthralgias and fatigue, whereas severe extraglandular organ involvement (vasculitis, glomerulonephritis, severe neuropathy) mandates systemic glucocorticoids combined with rituximab, cyclophosphamide, or mycophenolate mofetil.
