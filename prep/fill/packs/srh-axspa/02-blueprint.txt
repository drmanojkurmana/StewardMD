# Axial spondyloarthritis and ankylosing spondylitis (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison; ASAS criteria)
HLA-B27 is present in 85 percent to 90 percent of ankylosing spondylitis in Caucasian populations. Pathogenic ideas are arthritogenic peptide presentation, heavy chain misfolding with ER stress and IL-23 activation, and free heavy-chain homodimers triggering KIRs.
Inflammatory back pain by ASAS needs at least 4 of 5: onset below 40 years, insidious onset, improvement with exercise, no improvement with rest, night pain improving on rising; morning stiffness beyond 30 to 45 minutes supports it.
ASAS axial SpA classification needs back pain for 3 months or more with onset below 45 years, via the imaging arm (definite MRI or radiographic sacroiliitis by modified New York criteria plus 1 or more SpA features) or the clinical arm (HLA-B27 plus 2 or more other features).
Non-radiographic axial SpA lacks bilateral grade 2 or higher or unilateral grade 3 sacroiliitis on plain films; MRI STIR shows subchondral bone marrow edema with equal symptom burden and treatment response.
Assessment uses BASDAI, ASDAS with CRP, modified Schober below 5 cm, chest expansion below 2.5 cm at the fourth intercostal space, and occiput-to-wall distance.
NSAIDs at full anti-inflammatory doses are first-line for active symptomatic axial SpA, easing symptoms rapidly and possibly slowing radiographic change.
csDMARDs such as methotrexate, sulfasalazine, and leflunomide do not work for purely axial disease; active disease after at least 2 NSAIDs over 4 weeks qualifies for TNF inhibitors (infliximab, adalimumab, etanercept, golimumab, certolizumab), IL-17A blockers (secukinumab, ixekizumab), or dual IL-17A and F blocker bimekizumab.
Extra-skeletal disease includes acute unilateral anterior uveitis in 25 percent to 30 percent (topical glucocorticoids), inflammatory bowel disease, ascending aortitis with aortic regurgitation, conduction blocks, and apical pulmonary fibrosis colonized by Aspergillus.
Enthesitis at the Achilles insertion and plantar fascia marks peripheral SpA; dactylitis (sausage digit) and alternating buttock pain are SpA features counting toward classification.
Uveitis flares that need more than 2 drops per day of topical steroid beyond 3 months, or posterior segment involvement, require referral for systemic or biologic therapy rather than repeated topical courses.
