# Diagnostic and therapeutic endoscopy
# Source: StewardMD gastroenterology knowledge base / Sleisenger 11e Ch. 41, 126

## Colonoscopy preparation and lesion assessment
Boston Bowel Preparation Scale (BBPS) assesses mucosal visualization across 3 colon segments (right, transverse, left) on a scale of 0 to 3 each, where a total score >= 6 with no individual segment < 2 indicates adequate preparation.
Kudo pit pattern classification assesses colorectal polyp crypt orifice microarchitecture under magnification chromoendoscopy, where Types 3 and 4 indicate non-invasive adenomas and Type 5 indicates deep submucosal invasive cancer.
NICE (NBI International Colorectal Endoscopic) classification utilizes narrow-band imaging without optical magnification to differentiate hyperplastic polyps (Type 1), adenomas (Type 2), and deep submucosal invasive carcinomas (Type 3).

## Mucosal and submucosal resection techniques
Endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) utilizes submucosal injection to lift mucosal lesions above the muscularis propria, allowing snare excision of non-invasive polyps while minimizing perforation risks.
Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) employs specialized electrosurgical knives to dissect through the submucosal plane, enabling en bloc curative resection of large (> 20 mm) or fibrotic neoplastic lesions regardless of size (en bloc resection).

## Advanced imaging, tissue acquisition and enteral access
Endoscopic ultrasound-guided fine-needle biopsy (EUS-FNB) using Franseen or fork-tip needle geometries acquires preserved core tissue architecture, achieving superior diagnostic adequacy over standard cytology needles (fine-needle biopsy, Franseen needle).
Percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy (PEG) provides long-term enteral nutrition access in patients with functional GI tracts but impaired swallowing, placed under direct transillumination and finger-indentation guidance.

## Complications of therapeutic endoscopy
Major ERCP procedural complications comprise acute pancreatitis (3% to 10%), hemorrhage, duodenal perforation (Stapfer classification Types 1 to 4), and ascending bacterial cholangitis (ERCP complications, Stapfer classification).
Rectal indometacin or diclofenac suppositories administered immediately pre- or post-procedure significantly reduce post-ERCP pancreatitis rates.

## Additional high-yield clinical details from Sleisenger & Fordtran 11e
Submucosal injection cushions frequently incorporate dilute adrenaline (epinephrine 1:100,000 or 1:200,000) with a biocompatible contrast dye such as indigo carmine or methylene blue to clearly delineate deep submucosal resection margins from the underlying muscularis propria.
During diagnostic colonoscopy, withdrawal time from the cecal pole to the anal verge should be at least 6 minutes in intact non-resected colons to optimize adenoma detection rates (ADR).
Rectal indometacin or diclofenac suppositories administered immediately pre- or post-procedure significantly reduce post-ERCP pancreatitis rates.
Percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy establishes direct gastric transillumination and 1:1 finger indentation to ensure an unobstructed pathway free of intervening transverse colon.
