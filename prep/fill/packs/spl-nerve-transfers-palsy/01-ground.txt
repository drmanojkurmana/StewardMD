Exam grounding notes for nerve and tendon transfers in palsies, written for grounding, not for display. Paraphrased from Plastic Surgery 5E Volume 6 on nerve and tendon transfer.

A nerve transfer rewires a high proximal injury into a low distal coaptation placed beside the target neuromuscular junctions. The regenerating axons travel millimetres instead of decimetres, dodging the long delays that doom direct repair. Donor expendability is the price of admission.

In high radial nerve palsy, motor branches of the median nerve are redirected: pronator teres and FDS fascicles transfer onto the ECRB branch and the PIN. Wrist extension returns through ECRB and finger extension through the reinnervated posterior interosseous nerve.

In high median nerve palsy, the expendable extensor carpi radialis brevis branch of the radial nerve is transferred to the anterior interosseous nerve. The AIN then drives finger flexion again, restoring the pinch that median loss had stolen.

In ulnar nerve intrinsic palsy, the distal anterior interosseous nerve branch to pronator quadratus is transferred onto the deep motor branch of the ulnar nerve. Hand intrinsics wake up months sooner than any graft from the elbow ever could.

When nerves cannot be rewired, tendons are rerouted. The classic Boyes or Brand transfer for radial palsy moves pronator teres to ECRB for wrist extension, FCU or FDS to EDC for finger extension, and palmaris longus to EPL for thumb extension. One stage, immediate motion.

Opponensplasty rebuilds thumb opposition after low median nerve loss. Donor choices are the FDS of a finger, the abductor digiti minimi in the Huber transfer, or the extensor indicis proprius. Each is rerouted to pull the thumb into opposition.

A low ulnar lesion claws the hand: MCP joints hyperextend while IP joints flex because intrinsics no longer balance the extrinsics. Dynamic correction comes from Stiles-Bunnell or Zancolli lasso transfers that tether the flexors into an intrinsic-like line of pull.

Tendon transfer demands prerequisites. Joints must stay supple with full passive range of motion, the donor muscle needs grade 4 or 5 strength, and a synergic action pattern helps retraining. A stiff joint or a weak donor guarantees failure.
