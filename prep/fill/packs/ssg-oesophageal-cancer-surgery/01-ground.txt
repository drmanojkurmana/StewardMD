Squamous carcinoma dominates the upper and middle thirds of the oesophagus and follows smoking with alcohol use. Adenocarcinoma rises from Barrett metaplasia in the lower third and at the junction, driven by obesity and chronic reflux. This split decides the operation and the neoadjuvant plan.

Siewert types I to III locate junctional tumours by the epicentre relative to the gastric cardia. Type I grows upward from Barrett, type II sits astride the cardia, and type III grows downward from the stomach. Types I and II are treated by oesophagectomy, while type III needs an extended total gastrectomy.

Locally advanced adenocarcinoma receives CROSS chemoradiotherapy with carboplatin and paclitaxel plus 41.4 Gy, or perioperative FLOT chemotherapy. Restaging after neoadjuvant treatment decides whether resection proceeds.

Ivor Lewis oesophagectomy pairs abdominal and right thoracic phases with an intrathoracic anastomosis. McKeown adds a cervical anastomosis through a neck incision. Transhiatal resection avoids thoracotomy in frail patients but accepts blind mediastinal dissection with less complete nodal clearance.

A minimum harvest of 15 lymph nodes stages the disease accurately. The operative goal is R0 resection with 5 cm of clear proximal margin, since residual disease at the cut edge predicts early recurrence. A positive R1 margin is followed by recurrence at the join within months.

Anastomotic leak declares itself on days 5 to 7 with tachycardia, sepsis and neck crepitus after cervical joints or chest soilage after thoracic joints. Contained leaks are managed by drainage, antibiotics, endoscopic vacuum therapy or covered stenting. Conduit necrosis with a dead gastric tube needs reoperation.

Chyle leak over 1000 mL per day needs thoracic duct ligation, while smaller outputs may settle with medium-chain feeds and octreotide. Jejunostomy feeding intolerance and recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy with hoarseness are screened routinely after resection.

Definitive chemoradiotherapy without surgery suits cervical oesophageal cancer and medically unfit patients. Salvage oesophagectomy is reserved for residual or recurrent disease after full-dose radiation, accepting higher leak and pulmonary risk.
