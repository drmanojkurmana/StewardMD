Suspected testicular tumours are removed by radical inguinal orchiectomy with the cord clamped early and high ligation at the internal inguinal ring. Scrotal skin is never divided and trans-scrotal biopsy is banned, because violation seeds inguinal routes. Markers are drawn first: alpha-fetoprotein clears with a half-life of 5 to 7 days, beta-hCG with 24 to 36 hours, and LDH tracks tissue burden, with rising curves proving residue.

Pure seminoma never raises AFP and neither does pure choriocarcinoma, so AFP elevation in presumed seminoma is relabelled NSGCT and treated with NSGCT chemotherapy. Right testes drain to interaortocaval nodes and left testes to para-aortic nodes near the renal hilum, shaping RPLND templates. Nerve-sparing RPLND traces hypogastric plexus fibres from L1 to L3 to preserve antegrade ejaculation, with sperm banked beforehand.

NSGCT residue above 1 cm with normal markers is resected by post-chemotherapy RPLND for teratoma or viable cancer, while seminoma under 3 cm is watched and 3 cm or larger needs FDG PET-CT at least 6 to 8 weeks after chemotherapy, since early inflammation glows falsely. IGCCCG criteria band metastatic disease into good, intermediate, and poor prognostic groups by site, spread, and marker nadir to set intensity.

Prosthesis counselling and fertility discussion precede orchiectomy, and pathology review confirms yolk sac elements before NSGCT protocols start. Bilateral templates are dissected with ejaculation mapped, and avid PET hotspots are biopsied or resected for salvage planning. Surveillance schedules pair marker curves with cross-sectional imaging, because half-life mathematics separates persistence from new growth after surgery. Marker nadir after orchiectomy reclassifies IGCCCG bands before chemotherapy begins. Contralateral testis is examined and biopsied selectively for intratubular germ cell neoplasia in high-risk cases. Scrotal ultrasound with tumour size and rete testis invasion is recorded for every case, because staging starts with precise local mapping. Tumour size with rete testis invasion and lymphovascular emboli is transcribed from orchiectomy pathology, because local features refine relapse risk on surveillance.
