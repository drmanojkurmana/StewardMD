A suspected testicular cancer is never biopsied through the scrotum. Radical inguinal orchiectomy with high ligation of the spermatic cord at the internal inguinal ring is mandatory, because scrotal violation or trans-scrotal biopsy seeds tumour into a new lymphatic basin and upstages the patient. The testis is delivered through a groin incision, the cord is clamped high before manipulation, and the diagnosis is confirmed on the removed specimen.

Serum markers stage the disease and track treatment. Alpha-fetoprotein has a half-life of 5 to 7 days, beta-human chorionic gonadotropin 24 to 36 hours, and lactate dehydrogenase reflects bulk. After orchiectomy the markers should fall according to these half-lives; a slower fall or a plateau means residual disease. Marker nadir after chemotherapy feeds directly into prognostic grouping.

AFP draws a hard diagnostic line. It is never elevated in pure seminoma or in pure choriocarcinoma, so any AFP elevation in a presumed seminoma mandates management as a non-seminomatous germ cell tumour. The rule prevents under-treatment: NSGCT needs different chemotherapy and usually surgery, while pure seminoma follows its own pathway.

Lymphatic drainage follows the testicular vessels, not the scrotum. The right testis drains primarily into interaortocaval lymph nodes between the great vessels, while the left testis drains into para-aortic nodes near the left renal hilum. This embryological highway explains why retroperitoneal nodes are the first landing zone and why scrotal violation, which reroutes drainage to the groin, is so damaging.

Retroperitoneal surgery must protect ejaculation. Nerve-sparing RPLND meticulously preserves the postganglionic sympathetic fibers of the hypogastric plexus around L1 to L3, which drive antegrade ejaculation through the vas and bladder neck. The template removes the landing-zone nodes while leaving these microscopic fibers intact, so most patients keep dry-free forward ejaculation and fertility potential.

Residual masses after chemotherapy are not watched in NSGCT. Post-chemotherapy retroperitoneal masses measuring greater than 1 cm with normalized tumor markers mandate complete surgical excision, because teratoma or viable cancer hides in a large share of them. The operation is a post-chemotherapy RPLND, harder than primary surgery, and completeness of resection decides cure.

Seminoma residuals behave differently. Post-chemotherapy seminoma masses under 3 cm are managed with surveillance, since fibrosis is the rule. Masses of 3 cm or more need an 18F-FDG PET-CT scan at least 6 to 8 weeks after chemotherapy to test metabolic activity before any surgery is contemplated, because operating on dead fibrosis helps nobody.

Prognosis is grouped, not guessed. The IGCCCG criteria sort metastatic germ cell tumours into good, intermediate, and poor groups using primary site, non-pulmonary visceral metastases, and marker nadir after treatment. The group predicts cure rates and selects chemotherapy intensity, from three cycles of BEP in good-risk disease to four cycles and trials in poor-risk patients.
