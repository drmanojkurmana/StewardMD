Nocturia under the ICS definition means waking one or more times in the main sleep to void, each void both preceded and followed by sleep; daytime frequency alone does not qualify. Nocturnal polyuria is proven on a 3-day chart, a frequency-volume chart of every timed void, when night output exceeds 20% to 33% of the day total, a nocturnal polyuria index above 0.20 to 0.33 according to age. A single night misleads, so three full days are charted.

Global polyuria means 24-hour output above 40 mL/kg, roughly over 3.0 L daily, from diabetes mellitus, diabetes insipidus, or primary polydipsia, and it demands systemic treatment. In the elderly, night polyuria often follows lost circadian rhythm of arginine vasopressin secretion, with congestive failure, venous stasis, and sleep apnea returning leg fluid at night; snoring, swollen ankles, and evening drinking are asked after. Desmopressin melt, 25 to 50 mcg for men and 12.5 to 25 mcg for women, stimulates the renal V2 receptor agonist pathway of the collecting duct and cuts night urine, provided evening fluids are capped.

Hyponatremia monitoring with serum sodium before therapy, at 1 week and 1 month, then periodically past 65 years, guards against dilutional collapse; headache, nausea, or confusion stops the drug. Timed loop diuretics, furosemide 20 to 40 mg taken 6 hours prior to bed in the afternoon, clear leg oedema by day so less returns at night, since evening dosing itself provokes nocturia. Shrunken nocturnal bladder capacity, a nocturia index above 1.5 with night voids smaller than day voids, marks storage disease near an index of unity rather than polyuria, and the bladder, not desmopressin, is treated. Evening diuretics, late caffeine, and untreated sleep apnea are corrected alongside drugs, since behaviour change alone halves episodes in many charts. Reassess the chart after each intervention before escalating therapy. Nocturia management separates bladder, polyuric, and sleep mechanisms.
