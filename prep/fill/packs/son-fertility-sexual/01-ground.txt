Fertility, sexual function and gonadal damage after cancer treatment: grounding notes

Why it matters
- Cytotoxic drugs and radiation can injure the testis and ovary. Damage ranges from a short dip in sperm count to permanent loss of function.
- Severity depends on the agent, total dose, age and baseline gonadal state.

Combined regimens
- When several drugs are given together, their gonadal effects add up. No synergy has been shown, so total risk roughly equals the sum of each drug's contribution.
- Cumulative dose is the main driver. Spreading the same total over a shorter or longer period changes the outcome less than the total does.
- Older data: cyclophosphamide alone needed about 19 g/m2 to make half of men permanently sterile. Adding doxorubicin at a median of 450 mg/m2 lowered that threshold to about 15 g/m2.

Risk tiers in men
- Alkylating agents are the most sterilizing class. Chlorambucil and cyclophosphamide given alone cause prolonged azoospermia.
- Procarbazine and high-dose cisplatin are also strongly sterilizing. Busulfan is likely to cause azoospermia, usually alongside other drugs.
- Methotrexate, vincristine, bleomycin and most other agents produce temporary drops in sperm count at usual doses.
- Dacarbazine is the one alkylating agent in the temporary group.

Germ cells and Leydig cells
- Spermatogonia are the dividing stem cells of sperm production. They are very sensitive to cytotoxic drugs and to radiation.
- Later germ cells, from spermatocytes onward, resist killing, so sperm counts do not fall straight away.
- Leydig cells make testosterone. Most chemotherapy regimens leave their function intact, so puberty timing and adult testosterone are usually normal.
- The one notable exception is busulfan plus cyclophosphamide given before stem cell transplant, which delays puberty in roughly 30% of boys.
- Against radiation, Leydig output needs more than 18 Gy of fractionated radiotherapy before any effect, and major damage needs more than 30 Gy.
- When germ cells are lost, the pituitary raises luteinizing hormone so that testosterone stays near normal.

Female fertility preservation
- Women are born with a fixed oocyte pool, which cytotoxic therapy destroys more readily at older ages.
- Embryo cryopreservation is the most successful banking method. It is preferred whenever it is viable.
- Oocyte vitrification, which is rapid freezing of eggs, suits women without a partner.
- A conventional IVF cycle takes about 2 to 3 weeks. A random-start protocol begins stimulation at presentation and can cut the wait to about 12 days.
- Without random-start, oocyte banking can need a delay of 12 days to 6 weeks.
- Ovarian tissue cryopreservation removes ovarian tissue for freezing and later grafting. It needs no ovarian stimulation, so it suits girls before puberty.
- Its drawback is the possibility of returning malignant cells, which matters most in leukemia.

GnRH analogs
- Gonadotropin-releasing hormone analogs have been tested for ovarian protection, with inconsistent results across cancer treatments.
- Most studies do not support giving hormones to girls before puberty.
- Their clearer role is menstrual suppression, which limits heavy bleeding in blood cancers.
- Current ASCO guidance (2025) says these analogs must not substitute for proven methods. They may be added on, mainly for women with breast cancer.

Sexual function measures
- The IIEF is a validated scale that the patient fills in alone. It covers sexual function after prostate treatment.
- It takes 5 to 10 minutes, so it works well in clinic.
- The SHIM is an abridged, validated short form of the same scale.
- Clinicians use these tools to grade erectile dysfunction and to judge treatment response.
