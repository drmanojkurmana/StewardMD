The brachial plexus is the great junction box of the upper limb, formed by the anterior primary rami of C5 to T1. Five roots merge into three trunks, which split into six divisions behind the clavicle, regroup into three cords around the axillary artery, and finally give off the terminal branches to the arm.

Preganglionic avulsion is the worst pattern because the roots tear out of the spinal cord itself, leaving nothing to graft. Clues include Horner syndrome from T1 sympathetic disruption, a winged scapula from long thoracic nerve failure, and paradoxically preserved sensory nerve action potentials because the dorsal root ganglion survives distal to the tear.

Upper trunk injury gives Erb-Duchenne palsy of C5-C6, the classic waiter tip posture. The shoulder sits adducted and internally rotated, the elbow hangs extended, the forearm pronates, and the wrist flexes, as if the patient were tipping a waiter for a bill. Deltoid, biceps, and brachioradialis all fail.

Lower trunk injury gives Klumpke palsy of C7-T1 with a total claw hand. The intrinsics fail so the metacarpophalangeal joints hyperextend while the interphalangeal joints flex, and an ipsilateral Horner syndrome frequently joins because T1 carries sympathetic fibres.

When elbow flexion is the missing function, the Oberlin transfer borrows from nearby. An expendable motor fascicle of the ulnar nerve is transferred to the biceps motor branch of the musculocutaneous nerve, restoring active flexion without visibly weakening grip.

The Mackinnon double fascicular transfer strengthens the concept by adding a second donor. A motor fascicle of the median nerve joins the brachialis motor branch while the ulnar fascicle drives biceps, and the two engines together produce powerful elbow flexion in upper plexus injuries.

Shoulder abduction needs the suprascapular nerve alive. Transferring the spinal accessory nerve XI to the suprascapular nerve reinnervates supraspinatus and infraspinatus, restoring abduction and the external rotation that keeps the joint centered.

Pan-plexus avulsions with every root torn need distant donors. Intercostal nerves T3-T6 can be transferred to arm targets, and the phrenic nerve serves as a powerful neurotizer for motor reinnervation when nothing proximal survives. C5 T1 T3 T6.
