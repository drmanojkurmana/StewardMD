# Crimean-Congo haemorrhagic fever
Crimean-Congo haemorrhagic fever, Infectious Diseases, Infectious Diseases.
Aliases: CCHF; Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever; viral haemorrhagic fever (CCHF); VHF.
# Pathophysiology
CCHF virus is an enveloped RNA virus that, following tick bite or exposure to infected blood or tissues, replicates and disseminates widely. It infects endothelial cells and cells of the mononuclear phagocyte system, and a dysregulated inflammatory and cytokine response together with direct endothelial injury leads to increased vascular permeability, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and haemorrhage. Hepatic involvement and marrow suppression contribute to the coagulopathy and cytopenias, and severe cases progress to shock and multi-organ failure.
# Prognosis
Case fatality is high, especially with delayed recognition and severe coagulopathy or multi-organ failure; early supportive care and meticulous infection control improve outcomes, and survivors who pass the acute haemorrhagic phase generally recover.
# Clinical pearls
A tick-borne viral haemorrhagic fever caused by an orthonairovirus, transmitted mainly by Hyalomma ticks and by contact with blood or tissues of infected livestock
In India it is a recognised emerging threat, with documented outbreaks in Gujarat and neighbouring regions and cases among livestock handlers and healthcare workers
Onset is abrupt with fever, severe headache, myalgia, and prostration, progressing over days to a haemorrhagic phase with petechiae, ecchymoses, mucosal bleeding, and multi-organ involvement
Nosocomial and slaughterhouse transmission is well described, so a history of tick exposure, animal contact, or care of a bleeding patient is a critical clue
Laboratory features include thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, and rising transaminases, with deranged coagulation heralding severe disease
This is a medical emergency requiring strict infection-control and barrier precautions, patient isolation, and safe handling of body fluids and laboratory specimens
Management is chiefly supportive with careful correction of coagulopathy and organ support; suspected and confirmed cases must be promptly notified to public health authorities
