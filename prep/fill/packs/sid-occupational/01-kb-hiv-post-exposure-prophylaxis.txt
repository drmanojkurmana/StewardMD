# HIV post-exposure prophylaxis and hepatitis B exposure (adult)
Adults
Emergency department, occupational health, ICTC or ART centre
After a significant exposure, give first aid and start TLD (tenofovir DF, lamivudine, dolutegravir) one tablet daily for 28 days as soon as possible and no later than 72 hours; test at baseline and follow-up, and manage hepatitis B by vaccination and anti-HBs status.
Aliases: PEP; HIV PEP; needle-stick injury; NSI; sharps injury; occupational exposure; TLD; sexual assault PEP; hepatitis B exposure; HBIG.
# First aid and first dose
Wash the wound or skin at once with soap and running water. Do not scrub, squeeze, suck or apply bleach or antiseptic into the wound. Flush eyes or mucous membranes with water or saline; rinse the mouth and spit.
Start PEP as soon as possible: ideally within 2 hours (NACO; WHO 2024 advises within 24 hours) and never later than 72 hours after exposure. Give the first dose in the emergency department; do not wait for source test results.
Counsel, obtain consent, and document the exposure (date, time, device, depth, fluid, source details). Report to the infection control or occupational health team.
In India, PEP drugs and HIV testing are available free at government hospitals through NACO-supported ART centres and ICTCs. NACO 2021 covers occupational exposure and sexual assault; other non-occupational exposures are not covered by the national programme.
# Is PEP indicated?
Significant exposure: percutaneous injury, or mucous membrane or non-intact skin contact with blood, visibly bloody fluid, semen, vaginal secretions, CSF, or synovial, pleural, peritoneal, pericardial or amniotic fluid; or condomless anal or vaginal sex or shared injecting equipment with a source who is HIV-positive or of unknown status.
PEP is not indicated for exposure more than 72 hours earlier, intact skin exposure, or urine, saliva, tears, sweat, vomitus or faeces that are not visibly bloody.
Higher-risk features: hollow-bore needle, deep injury, visible blood on the device, needle used in an artery or vein, and a source with a high viral load. Average risk is about 0.3% after percutaneous and 0.09% after mucous membrane exposure to HIV-positive blood.
Test the source for HIV, HBsAg and anti-HCV with consent. Stop PEP if the source is confirmed HIV-negative and has no recent high-risk exposure.
If the source has known HIV, record ART history, viral load and resistance results; seek expert advice if the source has treatment failure.
# PEP regimen
Preferred (WHO 2024; NACO): tenofovir disoproxil fumarate 300 mg + lamivudine 300 mg + dolutegravir 50 mg (TLD fixed-dose tablet) orally once daily for 28 days.
Complete the full 28 days. Review within 3 days to check source results, adherence and side effects (nausea, headache, insomnia are usually mild).
If dolutegravir cannot be used, keep the tenofovir-lamivudine backbone with raltegravir 400 mg twice daily or a ritonavir-boosted protease inhibitor (atazanavir/ritonavir 300/100 mg once daily or darunavir/ritonavir 800/100 mg once daily); seek expert advice.
Creatinine clearance below 50 mL/min: the TLD fixed-dose tablet is not suitable, as tenofovir DF and lamivudine both need dose adjustment; get expert advice on a renally adjusted regimen.
Dolutegravir interactions: give it 2 hours before or 6 hours after antacids, calcium, iron or zinc; use 50 mg twice daily with rifampicin; it raises metformin levels.
# Baseline and follow-up tests
Baseline (exposed person): HIV test (rapid or antigen/antibody), HBsAg and anti-HBs, anti-HCV, serum creatinine, ALT, and a pregnancy test in women of reproductive age. After sexual exposure add syphilis serology and gonorrhoea and chlamydia testing.
A reactive baseline HIV test means prior infection: do not start PEP; refer to the ART centre for confirmation and treatment.
Repeat HIV testing at 4 to 6 weeks and 12 weeks after exposure (CDC 2025). Advise condoms and no blood, organ or semen donation until follow-up testing is complete.
Fever, rash, sore throat or lymphadenopathy during follow-up may be acute HIV: test urgently, including HIV RNA if available.
# Hepatitis B and C exposure
Vaccinated with a documented anti-HBs of 10 mIU/mL or more at any time: no hepatitis B prophylaxis is needed, whatever the source status.
Vaccinated but anti-HBs unknown: test anti-HBs urgently. If below 10 mIU/mL and the source is HBsAg-positive or unknown, give HBIG once plus a vaccine dose and complete revaccination. Known non-responders after 2 vaccine series: HBIG 2 doses, 1 month apart.
Hepatitis C: there is no PEP. If the source is HCV-positive or unknown, test the exposed person for HCV RNA at 3 weeks or later and anti-HCV at 6 months; refer early if infected (curable with direct-acting antivirals).
# Special situations
Pregnancy and breastfeeding: PEP is recommended, and the dolutegravir-based regimen can be used.
Sexual assault: offer PEP, emergency contraception (levonorgestrel 1.5 mg orally, single dose, within 72 hours), presumptive STI treatment, hepatitis B vaccination, medico-legal care and psychological support.
Ongoing risk of exposure: after completing PEP with a negative HIV test, move directly to pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP).
Healthcare workers: ensure hepatitis B vaccination with post-vaccination anti-HBs testing; use safety-engineered devices and never recap needles.
# Pitfalls
Delaying the first dose while waiting for source results or for a specialist.
Starting HIV PEP after 72 hours, when it is not effective; arrange testing and follow-up instead.
Giving PEP to someone whose baseline HIV test is reactive instead of confirming and referring for ART.
Forgetting hepatitis B: HBIG and vaccine are time-critical and often more relevant than HIV after needle-stick injury.
# Drugs
TLD (tenofovir DF + lamivudine + dolutegravir)
# Dose
300 mg + 300 mg + 50 mg, one tablet orally once daily for 28 days
Preferred PEP regimen. Needs creatinine clearance of 50 mL/min or more for standard dosing.
Raltegravir, 400 mg orally twice daily for 28 days (with tenofovir DF + lamivudine), Alternative third drug if dolutegravir cannot be used.
Atazanavir/ritonavir, 300/100 mg orally once daily for 28 days (with tenofovir DF + lamivudine), Alternative third drug; many drug interactions; jaundice from unconjugated bilirubin is benign.
Hepatitis B immunoglobulin (HBIG)
0.06 mL/kg IM once, ideally within 24 hours
Non-immune person exposed to an HBsAg-positive or unknown source; 2 doses 1 month apart in vaccine non-responders.
Hepatitis B vaccine (adult), 20 mcg (1 mL) IM deltoid at 0, 1 and 6 months, Start at the same visit as HBIG, at a different site.
Levonorgestrel, 1.5 mg orally, single dose, within 72 hours, Emergency contraception after sexual assault.
