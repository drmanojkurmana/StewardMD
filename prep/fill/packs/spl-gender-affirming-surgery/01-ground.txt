Gender-affirming surgery aligns the body with identity, and chest work comes first for most transmasculine patients. Chest masculinization top surgery in minimal tissue uses semicircular or periareolar concentric incisions hidden at the areolar border, removing small volumes while tightening mild laxity through the same opening.

Moderate to severe ptosis needs the workhorse operation. Double incision mastectomy with free nipple-areola grafting removes the breast through transverse chest incisions and replaces the nipples as grafts, sculpting a flat masculine contour with scars that fade along the pectoral lines.

Nipple design makes the male chest read correctly. The male nipple-areola complex is smaller at 22 to 28 mm diameter, oval rather than round, and sits more lateral and inferior, centred over the lateral border of pectoralis major near the fourth intercostal space.

Transfeminine genital surgery builds a vulva and vagina from natal tissue. Penile inversion vaginoplasty, the gold standard, creates a neovaginal cavity between rectum and prostate by inverting penile skin flaps into the dissected space, with scrotal grafts adding depth where needed.

Sensation is preserved by design. The neoclitoris is fashioned from the glans penis left pedicled on the dorsal neurovascular bundle, keeping its erotic sensory innervation and blood supply while reducing bulk to a clitoral hood position.

Transmasculine genital construction needs both a phallus and a urethra. Phalloplasty most frequently uses the microvascular radial forearm free flap with a tube-within-a-tube design, rolling one skin tube for the urethra inside a second tube for the shaft in a single stage.

Some patients cannot spare the forearm or decline its scar. The anterolateral thigh flap serves as the primary alternative phalloplasty donor, offering abundant tissue with a hidden scar, though the thicker fat makes urethral tubing more challenging in heavier patients.

No irreversible genital surgery proceeds without gatekeeping.
