# Community-acquired pneumonia (adult)
Adults
Outpatient, emergency department, ward, ICU
Stratify by CURB-65 and IDSA/ATS criteria; outpatients get co-amoxiclav (plus a macrolide or doxycycline if comorbid), inpatients ceftriaxone plus a macrolide or doxycycline, with anti-pseudomonal or MRSA cover only for specific risks. Avoid empirical fluoroquinolones in India (TB), and stop at 5 days (outpatient) or about 7 days (inpatient) once stable.
Aliases: CAP; lobar pneumonia; severe CAP; IDSA ATS criteria; LRTI; ICMR pneumonia.
# Recognise and grade severity
Acute lower respiratory symptoms (cough, dyspnoea, pleuritic pain) for under 1 week, a systemic feature (temperature above 37.7 C, rigors or severe malaise), new focal chest signs, and a new shadow on chest X-ray with no other cause (ICMR).
CURB-65, 1 point each: Confusion; Urea above 7 mmol/L (blood urea above 42 mg/dL); Respiratory rate 30/min or more; Blood pressure systolic below 90 or diastolic 60 mmHg or less; age 65 or more. Score 0 to 1: usually home; 2: hospital; 3 to 5: severe, assess for ICU (especially 4 to 5).
Severe CAP (IDSA/ATS): 1 major criterion (septic shock needing vasopressors, or respiratory failure needing mechanical ventilation) or 3 or more minor criteria.
IDSA/ATS minor criteria: respiratory rate 30/min or more, PaO2/FiO2 250 or less, multilobar infiltrates, confusion, BUN 20 mg/dL or more (urea about 43 mg/dL), WBC below 4000/microL, platelets below 100,000/microL, temperature below 36 C, hypotension needing aggressive fluids.
Always check SpO2: ICMR uses pulse oximetry alongside CURB-65, and hypoxaemia usually warrants admission whatever the score.
Indian aetiology: S. pneumoniae commonest, then H. influenzae, Mycoplasma and respiratory viruses; consider scrub typhus, leptospirosis and melioidosis, and TB when illness is protracted.
# Investigations
Chest X-ray (lung ultrasound is an acceptable alternative where trained operators are available, ATS 2025). CBC, urea, creatinine, electrolytes, LFT, glucose, CRP; blood gas if SpO2 is low or the patient is severe.
Blood cultures and sputum Gram stain and culture for severe CAP, intubated patients, anyone given empirical MRSA or Pseudomonas cover, or with hospitalisation and IV antibiotics in the past 90 days.
Influenza (and SARS-CoV-2) PCR when circulating; pneumococcal and Legionella urinary antigens in severe CAP.
Sputum NAAT (CBNAAT/Truenat) and smear for TB if cough is prolonged, there is cavitation or upper-lobe disease, or no response. Scrub typhus IgM, leptospira serology and melioidosis cultures in endemic settings.
Start antibiotics in confirmed CAP regardless of procalcitonin level.
# Outpatient regimens
No comorbidity: amoxicillin-clavulanate (preferred in India because pneumococcal macrolide resistance is 25 to 30%); alternatives cefuroxime or cefpodoxime. Doxycycline or macrolide monotherapy is not recommended by ICMR.
Comorbidity (chronic heart, lung, liver or kidney disease, diabetes, malignancy, alcoholism, immunosuppression) or antibiotics in the past 3 months: amoxicillin-clavulanate (or cefuroxime or cefpodoxime) plus azithromycin, clarithromycin or doxycycline.
Duration 5 days (ICMR). ATS 2025 allows stopping after a minimum of 3 days once clinically stable.
ATS 2025: if a respiratory virus test is positive in an outpatient without comorbidities, antibiotics can be withheld; give them if comorbid.
# Inpatient regimens (ward and ICU)
Ward: ceftriaxone plus azithromycin (or doxycycline). Alternatives: cefotaxime or IV amoxicillin-clavulanate plus a macrolide or doxycycline. Severe beta-lactam allergy: levofloxacin or moxifloxacin only after TB has been excluded.
ICU: ceftriaxone plus a macrolide (beta-lactam plus macrolide is preferred, IDSA/ATS); alternatives cefotaxime or piperacillin-tazobactam with a macrolide.
Pseudomonas or enteric Gram-negative risk (chronic lung disease such as bronchiectasis, neurological disease, tube feeding, immunocompromise, prior isolation, IV antibiotics in 90 days): piperacillin-tazobactam, cefepime or a carbapenem plus a macrolide or doxycycline; prefer a carbapenem in septic shock (ICMR).
MRSA risk (post-influenza, cavitation without aspiration, septic shock, empyema, prior MRSA): add vancomycin or teicoplanin; use linezolid only after TB is excluded (ICMR). Stop MRSA or Pseudomonas cover at 48 hours if cultures are negative and the patient is improving.
Add oseltamivir during an influenza outbreak or with a positive test. Do not add routine anaerobic cover for aspiration unless lung abscess or empyema.
Severe CAP (not influenza): systemic corticosteroid is suggested, ideally within 24 hours (ATS 2025); hydrocortisone 200 mg/day IV was the regimen studied. Do not give steroids for non-severe CAP.
# Response, IV to oral switch, duration
Clinical stability: temperature 37.8 C or less, heart rate 100/min or less, respiratory rate 24/min or less, systolic BP 90 mmHg or more, SpO2 90% or more on air, normal mentation, eating.
Switch IV to oral once improving and able to swallow. Inpatient total about 7 days (ICMR); severe CAP 5 days or more (ATS 2025). Discharge when afebrile, eating and haemodynamically stable for 48 hours.
Longer courses for bacteraemic pneumococcal, S. aureus, Legionella, Klebsiella or other enteric Gram-negative, Pseudomonas or Acinetobacter pneumonia, lung abscess, empyema, endocarditis or meningitis.
No response at 48 to 72 hours: re-image for effusion, empyema or abscess; consider TB, resistant organisms, tropical infections, and mimics (pulmonary embolism, heart failure, vasculitis, malignancy).
Routine follow-up chest imaging is not needed if recovery is complete; image persistent symptoms or smokers at risk of lung cancer.
# Escalate
ICU or HDU for any major IDSA/ATS criterion, 3 or more minor criteria, or CURB-65 of 4 to 5.
Septic shock: follow the sepsis protocol; give antibiotics within 1 hour after cultures.
Pleural effusion: aspirate under ultrasound; pH 7.2 or less or frank pus needs a chest drain (see pleural infection protocol).
# Special situations
Older adults may present with confusion, falls or reduced intake without fever or cough.
Pregnancy: avoid doxycycline and fluoroquinolones; use a beta-lactam with azithromycin.
CKD: adjust amoxicillin-clavulanate, cefepime, carbapenems, levofloxacin, vancomycin and oseltamivir for eGFR; ceftriaxone, azithromycin, doxycycline and linezolid need no renal adjustment.
# Pitfalls
Empirical fluoroquinolone or linezolid in India, masking TB and selecting resistance.
Macrolide or doxycycline monotherapy for pneumococcal pneumonia in high-resistance settings.
Missing scrub typhus, leptospirosis, melioidosis or TB in 'non-resolving pneumonia'.
Continuing broad-spectrum or MRSA cover after negative cultures, and courses longer than needed.
# Dose
625 mg orally three times daily or 1 g twice daily; 1.2 g IV every 8 hours
ICMR preferred outpatient agent. Reduce dose if eGFR below 30 mL/min.
Cefuroxime / cefpodoxime
# Dose
Cefuroxime 500 mg orally twice daily (1.5 g IV twice daily); cefpodoxime 200 mg orally twice daily
Outpatient alternatives.
Azithromycin, 500 mg orally or IV once daily, Atypical cover in combination; check QTc.
Clarithromycin, 500 mg orally twice daily, Alternative macrolide; drug interactions.
Doxycycline, 100 mg orally twice daily, Alternative to macrolide in combination; also covers scrub typhus and leptospirosis. Avoid in pregnancy.
Ceftriaxone, 2 g IV once daily (IDSA/ATS range 1 to 2 g daily), Ward and ICU backbone. Cefotaxime 2 g IV every 8 hours is an alternative.
4.5 g IV every 6 hours (IDSA/ATS; ICMR lists every 8 hours), Only with Pseudomonas risk. Renal adjustment.
Meropenem, 1 g IV every 8 hours, Pseudomonas or ESBL risk with septic shock. Renal adjustment.
