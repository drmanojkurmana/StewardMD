# Pierson Syndrome
Pierson Syndrome, Renal / Glomerular, Nephrology.
Aliases: Microcoria-congenital nephrosis syndrome; LAMB2-related disorder.
# Pathophysiology
Pierson syndrome arises from loss-of-function mutations in LAMB2, which encodes the laminin beta-2 chain, a component of laminin-521 and related heterotrimers that are critical structural elements of several specialised basement membranes. Laminin beta-2 is highly expressed in the glomerular basement membrane, where it anchors podocytes and maintains the integrity of the filtration barrier; its deficiency destabilises this barrier and produces congenital nephrotic syndrome with the histological pattern of diffuse mesangial sclerosis. The same laminin chain is essential in the intraocular basement membranes and in the developing pupil and lens, so its absence causes microcoria and other ocular malformations, and it is a key element of the synaptic basement membrane at the neuromuscular junction and within parts of the nervous system, accounting for the hypotonia and neurodevelopmental impairment. The severity of the phenotype broadly tracks the degree of residual laminin beta-2 function, with complete loss producing the full classic triad in early infancy and hypomorphic variants yielding milder, later-onset renal and ocular disease.
