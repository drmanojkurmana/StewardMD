Retroperitoneal fibrosis, eponymously Ormond disease, is a chronic fibro-inflammatory mantle that spreads across the posterior abdominal wall. The plaque centres on the lumbar vertebral column and the anterior surface of the aorta, typically spanning L4 to S1, and its slow contraction engulfs the ureters as they cross the pelvic brim, producing progressive extrinsic obstruction that threatens both kidneys.

A modern reclassification places 50% to 70% of idiopathic cases within systemic IgG4-related disease. Serology shows an elevated serum IgG4 concentration, and biopsy reveals a dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate rich in IgG4-positive plasma cells with storiform fibrosis. Recognising this signature matters, because IgG4-related plaques shrink briskly under corticosteroids while other fibrotic disorders respond poorly.

Urography, whether intravenous or CT based, displays the time honoured triad: medial deviation of the middle ureteral segments toward the spine, smooth extrinsic ureteral narrowing centred at L4-L5, and proximal hydroureteronephrosis with calyceal clubbing. The triad reflects tethering rather than invasion, and its symmetry across both sides is a further benign clue.

Malignant masquerade arises when lymphoma, retroperitoneal sarcoma, or metastatic carcinoma infiltrates the same compartment. Three features betray it: lateral deviation of the ureters away from the midline, cephalad extension above the renal vessels toward L2, and an anteriorly displaced aorta on cross sectional imaging. Any of these mandates image guided core biopsy, since corticosteroid monotherapy would merely mask progression.

First line therapy for active benign disease is medical. Prednisolone at 1 mg/kg/day for 4 weeks induces remission, followed by a gradual taper across 6 to 9 months with surveillance of symptoms, serum creatinine, inflammatory markers, and plaque bulk on imaging. Tamoxifen, acting through fibroblast modulation, and mycophenolate, through lymphocyte suppression, serve as steroid sparing adjuncts for refractory disease or intolerable steroid toxicity.

Operative ureterolysis is kept for obstruction that defies medicines, for diagnostic uncertainty, or for a solitary kidney under threat. The surgeon incises the fibrotic mantle, dissects each ureter free along its encased segment, biopsies the plaque edge, then secures the result by intraperitonealization, transposing the mobilised ureter laterally beneath the peritoneum, or by omental wrapping, cushioning it in vascularised fatty apron. Double J stents splint the freed ureters through healing.

Secondary causes must be excluded. Methysergide and ergotamine head the drug list, with beta-blockers and hydralazine also implicated; withdrawal can arrest early plaques. Finally, disease activity is quantified with 18F-FDG PET-CT, whose tracer avidity mirrors histologic inflammation and falls measurably with successful immunosuppression, allowing the taper to be steered by imaging rather than symptoms alone.
