Right-sided colon cancers bleed silently into the stool and present with iron deficiency anaemia and a palpable mass, while left-sided lesions narrow the lumen early and present with colicky pain and altered bowel habit. Rectal cancers add bleeding with tenesmus and urgency, and every new rectal bleed in an adult needs examination and endoscopy.

Rectal cancer surgery follows the holy plane. Total mesorectal excision removes the rectum wrapped in its fatty envelope down to the pelvic floor, preserving the autonomic nerves, with a 2 cm distal margin and a circumferential margin over 1 mm marking a clear resection.

Colon cancer surgery follows the vessels. Complete mesocolic excision takes the colon with its intact mesentery and central vascular ligation at the trunk origin, and harvesting at least 12 nodes stages the disease and guides chemotherapy decisions.

Radiation shrinks rectal tumours before the knife. Mid and low cancers receive long-course chemoradiotherapy of 50.4 Gy or short-course 25 Gy in 5 fractions, then total mesorectal excision at 6 to 12 weeks. Complete clinical responders with no residual tumour on reassessment may enter watch-and-wait surveillance instead of immediate surgery.

An obstructing left-sided cancer in a stable patient can be stented. A self-expanding metallic stent opens the lumen as a bridge to elective resection after bowel preparation, or as palliation when disease is incurable, while right-sided obstructions go straight to extended right hemicolectomy.

Liver spread does not always forbid cure. Synchronous metastases with an asymptomatic primary suit liver-first or simultaneous resection in expert centres, and disappearing metastases after chemotherapy still harbour viable cells, so the original liver sites must be resected or ablated.

Emergencies demand safe exits. The Hartmann procedure with end colostomy suits perforated cancers with faecal peritonitis and unstable patients, with reversal after 3 to 6 months. A defunctioning loop ileostomy protects low anastomoses within 5 cm of the anal verge and is reversed at 8 to 12 weeks once imaging confirms healing.

Heredity changes the operation. Lynch screening with microsatellite instability or mismatch repair testing on every colorectal cancer guides extended colectomy and triggers cascade testing of relatives for MLH1, MSH2, MSH6 and PMS2 carriers.
