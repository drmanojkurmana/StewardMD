# Atrial fibrillation: rapid ventricular rate, rhythm control and anticoagulation
Cardiovert immediately if unstable; otherwise control rate (initial target below 110/min) with a beta-blocker, diltiazem or verapamil (LVEF above 40%) or digoxin, delay cardioversion beyond 24 hours unless anticoagulated for 3 weeks or TOE-cleared, and anticoagulate with a DOAC when CHA2DS2-VA is 2 or more.
recognise
# Recognise and classify
Irregularly irregular narrow-complex rhythm without distinct P waves on a 12-lead ECG (or a 30-second single-lead strip). Irregular broad-complex tachycardia at very fast rates suggests pre-excited AF (WPW).
Classify: first diagnosed, paroxysmal (ends within 7 days), persistent (over 7 days), permanent (rhythm control no longer pursued).
Unstable features needing immediate cardioversion: hypotension or shock, acute pulmonary oedema, ongoing ischaemia, syncope.
Look for triggers: sepsis, thyrotoxicosis, alcohol, hypokalaemia or hypomagnesaemia, pulmonary embolism, acute MI, post-operative state, and in India rheumatic mitral valve disease.
immediate
# Acute management
Haemodynamically unstable: urgent synchronised DC cardioversion under sedation; start heparin without delaying the shock. If cardioversion fails or AF recurs, IV amiodarone for rate control.
Stable with rapid rate: treat the trigger, then rate control. Initial target resting heart rate below 110/min (lenient), tighter if symptoms persist.
LVEF above 40%: beta-blocker, diltiazem or verapamil, or digoxin. LVEF 40% or less or decompensated HF: beta-blocker and/or digoxin; avoid diltiazem and verapamil.
Pre-excited AF (WPW): do not give AV nodal blockers (adenosine, beta-blockers, diltiazem, verapamil, digoxin) or IV amiodarone; DC cardioversion if unstable, otherwise specialist advice.
Start anticoagulation early (LMWH or DOAC) in patients being considered for cardioversion or with CHA2DS2-VA of 2 or more, unless contraindicated.
investigations
# Investigations
12-lead ECG, CBC, creatinine and creatinine clearance (Cockcroft-Gault for DOAC dosing), electrolytes including magnesium, liver function, TSH, glucose.
Echocardiography: LVEF (guides drug choice), valve disease (mitral stenosis, prosthetic valves), left atrial size.
Troponin only if acute coronary syndrome is suspected (modest rises are common with fast rates). Chest X-ray or other tests for the trigger as indicated.
treatment
# Rhythm control and cardioversion
Safety first: if AF has lasted over 24 hours (or duration is unknown), do not cardiovert without at least 3 weeks of therapeutic anticoagulation or a TOE that excludes left atrial thrombus.
Recent-onset stable AF: a wait-and-see approach with rate control is reasonable, as many episodes end spontaneously within 48 hours; cardiovert if AF persists and the patient is symptomatic.
Pharmacological cardioversion: flecainide or propafenone only if there is no significant structural or ischaemic heart disease, LVH or reduced LVEF; amiodarone if structural heart disease or heart failure.
Continue anticoagulation for at least 4 weeks after cardioversion, and long term according to CHA2DS2-VA, whatever the rhythm outcome.
Consider early rhythm control (antiarrhythmic drugs or catheter ablation) within 12 months of diagnosis in selected patients; catheter ablation is an option first-line for symptomatic paroxysmal AF.
treatment
# Stroke prevention: anticoagulation
CHA2DS2-VA (ESC 2024, sex removed): congestive heart failure 1, hypertension 1, age 75 or older 2, diabetes 1, prior stroke, TIA or thromboembolism 2, vascular disease 1, age 65 to 74 1.
Score 2 or more: oral anticoagulation recommended. Score 1: anticoagulation should be considered. Score 0: no anticoagulation. ACC/AHA 2023 uses CHA2DS2-VASc (anticoagulate at 2 or more in men and 3 or more in women).
Prefer a DOAC over a VKA, at full dose unless the drug-specific dose-reduction criteria are met. Do not under-dose.
Use a VKA (warfarin or acenocoumarol, INR 2.0 to 3.0) for mechanical heart valves and moderate to severe mitral stenosis (common rheumatic cause in India); DOACs are contraindicated in these patients.
Do not use aspirin or other antiplatelets as a substitute for anticoagulation. Do not withhold anticoagulation because of a bleeding score; instead correct modifiable bleeding risks (BP, alcohol, NSAIDs, antiplatelets, labile INR).
special
# Special situations
CKD: dabigatran is contraindicated if CrCl is below 30 mL/min; avoid DOACs below 15 mL/min or on dialysis except under specialist advice.
Pregnancy: rate control with beta-1 selective beta-blocker or digoxin; LMWH for anticoagulation; electrical cardioversion is safe if unstable.
Thyrotoxicosis: beta-blocker plus antithyroid treatment; anticoagulate by stroke risk.
AF with ACS or PCI: triple therapy (OAC, aspirin, clopidogrel) for up to 1 week, then OAC plus clopidogrel up to 12 months, then OAC alone.
pitfalls
# Pitfalls
Cardioverting AF of more than 24 hours or unknown duration without anticoagulation or TOE.
Diltiazem or verapamil in reduced LVEF; AV nodal blockers in pre-excited AF.
Reduced-dose DOAC without meeting criteria (under-dosing increases stroke risk).
Giving a DOAC to a patient with rheumatic mitral stenosis or a mechanical valve.
