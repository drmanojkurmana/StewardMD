Non-muscle invasive bladder cancer, NMIBC, groups Ta tumours, non-invasive papillary growths above the basement membrane, T1 tumours invading the lamina propria and subepithelial connective tissue, and Tis, carcinoma in situ, a flat high grade lesion denoted Tis / CIS. Staging accuracy at first resection determines everything downstream, since missed muscle invasion converts a bladder sparing plan into delayed cystectomy.

Transurethral resection, TURBT, must therefore sample deeply. Guidelines insist the specimen contain detrusor muscle, the muscularis propria, because its absence leaves T1 versus T2 indeterminate. Surgeons document muscle presence explicitly, and its absence mandates early re-resection rather than watchful waiting.

Restaging through second-look TURBT follows within 2 to 6 weeks for T1 disease, high grade histology, or muscle absent specimens. Re-resection of the scar base detects residual tumour in a large fraction and corrects understaging, with upstaging to muscle invasion redirecting patients to definitive therapy.

A single immediate instillation of intravesical chemotherapy, mitomycin C 40 mg or gemcitabine, delivered within 24 hours of TURBT, eradicates circulating tumour cells and lowers recurrence by 35% in low-to-intermediate risk patients. Perforation or uncontrolled haematuria are the only accepted reasons to withhold it.

High-risk NMIBC receives Bacillus Calmette-Guerin immunotherapy: BCG induction weekly for 6 weeks, then 3-year maintenance under the SWOG protocol, with 3 weekly instillations at 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, and 36 months. Maintenance halves long term recurrence compared with induction alone, justifying the three year commitment.

BCG sepsis, with fever and hemodynamic instability, constitutes an emergency. Management stops all BCG permanently and institutes antitubercular therapy, isoniazid with rifampin and ethambutol, together with high-dose corticosteroids, mirroring disseminated mycobacterial treatment.

Failure is defined crisply: BCG-unresponsive disease means persistent or recurrent high-grade Ta or T1 within 6 months, or persistent CIS within 12 months, after adequate BCG. Radical cystectomy becomes the default recommendation, given the steep progression risk of neglected high grade disease.

For BCG-unresponsive CIS in patients unfit for or refusing cystectomy, pembrolizumab, systemic anti-PD-1 immunotherapy, and nadofaragene firadenovec, intravesical adenoviral vector gene therapy driving interferon expression, hold FDA approval as bladder preserving alternatives with documented complete response rates.
