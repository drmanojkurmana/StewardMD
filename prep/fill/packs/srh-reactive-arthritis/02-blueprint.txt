# Reactive arthritis (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison)
Reactive arthritis is sterile synovitis starting 1 to 4 weeks after distant gut or urogenital infection. Enteric triggers are Salmonella enterica, Shigella flexneri, Yersinia enterocolitica, and Campylobacter jejuni; the venereal trigger is Chlamydia trachomatis.
HLA-B27 is present in 60 percent to 85 percent of patients and predicts severe, axial, chronic, and relapsing disease with extra-articular features.
The classic triad, once called Reiter syndrome, is non-gonococcal urethritis or cervicitis with conjunctivitis and acute asymmetric oligoarthritis of large lower limb joints such as knees, ankles, and subtalar joints.
Keratoderma blennorrhagicum gives hyperkeratotic pustular papules on palms and soles resembling pustular psoriasis; circinate balanitis gives painless serpiginous erosions of glans and coronal sulcus.
Oral disease is small shallow painless ulcers of tongue, hard palate, and buccal mucosa, often missed without deliberate inspection.
Acute therapy is full-dose NSAID therapy with intra-articular glucocorticoid for refractory mono or oligoarthritis; systemic glucocorticoids suit severe polyarticular flares or systemic inflammation.
Confirmed Chlamydia by first-void urine or cervical NAAT requires doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 7 days or single-dose azithromycin 1 g for patient and partners; antibiotics do not change post-dysenteric enteric disease.
Chronic disease beyond 3 to 6 months affects 15 percent to 20 percent; sulfasalazine 2 g to 3 g daily is the csDMARD of choice for chronic peripheral arthritis, with anti TNF biologics for refractory cases.
Enthesitis of the Achilles tendon and plantar fascia is common; dactylitis and inflammatory back pain with alternating buttock pain mark patients who later satisfy axial SpA criteria. HIV positive patients develop more severe prolonged reactive arthritis with few axial features.
Synovial fluid is inflammatory but sterile with negative cultures; synovial biopsy shows non-specific chronic inflammation. Conjunctivitis is bilateral in above 30 percent; acute anterior uveitis occurs in a minority and needs topical glucocorticoids.
