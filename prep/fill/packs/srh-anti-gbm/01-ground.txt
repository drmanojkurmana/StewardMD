# Anti-GBM disease (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-anti-gbm: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Anti-glomerular basement membrane (anti-GBM) disease (Goodpasture disease) is an acute, severe autoimmune vasculitis caused by autoantibodies directed against the non-collagenous 1 (NC1) domain of the alpha-3 chain of type IV collagen [alpha3(IV)NC1].

The clinical presentation features rapidly progressive crescentic glomerulonephritis in nearly all patients, with concurrent life-threatening diffuse alveolar hemorrhage (pulmonary-renal syndrome, historically termed Goodpasture syndrome) occurring in 40% to 60%, heavily provoked by cigarette smoking, hydrocarbon inhalation, or respiratory infection.

Direct immunofluorescence of renal biopsy tissue reveals the pathognomonic finding: intense, unbroken, continuous, bright linear deposition of IgG (primarily IgG1 and IgG3) and C3 along the glomerular capillary basement membranes.

Light microscopy shows extensive, severe necrotizing glomerulonephritis with cellular crescents affecting >50% to 80% of glomeruli, which rapidly progress to circumferential fibrocellular crescents and irreversible global glomerulosclerosis if untreated.

'Double-positive' patients possess both circulating anti-GBM antibodies and ANCA (predominantly MPO-ANCA) in up to 20% to 30% of anti-GBM cases; they present acutely like severe anti-GBM disease but exhibit a higher long-term relapse rate characteristic of ANCA-associated vasculitis.

Immediate first-line triple therapy is mandatory: intensive therapeutic plasma exchange (daily 4-liter exchanges for 14 days or until anti-GBM titers are undetectable) combined with high-dose intravenous glucocorticoids and oral cyclophosphamide (2 mg/kg/day for 2 to 3 months).

Patients who present with anuria, 100% cellular crescents on biopsy, and are dialysis-dependent at presentation rarely recover renal function; however, if diffuse alveolar hemorrhage is present, aggressive plasma exchange and immunosuppression are still life-saving.

Unlike ANCA vasculitis or SLE, pure anti-GBM disease is a monophasic autoimmune illness with very low risk of late relapse once circulating antibodies are cleared, permitting discontinuation of cyclophosphamide after 2 to 3 months and steroid withdrawal within 6 to 9 months.
