# Infective endocarditis: diagnosis, empirical therapy and surgery
Take 3 sets of blood cultures before antibiotics, image with TTE then TOE, apply the 2023 ESC modified Duke criteria, start empirical IV antibiotics by setting (native or late prosthetic versus early prosthetic or healthcare-associated), and involve an Endocarditis Team early for surgery indications: heart failure, uncontrolled infection and embolic risk.
recognise
# When to suspect
Fever with a new or changed murmur, prosthetic valve or cardiac device, previous IE, rheumatic or congenital heart disease, injecting drug use, or recent healthcare exposure (lines, haemodialysis).
Unexplained embolic events (stroke, limb or splenic infarcts), spondylodiscitis, new heart failure, new AV block, or persistent bacteraemia with S. aureus, streptococci or enterococci.
Peripheral signs are often absent: splinter haemorrhages, conjunctival petechiae, Janeway lesions, Osler nodes, Roth spots, splenomegaly, haematuria.
India: rheumatic heart disease remains a major substrate, and prior antibiotic use makes culture-negative IE common.
investigations
# Investigations and diagnostic criteria
Blood cultures before antibiotics: 3 sets (aerobic and anaerobic bottles, about 10 mL each) from separate venepunctures, ideally 30 minutes apart; do not wait for fever spikes.
Transthoracic echo first; transoesophageal echo if TTE is negative or non-diagnostic with ongoing suspicion, in all prosthetic valve or device cases, and to look for abscess before surgery. Repeat TOE in 5 to 7 days if the first is negative and suspicion remains.
Cardiac CT for perivalvular abscess or pseudoaneurysm; FDG-PET/CT for prosthetic valve IE (more than 3 months after surgery) and device infection; brain and abdominal imaging for embolic events.
2023 ESC modified Duke major criteria: typical organisms (oral streptococci, Streptococcus gallolyticus, HACEK, S. aureus, Enterococcus faecalis) in 2 separate cultures or persistently positive cultures, Coxiella burnetii; imaging showing vegetation, abscess, pseudoaneurysm, fistula, perforation or new prosthetic dehiscence.
Minor criteria: predisposing heart condition or injecting drug use, fever above 38 degrees C, embolic phenomena (including silent lesions on imaging), immunological phenomena (glomerulonephritis, Osler nodes, Roth spots, rheumatoid factor), microbiological evidence not meeting a major criterion.
Definite IE: 2 major, or 1 major plus 3 minor, or 5 minor criteria. Possible: 1 major plus 1 to 2 minor, or 3 to 4 minor. The 2023 Duke-ISCVID criteria are similar and also count intraoperative findings.
Culture-negative IE: serology for Coxiella, Bartonella and Brucella, and PCR or culture of excised valve tissue. Baseline CBC, creatinine, CRP, urinalysis, ECG (PR prolongation or AV block suggests root abscess).
treatment
# Empirical antibiotics (after cultures)
Stable patient with subacute presentation: it is reasonable to wait for culture results for a short period, taking cultures first. Septic, unstable or high embolic risk: start empirical therapy immediately after cultures.
Community-acquired native valve IE or late prosthetic valve IE (12 months or more after surgery): ampicillin plus ceftriaxone, or ampicillin plus flucloxacillin (or cloxacillin) plus gentamicin.
Penicillin allergy in community-acquired native valve IE: cefazolin or vancomycin, plus gentamicin.
Early prosthetic valve IE (under 12 months) or healthcare-associated IE: vancomycin (or daptomycin) plus gentamicin plus rifampicin; add rifampicin only for prosthetic valves. Local MRSA and gram-negative resistance may need broader cover; seek microbiology advice.
Switch to targeted therapy as soon as the organism is known. Usual duration: 4 weeks for native valve (2 weeks in selected uncomplicated streptococcal cases), 6 weeks for prosthetic valve and E. faecalis IE, counted from the first negative blood culture.
treatment
# Indications for surgery (Endocarditis Team)
Emergency (within 24 hours): severe acute regurgitation, obstruction or fistula causing refractory pulmonary oedema or cardiogenic shock.
Urgent (within 3 to 5 days): severe valve dysfunction causing heart failure or poor haemodynamic tolerance; locally uncontrolled infection (abscess, pseudoaneurysm, fistula, enlarging vegetation, prosthetic dehiscence, new AV block); persistently positive blood cultures despite appropriate antibiotics and control of metastatic foci.
Urgent to prevent embolism: aortic or mitral vegetation 10 mm or more with one or more embolic episodes despite appropriate therapy; vegetation 10 mm or more with severe valve dysfunction and low operative risk; very large vegetations (over 30 mm).
Fungal or multidrug-resistant organisms, and prosthetic valve IE due to S. aureus or non-HACEK gram-negative bacteria, usually need surgery.
Ischaemic stroke without haemorrhage or coma should not delay indicated surgery. Intracranial haemorrhage usually postpones surgery; decide case by case with neurology and cardiac surgery.
monitoring
# Monitoring
Repeat blood cultures every 24 to 48 hours until negative; daily temperature, CRP trend, and examination for new murmur, heart failure or emboli.
Gentamicin: once-daily dosing with trough level below 1 mg/L; stop after 2 weeks where used, and monitor renal function and hearing.
Vancomycin: trough 15 to 20 mg/L or AUC-guided dosing; creatinine at least twice weekly.
ECG regularly (new AV block or PR prolongation suggests perivalvular extension). Repeat echocardiography with any new complication and at the end of treatment.
Partial oral step-down can be considered in stable patients with streptococci, E. faecalis, S. aureus or coagulase-negative staphylococci after at least 10 days of IV therapy (at least 7 days after surgery), with no abscess on TOE.
prevention
# Prevention
Antibiotic prophylaxis before dental procedures involving the gingiva, periapical region or oral mucosa perforation, only for high-risk patients: previous IE, prosthetic valves (including transcatheter valves and repair with prosthetic material), ventricular assist devices, untreated cyanotic congenital heart disease, and congenital heart disease repaired with prosthetic material (for 6 months, or lifelong if residual shunt or regurgitation).
Regimen: amoxicillin 2 g orally (or ampicillin 2 g IV) 30 to 60 minutes before the procedure. Penicillin allergy: cefalexin 2 g orally, azithromycin or clarithromycin 500 mg orally, or doxycycline 100 mg orally. Clindamycin is no longer recommended.
Good oral hygiene and dental review twice yearly for high-risk patients; strict aseptic care of IV lines and devices.
pitfalls
# Pitfalls
Starting antibiotics before blood cultures in a stable patient, producing culture-negative IE.
Excluding IE on a normal TTE when suspicion is high, especially with prosthetic valves or S. aureus bacteraemia.
Delaying surgical referral in heart failure due to valve destruction.
Giving thrombolysis for embolic stroke in IE (high haemorrhage risk).
