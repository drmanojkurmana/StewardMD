Neurofibromatosis type 1 shows optic pathway gliomas in 15 to 20 percent, focal areas of signal intensity, sphenoid wing dysplasia and plexiform neurofibromas. Tuberous sclerosis from TSC1 or TSC2 mutation produces subependymal nodules with candle guttering along the lateral ventricles, cortical tubers and radial migration lines. Subependymal giant cell astrocytoma grows near the foramen of Monro, enhances strongly, obstructs cerebrospinal fluid flow and is treated by resection or everolimus. Cardiac rhabdomyomas occur in up to 80 percent of infants and regress, renal angiomyolipomas contain macroscopic fat, and pulmonary lymphangioleiomyomatosis affects adolescent females. Sturge-Weber syndrome combines leptomeningeal angiomatosis, choroid plexus enlargement, tram-track gyriform calcification and cerebral hemiatrophy. Neurofibromatosis type 2, the MISME syndrome, requires bilateral vestibular schwannomas with meningiomas and spinal ependymomas. Von Hippel-Lindau disease forms cerebellar and spinal hemangioblastomas, cystic masses with an enhancing mural nodule on the pial surface, plus retinal tumours and renal cell carcinoma. Ataxia telangiectasia from ATM mutation causes cerebellar vermian atrophy, telangiectasia and raised alpha-fetoprotein. Gorlin syndrome from PTCH1 mutation shows odontogenic keratocysts, falx calcification, bifid ribs and medulloblastoma risk.
 Posterior fossa malformations include Dandy-Walker malformation with vermian agenesis, cystic fourth ventricle dilatation and enlarged posterior fossa, Blake pouch cyst with retrocerebellar fluid and intact vermis, and mega cisterna magna over 10 mm with normal vermis and fourth ventricle. Joubert syndrome shows the molar tooth sign from thickened superior cerebellar peduncles. Chiari II malformation combines myelomeningocele with small posterior fossa, tectal beaking and callosal dysgenesis. Paediatric brain tumours include medulloblastoma in the vermis, pilocytic astrocytoma with cyst and mural nodule, ependymoma extending through the foramina of Luschka and Magendie, craniopharyngioma with calcification above the sella, and diffuse midline glioma of the pons. Congenital TORCH infections cause periventricular calcification in cytomegalovirus with microcephaly, scattered calcification in toxoplasmosis with hydrocephalus, and lenticulostriate vasculopathy, while herpes simplex causes temporal and insular necrosis in neonates presenting with seizures and encephalitis.
 Neurofibromatosis type 1 maps to chromosome 17 and type 2 to chromosome 22.
