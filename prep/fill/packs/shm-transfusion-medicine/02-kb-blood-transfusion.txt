# Blood transfusion: thresholds, components and acute reactions
Adults
Ward, emergency department, ICU, operating theatre
Use restrictive red cell thresholds (Hb below 7 g/dL for most stable inpatients, 7.5 for cardiac surgery, 8 for orthopaedic surgery or cardiovascular disease), single units with reassessment, evidence-based platelet, plasma and cryoprecipitate triggers, and stop the transfusion at once if an acute reaction is suspected.
Aliases: PRBC; packed red cells; platelet transfusion; FFP; fresh frozen plasma; transfusion reaction; TACO; TRALI; FNHTR; haemolytic transfusion reaction; AABB 2023.
# Red cell thresholds (AABB 2023)
Haemodynamically stable hospitalised adults, including haematology and oncology patients: consider transfusion when Hb is below 7 g/dL.
Cardiac surgery: a threshold of 7.5 g/dL may be used. Orthopaedic surgery or pre-existing cardiovascular disease: 8 g/dL.
Acute coronary syndrome is outside the restrictive recommendation; NICE suggests a threshold of 8 g/dL with a post-transfusion target of 8 to 10 g/dL.
Thresholds do not apply to active major haemorrhage (use the massive transfusion protocol) or chronic transfusion-dependent anaemia (individualised thresholds). Always weigh symptoms and clinical context.
Non-bleeding adults: give one unit, then reassess symptoms and Hb before the next. One unit raises Hb by about 1 g/dL in an average adult.
Treat the cause where possible instead of transfusing (iron deficiency with IV iron, B12 or folate deficiency).
# Platelets, plasma and cryoprecipitate
Prophylactic platelets for hypoproliferative thrombocytopenia (chemotherapy, allogeneic stem cell transplant) when platelets are below 10 x 10^9/L. Not routinely in autologous transplant, aplastic anaemia, ITP, TTP or HIT.
Procedures: CVC at a compressible site below 10; lumbar puncture below 20; low-risk interventional radiology below 20 and high-risk below 50; major non-neuraxial surgery below 50 (x 10^9/L). Neurosurgery or posterior eye surgery: keep above 100 (NICE).
Bleeding: transfuse for clinically significant (WHO grade 2) bleeding with platelets below 30 x 10^9/L; use up to 100 x 10^9/L for severe or critical-site bleeding.
Do not transfuse platelets in dengue without major bleeding, whatever the count, or in non-operative intracranial haemorrhage with platelets above 100 x 10^9/L even on antiplatelets (AABB/ICTMG 2025).
Plasma (FFP): only for clinically significant bleeding with PT or APTT ratio above 1.5, or before a procedure with bleeding risk and abnormal coagulation. Not to correct INR in non-bleeding patients and not for warfarin reversal (use PCC with vitamin K).
Cryoprecipitate: clinically significant bleeding with fibrinogen below 1.5 g/L, or before a procedure with bleeding risk when fibrinogen is below 1.0 g/L. Recheck fibrinogen after each dose.
# Suspected acute transfusion reaction: first actions
STOP the transfusion immediately. Keep the IV line open with 0.9% saline through a new giving set and record temperature, pulse, BP, respiratory rate and SpO2.
Bedside check: patient identity against the unit label and compatibility tag. A wrong-blood error causes most ABO-incompatible reactions.
Severe features (hypotension, hypoxia, chest or loin pain, rigors, collapse, bronchospasm, dark urine, oozing): call senior help, do not restart, and return the bag and giving set to the blood bank.
Anaphylaxis (airway, breathing or circulation compromise with skin or mucosal changes): adrenaline 0.5 mg IM (0.5 mL of 1 mg/mL) into the anterolateral thigh, repeat after 5 minutes if needed; high-flow oxygen and IV fluid bolus.
Suspected ABO incompatibility or bacterial contamination: IV crystalloid resuscitation, catheterise and maintain urine output above 100 mL/hour, take blood cultures from patient and bag, start broad-spectrum antibiotics per the neutropenic sepsis protocol, and watch for DIC.
# Reaction-specific management (BSH 2023)
Mild febrile reaction (temperature above 38 C with a rise of 1 to 2 C, no other features): pause, give paracetamol 1 g orally, and restart slowly with closer observation. Antihistamines and steroids have no role in febrile reactions.
Moderate or severe febrile reaction (rise of 2 C or more, 39 C or above, rigors or chills): stop and investigate for haemolysis and bacterial contamination; do not restart that unit.
Mild allergic reaction (urticaria or rash only): slow or pause and give chlorphenamine 10 mg IV slowly; continue if symptoms do not progress. Steroids are not used routinely.
TACO (dyspnoea, hypertension, raised JVP, crackles, positive fluid balance within 12 hours): stop, sit upright, oxygen and IV furosemide 20 to 40 mg.
TRALI (hypoxaemia and bilateral infiltrates within 6 hours, often with fever and hypotension, no circulatory overload): stop, treat as ARDS with oxygen or ventilation, avoid diuretics, and inform the blood bank so the donor can be traced.
# Investigations after a moderate or severe reaction
Repeat group and antibody screen on a post-reaction sample, DAT, FBC, LDH, bilirubin, haptoglobin, renal profile, coagulation screen with fibrinogen, and urinalysis for haemoglobin.
Blood cultures from the patient and the component bag for moderate or severe febrile reactions.
Dyspnoea: chest X-ray, blood gas and BNP or NT-proBNP to help separate TACO from TRALI.
Anaphylaxis: serum IgA level (anti-IgA in IgA deficiency) and mast cell tryptase. Isolated mild febrile or allergic reactions need no laboratory work-up.
# Safe administration
Record vital signs before each unit, 15 minutes after starting it and at the end, and observe in an area where staff can recognise and manage reactions.
Complete each red cell unit within 4 hours of leaving controlled storage.
Assess TACO risk before transfusing (elderly, heart failure, renal impairment, hypoalbuminaemia, positive fluid balance, low body weight): use single units, slower rates and consider a diuretic.
# Documentation and reporting (India)
Document indication, consent, components, unit numbers and any reaction in the notes.
Report moderate and severe reactions to the hospital transfusion committee and the Haemovigilance Programme of India.
After an anaphylactic or recurrent allergic reaction, seek blood bank advice before the next transfusion (washed or IgA-deficient components may be needed).
# Pitfalls
Transfusing two units by reflex, or to a number without assessing symptoms.
FFP to correct a raised INR before low-risk procedures or for warfarin reversal.
Platelets in dengue without major bleeding, in TTP or in HIT.
Treating a febrile reaction with antihistamines or steroids and missing bacterial contamination (platelets stored at 22 C carry the highest risk).
Confusing TACO with TRALI: diuretics help TACO but may worsen TRALI.
# Drugs
Red cells, 1 unit IV over 90 to 120 minutes (complete within 4 hours), then reassess, Slower rate and single units if TACO risk.
Platelets
1 adult therapeutic dose (1 apheresis unit or 4 to 6 pooled random donor units)
Recheck count after each dose; more than one dose only for critical-site bleeding.
Fresh frozen plasma, 15 mL/kg IV, Recheck PT and APTT after transfusion. Not for warfarin reversal when PCC is available.
Cryoprecipitate, Adult dose 2 pools (about 10 single-donor units) IV, Recheck fibrinogen after each dose.
Adrenaline (epinephrine), 0.5 mg IM (0.5 mL of 1 mg/mL), repeat after 5 minutes if needed, Anaphylaxis.
Chlorphenamine, 10 mg IV slowly, Allergic reactions only. Avoid IM injection if thrombocytopenic.
Paracetamol, 1 g orally or IV, maximum 4 g/day, Febrile non-haemolytic reactions.
Furosemide, 20 to 40 mg IV, TACO treatment or prevention. Avoid in TRALI.
