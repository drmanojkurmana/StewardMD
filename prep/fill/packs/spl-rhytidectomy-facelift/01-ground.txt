## Rhytidectomy: SMAS Plication, Deep Plane and Facial Retaining Ligaments
Citations and Chapter Sources: Plastic Surgery 5E Vol 2 Ch 4 (Facelift: principles and techniques)

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Mitz and Peyronie defined the superficial musculoaponeurotic system SMAS as a continuous fibroaponeurotic network investing and connecting mimetic facial muscles.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Mitz and Peyronie, SMAS, fibroaponeurotic network

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Facial retaining ligaments true osteocutaneous ligaments zygomatic retaining ligament McGregor patch and retaining mandibular ligament anchor soft tissue to underlying facial skeleton.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: retaining ligaments, zygomatic ligament, McGregor patch

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: High-SMAS and extended SMAS techniques release zygomatic and upper masseteric retaining ligaments to mobilize midcheek malar fat pads superomedially.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: high-SMAS, malar fat pad

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Deep plane rhytidectomy Hamra releases retaining ligaments beneath the SMAS layer, elevating the SMAS, malar fat, and platysma as a composite tension-free flap.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: deep plane rhytidectomy, Hamra, composite flap

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Platysma bands in the neck are managed via submental incision with anterior platysmaplasty midline corset plication and lateral SMAS-platysma suspension.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: platysma bands, platysmaplasty, corset plication

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Great auricular nerve GAN is the most frequently injured nerve during rhytidectomy, running over sternocleidomastoid 6.5 cm below the external auditory meatus.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 6.5
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: great auricular nerve, GAN, sternocleidomastoid

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Major haematoma is the most common early complication of rhytidectomy occurring in 1% to 3% of patients, presenting with sudden pain, unilateral swelling, and skin ecchymosis.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 1%, 3%
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: haematoma, rhytidectomy complication

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Preoperative control of hypertension systolic BP maintained strictly under 140 mmHg reduces postoperative haematoma risk by more than half.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 140
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: hypertension, systolic BP


Comprehensive Textbook Context:
Surgical planning in Rhytidectomy and Facial Rejuvenation requires meticulous anatomical grounding, precise knowledge of blood supply, innervation, fascial planes, and tissue biomechanics.
Operative interventions balance functional restoration of speech, swallowing, mastication, sensory discrimination, and locomotion with aesthetic symmetry and minimal donor site morbidity.
Preoperative evaluation integrates acoustic Doppler, computed tomography angiography, indocyanine green fluorescence imaging, and patient-specific risk factor optimization including smoking cessation, strict glycemic control, and blood pressure management.
Postoperative care prioritizes early mobilization, monitoring of capillary refill, tissue turgor, pinprick bleeding, active rehabilitation, and vigilant surveillance for hematoma, seroma, infection, or microvascular thrombosis.
