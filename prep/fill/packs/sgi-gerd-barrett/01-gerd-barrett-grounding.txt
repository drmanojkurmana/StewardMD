# GERD and Barrett oesophagus: diagnostic thresholds and management
# Source: StewardMD gastroenterology knowledge base

## Diagnostic thresholds (Lyon Consensus 2.0)
Conclusive GERD requires at least one objective marker: erosive oesophagitis of LA classification grade C or D, columnar-lined oesophagus (Barrett) extending 1 cm or more above the gastro-oesophageal junction, a peptic stricture of oesophageal origin, or ambulatory acid exposure time exceeding 6.0% of the recording period.

Borderline evidence: AET 4.0% to 6.0%, LA grade A or B oesophagitis alone.
Normal AET: below 4.0%.

## Symptom-reflux correlation thresholds
Symptom index (SI): ratio of symptom events preceded by reflux to total symptom events. A positive SI is defined as greater than 50%. Symptom association probability (SAP): a statistical measure; values above 95% are considered positive. Both thresholds are used to diagnose reflux hypersensitivity when AET is below 4.0%.

## Barrett oesophagus surveillance
Prague C and M criteria: the C value represents the length in centimetres of circumferential Barrett mucosa measured from the gastro-oesophageal junction upward. The M value represents the maximum extent (longest tongue) in centimetres from the junction.

Seattle protocol sampling: four-quadrant biopsies at 1 to 2 cm intervals through the Barrett segment, combined with targeted sampling of any raised or nodular areas.

## Endoscopic eradication therapy
EET combines resection of visible nodules via EMR with ablation of flat Barrett epithelium using RFA. The indication is HGD (high-grade dysplasia) or intramucosal adenocarcinoma confined to the mucosa. En bloc histological assessment guides curative intent.

## Pharmacology
Potassium-competitive acid blockers (P-CABs) such as vonoprazan bind the proton pump at the potassium-competitive site. Unlike PPIs, they are active at any gastric pH and reach peak suppression within the first dose. Vonoprazan 20 mg daily provides faster and more sustained acid suppression than standard PPI therapy and is approved for healing of erosive oesophagitis.

Neuromodulators used for functional oesophageal disorders include tricyclic antidepressants (amitriptyline, imipramine), SSRIs (paroxetine, sertraline, fluoxetine), and SNRIs (duloxetine). These agents reduce visceral hypersensitivity and central sensitisation.

## Anti-reflux surgery
LINX magnetic sphincter augmentation and laparoscopic Nissen fundoplication both require confirmed abnormal AET and normal oesophageal peristalsis. Weak peristalsis (distal contractile integral below 450 mmHg.cm.s) increases dysphagia risk post-operatively and is a relative contraindication.
