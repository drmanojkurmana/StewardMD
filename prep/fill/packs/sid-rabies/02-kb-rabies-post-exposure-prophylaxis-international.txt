# Rabies post-exposure prophylaxis (WHO)
All ages
Emergency department, anti-rabies clinic, outpatient
Classify the exposure, wash every wound with soap and water for 15 minutes, and vaccinate Category II and III exposures at once (WHO 1-week ID: 0.1 mL at 2 sites on days 0, 3 and 7). Add RIG (HRIG 20 IU/kg, ERIG 40 IU/kg) or a monoclonal antibody infiltrated into all Category III wounds in unvaccinated patients.
Aliases: rabies PEP; dog bite; cat bite; bat exposure; animal bite; WHO rabies position paper 2018; IPC regimen; 1-week intradermal; Essen; Zagreb; RIG; HRIG; ERIG; rabies monoclonal antibody; PrEP rabies.
# Assess the exposure
Category I: touching or feeding animals, licks on intact skin. No exposure: wash the skin, no PEP.
Category II: nibbling of uncovered skin, minor scratches or abrasions without bleeding. Wound washing and immediate vaccination.
Category III: single or multiple transdermal bites or scratches, saliva on mucous membranes or broken skin, or direct contact with bats. Wound washing, immediate vaccination and RIG or rabies monoclonal antibody.
Wounds on the head, neck, face, hands or genitals, multiple or deep bites, a sick or unprovoked animal, and immunodeficiency all raise the risk and the urgency.
Only mammals transmit rabies; no human rabies death is known from a rodent bite. Eating meat or drinking milk from a rabid animal does not need PEP (processing its carcass may).
Start PEP whatever the delay since exposure, even months later. PEP can be stopped if a dog, cat or ferret stays healthy for 10 days from the bite, or the animal tests negative in a reliable laboratory.
# Wound care (every exposure)
Wash and flush every wound with copious running water and soap or detergent for 15 minutes as soon as possible; rinse exposed eyes and mucosa thoroughly with water.
Then apply an antiseptic such as povidone-iodine. Discourage home remedies on the wound.
Do not close or tightly bandage the wound. Avoid suturing; if unavoidable, delay it for some hours after RIG or monoclonal antibody infiltration and keep sutures loose and minimal.
Tetanus vaccine (primary course, or a booster if the last dose was over 10 years ago or per national schedule); consider antibiotics for deep or contaminated wounds.
# Vaccine schedules: never vaccinated
WHO preferred, 1-week intradermal (IPC) regimen: 0.1 mL ID at 2 sites (both deltoids, or thighs or suprascapular areas) on days 0, 3 and 7.
Alternatives: 2-week IM regimen, 1 full vial IM on days 0, 3, 7 and once between days 14 and 28 (4-dose Essen); or 3-week IM regimen, 2 vials on day 0 (one in each deltoid) then 1 vial on days 7 and 21 (Zagreb). Earlier WHO IM schedules remain acceptable.
The dose does not change with age or weight. IM: deltoid in adults and children aged 2 years or more, anterolateral thigh under 2 years; never gluteal; never at the RIG site.
Use cell culture or embryonated egg vaccines (potency 2.5 IU or more per vial); any of them can be given ID even if labelled for IM use, where the national regulator allows. Use opened vials within 6 to 8 hours.
Missed or late doses: resume the schedule, do not restart. Change route or product only if unavoidable, continuing with the schedule for the new route.
Never withhold or delay vaccine because RIG is unavailable; give the first dose and refer for RIG.
# Rabies immunoglobulin or monoclonal antibody (Category III)
Indicated for Category III exposures in people never vaccinated, and for Category II and III exposures in immunocompromised people even if previously vaccinated.
Maximum dose: HRIG 20 IU/kg, ERIG 40 IU/kg, single rabies monoclonal antibody 3.33 IU/kg, monoclonal antibody cocktail 40 IU/kg (WHO 2024); follow the product label for monoclonals. WHO encourages monoclonals instead of RIG where available.
Infiltrate as much as is anatomically feasible deep into and around all wounds, avoiding compartment syndrome; dilute with saline for large or multiple wounds. Do not inject the remainder IM at a distant site; fractionate it aseptically for other patients that day.
Exceptions: inject the remainder IM near the presumed site if unseen wounds are likely (for example a young child) or after bat contact; rinse mucosal exposures with RIG; give RIG IM after aerosol exposure.
Give once, with the first vaccine dose or as soon as possible; not after day 7 of vaccination. No skin test before ERIG; observe for 15 to 20 minutes with anaphylaxis treatment ready.
If supply is short, prioritise multiple or deep bites, bites to the head, neck or hands, severe immunodeficiency, a confirmed or probably rabid animal, and bat exposures.
# Previously vaccinated, immunocompromised and other groups
Previously vaccinated (documented PrEP, or at least 2 doses of a cell culture vaccine as PEP): wound care plus 0.1 mL ID at 1 site on days 0 and 3, or 0.1 mL ID at 4 sites on day 0, or 1 vial IM on days 0 and 3. No RIG. Re-exposure within 3 months of a completed PEP: wound care only.
