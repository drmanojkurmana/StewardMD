## Head and Neck Oncology Defect Coverage: Fibula, Radial Forearm and ALT
Citations and Chapter Sources: Plastic Surgery 5E Vol 3 Ch 33 (Head and neck reconstruction)

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Free vascularised fibula osteocutaneous flap based on the peroneal artery and veins is the primary workhorse for segmental mandibular and maxillary reconstructions.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: fibula, osteocutaneous flap, peroneal artery

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Fibula flap provides up to 25 to 30 cm of straight bicortical bone, can undergo multiple closing wedge osteotomies, and accepts osseointegrated dental implants.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 25, 30
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: bicortical bone, osseointegrated implants

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Distal 6 to 8 cm and proximal 6 to 8 cm of fibula must be preserved during harvest to safeguard lateral ankle stability and common peroneal nerve function.
Key numerical metrics, dimensions, intervals, and angles: 6, 8
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: fibula harvest, ankle stability, common peroneal nerve

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Radial forearm free flap RFFF based on radial artery and cephalic vein provides pliable thin skin ideal for oral cavity tongue and floor of mouth reconstruction.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: radial forearm, RFFF, oral cavity

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Mandatory pre-operative Allen test confirms palmar arch patency and adequate ulnar collateral circulation prior to radial forearm flap elevation.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: Allen test, ulnar circulation

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Anterolateral thigh ALT free flap provides versatile soft tissue and skin for complex head and neck defects with negligible donor site functional morbidity.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: anterolateral thigh, ALT, soft tissue

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Pectoralis major myocutaneous flap PMMC based on the pectoral branch of the thoracoacromial artery serves as the reliable regional salvage workhorse flap.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: pectoralis major, PMMC, thoracoacromial artery

Core Must-Know Examination Principle: Pharyngoesophageal circumferencial defects are reconstructed with a free jejunal transfer or tubed anterolateral thigh / radial forearm cutaneous flaps.
Standard anatomical nomenclature and operative terminology: pharyngoesophageal, jejunal transfer


Comprehensive Textbook Context:
Surgical planning in Head and Neck Defect Reconstruction requires meticulous anatomical grounding, precise knowledge of blood supply, innervation, fascial planes, and tissue biomechanics.
Operative interventions balance functional restoration of speech, swallowing, mastication, sensory discrimination, and locomotion with aesthetic symmetry and minimal donor site morbidity.
Preoperative evaluation integrates acoustic Doppler, computed tomography angiography, indocyanine green fluorescence imaging, and patient-specific risk factor optimization including smoking cessation, strict glycemic control, and blood pressure management.
Postoperative care prioritizes early mobilization, monitoring of capillary refill, tissue turgor, pinprick bleeding, active rehabilitation, and vigilant surveillance for hematoma, seroma, infection, or microvascular thrombosis.
