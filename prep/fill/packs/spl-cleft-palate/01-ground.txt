Exam grounding notes for cleft palate repair and velopharyngeal function, written for grounding, not for display. Paraphrased from Plastic Surgery 5E Volume 3 on palate repair.

The secondary palate forms when the two palatal shelves, outgrowths of the maxillary prominences, rise and fuse in the midline. Fusion failure between the 8th and 12th weeks of gestation leaves a cleft of the soft palate, hard palate, or both. Earlier lip fusion and later alveolar events follow their own timetable.

Surgeons close the palate between 9 and 12 months of age. Earlier closure favours speech development by giving the child a working velum before babbling patterns fix, while later closure spares maxillary growth. The 9 to 12 month window balances these competing demands.

In a cleft, the levator veli palatini fibres run forward and anchor wrongly into the posterior edge of the hard palate cleft margin, the bundle called the Laimer muscle. Repair detaches this misplaced insertion and swings the muscle back into a transverse sling across the soft palate. Without this muscle retropositioning, speech stays nasal whatever the skin closure.

Furlow double-reversing Z-plasty moves opposing Z-plasty flaps on the oral mucoperiosteal side and the nasal myomucosal side in opposite directions. The design pushes the levator into its sling and adds length to the soft palate. It suits narrow clefts with good tissue.

Two-flap palatoplasty in the Bardach style raises left and right mucoperiosteal flaps supplied by the greater palatine arteries and joins them in the midline without tension. Wide clefts needing large movement take this robust two-flap route to full oral closure.

Velopharyngeal insufficiency means the soft palate cannot seal against the back wall during speech. Children sound hypernasal, leak air through the nose, and develop compensatory articulation errors. Video nasoendoscopy watches the gap directly and guides the operation choice.

Gap pattern picks the operation. A coronal closure pattern with a central gap receives a superiorly based posterior pharyngeal flap that tethers the palate upward. A sagittal pattern with side gaps receives a sphincter pharyngoplasty that narrows the port from the sides.

A submucous cleft hides under intact mucosa. The Calnan triad defines it: a bifid uvula, a palpable notch at the posterior hard palate edge, and a central zone of muscular diastasis seen as zona pellucida translucency. Finding the triad explains hypernasality in a child with no open cleft.
