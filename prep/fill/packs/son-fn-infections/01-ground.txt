Febrile neutropenia, fungal markers and catheter infection grounding notes

Neutrophil output after cytotoxic treatment

- Most cytotoxic drugs act on rapidly dividing cells, so the supply of new neutrophils from the blood-forming tissue drops. Mainly a failure of production.
- Drugs of this type also injure dividing neutrophil precursors, and those cells then die by apoptosis, a programmed form of cell death.
- Agents that act on early progenitors, such as nitrosoureas and mitomycin C, produce neutropenia that comes later, lasts longer and builds up over cycles.
- Neutrophil kinetics sets the pattern. Circulating neutrophils last roughly 12 hours; in tissue, about 3 days.
- Granulocytes held in reserve in the blood-forming tissue can cover roughly 7 days of demand.
- Progenitors take 10 to 14 days to reach maturity. The lowest count therefore appears near day 10 of a cycle, and recovery finishes by day 21.

Growth factors

- Filgrastim is an engineered form of granulocyte colony-stimulating factor, abbreviated G-CSF. It helps neutrophils survive longer.
- Sargramostim is an engineered granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor, abbreviated GM-CSF. It is the only recombinant GM-CSF approved in the United States.
- Oprelvekin is recombinant interleukin-11, abbreviated IL-11. It was approved to raise low platelet counts in solid tumors and lymphoma, then withdrawn for poor effect and significant toxicity. Its adverse effects include fluid retention and atrial arrhythmias such as atrial fibrillation.

MASCC risk index

- The Multinational Association for Supportive Cancer Care index adds points for patient features.
- Absence of hypotension scores 5 points. Absence of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease scores 4. Outpatient status scores 3. Age under 60 years scores 2.
- A total of 21 or higher means low risk. A total below 21 means high risk.

Febrile neutropenia

- Fever meets the threshold with one oral reading of 38.3 degrees Celsius, or with 38.0 degrees held for 1 hour.
- Fever with a neutrophil count under 1,000 per microlitre is handled as a neutropenic infection until shown otherwise.
- Empirical antibiotics, picked before any organism is known, should start within 1 hour of onset.
- Hypotension or organ injury signals instability and calls for a broader regimen. High-risk patients, including those with hypotension, are admitted to hospital for intravenous antibiotics according to the 2010 IDSA guideline.
- Outpatient oral treatment suits stable, lower-risk patients with solid tumors and neutropenia of 7 days or less.

Empirical drug choice

- Pseudomonas aeruginosa, a gram-negative organism, must be covered by the first regimen. Gram-negative bacteremia carries high mortality here.
- The best-studied single agents are cefepime, ceftazidime, imipenem, meropenem and piperacillin-tazobactam.
- Ceftriaxone does not appear among these options.
- Vancomycin is not routine. Adding it to every regimen gives no better outcome and more nephrotoxicity.
- Gram-positive cover is added for sepsis, a visible soft tissue infection, a suspected line exit-site infection, pneumonia, severe mucositis when ceftazidime is the starting drug, or known carriage of MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus) or penicillin-resistant pneumococcus.

Fungal biomarkers

- Galactomannan is most useful on bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid for aspergillosis. Preventive antifungal drugs make false negatives common.
- Other fungal infections can give false positives. Piperacillin-tazobactam was linked to false positives in patients with hematologic malignancy in a 2004 letter in a major infectious diseases journal and a 2025 study again linked it to false positives.
- Beta-D-glucan (BDG) has a high negative predictive value for invasive candidiasis, so a negative result makes that infection unlikely. The test lacks specificity.
- Mucormycosis gives negative biomarker results. Histology showing ribbon-like hyphae and culture are needed instead.

Catheter-related infection

- The same organism growing from a central line culture and a peripheral vein culture supports catheter-related bacteremia.
- A differential time to positivity of at least 2 hours, with the central culture turning positive first, points to the catheter as the source.
- For confirmed catheter infection, the preferred step is removing the catheter.
