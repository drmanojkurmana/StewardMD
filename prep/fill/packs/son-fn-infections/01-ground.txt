Neutrophil timing after cytotoxic treatment

- Neutrophil output follows kinetics: how fast cells are produced and how fast they leave the circulation.
- Mature neutrophils circulate for roughly 12 hours. Once they settle in tissue they last around 3 days.
- A stock of mature granulocytes held in the cell-producing compartment can meet demand for about 7 days.
- Committed progenitors need 10 to 14 days to become mature cells.
- After a standard cycle the lowest count tends to appear near day 10. Recovery finishes by day 21.
- Growth factors shift these numbers. Filgrastim, sargramostim and oprelvekin are the three agents covered here.

Growth factor products

- Filgrastim is an engineered granulocyte colony-stimulating factor, abbreviated G-CSF. It prolongs neutrophil survival and is used to prevent chemotherapy-induced neutropenia and its infections.
- Sargramostim is an engineered granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor, abbreviated GM-CSF. It acts on myeloid precursors and on mature neutrophils and monocytes. It is the only recombinant GM-CSF licensed in the United States.
- Oprelvekin is recombinant interleukin-11, abbreviated IL-11. It acts on megakaryocytes. It was once approved to ease low platelet counts in solid tumors and lymphoma, then withdrawn because it worked poorly and caused substantial toxicity.
- Cytokines regulate how long neutrophils live. G-CSF lengthens that lifespan.

Predicting neutropenic infection

- The Multinational Association of Supportive Care in Cancer index, abbreviated MASCC, adds points for patient features.
- Feature points: no hypotension scores 5, absence of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease scores 4, outpatient status scores 3, age under 60 scores 2.
- A total of 21 or higher is low risk. A total below 21 is high risk.

Febrile neutropenia

- Febrile neutropenia needs one oral temperature of 38.3 or a reading of 38.0 held for 1 hour, together with a neutrophil count under 500 per microlitre.
- Empirical antibiotics, chosen before the organism is known, should start within 1 hour of onset.
- Hypotension or organ dysfunction signals instability and calls for a broader regimen.
- Outpatient oral treatment suits only stable patients with low risk, usually solid tumors and neutropenia lasting 7 days or less.

Empirical drug choice

- Single agents with broad coverage that have been studied most: ceftazidime, cefepime, imipenem, meropenem and piperacillin-tazobactam.
- Empirical cover must reach Pseudomonas aeruginosa, a gram-negative organism.
- Ceftriaxone does not appear on that list.
- Vancomycin is not routine. Adding it to every regimen gives no better outcome and more nephrotoxicity.
- Glycopeptide cover is added only in specific settings: systemic sepsis, a soft tissue infection that is visible to the eye, a suspected line exit-site problem, pneumonia, severe mucositis when ceftazidime is the initial drug, or a patient known to carry MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus).

Fungal biomarkers

- Galactomannan is most useful on bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid when aspergillosis is suspected. Preventive antifungal drugs make false negatives common. Other fungal infections can give false positives.
- Beta-D-glucan (BDG) has a high negative predictive value for invasive candidiasis. A negative result makes the infection less likely. The test lacks specificity.
- Biomarker tests are reported as negative in mucormycosis.

Catheter-related infection

- Same organism growing from a central line culture and a peripheral vein culture confirms catheter-related bacteremia.
- A differential time to positivity of at least 2 hours, with the central culture turning positive first, points to the catheter as the source.
- For confirmed catheter infection the preferred step is removing the catheter.
