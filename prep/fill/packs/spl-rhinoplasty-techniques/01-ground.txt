Rhinoplasty reshapes the nose through two routes to the same framework. Open rhinoplasty lifts the skin envelope via an inverted-V transcolumellar incision joined to bilateral marginal incisions, giving complete unhindered exposure of the cartilaginous framework for complex tip work.

Closed endonasal rhinoplasty reaches the same structures from inside. Intercartilaginous or intracartilaginous incisions hide every cut within the nostril, avoiding external columellar scars and minimizing the postoperative tip edema that prolongs the open result.

Function lives at the internal nasal valve, the narrowest airway cross-section. It is bounded by the upper lateral cartilage, the nasal septum, the anterior head of the inferior turbinate, and the floor of the pyriform aperture, forming an angle of only 10 to 15 degrees that governs most nasal resistance.

Hump reduction threatens this valve by detaching the upper lateral cartilages from the septum. Sheen spreader grafts placed between the dorsal septum and the upper lateral cartilages rebuild the valve walls and stent the middle vault, preventing the inverted-V roof collapse that pinches the airway.

Tip behaviour follows the Anderson tripod model of two lateral crura with the conjoined medial crura forming the third leg. Shortening the medial crura drops tip projection and rotates the tip downward, while lengthening support pushes the tip up and out.

Weak tips need a post. A columellar strut graft placed between the medial crura strengthens tip support, maintains projection against scar contracture, and corrects columellar retraction that shows too much nostril.

Bony work narrows the vault after hump resection leaves an open roof. Lateral osteotomies mobilized along the nasofacial groove infracture the nasal bones inward, closing the open-roof deformity and restoring the smooth dorsal lines from radix to tip.

Cartilage trimming demands a safety margin.
