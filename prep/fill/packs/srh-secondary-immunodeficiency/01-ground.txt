# Acquired immunodeficiency and immune dysregulation (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-secondary-immunodeficiency: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection triggers paradoxical rheumatic and autoimmune syndromes including severe reactive arthritis, psoriatic arthritis flares, polymyositis, and diffuse infiltrative lymphocytosis syndrome (DILS), even in the face of progressive CD4+ T-cell depletion.

Diffuse infiltrative lymphocytosis syndrome (DILS) occurs in HIV-infected patients, characterized by marked bilateral parotid gland enlargement, sicca symptoms, generalized lymphadenopathy, and a striking circulating and tissue-infiltrating CD8+ hyperlymphocytosis with positive HLA-DR5/DR6 associations, in the absence of anti-Ro and anti-La antibodies.

Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) represents an exaggerated, hyperinflammatory response following antiretroviral therapy (ART) initiation in patients with low CD4 counts, unmasking or paradoxically worsening occult opportunistic infections (tuberculosis, cryptococcosis, CMV) or preexisting autoimmune diseases.

Post-rituximab secondary hypogammaglobulinemia develops in up to 10% to 30% of patients receiving repeated B-cell depletion courses, especially those with baseline low-normal IgG or concomitant cyclophosphamide; persistent IgG levels below 400 mg/dL accompanied by recurrent sinopulmonary bacterial infections necessitate regular immunoglobulin replacement therapy.

Anatomic or functional asplenia markedly impairs clearance of unopsonized encapsulated bacteria and circulating Howell-Jolly bodies, predisposing to overwhelming post-splenectomy infection (OPSI) primarily caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae, Neisseria meningitidis, and Haemophilus influenzae type b, demanding comprehensive vaccination and standby oral antibiotics.

Severe secondary hypogammaglobulinemia arises from non-immune protein-losing states including nephrotic syndrome and protein-losing enteropathy; selective urinary or gastrointestinal loss of lower molecular weight IgG occurs with relative preservation of IgM, retaining normal specific vaccine-induced antibody responses.

Good syndrome is the rare clinical association of thymoma with adult-onset hypogammaglobulinemia, combined with low or absent circulating CD19+ B cells, inverted CD4/CD8 T-cell ratios, and profound anergy, predisposing to severe opportunistic fungal (Candida, Pneumocystis), viral (CMV, HSV), and sinopulmonary bacterial infections.

Therapeutic immunosuppression with calcineurin inhibitors (cyclosporine, tacrolimus) or mycophenolate mofetil impairs cell-mediated and humoral immune surveillance, mandating regular screening for BK polyomavirus nephropathy, cytomegalovirus reactivation, and post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorders.
