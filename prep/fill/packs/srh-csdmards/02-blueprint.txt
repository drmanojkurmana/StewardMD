# Conventional synthetic DMARDs and toxicity (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-csdmards: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Methotrexate acts primarily via intracellular polyglutamylation and inhibition of aminoimidazolecarboxamide ribonucleotide (AICAR) transformylase, leading to extracellular accumulation of adenosine which exerts potent anti-inflammatory effects through A2A receptors on leukocytes.

Methotrexate-induced pneumonitis is an idiosyncratic non-dose-dependent hypersensitivity reaction occurring in 1% to 2% of patients; it presents with fever, non-productive cough, and dyspnea, showing bilateral ground-glass opacities with bronchoalveolar lavage lymphocytosis, mandating immediate drug discontinuation and systemic glucocorticoids.

Leflunomide is an isoxazole prodrug that is converted to active metabolite teriflunomide (A77 1726), which selectively inhibits dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH), blocking de novo pyrimidine synthesis in rapidly proliferating lymphocytes and causing G1 cell cycle arrest.

Due to extensive enterohepatic recirculation, leflunomide has a prolonged elimination half-life of 2 weeks and takes up to 2 years to clear completely; accelerated elimination washout using cholestyramine 8 g three times daily for 11 days (or activated charcoal 50 g 4 times daily) is mandatory prior to planned conception or in acute toxicity, ensuring plasma levels fall below 0.02 mg/L.

Sulfasalazine is cleaved by colonic azoreductase bacteria into 5-aminosalicylic acid and sulfapyridine, with sulfapyridine acting as the active moiety for peripheral arthritis; key adverse effects include dose-dependent nausea, reversible male infertility with oligospermia, agranulocytosis (most common in first 3 months), and hemolysis in G6PD deficiency.

Hydroxychloroquine concentrates in acidic lysosomes, inhibiting antigen presentation, TLR7/TLR9 signaling, and matrix metalloproteinases; the American Academy of Ophthalmology recommends maintaining daily dosing at or below 5.0 mg/kg actual body weight to minimize long-term retinal toxicity, with baseline and annual spectral-domain OCT and visual field testing after 5 years.

Azathioprine is a prodrug of 6-mercaptopurine; patients with genetic deficiency of thiopurine S-methyltransferase (TPMT) or NUDT15 (especially prevalent in Asian populations) accumulate toxic 6-thioguanine nucleotides resulting in life-threatening pancytopenia, requiring genotyping or enzyme activity screening prior to therapy initiation.

Mycophenolate mofetil is a potent inhibitor of inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH), selectively depleting guanosine nucleotides in lymphocytes; it carries severe embryofetal toxicity (microtia, cleft lip/palate, cardiac anomalies), demanding two forms of contraception and drug cessation at least 6 weeks before planned pregnancy.
