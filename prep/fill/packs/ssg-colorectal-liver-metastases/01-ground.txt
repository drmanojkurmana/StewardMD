Resectability needs R0 clearance with adequate remnant and no unresectable extrahepatic disease, and bilobar or large lesions are converted by chemotherapy rather than judged by number alone. Future liver remnant over 30 percent, or over 40 percent after chemotherapy, keeps the patient safe.

Fong clinical risk scores node-positive primary, disease-free interval under 12 months, over 1 metastasis, largest over 5 cm and CEA over 200, with higher scores predicting recurrence. Carcinoembryonic antigen above 200 marks heavy burden.

Two-stage hepatectomy clears one lobe first with portal vein embolisation, then the second lobe after 4 to 6 weeks of hypertrophy, suiting bilobar disease with small remnant. The embolised lobe shrinks while the remnant grows.

Parenchymal-sparing nonanatomical resections with 1 mm margins preserve remnant for repeat hepatectomy, and wider margins add no survival over R0. Repeat resection at recurrence beats the first operation for survival.

Disappearing metastases after chemotherapy still harbour viable tumour in 80 percent of sites, so the original liver sites must be resected or ablated. Contrast MRI maps the treated sites before surgery.

Simultaneous colon and liver resection suits minor hepatectomy with uncomplicated primary, while major hepatectomy or obstructed primaries need staged surgery. Rectal primaries with chemoradiation always stage the liver first.

Radiofrequency ablation with 1 cm margins treats deep lesions under 3 cm sparing parenchyma, combined with resection for bilobar disease in one sitting. Microwave ablation extends the size range with faster zones. Future liver remnant must exceed 30 percent, or 40 percent after chemotherapy, with indocyanine green clearance guiding borderline cases. Rectal primaries finish chemoradiation with restaging before any liver operation, since pelvic response changes the plan. Positron emission tomography excludes occult extrahepatic spread before major hepatectomy.

Hepatic arterial infusion with floxuridine through an implantable pump controls liver-only disease after resection in high-risk patients at specialised centres. Biliary sclerosis limits its use.
