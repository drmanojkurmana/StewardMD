# Amyloidosis
Amyloidosis, Hematology / Multisystem.
Aliases: AL amyloidosis; ATTR amyloidosis; AA amyloidosis.
# Pathophysiology
Genetic mutation, abnormal processing, or aging-related decline in protein quality control causes precursor proteins to undergo reversible unfolding/misfolding. Misfolded subunits adopt a common antiparallel beta-pleated-sheet conformation and assemble into oligomers, higher-order polymers, and ultimately 10-nm fibrils that deposit extracellularly in tissues/organs. Soluble oligomeric intermediates are thought to be the most toxic species (generating reactive oxygen species and stress signaling), while fibrillar deposits mechanically interfere with organ function. Fibrils co-deposit accessory molecules including serum amyloid P component, apolipoproteins E and A-IV, glycosaminoglycans, and metal ions. Precursors include monoclonal immunoglobulin light chains (AL; lambda subtypes predominate), transthyretin (ATTR; wild-type or >130 described mutations), and the hepatic acute-phase protein serum amyloid A (AA).
