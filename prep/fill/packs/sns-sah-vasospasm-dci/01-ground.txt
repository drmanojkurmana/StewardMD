Second stroke peaks at a week. Vasospasm starts day 4, peaks days 7 to 10 and ends by day 21, and new deficits there mean delayed ischaemia.

Watch velocities daily. Transcranial Doppler above 120 suggests spasm, while above 200 or a Lindegaard ratio above 3 marks severe disease.

Give nimodipine fully. Sixty milligrams every 4 hours for 21 days is the only proven drug, continued through dips with fluids and pressors.

Keep fluids normal. Triple H is abandoned for euvolemia, with induced hypertension reserved for symptomatic spasm with a secured aneurysm.

Open vessels from inside. Intra-arterial verapamil or nicardipine with balloon angioplasty rescues refractory spasm, repeated until velocities settle.

Separate salt syndromes. Cerebral salt wasting is dry and salt treated, while SIADH is euvolemic and fluid restricted.

Cover fits briefly. High-risk bleeds get short prophylaxis, long drugs need a witnessed seizure, and lagging consciousness earns an EEG.

Fix fever, blood and sugar. Each raises delayed infarction independently, and hypotension is corrected to protect perfusion.
