IC/BPS means chronic pelvic pain with bladder pressure or discomfort plus lower urinary tract symptoms beyond 6 weeks with sterile cultures, after overactive bladder and endometriosis are excluded. Hunner lesions in 5 to 10 percent show circumscribed red patches with radiating capillaries that tear and bleed on hydrodistension and are biopsied for carcinoma in situ, then fulgurated or injected with triamcinolone for rapid remission. Glomerulations are petechial bleeds after low pressure hydrodistension, non-specific and no longer mandatory, so diagnosis rests on pain with exclusion.

Diaries grade pain and frequency to steer therapy, and counselling sets expectations for a relapsing course with layered oral, instilled, and endoscopic options. Ulcer-type disease follows fulguration pathways while non-ulcer disease leans on drugs plus instillations, with cystectomy reserved for end-stage fibrotic bladders. Potassium sensitivity testing is historical and no longer guides care, while hydrodistension capacity is recorded at each sitting to track bladder shrinkage over time. Behavioural measures with diet trigger avoidance and timed voiding support every drug course, because irritants and holding patterns worsen flares. Pelvic floor physiotherapy releases guarded muscles that amplify urgency, completing the multimodal plan. Stress management with sleep hygiene lowers central sensitization between flares. Cyclosporine is reserved for severely refractory non-ulcer disease under specialist supervision, and major surgery stays a last resort after all layered options are exhausted. Multidisciplinary review with pain specialists supports refractory cases, while shared decision making balances repeated procedures against long term bladder preservation at each visit.
