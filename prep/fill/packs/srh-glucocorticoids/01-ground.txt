# Glucocorticoids in rheumatology (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-glucocorticoids: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Glucocorticoids exert therapeutic effects via classical genomic mechanisms (cytosolic glucocorticoid receptor homodimerization and nuclear transactivation or transrepression of NF-kappa-B and AP-1) and rapid non-genomic mechanisms (membrane receptor signaling and physicochemical intercalation into cell membranes).

Comparative glucocorticoid equivalent dosages, anti-inflammatory potencies, and mineralocorticoid potencies: hydrocortisone 20 mg (potency 1, mineralocorticoid 1), prednisone 5 mg (potency 4, mineralocorticoid 0.8), methylprednisolone 4 mg (potency 5, mineralocorticoid 0.5), and dexamethasone 0.75 mg (potency 25 to 30, mineralocorticoid 0).

High-dose intravenous pulse methylprednisolone (typically 500 to 1,000 mg daily infused over at least 60 minutes for 3 consecutive days) saturation of cytosolic receptors engages rapid non-genomic anti-inflammatory pathways; cardiac monitoring is indicated during infusion due to risks of sudden cardiac arrhythmias and fatal cardiovascular collapse.

Hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis suppression should be assumed in any patient receiving prednisone >20 mg/day for >3 weeks, any patient receiving an evening dose of >5 mg prednisone for >3 weeks, or anyone with a clinical Cushingoid appearance, mandating gradual tapering to permit adrenal recovery.

Stress-dose glucocorticoid coverage is required during major surgical stress, acute trauma, or critical illness in patients with HPA axis suppression: hydrocortisone 50 to 100 mg intravenously immediately, followed by 50 mg every 8 hours (or 200 mg continuous infusion daily) with rapid tapering as physiological stress resolves.

Osteonecrosis (avascular necrosis, AVN) of bone is a severe dose- and duration-dependent adverse effect of glucocorticoid therapy; the femoral head is the most frequent anatomical site (bilateral in >70%), presenting with groin pain aggravated by weight-bearing, with MRI demonstrating the diagnostic 'double line sign' on T2-weighted images.

Antimicrobial prophylaxis against Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP) with oral trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (one single-strength tablet daily or one double-strength tablet three times weekly) is strongly recommended for patients receiving prednisone >=20 mg/day for >=4 to 8 weeks combined with a second immunosuppressant.

Primary prevention of glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis (GIOP) with oral bisphosphonates (alendronate, risedronate) or IV zoledronic acid is recommended for all adults aged >=40 years at moderate-to-high fracture risk who anticipate long-term glucocorticoid therapy (prednisone >=2.5 mg/day for >=3 months).
