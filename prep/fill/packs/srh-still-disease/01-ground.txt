# Adult onset Still disease (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-still-disease: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Adult-onset Still disease (AOSD) is a severe systemic autoinflammatory disorder of young adults; the Yamaguchi criteria require at least 5 features with at least 2 major criteria (fever >=39C lasting >=1 week, arthralgia/arthritis >=2 weeks, typical salmon-pink evanescent non-pruritic maculopapular rash, and leukocytosis >=10,000/microL with >=80% granulocytes).

Minor Yamaguchi criteria for adult-onset Still disease include sore throat (non-suppurative pharyngitis, often the herald symptom), lymphadenopathy and/or splenomegaly, hepatic dysfunction (elevated AST, ALT, LDH), and negative testing for antinuclear antibodies and rheumatoid factor.

Serum ferritin is massively elevated in adult-onset Still disease, often 5-fold to 20-fold above upper normal limit (frequently exceeding 3,000 to 5,000 ng/mL); a depressed glycosylated ferritin fraction <=20% (normal >50%) has high diagnostic specificity of 80% to 90% for AOSD in a febrile patient.

Fever in adult-onset Still disease classically follows a daily quotidian or double quotidian pattern with rapid temperature spikes exceeding 39 degrees Celsius characteristically in the late afternoon or early evening, returning to baseline without antipyretics, during which the fleeting salmon-pink rash becomes prominent.

Articular involvement in AOSD can evolve into a distinctive, progressive, non-erosive intercarpal and carpometacarpal ankylosis with preservation of joint spaces at the MCP and PIP joints, producing an isolated pericapitate or pancarpal ankylosis on wrist radiography.

Initial therapy for moderate-to-severe adult-onset Still disease relies on systemic glucocorticoids (prednisone 0.5 to 1 mg/kg/day); however, steroid dependency or refractoriness occurs in over 40% to 60% of patients, requiring early initiation of steroid-sparing agents.

Biological agents targeting IL-1 (anakinra, canakinumab) demonstrate remarkable efficacy, particularly for systemic features (fever, rash, hyperferritinemia) and in preventing macrophage activation syndrome; the IL-6 receptor antagonist tocilizumab is equally potent, especially for patients with persistent polyarticular manifestations.

Macrophage activation syndrome is the most devastating acute complication of adult-onset Still disease, manifesting with paradoxical sudden drop in ESR (due to consumptive hypofibrinogenemia), dropping platelet counts, extreme hyperferritinemia, coagulopathy, and neurological dysfunction, requiring immediate high-dose pulse methylprednisolone and IL-1 blockade.
