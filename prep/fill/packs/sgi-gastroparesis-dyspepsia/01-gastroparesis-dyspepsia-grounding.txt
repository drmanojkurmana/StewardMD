# Functional dyspepsia and gastroparesis
# Source: StewardMD gastroenterology knowledge base / Sleisenger 11e Ch. 50, 51

## Classification and diagnostic criteria
Rome IV criteria categorize functional dyspepsia into postprandial distress syndrome (bothersome postprandial fullness or early satiation) and epigastric pain syndrome (bothersome epigastric pain or burning unrelated to meals).

Gastric emptying scintigraphy using a standardized radiolabeled 99mTc solid meal is the gold standard for diagnosing gastroparesis, with retention > 10% at 4 hours or > 60% at 2 hours diagnostic of delayed emptying.

## Pathophysiology of diabetic gastroparesis
Diabetic gastroparesis arises from autonomic diabetic neuropathy, loss of interstitial cells of Cajal, and neuronal nitric oxide synthase downregulation, leading to postprandial nausea, vomiting, and erratic glycemic control.

## Medical and endoscopic management
Metoclopramide is the only FDA-approved dopamine D2 antagonist for diabetic gastroparesis but carries a black box warning for irreversible tardive dyskinesia with usage exceeding 12 weeks.

Gastric peroral endoscopic myotomy (G-POEM) selectively divides the hypertrophied pyloric circular muscle layer, providing substantial symptomatic and physiological improvement in refractory gastroparesis.

## Differential diagnosis and related functional emetic disorders
Cyclic vomiting syndrome (CVS) features stereotypic recurrent episodes of acute intractable vomiting separated by symptom-free intervals, strongly associated with migraine headaches and responsive to tricyclic antidepressants.

Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome (CHS) occurs in chronic daily cannabis users, characterized by severe episodic cyclical nausea and vomiting with compulsive hot-water bathing behavior that temporarily alleviates symptoms.

Rumination syndrome is an involuntary functional behavioural disorder characterized by effortless repetitive postprandial regurgitation of recently ingested food into the mouth, treated with diaphragmatic breathing exercises.

## Additional clinical details from Sleisenger & Fordtran 11e
Functional dyspepsia affects approximately 10% to 20% of the adult population globally and represents a disorder of gut-brain interaction.
Diagnostic workup mandates exclusion of structural organic pathology by upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, particularly in patients aged 60 years or older or those presenting with alarm features like unintentional weight loss, dysphagia, or persistent vomiting.
Pharmacological therapy for epigastric pain syndrome prioritizes proton pump inhibitor acid suppression and low-dose tricyclic antidepressants such as amitriptyline or nortriptyline.
In postprandial distress syndrome, prokinetic agents such as acotiamide (acetylcholinesterase inhibitor) and buspirone (5-HT1A receptor agonist relaxing the gastric fundus) promote accommodation.
Gastric emptying studies require strict pre-procedural withdrawal of medications altering motility: prokinetics, anticholinergics, and opioids must be ceased for at least 48 to 72 hours beforehand.
