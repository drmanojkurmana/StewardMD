Catheter-associated urinary tract infection, CAUTI, invades by two routes: extraluminal biofilm creeping between catheter and urethral mucosa, and intraluminal ascension of organisms introduced through drainage bag contamination or broken closed systems. Biofilm shields bacteria from antibiotics and host defences, so prevention targets dwell time and circuit integrity rather than prophylactic drugs.

Bacteriuria accumulation follows a near linear clock: each day of indwelling catheter use adds 3% to 7% risk, reaching nearly 100% by 30 days of continuous drainage. Most bacteriuria stays asymptomatic, but the colonised biofilm seeds bacteraemia, making daily necessity review the single most effective intervention.

Suprapubic cystostomy bypasses the urethra when urethral passage is impossible or a contraindicated urethral catheter situation exists: acute bacterial prostatitis, where instrumentation spreads infection; traumatic urethral disruption, where passage completes the tear; and severe stricture, where blind attempts create false passages. Diversion rests the urethra for later reconstruction.

Safety of percutaneous puncture depends on bladder fullness: the bladder must be palpably distended or fixed under real-time ultrasound guidance, since an empty viscus invites fatal bowel perforation as the trocar traverses small bowel loops. Elective placement under vision replaces blind attempts whenever fullness is uncertain.

Difficult urethral passage over raised bladder necks yields to the Coude-tip catheter, the Tiemann design with a 45-degree anterior curve that vaults the median lobe and prostatic shelf. Gentle curved-tip advancement with the marker cephalad succeeds where straight catheters impact and tunnel falsely.

Balloon deflation failure follows a fixed escalation: sever the inflation arm to bypass valve faults, thread a stylet to pierce internal valves, perform ultrasound-guided puncture of the balloon under transabdominal vision, and finally aspirate transrectally or transvaginally. Traction against an inflated balloon avulses urethral mucosa and must never substitute for the ladder.

Antimicrobial catheters, silver alloy coated or nitrofurazone impregnated, lower short-term asymptomatic bacteriuria in hospitalised patients yet show no consistent reduction in symptomatic sepsis or mortality, so guidelines decline routine adoption while endorsing early removal universally.

Catheter-induced bladder spasms, painful detrusor contractions against the foreign body with pericatheter leakage, respond to anticholinergic therapy with solifenacin or oxybutynin, or to beta-3 agonists, combined with confirming correct balloon seating and free drainage before any pharmacologic escalation.
