# Severe alcohol-associated hepatitis
Adults
Ward, HDU, hepatology unit
Diagnose clinically (NIAAA criteria), grade with MELD (above 20 severe) or Maddrey (32 or more), screen for infection, then give prednisolone 40 mg daily for 28 days (plus IV N-acetylcysteine for 5 days) if no contraindication; stop steroids if Lille score at day 7 is above 0.45. Nutrition and abstinence drive survival.
Aliases: AH; alcoholic hepatitis; severe alcoholic hepatitis; ASH; Maddrey discriminant function; MDF; Maddrey 32; MELD; Lille score; prednisolone alcoholic hepatitis; alcohol-related liver disease; ALD.
# Recognise
NIAAA probable AH: jaundice onset within 8 weeks in a person drinking more than 40 g/day (women) or 60 g/day (men) for 6 months or more, with less than 60 days of abstinence before jaundice; bilirubin above 3 mg/dL; AST above 50 IU/L with AST/ALT above 1.5, both below 400 IU/L.
Liver biopsy is not needed when criteria are met and there are no confounders; consider it (often transjugular) when the diagnosis is uncertain.
Typical features: jaundice, tender hepatomegaly, fever, leucocytosis, ascites or encephalopathy. SIRS can occur without infection but triples mortality.
Exclude other causes: viral hepatitis (A, B, E), drug-induced injury, autoimmune hepatitis, biliary obstruction, Budd-Chiari syndrome, portal vein thrombosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (ultrasound with Doppler).
# Severity and pre-steroid screen
MELD above 20 = severe AH (ACG 2024): admit, consider corticosteroids. MELD 20 or less = moderate AH.
Maddrey discriminant function = 4.6 x (patient prothrombin time minus control, seconds) + bilirubin (mg/dL). A value of 32 or more = severe, with 30-day mortality of about 20 to 50%.
Steroid benefit is greatest at MELD 21 to 39, limited at 40 to 50 and absent above 50.
Before steroids: blood and urine cultures, chest X-ray, diagnostic paracentesis if ascites; HBsAg (and HBV DNA if positive), HIV, and assessment for tuberculosis; glucose, creatinine and signs of GI bleeding.
# Supportive care (all patients)
Assess alcohol withdrawal with CIWA-Ar and treat symptom-triggered; use benzodiazepines cautiously (short or intermediate-acting such as lorazepam or oxazepam are safer with poor liver function) and distinguish withdrawal from hepatic encephalopathy.
Give thiamine before glucose-containing fluids; replace vitamin B12 and zinc if deficient.
Nutrition: 35 kcal/kg/day with protein 1.2 to 1.5 g/kg/day. Add oral supplements, then nasogastric feeding if intake stays inadequate (intake below 21 kcal/kg/day predicts death). Varices without recent banding or bleeding do not contraindicate an NG tube.
No universal prophylactic antibiotics (ACG 2024); treat proven infection promptly and reassess daily.
Avoid nephrotoxins and NSAIDs; manage AKI, ascites, variceal bleeding and encephalopathy per the specific protocols.
# Corticosteroids and adjuncts (severe AH)
Severe AH (MELD above 20 or Maddrey 32 or more) without contraindications: prednisolone 40 mg orally once daily for 28 days; IV methylprednisolone 32 mg daily if unable to take oral drugs. No evidence favours a particular taper.
Contraindications until controlled: active infection, untreated HBV, uncontrolled diabetes, active GI bleeding, severe renal failure. Steroids can be started once infection, bleeding or AKI is controlled.
Add IV N-acetylcysteine for 5 days to prednisolone (ACG 2024 strong recommendation; AASLD 2019: may improve 30-day survival).
Do not use pentoxifylline (ACG 2024 recommends against). G-CSF and microbiome therapies are not routinely recommended.
# Response and monitoring
Calculate the Lille score on day 7 (day 4 is similarly accurate). Above 0.45 = non-response: stop corticosteroids.
Daily: temperature and infection surveillance (including fungal infection), capillary glucose for steroid-induced hyperglycaemia, creatinine and urine output, bilirubin trend and mental state.
Bilirubin falling to below 0.8 times the admission value by day 7 indicates a good trajectory.
Steroid non-responders with high risk of death: consider early liver transplantation in highly selected patients according to regional and institutional protocols, or clinical trials.
Non-responders who are not transplant candidates and have 4 or more organ failures: palliative care is appropriate.
Combine baseline MELD with the day-7 Lille score for the best estimate of 2 and 6-month mortality when counselling patients and families.
# Abstinence and long-term care
Sustained abstinence is the strongest determinant of long-term survival. Offer brief intervention and refer to integrated addiction care before discharge.
Relapse-prevention drugs in liver disease: baclofen (preferred), acamprosate (dose-adjust if creatinine clearance 30 to 50 mL/min, avoid below 30), naltrexone only in compensated disease; avoid disulfiram.
Vaccinate against hepatitis A and B, influenza and pneumococcus; refer decompensated patients for transplant assessment.
# Pitfalls
Starting steroids without an infection screen, or in untreated HBV.
Continuing steroids for 28 days in Lille non-responders, which adds infection risk without benefit.
Neglecting nutrition or missing Wernicke encephalopathy.
Treating withdrawal with repeated long-acting benzodiazepines and precipitating hepatic encephalopathy.
# Drugs
Prednisolone, 40 mg orally once daily for 28 days, Stop at day 7 if Lille score above 0.45. Monitor glucose and infection.
Methylprednisolone, 32 mg IV once daily, If oral prednisolone cannot be taken.
N-acetylcysteine (IV, with prednisolone)
# Dose
Day 1: 150 mg/kg in 250 mL 5% dextrose over 30 minutes, then 50 mg/kg in 500 mL over 4 hours, then 100 mg/kg in 1,000 mL over 16 hours. Days 2 to 5: 100 mg/kg/day
5-day regimen used in the trial of prednisolone plus NAC (Nguyen-Khac 2011).
Thiamine, 100 mg IV or orally daily, given before glucose, Higher treatment doses if Wernicke encephalopathy is suspected.
Baclofen (alcohol use disorder)
# Dose
5 mg orally three times daily, increased every 3 to 5 days to a maximum of 15 mg three times daily
Most studied relapse-prevention drug in advanced liver disease; monitor for sedation.
