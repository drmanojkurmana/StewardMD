Thyroid lymphoma: orientation

- Lymphomas arising inside the thyroid are uncommon. They make up roughly 1% to 5% of thyroid malignant tumours.
- Background autoimmune thyroid disease is the key predisposition. Around 55% of affected people carry a diagnosis of Hashimoto disease, and observational reviews suggest the lymphoma risk in that group sits somewhere near 40 to 80 times the general level.
- Presentation tends to be a swelling that grows over days to weeks, sometimes with pressure symptoms such as swallowing trouble or a changed voice. Systemic features (fever, weight loss, drenching sweats) show up in about 11% of cases.

Tissue diagnosis

- Once a lymphoma is strongly suspected, a wider needle biopsy of the gland gives enough material for further testing.
- Flow cytometry sorts cells by the proteins on their surface. Immunohistochemistry stains tissue sections with labelled antibodies. Both are used to type the lymphoma.
- The subtype most often encountered in the thyroid is diffuse large B-cell lymphoma.
- Mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma is a marginal zone B-cell lymphoma. It is less frequent overall, but some Asian case series report it as the leading type. The 5th edition of the WHO haematolymphoid classification treats it as its own entity.

Management principles

- Operating is mainly a diagnostic step, and removing the gland as first therapy is avoided.
- In stage IE disease, which describes lymphoma confined to the thyroid and makes up about half of cases, surgery can clarify whether the mass stays within the capsule. When it does, surgery combined with radiation alone may be an acceptable plan.
- Spread beyond the gland calls for multi-drug chemotherapy.
- Low-grade localised disease can often be handled with radiotherapy alone, unless the disease is systemic.

Follicular carcinoma and iodine

- Under the microscope its cell nuclei look much like a follicular adenoma, so the call depends on whether tumour has broken through the capsule or entered vessels.

Encapsulated, noninvasive follicular lesions

- The newer label is noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features. It replaces an older name, encapsulated follicular variant of papillary carcinoma.
- Neither capsular nor vascular invasion is present. The entity is no longer regarded as cancer, although it is said to behave like a precursor state.
- Radioactive iodine is kept for tumours showing vascular or extrathyroidal invasion, heavy nodal burden, large size or aggressive papillary patterns.
- After a lobe is removed, a 2015 American Thyroid Association statement suggests a single lobectomy may suffice for low-risk disease. The same document describes recurrence as very unlikely for encapsulated noninvasive lesions, even after lobectomy alone. Finishing the thyroid is reserved for high-risk pathology.

Radioiodine-refractory disease

- Between 25% and 50% of people with widespread, advanced differentiated carcinoma become refractory to radioiodine.
- The American Thyroid Association recognises four patterns: never concentrating the isotope, losing uptake over time, uptake in only some deposits, and growth despite considerable uptake.
- A kinase inhibitor is considered for symptomatic, rapidly progressing or heavy-burden disease.
- Sorafenib was studied against placebo in the DECISION trial. It extended progression-free survival and received FDA approval in November 2013.
- Lenvatinib was tested against placebo in the SELECT trial, improved progression-free survival and gained approval in 2015.
- The sources read place neither drug ahead of the other, and the American Thyroid Association accepts either as first-line.
- Cabozantinib was assessed in the COSMIC-311 trial in people already treated with lenvatinib or sorafenib, with as many as two VEGFR-directed agents allowed before enrolment.

Mutation-directed options

- Anaplastic thyroid carcinoma that cannot be resected and carries a BRAF V600E change can be treated with dabrafenib combined with trametinib.
- In metastatic, RAI-resistant papillary carcinoma with the same BRAF change, dabrafenib restored iodine uptake in around 60% of patients.
- RET rearrangements respond to selpercatinib or pralsetinib.
- NTRK rearrangements respond to larotrectinib or entrectinib, approved across many solid tumour types.
