# Organ manifestations of SLE (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison)
Cardiac disease in SLE spans pericardium, myocardium, endocardium, and coronaries; pericarditis is commonest at 25 percent to 50 percent with positional pleuritic pain and friction rub.
Libman-Sacks endocarditis gives sterile fibrovascular vegetations on mitral and aortic valves, linked to antiphospholipid antibodies, with cardioembolic stroke and secondary bacterial endocarditis risk.
Accelerated atherosclerosis raises coronary disease and infarction 5-fold to 10-fold, above 50-fold in young premenopausal women, from chronic inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and glucocorticoids.
Pulmonary disease runs from acute lupus pneumonitis and diffuse alveolar hemorrhage (hemoptysis with falling hematocrit) through exudative pleural effusions to chronic fibrosing interstitial disease.
Shrinking lung syndrome gives progressive dyspnea with pleuritic pain, raised hemidiaphragms on imaging, and restrictive volumes without parenchymal interstitial disease.
Lupus enteritis (mesenteric vasculitis) gives severe acute abdominal pain with nausea and vomiting; CT shows bowel wall thickening with target sign and engorged mesenteric vessels (comb sign).
Blood disease includes warm IgG Coombs-positive hemolytic anemia, immune thrombocytopenia, and leukopenia with lymphopenia; lymphopenia below 1,000 per microliter tracks active flares.
Eye disease runs from dry eye keratoconjunctivitis sicca and episcleritis to retinal vasculitis with cotton-wool spots and hemorrhages, mirroring cerebral and systemic microvascular activity.
Myocarditis presents with unexplained tachycardia, new conduction defects, and troponin leak without coronary obstruction; endomyocardial biopsy shows immune complex deposition with complement.
Pulmonary hypertension in SLE is usually secondary to interstitial disease, chronic thromboembolism from antiphospholipid antibodies, or left heart disease; isolated vasculopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension is uncommon and needs right heart catheter proof.
Pleuritic pain in SLE is usually pleuritis with small exudative effusions rich in ANA; large effusions, empyema neutrophils, or pneumothorax point away from lupus toward infection or embolism.
Cognitive dysfunction and psychosis with anti-ribosomal P or antiphospholipid antibodies need MRI brain with contrast plus EEG; seizures below 2 per year on stable therapy still require neurology review.
