# Raynaud phenomenon (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-raynaud: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Raynaud phenomenon is an episodic, reversible digital vasospasm provoked by cold temperatures or emotional stress, presenting with classical triphasic color changes: pallor (white, ischemic arteriolar vasospasm), cyanosis (blue, tissue deoxygenation), and rubor (red, reactive hyperemic reperfusion).

Primary Raynaud phenomenon (Raynaud disease) is idiopathic, occurring in young women (15 to 30 years), characterized by symmetric episodic attacks, normal physical examination, absence of digital tissue necrosis or ulceration, normal nailfold capillaroscopy, and negative ANA.

Secondary Raynaud phenomenon is caused by an underlying systemic disease (most commonly systemic sclerosis, SLE, MCTD); clinical clues include onset after age 30, painful asymmetric attacks, digital pitting scars, tissue necrosis or gangrene, abnormal nailfold capillaroscopy, and positive ANA.

Nailfold capillaroscopy at 200x magnification distinguishes primary from secondary Raynaud: the presence of enlarged or giant capillary loops (>50 micrometers) and avascular areas has >90% predictive value for the evolution of systemic sclerosis or scleroderma spectrum disorders.

Lifestyle and non-pharmacological measures are the essential first-line foundation: total-body cold avoidance, layered warm clothing, thermal mittens, chemical hand warmers, avoidance of emotional stress, and strict cessation of tobacco and sympathomimetic drugs.

Dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (oral nifedipine 30 to 60 mg daily or amlodipine) are the first-line pharmacological treatment of choice, significantly reducing the frequency and severity of vasospastic attacks.

Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE-5) inhibitors (sildenafil 20 mg three times daily or tadalafil) are effective add-on or alternative therapy for severe Raynaud phenomenon refractory to calcium channel blockers, significantly improving digital microvascular blood flow.

For severe digital ischemia, critical digital ulcers, or impending gangrene, intravenous prostacyclin analogs (iloprost infusions) are the most effective therapy, accelerating ulcer healing; oral bosentan (endothelin receptor antagonist) is approved to prevent the recurrence of new digital ulcers.
