# acrocyanosis
Cardiovascular system, Vascular medicine.
Aliases: acral cyanosis; Crocq disease.
# Pathophysiology
Acrocyanosis results from persistent vasospasm of the small arterioles and cutaneous arteries supplying the acral skin, causing reduced arterial inflow and sluggish capillary perfusion. Compensatory dilatation of the subpapillary venous plexuses leads to pooling of slow-flowing, deoxygenated blood, which produces the sustained bluish discolouration and the associated coolness and sweating. Unlike Raynaud phenomenon, the vasoconstriction is continuous rather than paroxysmal and does not fully occlude flow, so tissue viability is preserved and ischaemic complications do not occur in the primary form. Increased vasomotor tone, heightened sensitivity to cold, and in some cases increased blood viscosity or circulating cryoproteins in secondary disease all contribute. The precise trigger of the primary form is unknown but is thought to reflect a functional abnormality of local vascular reactivity.
