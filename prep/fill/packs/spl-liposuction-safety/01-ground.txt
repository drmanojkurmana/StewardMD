Exam grounding notes for tumescent liposuction safety, written for grounding, not for display. Paraphrased from Plastic Surgery 5E Volume 2 on liposuction.

The Klein tumescent mix fills 1 liter of normal saline with 500 to 1000 mg of lidocaine, 1 mg of epinephrine at a 1 to 1,000,000 dilution, and 10 mEq of sodium bicarbonate. Lidocaine numbs, epinephrine blanches vessels, and bicarbonate buffers the sting.

Tumescent technique permits far more lidocaine than usual: the maximum safe dose reaches 35 to 55 mg per kg of body weight, against only 7 mg per kg for standard commercial lidocaine with epinephrine. Dilution plus vasoconstriction explains the gap.

Absorption runs slow. Peak serum lidocaine concentrations arrive 8 to 14 hours after infiltration because profound epinephrine vasoconstriction holds the drug in fat. Monitoring and dose math must respect this delayed peak, not the injection moment.

Toxicity climbs a ladder: perioral numbness with a metallic taste and tinnitus, then slurred speech, then seizures, then respiratory arrest and cardiovascular collapse. Early signs during or after a case demand lipid rescue thinking and resuscitation readiness.

Large-volume liposuction is defined at removal of 5000 mL or more of total lipoaspirate, meaning fat plus fluid together in one procedure. Volumes at or above this line mandate overnight inpatient observation with fluids and monitoring.

The super-wet method infiltrates 1 mL of fluid for every 1 mL of expected aspirate. Blood loss stays below 1 percent of total aspirate volume, far less than the dry technique ever allowed. True tumescent uses two to three times more fluid.

Buttock fat grafting kills through pulmonary fat embolism when the cannula runs deep. Fatal emboli follow intramuscular injection that lacerates gluteal venous tributaries, letting fat reach the lungs. Subcutaneous placement never produced this syndrome.

Multi-society guidance therefore confines gluteal fat grafting strictly to the subcutaneous space. Subfascial and intramuscular planes are forbidden. Ultrasound-guided subcutaneous delivery with blunt cannulas is the audited standard.
