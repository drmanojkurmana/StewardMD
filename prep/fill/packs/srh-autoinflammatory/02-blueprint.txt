# Monogenic autoinflammatory syndromes (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-autoinflammatory: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Monogenic autoinflammatory syndromes are inherited disorders of the innate immune system characterized by unprovoked, recurrent episodes of systemic inflammation without high-titer autoantibodies or antigen-specific autoreactive T cells.

Familial Mediterranean fever (FMF) is caused by autosomal recessive MEFV mutations encoding pyrin; attacks last 12 to 72 hours with fever, peritonitis, pleuritis, and erysipelas-like rash, treated with daily lifelong oral colchicine to prevent fatal renal AA amyloidosis.

Cryopyrin-Associated Periodic Syndromes (CAPS) are autosomal dominant disorders caused by gain-of-function NLRP3 mutations, comprising FCAS, Muckle-Wells syndrome (urticaria, sensorineural deafness, amyloidosis), and NOMID/CINCA (aseptic meningitis, arthropathy), all responding dramatically to IL-1 blockade.

TNF Receptor-Associated Periodic Syndrome (TRAPS) is autosomal dominant caused by TNFRSF1A mutations (55-kDa TNF receptor), presenting with prolonged attacks lasting 1 to 3 weeks with migratory myalgias, monocytic fasciitis, periorbital edema, and high AA amyloidosis risk, treated with anakinra or canakinumab.

Hyper-IgD Syndrome / Mevalonate Kinase Deficiency (HIDS/MKD) is autosomal recessive caused by MVK mutations, presenting with attacks every 4 to 6 weeks lasting 3 to 7 days, often triggered by childhood vaccinations, featuring cervical lymphadenopathy, mouth ulcers, and elevated IgD/IgA.

Blau syndrome is an autosomal dominant pediatric autoinflammatory granulomatous disorder caused by gain-of-function NOD2 (CARD15) mutations, characterized by the clinical triad of granulomatous polyarthritis, panuveitis, and camptodactyly with tan papular rash.

PAPA syndrome (Pyogenic Arthritis, Pyoderma gangrenosum, and Acne) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by PSTPIP1 (CD2BP1) mutations, leading to hyperphosphorylation and uninhibited binding to pyrin, driving sterile neutrophilic articular and cutaneous destruction.

Type I interferonopathies (e.g., Aicardi-Goutieres syndrome, STING-associated vasculopathy with onset in infancy [SAVI]) are monogenic disorders characterized by constitutive upregulation of type I interferons, presenting with microangiopathy, chilblains, pulmonary fibrosis, and stroke, responsive to oral JAK inhibitors.
