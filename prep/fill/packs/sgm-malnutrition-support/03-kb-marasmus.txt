# Marasmus
Marasmus, Nutrition / Metabolic, Nutrition & Metabolic Medicine.
Aliases: severe wasting; protein-energy malnutrition (non-oedematous); energy malnutrition.
# Pathophysiology
Marasmus is a form of severe acute malnutrition dominated by deficiency of overall energy intake. The body adapts to prolonged starvation by mobilising fat and muscle for fuel, lowering metabolic rate, and preserving visceral protein and albumin, which is why oedema is characteristically absent. Depletion of energy stores leads to profound wasting, immune suppression, gut mucosal atrophy, and impaired thermoregulation and glucose homeostasis. As with other forms of severe malnutrition, the depleted state creates vulnerability to hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, infection, and to metabolic disturbance during refeeding.
# Prognosis
Gradual nutritional rehabilitation with treatment of infection allows recovery, but delayed presentation, sepsis, or mismanaged refeeding carry a high mortality, and prolonged early malnutrition can impair growth and development.
# Clinical pearls
Marked wasting of muscle and subcutaneous fat gives an emaciated, wizened appearance with an apparently large head and loose skin folds
Unlike kwashiorkor there is no oedema, and the child is often alert but irritable and ravenously hungry
It results from severe, prolonged deficiency of total energy intake, frequently compounded by chronic infection or feeding difficulties
It remains a significant problem among food-insecure young children in India and other low-resource settings
Hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, dehydration, and infection are common and dangerous accompaniments
Assessment relies on anthropometry, especially low weight-for-height and reduced mid-upper-arm circumference
Nutritional rehabilitation must be gradual to avoid refeeding complications
# Additional differentials
Kwashiorkor and marasmic-kwashiorkor
Wasting from chronic infection such as tuberculosis or HIV
Malabsorptive disorders
Malignancy and other chronic wasting illnesses
Congenital or metabolic disease causing failure to thrive
# Additional investigations
Anthropometry with weight-for-height and mid-upper-arm circumference
Blood glucose and body temperature to detect hypoglycaemia and hypothermia
Electrolytes including potassium, magnesium, and phosphate
Screening for underlying and intercurrent infection
Assessment for micronutrient deficiencies and anaemia
