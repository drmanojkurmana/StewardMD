Gynecomastia is the benign proliferation of male breast glandular tissue, driven by an altered estrogen-to-androgen activity ratio. When estrogen action outweighs androgen action at the breast, ducts and stroma grow even in an otherwise virilized man.

Severity follows the Simon classification. Grade 1 is minor enlargement without skin excess, Grade 2a moderate enlargement without excess skin, Grade 2b moderate enlargement with skin excess, and Grade 3 marked enlargement with severe redundancy mimicking the female breast. The grade decides whether excision alone or skin reduction joins the plan.

Physiological gynecomastia clusters in three life periods. Neonatal breast buds come from maternal estrogen transfer across the placenta, pubertal enlargement strikes with the hormonal surge around 13 to 14 years, and senescent enlargement follows declining testosterone in older males. All three usually resolve without surgery.

Pathology must be excluded first. Cirrhosis impairs estrogen degradation so levels rise, chronic kidney disease disturbs the hormonal axis, hypogonadism including Klinefelter 47,XXY starves tissue of androgen, Leydig or Sertoli testicular tumours secrete estrogen, and thyrotoxicosis raises sex hormone binding globulin with free hormone shifts.

Drugs are the commonest reversible trigger. Spironolactone blocks androgen receptors, cimetidine displaces androgen, finasteride removes dihydrotestosterone, ketoconazole halts steroid synthesis, anabolic steroids aromatize to estrogen after withdrawal, and antiretroviral protease inhibitors disturb metabolism. Stopping the culprit often reverses early disease.

Surgery starts with access. The Webster intra-areolar semicircular inferior periareolar incision runs along the lower areolar border and gives direct exposure for sharp glandular excision beneath the nipple, hiding the scar at the pigment junction.

Resection demands restraint behind the nipple. A 1.0 cm thick disc of retroareolar glandular tissue is deliberately preserved to prevent saucerization, the permanent crater deformity where the nipple collapses into an over-excavated bed.

Excision alone leaves edges, so contouring finishes the job.
