# Septic arthritis of a native joint (adult)
Adults
Emergency department, orthopaedic and rheumatology wards
Treat an acute hot swollen joint as septic until proven otherwise: aspirate for Gram stain, culture and crystals and take blood cultures before antibiotics, start empirical IV antibiotics chosen by risk group, and drain the joint by repeated aspiration or arthroscopic or open washout.
Aliases: septic joint; infective arthritis; pyogenic arthritis; hot swollen joint; acute monoarthritis; joint washout; gonococcal arthritis; BSR hot joint guideline.
# Recognise
Acute monoarthritis with pain, warmth, effusion and restricted movement; the knee is most often affected, then hip, shoulder, ankle and wrist. Fever is often absent.
Risk factors: age over 80, diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, prosthetic joint, recent joint surgery or injection, skin infection or ulcers, injecting drug use, and immunosuppression.
