# Systemic sclerosis classification and skin disease (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison; 2013 ACR EULAR criteria)
Limited cutaneous SSc covers skin distal to elbows and knees plus face; diffuse cutaneous SSc extends proximal to elbows and knees onto chest, abdomen, or upper arms, by LeRoy criteria.
The 2013 ACR EULAR criteria need a total of 9 points or more; finger skin thickening past the metacarpophalangeal joints alone scores 9 points and suffices.
Skin moves through early puffy fingers edema, indurative thickening, and late atrophy, tracked by the modified Rodnan skin score (mRSS) from 0 to 51 points.
Diffuse disease links to anti-topoisomerase I (Scl-70) and anti-RNA polymerase III with rapid skin change, early organ injury, and higher mortality.
Limited disease covers CREST (calcinosis, Raynaud, esophageal dysmotility, sclerodactyly, telangiectasia) with anti-centromere antibodies and late isolated pulmonary arterial hypertension.
Anti-RNA polymerase III marks diffuse SSc with tendon friction rubs, renal crisis in up to 25 percent, and close synchronous malignancy in time.
Nailfold capillaroscopy shows early giant loops above 50 micrometers, active microhemorrhages, dropout with avascular areas, and late ramified neoangiogenesis.
Sine scleroderma affects below 5 percent: full internal organ disease with SSc autoantibodies and no cutaneous thickening at all.
Puffy fingers with Raynaud and positive ANA plus scleroderma-pattern capillaries define very early SSc; progression to definite disease within 5 years exceeds 60 percent without therapy.
Digital ulcers complicate diffuse disease in up to 50 percent of patients, driven by intimal proliferation and thrombosis; bosentan (endothelin blocker) reduces new ulcer formation by about 30 percent.
The 2013 criteria weight items from 2 points for puffy fingers to 9 points for proximal skin; the mRSS sums 17 sites each scored 0 to 3 for a 0 to 51 total.
Telangiectasia count above 20 matted vessels on face and hands marks long-standing limited disease; calcinosis complicated by ulceration or infection needs surgical debridement with calcium acetate binding considered.
