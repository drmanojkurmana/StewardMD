A perforated viscus declares itself on film. Perforated viscus shows free air under the diaphragm on erect chest film in 70 percent, and CT with oral contrast locates the site with 90 percent accuracy when films are negative.

Scores predict death. Mannheim peritonitis index over 26 and APACHE II over 15 predict mortality and intensive care need, guiding damage control against definitive repair in sick patients.

The sickest get abbreviated surgery. Damage control laparotomy with abbreviated resection, stapled ends and temporary closure suits unstable acidotic coagulopathic patients, with relook at 24 to 48 hours for definitive reconstruction.

The open abdomen grades. Open abdomen with Bjorck grades 1A clean to 4 frozen abdomen guides temporary closure choice, with negative-pressure therapy and early fascial traction for primary closure within the first week.

Faecal soilage resects. Colonic perforation with faecal peritonitis gets Hartmann resection, while right-sided perforations with stable physiology suit resection with ileocolic anastomosis in one sitting.

Typhoid perforates multiply. Typhoid ileal perforation clusters within 60 cm of the valve with multiple antimesenteric ulcers, treated by segmental resection rather than simple closure in endemic zones where reperforation is common.

Tuberculosis needs drugs first. Tuberculous peritonitis with doughy abdomen, ascitic adenosine deaminase over 30 units per L and omental caking needs antitubercular therapy first, with surgery only for obstruction or perforation.

Strictures preserve bowel. Abdominal tuberculosis strictures get stricturoplasty for short lesions and resection for long or multiple ones after 2 months of antitubercular therapy, preserving maximum absorptive length.
