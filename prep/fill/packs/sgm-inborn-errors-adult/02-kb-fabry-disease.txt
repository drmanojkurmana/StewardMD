# Fabry disease
Fabry disease, Genetic / Metabolic, Metabolic Medicine.
Aliases: alpha-galactosidase A deficiency; Anderson-Fabry disease; angiokeratoma corporis diffusum.
# Pathophysiology
Fabry disease is caused by pathogenic variants in the GLA gene on the X chromosome, which encodes lysosomal alpha-galactosidase A. Deficient enzyme activity prevents cleavage of terminal galactose from globotriaosylceramide (Gb3), causing progressive glycosphingolipid accumulation within lysosomes, most notably in vascular endothelial and smooth muscle cells, renal podocytes and tubular cells, cardiomyocytes, and neurons. This storage triggers endothelial dysfunction, a prothrombotic and pro-inflammatory state, and progressive fibrosis of the kidney, heart, and brain vasculature, producing the multisystem adult phenotype.
# Prognosis
Untreated classic disease progresses to end-stage renal disease, cardiac complications, and premature stroke, shortening life expectancy. Early initiation of enzyme replacement or chaperone therapy, together with organ-directed supportive care, can slow progression and improve outcomes, though established fibrosis is not reversible.
# Clinical pearls
An X-linked lysosomal storage disorder caused by deficient activity of the enzyme alpha-galactosidase A, leading to accumulation of globotriaosylceramide (Gb3) in vascular endothelium and other tissues
Hemizygous males are typically most severely affected, but heterozygous females can develop significant disease because of variable X-inactivation
Childhood often begins with episodic burning pain in the hands and feet (acroparaesthesias) that may be provoked by fever, heat, or exercise
Angiokeratomas are clusters of small dark-red skin lesions, classically over the lower trunk and groin (bathing-trunk distribution)
A whorl-like corneal opacity (cornea verticillata) is a common and useful ophthalmological clue that usually does not impair vision
Reduced or absent sweating (hypohidrosis) contributes to heat intolerance and unexplained fevers
Progressive involvement of the kidneys, heart, and cerebrovascular system in adulthood causes renal failure, cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia, and early stroke
Enzyme replacement therapy and, for eligible variants, an oral pharmacological chaperone can stabilise disease, and adjunctive treatments target pain and organ protection
