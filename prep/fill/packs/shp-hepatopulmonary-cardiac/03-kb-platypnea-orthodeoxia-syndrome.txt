# Platypnea-orthodeoxia syndrome
Platypnea-orthodeoxia syndrome, Respiratory / Pulmonary vascular, Pulmonary Hypertension.
Aliases: POS; platypnoea-orthodeoxia syndrome; positional hypoxaemia.
# Pathophysiology
Platypnoea-orthodeoxia syndrome arises from a right-to-left shunt whose magnitude increases in the upright posture. In the intracardiac form there is an interatrial communication such as a patent foramen ovale, but the shunt only becomes haemodynamically significant when an additional anatomical or functional factor, for example an elongated or dilated aortic root, right pneumonectomy, or pericardial change, alters the geometry of the atria so that inferior vena caval flow is preferentially directed across the septum on standing. In the intrapulmonary form, dilated or abnormal vessels at the lung bases, as seen in hepatopulmonary syndrome or arteriovenous malformations, receive more perfusion when upright, increasing the fraction of venous blood bypassing gas exchange. In both mechanisms, standing augments the shunt and produces the characteristic positional hypoxaemia and breathlessness.
# Prognosis
Prognosis depends on the underlying cause; closure of a responsible intracardiac defect is frequently curative with immediate resolution of the positional hypoxaemia. Intrapulmonary causes carry the prognosis of the underlying pulmonary or hepatic disease.
# Clinical pearls
Defined by breathlessness (platypnoea) and arterial desaturation (orthodeoxia) that appear or worsen on sitting or standing upright and improve on lying flat
Reflects a posture-dependent right-to-left shunt, most often intracardiac through a patent foramen ovale or atrial septal defect, but sometimes intrapulmonary
Intracardiac cases frequently require an anatomical or functional distortion, such as an enlarged or unfolded aorta, that redirects venous flow across the septum when upright
Intrapulmonary causes include hepatopulmonary syndrome and pulmonary arteriovenous malformations, especially with basal-predominant shunting
Contrast (bubble) echocardiography, ideally with postural manoeuvres, is central to localising the shunt as intracardiac or intrapulmonary
