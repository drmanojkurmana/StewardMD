Cardiac involvement in rheumatic disease: Libman-Sacks vegetations, accelerated atherosclerosis, amyloid restriction, neonatal heart block, Takayasu coronary disease, EGPA myocarditis, scleroderma myocardial fibrosis, pericarditis.

Libman-Sacks endocarditis presents with sterile non-bacterial thrombotic vegetations, small verrucous clumps of fibrin and platelets, on the left-sided heart valves with the mitral valve hit more often than the aortic valve, sitting on both atrial and ventricular surfaces of the leaflets. It is heavily linked with high-titer antiphospholipid antibodies, especially lupus anticoagulant and high anticardiolipin levels, and throws cardioembolic strokes while getting superinfected like any damaged valve. Long-term warfarin anticoagulation is indicated after an embolic event, with valve surgery for large mobile masses.

Rheumatoid arthritis accelerates ischemic atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease with a 1.5 to 2.0-fold increased risk of myocardial infarction compared with the general population. Systemic inflammation marked by CRP elevation and TNF-alpha excess mediates endothelial dysfunction and plaque instability independently of traditional Framingham risk factors such as smoking, lipids, and hypertension. Disease control with DMARDs lowers the extra risk, while NSAIDs and glucocorticoids add their own hazard. Cardiology review with lipid and blood pressure targets applies from diagnosis.

Cardiac amyloidosis, both AL light-chain and wild-type or mutant ATTR transthyretin forms, causes rapidly progressive restrictive cardiomyopathy with concentric biventricular thickening and stiff noncompliant chambers. The signature mismatch is low ECG voltage discordant with echocardiographic wall thickness above 12 mm, with an apical sparing pattern on global longitudinal strain echocardiography where basal segments fail first. Bone scintigraphy with pyrophosphate confirms ATTR without biopsy in the right setting.

Complete congenital heart block, a third-degree AV block present from birth, in neonatal lupus erythematosus results from transplacental passage of maternal anti-Ro/SSA antibodies, specifically the 52-kDa Ro specificity, with anti-La/SSB antibodies as companions, binding fetal L-type calcium channels and leading through autoimmune myocarditis to AV nodal fibrosis and calcification. Most affected infants need permanent pacing, and subsequent pregnancies carry about a 20 percent recurrence risk, reduced by maternal hydroxychloroquine.

Takayasu arteritis induces severe ostial coronary arteritis with ostial stenosis, plus thoracic ascending aortic dilation leading to aortic valve regurgitation with left ventricular volume load and congestive heart failure in young females. Pulseless arms with arm claudication and bruits point to the arch vessels. Disease activity is monitored via 18F-FDG PET-CT showing mural uptake and MR angiography mapping stenosis and wall edema, guiding immunosuppression before any bypass grafting.

Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis cardiac involvement carries the highest mortality of any EGPA organ, accounting for above 50 percent of deaths, manifesting as eosinophilic myocarditis with troponin leak, endomyocardial fibrosis stiffening the chambers, and intracardiac apical mural thrombus throwing systemic emboli. ANCA-negative EGPA with high eosinophil counts is the phenotype most at risk. Urgent high-dose glucocorticoids with cyclophosphamide induction, plus anticoagulation while mural thrombus persists, saves lives.

Systemic sclerosis primary myocardial disease involves patchy myocardial band necrosis with contraction bands and focal ischemia from cardiac Raynaud phenomenon, the same vasospasm that blanches fingers striking the coronary microvessels, culminating in interstitial myocardial fibrosis with ventricular tachyarrhythmias and diastolic dysfunction visible as late gadolinium enhancement on cardiac MRI. Standard heart failure drugs apply, with implantable defibrillators for sustained arrhythmia and calcium-channel blockade for the vasospastic component.

Pericarditis with or without cardiac tamponade is the most common cardiac manifestation across SLE, seen in up to 40 to 50 percent in autopsy series, and also in adult-onset Still disease and systemic juvenile idiopathic arthritis, presenting with positional pleuritic pain, friction rub, low-voltage complexes with PR depression and diffuse ST elevation on ECG. Most episodes yield to NSAIDs with colchicine cover, while tamponade physiology needs urgent pericardial drainage.
