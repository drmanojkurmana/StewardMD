# Cogan syndrome and single organ vasculitis (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-single-organ: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Cogan syndrome is a rare variable-vessel vasculitis characterized by non-syphilitic interstitial keratitis (IK) and progressive sensorineural hearing loss with vestibular dysfunction (vertigo, ataxia, tinnitus) resembling Meniere disease.

Cardiovascular involvement occurs in 10% to 15% of Cogan syndrome patients, manifesting as severe ascending aortitis, aortic root dilatation leading to acute aortic regurgitation, coronary arteritis, and systemic vasculitis.

Early high-dose systemic glucocorticoids (prednisone 1 mg/kg/day) combined with steroid-sparing agents (methotrexate, azathioprine, or anti-TNF infliximab) are critical in Cogan syndrome to prevent permanent, irreversible bilateral deafness.

Primary angiitis of the central nervous system (PACNS) is a single-organ vasculitis strictly confined to the brain and leptomeninges, presenting with chronic headache, encephalopathy, and multifocal ischemic strokes, with abnormal CSF in >90%.

Isolated cutaneous leukocytoclastic angiitis (hypersensitivity vasculitis) is a single-organ small-vessel vasculitis restricted to the skin, typically presenting with self-limiting palpable purpura following drug exposure or viral infection.

Isolated testicular vasculitis is a medium-vessel single-organ necrotizing vasculitis presenting with unilateral testicular pain and swelling, often mimicking a testicular neoplasm or torsion, diagnosed after orchiectomy without requiring systemic immunosuppression.

Isolated breast arteritis and isolated gastrointestinal vasculitis represent other localized single-organ vasculitides; long-term longitudinal monitoring is mandatory because up to 10% to 15% subsequently develop widespread systemic necrotizing vasculitis.

Brain biopsy remains the diagnostic gold standard for PACNS, demonstrating transmural granulomatous, lymphocytic, or necrotizing arteritis of parenchymal and leptomeningeal vessels while excluding infection and lymphoma.
