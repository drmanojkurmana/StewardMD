# Relapsing polychondritis and VEXAS (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison)
Relapsing polychondritis is episodic immune-mediated inflammation of cartilaginous tissues, chiefly ears, nose, larynx, tracheobronchial tree, and joints, with cumulative bilateral auricular chondritis that spares the lobule.
Auricular chondritis gives tender violaceous swelling of pinna and external canal with lobule sparing, since lobules lack cartilage.
Nasal chondritis causes pain, epistaxis, and rhinorrhea with saddle-nose deformity from collapsed bridge, indistinguishable from granulomatosis with polyangiitis without biopsy.
Laryngotracheal disease affects up to 50 percent: hoarseness, cough, wheeze, stridor, and cartilage collapse risking fatal obstruction, monitored by flow-volume loops and dynamic CT.
Ocular disease hits above 50 percent: episcleritis, scleritis, conjunctivitis, iritis, and retinopathy with proptosis.
McAdam criteria need 3 of 6: recurrent chondritis of both auricles, non-erosive seronegative inflammatory polyarthritis, nasal chondritis, ocular inflammation, respiratory tract chondritis, and audiovestibular damage.
VEXAS is adult-onset autoinflammatory disease from somatic X-linked UBA1 mutation in men above 50 years with macrocytic anemia, vacuoles in marrow precursors, chondritis, Sweet-like neutrophilic dermatosis, pulmonary infiltrates, and venous thromboembolism.
Myeloid UBA1 mosaicism drops into the 20 percent to 60 percent range in blood; low-dose glucocorticoids with ruxolitinib, azacitidine, or allogeneic transplant in selected patients form therapy.
Audiovestibular damage gives sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus, and vertigo from inner-ear vasculitis; arthritis is episodic, asymmetric, non-erosive, and seronegative, favoring costochondral and sternoclavicular joints.
Respiratory flares take high-dose glucocorticoids with airway stenting or tracheostomy when fixed tracheomalacia threatens; mortality clusters in airway, cardiac valvular, and infectious complications over 10 years.
Costochondritis with chest wall tenderness mimics cardiac pain in polychondritis; normal troponins with reproducible tenderness point to cartilage, while exertional pressure with ECG change points to coronaries.
Valvular disease with aortic regurgitation complicates long-standing polychondritis in up to 10 percent; annual echocardiography with early valve surgery referral prevents decompensation.
Pregnancy in quiescent polychondritis usually proceeds well, but active airway disease threatens ventilation under anesthesia; pre-conception laryngoscopy with anesthetic planning is required, and cyclophosphamide with methotrexate stay contraindicated throughout gestation.
