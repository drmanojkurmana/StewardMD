AAST grades pancreatic injury I to V from contusion through distal transection to massive head disruption, with ductal disruption from grade III upward mandating surgery. Grading standardises reports across centres.

Serum amylase is unreliable early, and contrast CT with pancreatic-phase cuts plus MRCP within 24 hours detects ductal injury guiding distal resection against drainage. Repeat scanning catches evolving leaks.

Distal transection left of the mesenteric vessels gets spleen-preserving distal pancreatectomy when stable, with splenectomy when hilar bleeding or time pressure dominates. Spleen preservation avoids lifelong sepsis risk in the young.

Proximal head injuries with intact ampulla and bile duct get drainage with pyloric exclusion and gastrojejunostomy, while ampullary avulsion or devascularised duodenum needs trauma Whipple. Duct and ampulla status decides the operation.

Duodenal haematoma with high obstruction after blunt trauma in children resolves with 10 to 14 days of nasogastric decompression, confirmed by contrast study before feeding. Operation converts haematoma to leak.

Duodenal perforation under 50 percent circumference gets primary transverse closure with omental patch, while over 75 percent or delayed cases need pyloric exclusion with gastrojejunostomy. Transverse closure avoids narrowing the lumen.

Post-traumatic fistula under 200 mL per day closes with octreotide (somatostatin analogue) and enteral nutrition in 80 percent, while high-output or persistent leaks beyond 6 weeks need ERCP stenting or resection. Output volume guides the ladder.

Missed ductal injury presents as pseudocyst or fistula weeks later, managed by internal drainage with cystgastrostomy once the wall matures at 6 weeks. Early external drainage recurs. Pancreatic protocol CT uses thin slices through the gland with enteric contrast to show ductal discontinuity. MRCP with secretin stimulation improves duct visualisation. Children with seat-belt ecchymosis need amylase trending with observation even when the first scan is normal. ERCP with transpapillary stenting bridges minor duct leaks that persist despite drainage. Follow-up amylase trending guides discharge timing. Repeat CT at 48 hours regrades borderline duct injuries.
