Active surveillance suits low-risk prostate cancer, defined by ISUP Grade Group 1 histology, PSA <10 ng/mL, and clinical stage cT1c-cT2a. Surveillance combines serial PSA measurement, multiparametric MRI, and scheduled protocol biopsies, deferring radical treatment until progression signals appear, which preserves continence and potency in the majority who never progress.

Robotic-assisted radical prostatectomy, RARP, excises the gland while preserving the neurovascular bundles coursing between the prostatic fascia and the levator fascia. This nerve-sparing dissection maintains erectile function in appropriately selected localised cases without compromising surgical margins.

Surveillance after treatment hinges on PSA thresholds. Biochemical recurrence following prostatectomy is declared after two consecutive prostatectomy PSA rise values of 0.2 ng/mL or higher. After radiotherapy, the Phoenix criteria set recurrence at a PSA increase of 2.0 ng/mL or more above the radiation nadir, the lowest value achieved post-treatment.

Molecular imaging rescues early relapse detection. 68Ga-PSMA PET-CT, alongside 18F-PSMA variants, visualises prostate specific membrane antigen avid disease with sensitivity surpassing CT and bone scintigraphy at PSA concentrations between 0.2 and 1.0 ng/mL, guiding metastasis directed salvage while tumour burden stays minimal.

Androgen deprivation therapy, ADT, suppresses testosterone to the castrate level below 50 ng/dL, equivalent to under 1.7 nmol/L. LHRH agonists such as leuprolide desensitise pituitary signalling after an initial surge, LHRH antagonists including degarelix and oral relugolix block receptors directly without flare, and bilateral orchiectomy offers immediate permanent surgical castration.

Metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer, mHSPC, now receives intensified doublets or triplets: ADT combined with androgen receptor signaling inhibitors, enzalutamide, apalutamide, or abiraterone, plus docetaxel for high volume presentations. Upfront intensification extends survival well beyond ADT alone.

Once castration resistance emerges, genomics directs care. Metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer, mCRPC, with homologous recombination repair defects, particularly BRCA1/2, responds profoundly to PARP inhibitors olaparib and rucaparib, making germline and somatic HRR testing standard in advanced disease.
