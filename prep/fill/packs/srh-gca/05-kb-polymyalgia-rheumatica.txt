# Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR)
Adults
Primary care, outpatient rheumatology clinic
Confirm PMR, exclude mimics and giant cell arteritis, then start prednisolone 12.5 to 25 mg once daily, taper to 10 mg/day within 4 to 8 weeks and then by 1 mg every 4 weeks; add methotrexate when relapse or steroid-toxicity risk is high, and protect bone.
Aliases: PMR; shoulder girdle pain; hip girdle pain; prednisolone taper; giant cell arteritis; GCA; EULAR/ACR PMR 2015.
# Recognise
Age 50 or over (usually over 60) with subacute bilateral shoulder and/or hip girdle pain, morning stiffness over 45 minutes, difficulty rising from a chair or lifting the arms, and raised ESR or CRP, for 2 weeks or more.
Ask every patient about giant cell arteritis: new headache, scalp tenderness, jaw claudication, visual disturbance, abnormal temporal arteries, limb claudication. If present, follow the GCA pathway the same day (higher-dose glucocorticoid).
Exclude mimics: elderly-onset RA, spondyloarthritis, CPPD, shoulder disease, myositis, hypothyroidism, statin myopathy, infection (endocarditis, TB), myeloma or other malignancy, and immune checkpoint inhibitor-induced PMR.
Refer to rheumatology if atypical (age under 60, peripheral inflammatory arthritis, systemic symptoms, low inflammatory markers), at high risk of steroid side effects, refractory to glucocorticoid, or with relapses or prolonged therapy.
# Baseline assessment before treatment (EULAR/ACR)
ESR and/or CRP, CBC, glucose, creatinine, liver function, bone profile (calcium, alkaline phosphatase), RF and/or anti-CCP, dipstick urinalysis.
Consider serum protein electrophoresis, TSH, creatine kinase and vitamin D; ANA, ANCA, TB tests or chest X-ray when clinically indicated to exclude mimics.
Record comorbidities that raise steroid risk: diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, dyslipidaemia, osteoporosis or recent fracture, cataract or glaucoma, peptic ulcer, recurrent infection, NSAID use.
Ultrasound (subdeltoid bursitis, biceps tenosynovitis, trochanteric bursitis) supports the diagnosis when it is uncertain.
# Glucocorticoid regimen (EULAR/ACR 2015)
Use glucocorticoid, not NSAIDs. Start prednisolone at the minimum effective dose within 12.5 to 25 mg once daily: higher in the range if relapse risk is high and steroid risk low; lower with diabetes, osteoporosis or glaucoma. Avoid starting at 7.5 mg/day or less, or above 30 mg/day.
Expect marked improvement in symptoms and falling ESR or CRP within 1 to 2 weeks; a poor response should prompt review of the diagnosis.
Initial taper: reduce to 10 mg/day within 4 to 8 weeks. Once in remission, reduce by 1 mg every 4 weeks (or by 1.25 mg using alternate-day schedules such as 10 mg and 7.5 mg) until stopped.
Relapse: increase to the pre-relapse dose, then reduce gradually over 4 to 8 weeks to the dose at which relapse occurred, then resume slow tapering.
Give a single morning dose; divided doses only for prominent night pain when below 5 mg/day. IM methylprednisolone (120 mg every 3 weeks initially, as in one trial) is an alternative to oral treatment.
Many patients need 1 to 2 years or more of treatment; individualise duration to the minimum effective.
# Steroid-sparing therapy
Consider early methotrexate 7.5 to 10 mg once weekly with folic acid in patients at high risk of relapse or prolonged therapy, or with comorbidities that make steroid adverse effects likely; also for relapse, poor response or steroid adverse effects.
Possible relapse risk factors (low to moderate evidence): female sex, ESR above 40 mm/h, peripheral inflammatory arthritis.
IL-6 receptor inhibitors reduce relapses and cumulative steroid dose in trials: sarilumab 200 mg SC every 2 weeks is FDA approved (2023) for PMR with inadequate response to glucocorticoid or inability to taper; tocilizumab is used off-label. Specialist use; these post-date the 2015 recommendations.
Do not use TNF inhibitors (strong recommendation against).
# Follow-up and steroid safety
Review every 4 to 8 weeks in the first year, every 8 to 12 weeks in the second year, and at relapse or while tapering: symptoms, ESR or CRP, GCA symptoms, glucose, BP, weight, mood and eyes.
Bone protection: calcium and vitamin D if intake is low, fracture risk assessment adjusted for glucocorticoid dose, and a bisphosphonate for those at high risk (for example older age or previous fragility fracture), per glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis guidance.
Offer an individualised exercise programme to maintain muscle mass and reduce falls, especially in frail older people on long-term glucocorticoid.
Steroid card and sick-day advice; never stop abruptly after prolonged use. Give rapid access for flares and teach GCA warning symptoms that need same-day assessment.
# Pitfalls
Missing GCA, which can cause sudden permanent visual loss: ask about headache, jaw claudication and visual symptoms at diagnosis and at every relapse.
Starting prednisolone before baseline tests, or labelling aches with a raised ESR in an older person as PMR without excluding infection, malignancy or RA.
Tapering too fast (relapse), or starting doses above 30 mg/day that add toxicity without benefit.
Increasing the dose for an isolated rise in ESR or CRP without symptoms.
Omitting bone protection and glucose and BP monitoring.
# Drugs
Prednisolone
# Dose
12.5 to 25 mg orally once daily; taper to 10 mg/day within 4 to 8 weeks, then by 1 mg every 4 weeks
Avoid initial doses of 7.5 mg/day or less or above 30 mg/day.
Methylprednisolone acetate (IM), 120 mg IM every 3 weeks initially (trial regimen), then reduced by the specialist, Alternative to oral glucocorticoid at physician discretion.
Methotrexate, 7.5 to 10 mg orally once weekly, With folic acid. Weekly only. For high relapse risk or steroid toxicity.
Sarilumab
200 mg subcutaneously every 2 weeks
Relapsing or steroid-dependent PMR, specialist only. Screen TB and hepatitis B; monitor CBC, liver enzymes and lipids.
Alendronic acid
70 mg orally once weekly
Bone protection when fracture risk is high; with calcium and vitamin D; avoid if eGFR below 35.
