# Giant cell arteritis (GCA): urgent assessment and treatment
Adults
Emergency department, GP or outpatient clinic, rheumatology fast-track clinic
In anyone aged 50 or over with new headache, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness or visual symptoms and raised ESR or CRP, start prednisolone 40 to 60 mg at once (IV methylprednisolone if vision is threatened), refer the same day, confirm by temporal and axillary ultrasound or biopsy, and consider tocilizumab to spare steroids.
Aliases: GCA; temporal arteritis; cranial arteritis; Horton disease; jaw claudication; large vessel vasculitis; LVV; halo sign; temporal artery biopsy; TAB; tocilizumab GCA; polymyalgia rheumatica.
# Recognise
Age 50 or over with new headache (often temporal), scalp tenderness, jaw or tongue claudication, visual symptoms (amaurosis fugax, diplopia, sudden visual loss), fever, weight loss, or polymyalgia symptoms (shoulder and hip girdle pain and stiffness).
Signs: tender, thickened, nodular or pulseless temporal artery; unequal arm BP, arm claudication or bruits suggest large vessel (extracranial) GCA.
ESR and CRP are usually high; normal values make GCA less likely but do not exclude it.
Visual loss (usually anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy) is often permanent, and the other eye is at risk within days if untreated.
GCA is less common in India than in Northern Europe but does occur. In an older patient with fever and a high ESR also consider TB, infective endocarditis and malignancy, and screen before prolonged immunosuppression.
# Immediate actions
Strong clinical suspicion: start glucocorticoids immediately, before confirmatory tests (EULAR 2025, BSR 2020). Do not wait for biopsy or imaging.
No visual or other cranial ischaemic symptoms: prednisolone 40 to 60 mg orally once daily (high-dose oral is preferred over IV pulses, ACR 2021).
Threatened or recent visual loss, amaurosis fugax or other cranial ischaemia: IV methylprednisolone 500 mg to 1 g daily for up to 3 days, then high-dose oral prednisolone (ACR 2021, BSR 2020). Do not delay treatment to obtain IV access; give oral prednisolone first if needed.
Refer for specialist assessment within 24 hours (EULAR 2025; rheumatology fast-track clinic where available), and the same day to ophthalmology if there are any visual symptoms.
Take ESR, CRP, CBC, renal and liver function and glucose, ideally before the first dose, but never let this delay treatment.
Low-dose aspirin is not routine; ACR 2021 suggests adding it only for critical or flow-limiting carotid or vertebral involvement.
# Confirm the diagnosis
Ultrasound of the temporal AND axillary arteries is the first-line imaging test (EULAR 2023): look for a halo sign and a non-compressible artery (compression sign). Do it as early as possible, because findings fade within days of glucocorticoids.
Alternatives: FDG-PET or MRI of cranial and extracranial arteries. CT or MR angiography of the aorta and branches at baseline detects large vessel involvement and aneurysm (ACR 2021).
Temporal artery biopsy where ultrasound expertise is unavailable or results are inconclusive: unilateral, long segment (more than 1 cm), ideally within 2 weeks of starting glucocorticoids (ACR 2021). A negative biopsy does not exclude GCA.
Baseline for treatment: HbA1c or glucose, lipids, BP, bone density where available; latent TB (IGRA or tuberculin test) and hepatitis B and C serology before tocilizumab or other immunosuppressants.
# Glucocorticoid taper and steroid-sparing therapy
BSR 2020 taper: continue 40 to 60 mg until symptoms and inflammatory markers have resolved, then reduce by 10 mg every 2 weeks to 20 mg, then by 2.5 mg every 2 to 4 weeks to 10 mg, then by 1 mg every 1 to 2 months if there is no relapse.
Tocilizumab 162 mg subcutaneously once weekly with a glucocorticoid taper: ACR 2021 conditionally prefers it for newly diagnosed GCA; EULAR 2025 recommends adjunctive IL-6 receptor inhibition for selected patients, such as those with relapsing disease or at high risk of glucocorticoid toxicity.
Upadacitinib is an alternative adjunct for GCA in EULAR 2025 (15 mg once daily in the SELECT-GCA trial). Methotrexate (typically 10 to 15 mg once weekly with folic acid) is a cheaper alternative with a smaller steroid-sparing effect.
Relapse with cranial ischaemic symptoms: increase glucocorticoid and add tocilizumab (ACR 2021). Relapse with polymyalgia symptoms only: a modest glucocorticoid increase is often enough.
Tocilizumab suppresses CRP, ESR and fever, so judge disease activity and infection clinically; avoid it with a history of diverticulitis or bowel perforation.
# Prevent treatment harm and follow up
Bone protection from the start: calcium and vitamin D, and consider a bisphosphonate (for example alendronic acid 70 mg weekly) once vitamin D and renal function are checked.
Consider a proton pump inhibitor while on high-dose glucocorticoids; monitor glucose and BP (steroid-induced diabetes is common), and watch for cataract, glaucoma and infection.
Consider Pneumocystis prophylaxis when high-dose glucocorticoids are combined with other immunosuppressants.
Review regularly for relapse (return of headache, jaw claudication, visual or polymyalgia symptoms, rising markers) and for late aortic aneurysm or stenosis.
Never stop glucocorticoids abruptly after prolonged use (adrenal insufficiency); give sick-day advice and a steroid card.
# Pitfalls
Delaying glucocorticoids for biopsy or imaging when vision is at risk.
Starting the polymyalgia dose (15 to 25 mg prednisolone) for suspected GCA; GCA needs 40 to 60 mg.
Attributing transient visual loss or diplopia to migraine or cataract in an older patient with headache.
Relying on a normal ESR or a negative biopsy to exclude GCA when the clinical picture is typical.
Forgetting bone protection and TB screening in long-term glucocorticoid or tocilizumab treatment.
# Drugs
Prednisolone
# Dose
40 to 60 mg orally once daily, then taper (BSR: by 10 mg every 2 weeks to 20 mg, 2.5 mg every 2 to 4 weeks to 10 mg, then 1 mg every 1 to 2 months)
Start immediately on suspicion. Daily, not alternate-day, dosing.
Methylprednisolone (IV), 500 mg to 1 g IV daily for up to 3 days, Threatened or recent visual loss or other cranial ischaemia; follow with oral prednisolone.
Tocilizumab, 162 mg subcutaneously once weekly, Steroid-sparing. Screen for TB and hepatitis B first. Masks CRP and fever.
Methotrexate, Typically 10 to 15 mg orally once weekly, with folic acid on another day, Alternative steroid-sparing agent. Check CBC, liver and renal function.
Alendronic acid, 70 mg orally once weekly, Bone protection with calcium and vitamin D. Check renal function and vitamin D first.
