# Giant cell arteritis and polymyalgia rheumatica (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison; 2022 ACR EULAR GCA criteria; GiACTA trial)
GCA strikes at 50 years or older with peak at 70 to 80 years and female to male ratio of 3:1. The 2022 ACR EULAR criteria take age 50 or older as entry with 6 points or more confirming, across clinical, laboratory (ESR 50 mm per hour or higher, CRP 10 mg per L or higher), and imaging or biopsy domains.
Jaw claudication carries likelihood ratio above 4.0 for positive biopsy, the most specific cranial symptom.
Permanent visual loss hits 15 percent to 20 percent of untreated patients, usually anterior ischemic optic neuropathy from short posterior ciliary artery occlusion with sudden painless altitudinal deficit and pale swollen disc.
Biopsy needs at least 1 cm to 2 cm length for skip lesions, showing transmural granulomas, internal elastic lamina fragmentation, giant cells, and intimal hyperplasia.
Temporal and axillary halo sign on Doppler gives pooled sensitivity of 75 percent with specificity above 95 percent in expert hands.
Emergency therapy is prednisone 40 mg to 60 mg daily at once, or IV methylprednisolone 500 mg to 1,000 mg daily for 3 days with visual symptoms, never awaiting biopsy.
GiACTA proved tocilizumab 162 mg weekly or every 2 weeks with a 26-week prednisone taper gives sustained remission of 56 percent against 14 percent at 52 weeks with less steroid load.
