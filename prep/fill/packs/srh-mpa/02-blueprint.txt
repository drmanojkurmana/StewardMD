# Microscopic polyangiitis and AAV management (grounding reference: Kelley & Firestein, Harrison, ACR/EULAR criteria)

Rheumatology topic srh-mpa: overview of pathophysiology, diagnostic criteria, clinical features, and management guidelines.

Microscopic polyangiitis (MPA) is a systemic necrotizing small-vessel vasculitis without granulomatous inflammation, characteristically presenting with rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis and pulmonary capillaritis causing diffuse alveolar hemorrhage.

Approximately 70% to 85% of MPA patients have circulating perinuclear ANCA (p-ANCA) targeting myeloperoxidase (MPO-ANCA); anti-MPO antibodies stimulate neutrophil oxidative burst, degranulation, and endothelial cell lysis.

Renal involvement occurs in >90% of MPA patients, manifesting as pauci-immune necrotizing and crescentic glomerulonephritis; light microscopy shows segmental fibrinoid necrosis and crescents with absent immune complexes on immunofluorescence.

Unlike granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA), microscopic polyangiitis is clinically distinguished by the complete absence of destructive upper respiratory granulomatous inflammation (no saddle-nose deformity, no cavitary lung nodules, and no subglottic stenosis).

Fibrosing interstitial lung disease (ILD) with a usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) or NSIP pattern occurs in up to 15% to 30% of MPO-ANCA positive MPA patients, often preceding vasculitic manifestations and carrying a high mortality.

First-line induction therapy for organ- or life-threatening MPA combines high-dose systemic glucocorticoids with either rituximab (375 mg/m2 weekly for 4 doses) or intravenous cyclophosphamide, both demonstrating equivalent remission induction.

The PEXIVAS randomized trial demonstrated that routine therapeutic plasma exchange (TPE) did not reduce death or end-stage renal disease in severe AAV with renal failure (serum creatinine >=5.7 mg/dL) or alveolar hemorrhage, leading guidelines to restrict plasma exchange to concurrent anti-GBM disease or refractory DAH.

Remission maintenance relies on low-dose rituximab (500 mg every 6 months) or oral azathioprine (2 mg/kg/day) continued for at least 24 months, alongside mandatory trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia prophylaxis.
