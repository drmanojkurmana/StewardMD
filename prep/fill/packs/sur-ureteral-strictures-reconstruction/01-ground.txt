Distal ureteral strictures and transections within 4 cm to 5 cm of the ureterovesical junction are repaired by ureteroneocystostomy, the reimplantation of the ureter into the bladder. The scarred tip is excised, the healthy end spatulated, and a submucosal tunnel fashioned to stop reflux. A short sound ureter reaches the bladder without tension. When the gap measures 6 cm to 8 cm, the psoas hitch bridges it: the bladder dome is mobilised and anchored to the psoas minor tendon with nonabsorbable sutures, so the join lies slack.

The Boari bladder flap covers mid to lower defects of 12 cm to 15 cm. A pedicled strip of bladder wall is rolled into a tubularized flap conduit, keeping a base to apex ratio of 2:1 to protect the tip blood supply; a narrow long flap necroses. Transureteroureterostomy, abbreviated TUU, swings the injured donor ureter across the midline anterior to the aorta for an end to side join to the healthy recipient ureter, rescuing long proximal loss with a single draining system. Most injuries needing TUU follow pelvic operations near the ureter, so its danger sites must be known.

The TUU contraindications are urothelial carcinoma, chronic stone disease, retroperitoneal fibrosis, prior pelvic radiation, and severe bilateral ureteral disease, since each threatens the lone recipient system. The ileal ureter uses an isoperistaltic segment of terminal ileum for pan ureteral loss, always sparing the distal 15 cm that absorbs vitamin B12 and bile salts, and only when serum creatinine lies below 2.0 mg/dL, because a failing kidney cannot clear bowel mucus and acid. Buccal mucosa graft onlay with an omental wrap rescues non obliterative proximal strictures: cheek lining tolerates urine and the fat apron feeds the graft. Renal autotransplantation to the iliac fossa, joining vessels to the iliac artery and vein, is the last resort when bladder and bowel are both unusable. Ureteral strictures demand a tension free repair matched to gap length.
