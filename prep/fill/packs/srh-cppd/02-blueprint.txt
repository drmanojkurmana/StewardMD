# Calcium pyrophosphate deposition disease (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison; 2023 ACR EULAR CPPD criteria)
CPPD is driven by excess extracellular inorganic pyrophosphate (PPi) made by ENPP1 and exported from chondrocytes through the ANKH transporter.
Chondrocalcinosis is the radiographic hallmark: fine linear or punctate calcific densities inside fibrocartilage of knee menisci, wrist triangular fibrocartilage, and symphysis pubis, plus hyaline articular cartilage.
The 2023 ACR EULAR criteria require microscopic proof of positively birefringent rhomboid crystals or bilateral imaging chondrocalcinosis, standardizing all clinical phenotypes.
Acute pseudogout is intensely painful mono or oligoarthritis of knee in above 50 percent, wrist, or shoulder, triggered by acute illness, surgery such as parathyroidectomy or arthroplasty, or hyaluronan injection.
Chronic CPPD arthritis mimics rheumatoid with symmetric polyarthritis of wrists, MCP joints, and elbows plus subchondral cysts and morning stiffness, but RF and ACPA are negative.
Crowned dens syndrome is CPPD of cruciform and transverse ligaments around the odontoid in elderly patients with severe neck pain, stiffness, fever, and raised markers, diagnosed by retro-odontoid calcification on non-contrast cervical CT.
Early or extensive CPPD points to hemochromatosis with HFE mutations, primary hyperparathyroidism with excess parathyroid hormone (PTH), hypomagnesemia including Gitelman syndrome, and hypophosphatasia with ALPL mutations.
Acute attacks take aspiration with glucocorticoid injection, oral NSAID therapy, or low-dose colchicine; frequent flares take daily colchicine 0.5 mg to 0.6 mg for prophylaxis.
Synovial fluid shows weakly positively birefringent rhomboid crystals under compensated polarized light, with moderate leukocyte counts that overlap gout and early sepsis, so Gram stain and culture accompany every first attack.
Tophaceous CPPD forms tumoral calcinosis-like masses around large joints in chronic disease; spinal CPPD deposits in discs and ligamentum flavum mimic ankylosing spondylitis on plain films but spare the sacroiliac joints.
Colchicine prophylaxis needs dose reduction below 0.5 mg daily when creatinine clearance falls under 30 mL per minute, with monitoring for myopathy and cytopenias during long-term use.
