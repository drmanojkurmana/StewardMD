# Extra-articular rheumatoid arthritis (blueprint grounding: Kelley and Firestein, Harrison; ACR guidance)
Rheumatoid nodules occur in 20 percent to 30 percent of RA patients, almost always seropositive for RF and ACPA. Histology shows central fibrinoid necrosis rimmed by palisading CD68 positive epithelioid histiocytes with outer chronic mononuclear cells, over extensor pressure points such as the olecranon.
RA-ILD is clinical in 10 percent of patients. Unlike other CTDs where NSIP leads, the commonest RA pattern is UIP with subpleural basilar reticulation and honeycombing on HRCT, carrying poor prognosis.
Felty syndrome is seropositive RA with splenomegaly and severe neutropenia (absolute neutrophil count below 1,000 to 1,500 per microliter), linked to HLA-DR4 0401, high-titer RF, leg ulcers, and recurrent bacterial infections.
LGL leukemia mimics Felty with neutropenia and splenomegaly; clonal CD3 positive CD8 positive CD57 positive T cells with STAT3 mutations occur in above 40 percent and respond to methotrexate or cyclophosphamide.
Rheumatoid vasculitis is necrotizing small and medium vessel arteritis of long-standing seropositive RA with deep ulcers, digital gangrene, purpura, and mononeuritis multiplex, carrying high mortality and requiring cyclophosphamide or rituximab with high-dose glucocorticoids.
Ocular disease runs from secondary Sjogren dry eye (commonest) through episcleritis to necrotizing anterior scleritis; scleromalacia perforans is painless anterior necrosis without inflammation in nodular seropositive RA that risks globe rupture.
Cervical disease erodes the dens and loosens the transverse ligament; atlantoaxial subluxation is diagnosed when the anterior atlanto-dens interval exceeds 3 mm (above 5 mm is severe), risking cord compression, so flexion views precede intubation.
Chronic high-grade inflammation drives AA amyloidosis through IL-6 mediated serum amyloid A overproduction; glomerular deposits cause nephrotic syndrome and renal failure, controlled with anti IL-6 or anti TNF biologics.
Methotrexate accelerates nodulosis in some patients, producing small accelerated nodules on fingers despite good joint control; nodules that ulcerate or compress nerves require intralesional glucocorticoid or surgical excision with continued DMARD therapy.
