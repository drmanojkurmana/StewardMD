fix: a made-up approval reference was clearing antimicrobial stewardship

formulary.js blocks a restricted drug until it is handed an approval reference, which is
right, and then read `if (entry.requiresApproval && approvalRef)` - ANY non-empty string.
approvalRef came off the request body and nothing ever looked it up. A prescriber blocked
from meropenem at 2am could type one character and be through. The control the hospital
had configured to protect its last-line antibiotics was a formality that looked like a
control, which is worse than not having one, because everybody believed in it.

_wardsynq/verification.js is the fix, and its shape is the one thing from the whole
OpenMRS/Bahmni/Danphe audit worth taking wholesale - Danphe's append-only approval chain.
An approval is never a flag on the thing approved. It is its own record pointing at the
one before it, so who decided what, at which level, in what order, is always
reconstructable. A boolean cannot answer "who approved it, and was it later withdrawn",
and by the time anybody asks, the answer is gone.

The rules that make it worth having:
  - The person who asked cannot be a person who grants. Without this the chain is just an
    audit trail of people approving themselves, which is what the free-text reference
    already was.
  - Two approvals from one person are one person agreeing with themselves.
  - An approval covers the subject it was granted for. Meropenem's approval does not
    prescribe vancomycin.
  - A withdrawal is a new row after the approval it withdraws, never an edit of it. The
    approval stays readable, because it happened, and because somebody reviewing a death
    needs to see that it was given before it was taken back.
  - How many approvers a hospital wants is org config, the same way note writers are.
  - Reading the chain can fail, and a failure resolves to NOT approved. Failing closed is
    the only safe direction: an approval that cannot be read is not an approval.

Closing the lock without cutting a key would have made restricted drugs unprescribable,
so this also ships the door: ward/approval-request, ward/approval-decide, ward/approvals,
and an Approvals screen showing each chain in full, oldest first, withdrawn approvals
included.

Two existing tests asserted the old behaviour - they encoded the hole as intended
behaviour, which is how it survived this long. Both now assert the fix, and one proves
end to end that a real chain read out of the record store does clear the block. The
drug-scoping case moved to a unit test rather than another live order, because this file
shares one rate-limit budget and an extra write was failing an unrelated FHIR test two
thousand lines below.
