feat: the timeline becomes the whole clinical story, readable and filterable

The timeline said "progress note drafted" and stopped there - so the one thing a doctor
picking up an unfamiliar patient most needs, what the last doctor actually thought, was
the one thing the history would not show them. They had to open every note in turn to
find out. It also said WHAT happened and almost never WHO did it, and it covered about
half of what happens to a patient.

WHAT WAS WRITTEN. A note's own sections are on the timeline now, headings included,
because a heading is how a reader tells an examination finding from a plan. Nothing is
summarised, shortened or rephrased - it is the clinician's words or it is nothing.

WHO DID IT. Every record carries writtenBy, stamped by the store rather than supplied by
the caller, so this is the session that actually wrote it and not a claim. The
role-specific fields win where they exist, because a prescription's prescriber and an
order's requester are the person answerable for it, which is not always the session that
saved the row. Rows now read "dr.mehta wrote a progress note" and "dr.mehta ordered CBC".
An account id that cannot be turned into words stays "a clinician account" rather than
being dressed up as a name.

THE WHOLE STORY, not notes and vitals. Added: procedures (operation, anaesthetic,
implant, delivery), critical events, dispensing, medicines reconciliation, specimen
collection, admission requests, allergies, resuscitation, hospital emergencies,
break-glass access, and billing. Admitted, moved and discharged are said as three
different things rather than as one status word - and a ward stay is ADMITTED while an
outpatient visit CHECKS IN, because one word for both reads wrong in whichever half it
does not belong to.

AN ORDER AND ITS RESULT, JOINED. DiagnosticReport already carries the ServiceRequest it
answers, so an investigation now says whether the report is back and offers a button that
opens it; one still waiting says so in those words instead of looking identical to the
finished one. A corrected report supersedes the preliminary one. Imaging answers its
order the same way.

FILTERS, by kind and by date, with counts. A kind with nothing in it is not offered at
all - thirteen chips of which nine are empty is a worse screen than four that mean
something. The active filter is filled, not outlined, and is restated in words above the
list, because a filter a reader does not notice is a reader looking at an incomplete
history without knowing it. The glance card on the chart is deliberately never filtered.

EXPAND AND COLLAPSE. Collapsed is the default and the preview is the real first line
rather than an ellipsis, so a whole stay's assessments can be skimmed without opening one.

COLOUR, under this file's own rule about colour. ward.css says why the primary action
here is near-ink and not a brand colour: an interface where colour means "clickable" has
spent the association that must stay free for "clinically abnormal". A thirteen-colour
timeline would spend it far more thoroughly, and the one genuinely red thing on a chart
would stop being seen. So kinds are separated by weight in the same ink, real colour is
spent only on an unacknowledged critical result and an allergy, and the category is
written on every row in words - the edge groups, the word explains.

Money is the one deliberate narrowing: a bill says WHAT was billed and never how much.
The amounts live on the billing screen, which has its own permission; a clinical history
quietly carrying a patient's money would put it on every clinical reader's screen. Tested.

Two bugs caught while building: "Open the report" pointed at an investigations view that
does not exist (results are a card on the chart), which would have shown a blank screen;
and the report id was being dropped in the results mapping, so nothing could be picked
out to open.
