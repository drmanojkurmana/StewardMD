feat: the pharmacy can order stock and book it in

stock.js has said "NOTHING HERE PURCHASES ANYTHING" since it was written, and was right
to defer it. This is the missing half, and it was the one confirmed, explicit gap in
WardSynQ's own pharmacy that the OpenMRS/Bahmni/Danphe audit found - neither OpenMRS nor
Bahmni has a first-party answer to it at all, so the shape is taken from Danphe's
purchase-order chain, which is the part of that codebase that is genuinely good.

Three disciplines carried over from stock.js rather than reinvented:

HOW MUCH HAS ARRIVED IS NEVER STORED. It is summed from the goods received against the
order, exactly as a stock level is summed from movements. There is no receivedQty column
to drift and no status field to disagree with the events beneath it. A part-delivered
order is not a state somebody remembered to set - it is what the arithmetic says.

A GOODS RECEIPT IS A STOCK MOVEMENT, NOT A SECOND LEDGER. Receiving against an order
writes the receipt stock.js already understands, carrying the order it belongs to. Danphe
keeps a separate inventory ledger beside its stock ledger and the two can disagree; when
two records describe the same physical box, one is wrong and nobody knows which.

UNITS ARE NOT CONVERTED. A line ordered in boxes and a delivery in tablets are not added
together, and nothing here guesses the ratio - that is a product catalogue this build does
not have, and guessing produces a confident number wrong by a factor of twenty-eight. A
delivery in the wrong unit is recorded and flagged, and does not count towards its line.

Over-delivery is recorded and named, never refused. If a hundred arrive against an order
for ninety, a hundred arrived - the boxes are on the shelf either way, and a receipt
turned away is stock no record knows about. Same reasoning as stock.js refusing to clamp a
negative level to zero.

Approval is the chain from the previous commit, not a new flag: one approval mechanism in
WardSynQ rather than one per feature, so a purchase order cannot be approved by whoever
raised it, for exactly the reason a restricted antibiotic cannot be. Stock is not booked
in against an unapproved order - not because the stock is not real, but because doing so
would make an order nobody approved look like one placed properly. It can always be
recorded as an ordinary receipt.

Two bugs caught while writing it: a receipt naming line 0 was double-counting against
every other line holding the same item, and the approval lookup had a ternary whose
branches were identical, so an approval for a different subject would have read as this
order's. Both fixed, both covered.
