feat(P0.7): who to ring about a patient, and whether they have died

Two of the gaps the audit confirmed were genuinely absent: the Patient model carried no
deceased status and no relationships at all, so a ward had nowhere to record a next of
kin and no way to state that a patient had died.

_wardsynq/patient-identity.js holds both, because both are facts about a PERSON rather
than findings about a body. A next of kin is not an observation and a death is not a
diagnosis.

A DEATH IS A NEW VERSION OF THE PATIENT, NEVER A DELETION AND NEVER A LOCK. The record
moves to its next version and every clinical record underneath stays exactly as it was,
because a death is the moment a chart becomes more important to read, not less. Nothing
here closes an encounter, cancels an order or stops a write: post-mortem documentation is
real work, and a system that seals the chart at the moment of death forces the people
doing that work to record it somewhere the record cannot see.

It also refuses to be casual about it:
  - an explicit confirmation is required, and the server refuses without it, so a
    mis-click on a ward list cannot record the wrong person dead
  - a death dated in the future is refused rather than clamped to now, because clamping
    hides the typo that produced it
  - recording one twice is refused rather than overwriting the first
  - withdrawing one is its own governed act needing a reason, and it keeps what was
    withdrawn: somebody investigating how a living patient came to be recorded dead has
    to be able to read it

CONTACTS ARE APPEND-ONLY AND DEACTIVATED, NEVER DELETED. "Who do we call" is asked at the
worst moments, and an emergency contact quietly overwritten last month leaves nobody to
call. Next of kin, guardian and emergency contact are three things one person can be, not
three lists that would disagree - in a real family they are usually the same person. A
next of kin or emergency contact with no telephone number is refused, because that is the
name somebody rings. And every read says plainly when there is nobody recorded at all,
rather than returning an empty list nobody notices.

Reception can record a contact - it is the same front-desk act as registering the patient,
and asking a doctor to type a telephone number is how the field stays empty. Recording a
death is emr.treat, because it is the clinical statement a certificate rests on.
Withdrawing one sits at the same bar deliberately.
