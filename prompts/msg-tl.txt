Timeline becomes a page, a doctor can write on it, and radiology gets staff

THE HISTORY WAS A CARD AMONG A DOZEN CARDS, which is the wrong shape for the one
thing a doctor picking up an unfamiliar patient does first: read the stay from
the beginning. It is now a page of its own, reached from the chart header and
from the card it replaces, and the note box sits at the TOP of it, because the
note a ward round produces is written while reading the history that prompted
it, not on a different screen.

NO HOSPITAL COULD WRITE A CLINICAL NOTE UNTIL IT CONFIGURED A TEMPLATE.
writeTemplatedNote refused anything not defined in org config, and a hospital
that had defined none - which is every hospital on the day it opens, including
the demonstration one - got template_not_found for whatever it sent. So
/ward/note existed, the chart had a compose box, and neither could produce a
note. BUILT_IN_TEMPLATES is one free-text progress note with a single "Clinical
course" section. It invents no clinical structure, asserts nothing about what an
examination should contain and carries no default text, which resolveTemplate
refuses outright and is right to. An org template of the same id replaces it, so
it is a floor and never a ceiling. The file header said it shipped no template of
its own; that paragraph now records why this one exists and what it deliberately
is not.

WHO ORDERED IT travels on the timeline. The entry said what was ordered and when
but never by whom, and "who ordered this chest film, and when" is the first
question asked about an investigation nobody can account for. requesterId is the
authenticated ordering clinician (migrate-inv-order.js: "never a name typed
anywhere"), so this is the session's own record rather than a free-text claim. An
order carrying no requester says nothing rather than guessing.

RADIOGRAPHER AND RADIOLOGIST NOW EXIST. Imaging had a worklist, a reporting
endpoint and a board, and nobody who could be given the job. They are separate
roles because the acts are separate: a radiographer acquires the study and needs
the worklist (emr.view, identity beside a requested procedure); reporting is a
different act with different accountability, which the cap table already states,
so only the radiologist holds lab.result and can file a report or protocol a
study. Neither orders, prescribes, dispenses or bills.

EMR_VIEW WAS TRIED ON THE LAB ROLE AND REVERTED, recorded in the file so nobody
spends an afternoon rediscovering it. The bench board needs patient identity and
emr.view is how the imaging worklist solved that, but emr.view is not an identity
grant: it also opens the discharge summary and the ward critical list, and
wardsynq-inpatient-emar.test.mjs asserts 403 on both. The test is right and the
grant was too wide. Naming a specimen needs name, MRN and date of birth, not a
discharge summary, so the answer is a narrow identity capability and that is not
this commit.
