# Sertoli Cell Tumour — Management

## Overview and treatment intent

Sertoli cell tumours are uncommon sex cord stromal tumours of the testis. DeVita states that the majority of these tumours are benign, and that treatment is radical orchiectomy. Because most patients are cured by surgery alone, the treatment intent for a typical, localised, non-syndromic Sertoli cell tumour is curative surgery with no systemic therapy required.

## Surgery: the primary and usually definitive treatment

- Radical inguinal orchiectomy is the standard treatment described by DeVita for Sertoli cell tumour.
- (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease) For a small mass that is confidently benign on imaging and intraoperative assessment, testis-sparing surgical excision may be considered instead of orchiectomy, particularly when preserving fertility or hormonal function matters to the patient; this is a general urologic-oncology approach rather than a claim sourced from DeVita's section on Sertoli cell tumour.
- DeVita links risk of metastasis to specific pathological features found on the surgical specimen: vascular invasion, cellular atypia, tumour necrosis, infiltrative margins, an increased mitotic rate, tumour size greater than five centimetres, older age at presentation, an increased proliferation index, and aneuploidy. These features on final histopathology are what should prompt staging and closer follow-up rather than treating the case as a routine benign tumour.

## When features suggest malignant potential

- DeVita notes that Sertoli cell tumours are occasionally metastatic, and that when they do spread, the pattern of metastasis is initially to the retroperitoneal lymph nodes.
- For a tumour with the adverse histologic features above, or with proven nodal or distant spread, staging cross-sectional imaging of the retroperitoneum, chest, and abdomen is the reasonable next step (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, though consistent with the retroperitoneal-first spread pattern DeVita describes).
- DeVita does not describe an established, effective systemic chemotherapy or radiotherapy regimen specifically for malignant Sertoli cell tumour in the section reviewed; this gap should be stated plainly rather than filled with an assumed regimen. Where malignant sex cord stromal tumour disease is nodal or oligometastatic, surgical resection (for example retroperitoneal lymph node dissection) is the modality with the clearest rationale, by analogy to the surgical-first approach DeVita describes for other testicular sex cord and adjacent tumours in the same section (Leydig cell tumour treated the same way, and adenocarcinoma of the rete testis, for which DeVita explicitly notes poor response to radiotherapy and chemotherapy with surgery, including retroperitoneal lymph node dissection, as the mainstay for selected low-volume metastatic disease). Extrapolating a chemotherapy regimen from germ cell tumour protocols would not be grounded and is therefore omitted.

## Hormonal and endocrine considerations

- DeVita states that Leydig and Sertoli cell tumours are associated with steroid hormone hypersecretion, and specifically that Sertoli cell tumours may be accompanied by precocious puberty in boys.
- (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease) Where a tumour is hormonally active, baseline and postoperative hormone assessment and monitoring for resolution of endocrine features such as precocious puberty or gynaecomastia after orchiectomy is reasonable clinical practice, though DeVita's text on this disease does not itself describe a monitoring protocol.
- Large cell calcifying and other syndromic subtypes carry associations with Carney complex and Peutz-Jeghers syndrome; genetic evaluation and family screening for these syndromes when a syndromic subtype is identified is a general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, since DeVita's text reviewed here does not discuss these syndromic associations, only the WHO histologic classification listing the subtypes by name.

## Follow-up and monitoring

- For the common benign, completely excised tumour, DeVita's text implies that orchiectomy alone is curative and does not describe a specific post-treatment surveillance schedule; a period of clinical follow-up with attention to the local surgical site and, where relevant, resolution of hormonal symptoms is a general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease.
- For tumours with adverse histologic features or confirmed malignant behaviour, surveillance should target the retroperitoneal lymph nodes first, consistent with DeVita's statement that this is the initial site of metastatic spread, then extend to distant sites if retroperitoneal disease is found (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, for the specific surveillance cadence and imaging modality choice).

## When to refer

- Any tumour with adverse pathological features (vascular invasion, necrosis, high mitotic rate, size over five centimetres, infiltrative margins, aneuploidy) or with imaging evidence of retroperitoneal or distant spread should be referred to a multidisciplinary genitourinary oncology team, since these are exactly the features DeVita uses to define malignant risk in this tumour type.
- Suspected large cell calcifying subtype, especially if bilateral or multifocal, warrants referral for genetics and syndrome evaluation given its recognised association with Carney complex and Peutz-Jeghers syndrome (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## What DeVita does not cover for this disease

DeVita's section on Sertoli cell tumour is brief and focused on diagnosis, the WHO histologic subtype list, orchiectomy as treatment, and the pathological predictors of metastatic risk. It does not describe a specific chemotherapy regimen, radiotherapy protocol, retroperitoneal lymph node dissection technique, or a defined surveillance imaging schedule for this tumour, and no such specifics have been invented here; where general oncology practice is described above to fill these gaps, it is labelled accordingly.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
