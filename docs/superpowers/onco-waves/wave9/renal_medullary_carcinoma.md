# Renal medullary carcinoma (RMC) — Management

## Sourcing note
DeVita's chapter on renal cell carcinoma lists renal medullary carcinoma only inside a summary
classification table (associated with sickle cell trait, aggressive, lethal within about a year,
young age, male predominance) and does not carry a dedicated management discussion for this
entity. All treatment-approach content below is therefore marked as general oncology standard
rather than attributed to DeVita, except where noted.

## Overview
Renal medullary carcinoma is a rare, highly aggressive collecting-duct-derived kidney cancer that
occurs almost exclusively in people with sickle cell trait (or other sickling haemoglobinopathies),
typically at a young age (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
Because most patients present with locally advanced or metastatic disease and the tumour behaves
very differently from clear cell renal cell carcinoma, management is intensive, multidisciplinary,
and time-sensitive from the point of diagnosis.

## Treatment approach by stage and intent

- **Localised or locally advanced disease (no distant metastases):** surgical resection (radical
  nephrectomy with regional lymphadenectomy) is the mainstay for anatomically resectable disease,
  but because of the very high rate of occult micrometastatic spread at presentation, perioperative
  (neoadjuvant and/or adjuvant) systemic chemotherapy is generally incorporated rather than surgery
  alone (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Metastatic disease at diagnosis (the majority of patients):** systemic chemotherapy is the
  primary treatment intent, with surgery to the primary tumour and/or metastasis-directed local
  therapy considered selectively in patients who respond well, to consolidate disease control
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Because of its rarity and the poor outcomes with conventional therapy, treatment decisions should
  be made by a uro-oncology multidisciplinary team, and enrollment in a clinical trial should be
  actively pursued at every stage of disease when available (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).

## Lines of systemic therapy

- **First line:** intensive combination cytotoxic chemotherapy is the standard approach; platinum-
  based combination regimens (a platinum agent paired with other cytotoxic classes) form the backbone
  of most reported treatment, reflecting the tumour's origin as a poorly differentiated, aggressive
  carcinoma rather than a typical clear cell renal cancer (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Targeted therapy used in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (anti-angiogenic and mTOR-pathway
  agents) has limited and inconsistent activity in renal medullary carcinoma**, so these agents are
  not considered a reliable substitute for cytotoxic chemotherapy in this tumour (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Subsequent lines:** on progression, further cytotoxic combinations or single agents may be
  tried, and given the limited evidence base for any specific regimen, referral for clinical trial
  participation is preferred over empiric sequencing of standard agents whenever a trial is
  accessible (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Investigational approaches directed at the tumour's defining molecular lesion, loss of the
  SMARCB1 (INI1) chromatin-remodelling protein, are an area of active clinical trial interest but
  are not established standard-of-care therapy (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section
  on this disease).

## Role of surgery

- Radical nephrectomy has a role in patients with resectable local disease and can be used to debulk
  or consolidate disease in selected patients who respond to systemic therapy, but surgery alone is
  not considered curative given the tumour's propensity for early micrometastatic spread (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Resection or ablation of isolated, symptomatic, or oligometastatic sites may be considered case by
  case as part of a multimodal plan, again in the context of multidisciplinary decision-making
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of radiotherapy

- Radiotherapy is used mainly in the palliative setting, for control of pain or other local symptoms
  from metastatic deposits (for example bone metastases), rather than as a curative-intent modality
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Supportive and disease-specific measures

- Confirm the underlying sickling haemoglobinopathy (sickle solubility testing and haemoglobin
  electrophoresis) and provide supportive haematological care aimed at reducing medullary sickling
  stress, since the tumour arises in this specific physiological context (general oncology standard,
  not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Offer genetic counselling and family sickle cell screening given the strong association with
  sickling haemoglobinopathies (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).
- Provide early palliative and supportive care alongside active treatment, including symptom control
  and management of tumour burden, because of the generally short survival and high symptom burden
  of advanced disease (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Monitor with periodic cross-sectional imaging (CT of chest, abdomen and pelvis) to assess response
  to systemic therapy and detect progression, at intervals guided by clinical status and treatment
  response rather than a fixed disease-specific schedule (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).
- Monitor renal function and overall tumour burden markers as clinically indicated, particularly in
  patients with sickling haemoglobinopathies who may have baseline renal impairment (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Refer promptly to a uro-oncology multidisciplinary team as soon as the diagnosis is suspected or
  confirmed, given the rapid pace of progression typical of this tumour (general oncology standard,
  not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer to a centre with access to clinical trials in rare or aggressive renal cancers, since
  outcomes with standard therapy remain poor and trial-based approaches are preferred where feasible
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer for haematology input to confirm and manage the underlying sickle cell trait or disease, and
  for genetic counselling of the patient and family (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
