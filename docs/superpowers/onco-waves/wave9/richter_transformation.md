# Richter transformation — management

## Confirming the diagnosis before any treatment decision
Richter transformation is the evolution of chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL) or small lymphocytic lymphoma into a high grade lymphoma, most commonly of the diffuse large B-cell type and less commonly of classic Hodgkin histology. Onset is heralded by fever, weight loss, a rapidly rising lactate dehydrogenase, and asymmetric rapid lymph node enlargement. Because the transformed clone can be patchy within otherwise typical CLL nodal disease, a PET scan showing a node with a high standardized uptake value (uptake that is usually low in plain CLL) helps identify the "hot" site to target for biopsy. Transformation must be confirmed by histology before treatment is planned; clinical suspicion alone is not sufficient and imaging changes are not a substitute for tissue diagnosis.

## Treatment approach by clinical context
The treatment history and clonal origin of the transformed disease materially change the approach and the expected outcome.

- **Richter transformation arising in a previously treated CLL patient** is the more common scenario and carries a poor prognosis. There is no single standard chemoimmunotherapy regimen for this group. The realistic treatment objective is to achieve good disease control, ideally a remission, and then move a fit, responding patient to allogeneic stem cell transplantation to try to achieve durable disease control, since allogeneic transplant is the modality most likely to be curative in this setting.
- **Richter transformation arising in a treatment-naive CLL patient** is rare but carries a substantially better prognosis. This clinical pattern raises the possibility that the aggressive lymphoma is a separate, clonally unrelated neoplasm rather than a direct evolution of the CLL clone. In this situation the transformed lymphoma can potentially be cured with intensive chemoimmunotherapy regimens, for example a hyperfractionated cyclophosphamide/vincristine/doxorubicin/dexamethasone-type regimen, an oxaliplatin/fludarabine/cytarabine/rituximab-type regimen, or an anthracycline-based rituximab-containing regimen (R-CHOP-type). Even when the lymphoma component responds well, the underlying CLL is expected to eventually relapse and needs its own follow-up plan.
- **Hodgkin-type transformation** is a distinct histology within Richter transformation. ## Lines of therapy
- **First line**: induction chemoimmunotherapy chosen by clonal context as above, aiming for the best attainable remission.
- **Consolidation in responders**: allogeneic stem cell transplantation is described as potentially curative for CLL and for Richter transformation and is the preferred consolidation for a fit patient who has achieved disease control, particularly when the transformation arose after prior CLL treatment.
- **Later lines / refractory disease**: DeVita's Richter-transformation discussion does not lay out a defined second-line regimen. Given the generally poor response to further chemoimmunotherapy in this setting, enrolment in clinical trials of novel targeted or cellular therapies is a reasonable route to discuss with the patient, but this is (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease) rather than a claim drawn from that section.

## Role of surgery and radiotherapy
DeVita's discussion of Richter transformation does not describe a role for surgery or for radiotherapy in this disease, and none should be inferred from it. Splenectomy is discussed elsewhere in the same chapter for CLL-associated immune cytopenias (autoimmune hemolytic anaemia, immune thrombocytopenia) after corticosteroid failure, not for Richter transformation itself, so it is not part of this management pathway.

## Monitoring
- Track lactate dehydrogenase trend, B symptoms, and the pace of lymph node growth in any CLL patient, since a steep LDH rise with new B symptoms and asymmetric rapid adenopathy is the pattern that should trigger a transformation work-up.
- Use PET to localize the most metabolically active site for biopsy rather than sampling the most accessible or largest node, since transformed disease can be patchy.
- Patients being treated with a BTK inhibitor for CLL should be followed with this pattern in mind: transformation is one of the recognised reasons for BTK-inhibitor discontinuation, with a reported median time to discontinuation for transformation in the range of about 9 to 12 months of therapy.

## When to refer
Any CLL or SLL patient with a rapidly enlarging or asymmetric node, new B symptoms, or a rising LDH should be referred promptly for PET-directed biopsy to rule out transformation, given how strongly the outcome depends on prompt histological confirmation and treatment. Once transformation is confirmed, referral to a haematology-oncology service capable of coordinating chemoimmunotherapy and, for responders, allogeneic stem cell transplantation is appropriate given the poor prognosis and narrow window in which consolidation can be pursued.
