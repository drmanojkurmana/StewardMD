## 1. SAFETY — PASS
No unsafe, misleading, or absolute directives. Management is correctly phased
(acute self-limited vs late proctopathy) and conservative-first. Key safety
guards are present and correct:
- Fragile irradiated tissue: deep biopsy / cautery avoided due to fistula risk
  (lines 28-30) — this is the critical safety point and it is stated well.
- New/progressive bleeding is explicitly routed to exclude recurrent/new pelvic
  malignancy, IBD, infective and ischemic proctitis before attributing to
  radiation (lines 32-34, 49) — prevents the dangerous anchoring error of
  labelling a tumor bleed as radiation injury.
- Treatment-continuation/break decision deferred to the treating radiation
  oncologist (line 12); refractory bleeding escalation ladder (topical → APC →
  HBOT) and surgical referral thresholds are appropriately hedged.
No harm risk if a clinician follows this narrative.

## 2. GROUNDING — PASS
Treatment claims are consistent with DeVita 12th ed / NCCN standard of care:
acute supportive care (diet, loperamide/diphenoxylate-atropine), topical steroid
suppositories, argon plasma coagulation for telangiectatic bleeding, hyperbaric
oxygen for refractory disease, rectal spacer at prostate-RT planning, and the
brachytherapy-boost toxicity trade-off. Every claim not in DeVita's proctitis
section (sucralfate, formalin, iron-deficiency workup, DDx exclusion list,
surgical-referral caution, endoscopic follow-up, treatment-break line) is
explicitly relabelled "general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
this disease." No regimen fabricated; no claim mis-attributed to DeVita.

## 3. DOSE-FREE — PASS
No mg, mg/m2, AUC, Gy/cGy, %, or numbered fraction/session/cycle schedule.
Timeframes (6 weeks, 2-6 months, 3/5 years) and toxicity grade ("grade 3 or
higher") are severity/temporal descriptors, not doses. DeVita's leakable numbers
(HBOT atmospheres/sessions, ASCENDE-RT % toxicity increases) are correctly
omitted/qualitative.

## 4. SCOPE — PASS
Framed as decision-support, not a directive: joint decisions with the radiation
oncologist, clear referral triggers, and repeated "exclude other causes"
hedging. Sits alongside, not replacing, management of the underlying cancer.
