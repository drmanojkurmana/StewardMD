# Hibernoma - Management

## Nature of the disease and treatment intent

Hibernoma is a rare, slow-growing, benign neoplasm of brown adipose tissue. DeVita describes it as arising most often within the thorax, though tumours of the trunk, retroperitoneum, and extremities are also reported, and the literature is dominated by case reports rather than trials. Because the lesion is entirely benign with no capacity for local aggression or metastasis, there is no "stage" in the sarcoma sense and no systemic oncology treatment intent applies. The entire management question is a local, surgical one.

## Diagnosis before treatment

Before committing to excision, the diagnosis should be secured because hibernoma can mimic malignancy on functional imaging:

- Cross-sectional imaging (MRI preferred) to characterise the fatty mass and its vascularity and to define the compartment for surgical planning (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Awareness that hibernomas are often intensely FDG-avid on PET due to their high mitochondrial content; this avidity is expected and should not, by itself, trigger a work-up for malignancy or a more aggressive treatment plan (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Image-guided core biopsy when the diagnosis is not confidently made on imaging alone, to distinguish hibernoma from lipoma, atypical lipomatous tumour/well-differentiated liposarcoma, or other fatty/vascular lesions before proceeding to surgery (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Treatment approach

- Complete surgical excision is the definitive and, per DeVita, generally curative treatment for hibernoma.
- Because DeVita's text on hibernoma is limited to a short descriptive paragraph, it does not specify a margin standard; a marginal (rather than wide) excision is generally accepted as adequate given the tumour's benign, non-infiltrative biology (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Surgeons should anticipate that these lesions are vascular, since a related brown-fat physiology drives the metabolic and blood-flow characteristics that make them PET-avid; planning for intraoperative bleeding is a reasonable precaution (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). This point is not explicitly stated in the DeVita paragraph itself.
- There is no defined role for radiotherapy or systemic (chemotherapy or targeted) therapy in hibernoma. DeVita's section does not mention any adjuvant treatment, consistent with the lesion's benign nature; no such therapy should be inferred or offered.
- There are no lines of therapy to sequence, since hibernoma is not a malignant, progressive, or recurrent disease requiring escalation of treatment.

## Monitoring and follow-up

DeVita states that excision is generally curative and does not describe a specific surveillance schedule. In practice, this supports routine post-operative wound and clinical follow-up only, without the imaging surveillance protocols used for sarcomas (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). If a mass recurs after excision, or if imaging or histology shows features atypical for hibernoma, the diagnosis itself should be reconsidered rather than assuming aggressive hibernoma biology, since malignant transformation or true recurrence is not an expected behaviour of this tumour.

## When to refer

- Refer to a soft tissue surgical oncologist or an orthopaedic/general surgeon experienced with deep or vascular soft tissue masses when the lesion is large, deep-seated, retroperitoneal, mediastinal, or in a location where excision carries meaningful risk to adjacent structures (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer for sarcoma-pathway review (imaging plus biopsy) whenever a fatty mass cannot be confidently distinguished from atypical lipomatous tumour/well-differentiated liposarcoma or another fat-containing sarcoma before any excision is planned, given that DeVita situates hibernoma within a broader differential of adipocytic tumours in the same chapter section.

## What DeVita does not address

DeVita's dedicated text on hibernoma is a brief descriptive paragraph and does not discuss staging, systemic therapy, radiotherapy, specific surgical margins, or a follow-up interval. These gaps are noted above rather than filled with invented detail.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
