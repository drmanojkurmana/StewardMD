# Hereditary diffuse gastric cancer (HDGC) - Management

Hereditary diffuse gastric cancer is a rare autosomal-dominant syndrome, with an average age of
onset around 37 years, characterised by diffuse-type (signet-ring cell) tumours, late-stage
presentation, and poor prognosis. Germline pathogenic variants in CDH1, the gene encoding the
cell-adhesion protein E-cadherin, are found in roughly 30 to 50 percent of HDGC cases, so a
substantial minority of clinically-diagnosed families do not have an identifiable CDH1 variant
(general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease: a smaller share of the
remainder is attributable to CTNNA1). Management is organised around risk stratification and
risk-reduction in unaffected carriers rather than around cytotoxic treatment of an established
tumour, because the infiltrative, poorly-cohesive growth pattern of diffuse-type disease is
frequently invisible on endoscopy and can be missed on random biopsy.

## Who should be evaluated and tested

Genetic counselling and germline testing should be offered when a patient or family meets any of
the following (per consensus criteria cited by DeVita):
- Two gastric cancers in a family with at least one confirmed diffuse-type cancer.
- Diffuse gastric cancer diagnosed before age 50.
- A personal or family history of diffuse gastric cancer together with lobular breast cancer, with
  one of the two diagnosed before age 70.
- Diffuse gastric cancer in a patient of Maori ethnicity, or a personal/family history of cleft
  lip/palate.
- Bilateral lobular breast cancer diagnosed before age 70.

Testing is germline CDH1 sequencing and deletion/duplication analysis (general oncology standard,
not from DeVita's section on this disease: multigene panel testing, sometimes including CTNNA1, is
used when CDH1 is negative but suspicion remains). Cascade testing should be offered to at-risk
relatives once a pathogenic variant is confirmed in a proband, and results (including variants of
uncertain significance) should be interpreted cautiously.

## Management of confirmed CDH1 carriers without an established cancer

- **Prophylactic (risk-reducing) total gastrectomy is recommended for CDH1 mutation carriers
  between ages 18 and 40**, reflecting the unreliability of endoscopic surveillance for this
  infiltrative, often submucosal disease. Prophylactic gastrectomy specimens frequently show
  otherwise-occult foci of early signet-ring cell carcinoma even when preoperative endoscopy was
  normal.
- For carriers who decline or wish to defer surgery, upper endoscopy with multiple systematic
  (random) biopsies should be performed every 6 to 12 months, with the explicit understanding that
  this surveillance has limited sensitivity for diffuse-type disease and is not an equivalent
  substitute for gastrectomy.
- Because CDH1 also confers an autosomal-dominant predisposition to lobular breast cancer, female
  carriers need enhanced breast surveillance (NCCN CDH1-carrier standard, not from DeVita's section
  on this disease): annual breast MRI and clinical breast examination are the standard approach,
  with risk-reducing mastectomy discussed as an option given this risk.
- Decision-making about the timing and acceptance of prophylactic gastrectomy should occur in a
  multidisciplinary setting (upper gastrointestinal surgery, clinical genetics, dietetics, and
  psychology), given the major, permanent nutritional and lifestyle consequences of total
  gastrectomy.

## Postoperative and ongoing care

- After total gastrectomy, patients need structured nutritional follow-up (general post-gastrectomy
  standard, not HDGC-specific in DeVita): dietitian support, and supplementation for vitamin B12,
  iron, calcium, and fat-soluble vitamins, with adaptation to small, frequent meals.
- Ongoing surveillance in carriers who have not yet undergone gastrectomy, and breast surveillance
  in female carriers, continues indefinitely as part of long-term follow-up.
- Family members should remain engaged with genetic counselling for cascade testing as the family
  structure evolves (new relatives reaching an age for testing, reproductive counselling, etc.).

## When a diffuse gastric cancer is already present

DeVita's discussion of HDGC in this section is centred on the hereditary/previvor pathway (testing
criteria, gene, and prophylactic surgery) rather than on systemic treatment of established diffuse
gastric cancer in a CDH1 carrier; a CDH1-associated cancer that has already declared itself should
be staged and treated as gastric adenocarcinoma of diffuse histology using the same
surgical/multimodal and systemic-therapy framework used for sporadic gastric cancer (general
oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease), i.e. surgical resection for localised
disease, perioperative or adjuvant chemotherapy (and chemoradiotherapy in appropriate settings) for
locally advanced disease, and a platinum-based doublet or comparable systemic regimen with
consideration of targeted or immune-modulating agents based on tumour biomarkers for metastatic
disease. DeVita's text does not describe HDGC-specific modifications to systemic therapy once
cancer is established, so no disease-specific claim beyond standard gastric cancer management is
made here.

## When to refer

- Any patient meeting the family-history or age-of-onset criteria above should be referred for
  clinical genetics evaluation and counselling before testing.
- Confirmed CDH1 (or CTNNA1) carriers should be referred to a centre with experience in
  prophylactic total gastrectomy and multidisciplinary HDGC management, given the complexity of the
  decision and the surgery's consequences.
- Female carriers should be referred for breast surveillance/high-risk breast clinic care in
  parallel with gastric risk management.
- Patients with an established diffuse gastric cancer should be referred through the standard
  gastric cancer multidisciplinary pathway (surgical oncology, medical oncology, and, where
  relevant, radiation oncology).

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
NCCN Guidelines (Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Colorectal, Endometrial, and Gastric).
