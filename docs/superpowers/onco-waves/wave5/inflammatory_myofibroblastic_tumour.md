# Inflammatory Myofibroblastic Tumour (IMT) — Management Expansion

Also known as inflammatory pseudotumour and plasma cell granuloma. Predominantly affects
patients under 40. ALK gene rearrangements (fused to a range of N-terminal partners) are
present in roughly 50 to 60 percent of cases; the inflammatory component of the tumour does
not itself carry the ALK fusion. A subset of ALK-negative lesions instead carries ROS1 or NTRK3
rearrangements, and rarely RET or PDGFRB. RANBP2- or RRBP1-ALK fusions define a more
aggressive subset termed epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma.

## Treatment approach by presentation and intent

**Localised, resectable disease**
Surgical resection is the primary treatment and is diagnostic as well as therapeutic, since
preoperative diagnosis is often difficult to confirm. When complete resection is achievable it is
curative in approximately 75 percent of cases. Local recurrence is more common than distant
spread; metastasis occurs in under 5 percent of patients.

**Locally advanced or unresectable disease**
- If ALK-positive: systemic treatment is guided by ALK fusion status. ALK-targeted kinase
  inhibition (crizotinib) has produced RECIST-defined responses in ALK-positive advanced IMT.
  Crizotinib can also act against ROS1 and may be effective in a subset of patients with ROS
  translocations.
- Case reports describe responses to other ALK inhibitors (brigatinib, ceritinib, lorlatinib) in
  patients who progress on or acquire resistance to crizotinib, though this evidence is at the
  case-report level rather than from controlled trials.
- If ALK-negative: treatment intent is palliative, since few of these tumours have metastatic
  potential and the disease is rarely fatal (reported 5-year survival around 87 percent).
- (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease) Where a tumour board
  judges pre-operative shrinkage would improve resectability, a trial of the matched kinase
  inhibitor (ALK, ROS1, NTRK, or RET/PDGFRB agent as dictated by the fusion found) before
  reattempting surgery is a reasonable strategy, but this specific neoadjuvant sequencing is not
  described in DeVita's IMT section itself.

## Lines of therapy

1. Complete surgical excision, whenever anatomically feasible, regardless of ALK status.
2. For unresectable, recurrent, or metastatic ALK-positive disease: ALK-directed kinase
   inhibition (crizotinib first; brigatinib, ceritinib, or lorlatinib reported on progression).
3. For ALK-negative disease with an alternative driver fusion (ROS1, NTRK3, RET, PDGFRB):
   (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease) the corresponding
   targeted kinase inhibitor may be considered, though DeVita's IMT section only names
   crizotinib's activity against ROS1 translocations specifically and does not detail agents for
   NTRK3, RET, or PDGFRB-driven IMT.
4. For ALK-negative disease without an actionable fusion or where kinase inhibition is not
   applicable: palliative, symptom-directed management, reflecting the generally indolent,
   rarely fatal course.

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy

- Surgery is the cornerstone of curative-intent treatment; DeVita's section does not describe a
  defined role for radiotherapy in IMT, so no RT recommendation is made here.
- Systemic therapy is reserved for disease that is not surgically resectable, or that recurs after
  resection, and is directed by molecular fusion testing rather than given empirically.
- Because size and location can cause compressive symptoms (for example bowel obstruction in
  abdominal IMT), the anatomic site and mass effect, not tumour grade alone, often drive the
  urgency of intervention.

## Monitoring

- Watch for the paraneoplastic inflammatory syndrome (fever, growth failure or weight loss,
  malaise, anaemia, thrombocytosis), reported in roughly a third of patients; resolution of these
  features after resection is a useful marker that the tumour has been adequately treated, and
  their return should prompt evaluation for recurrence.
- Local recurrence is more common than metastasis, so follow-up should prioritise site-directed
  imaging surveillance of the original tumour bed over routine distant staging.
- The epithelioid inflammatory myofibroblastic sarcoma variant (RANBP2/RRBP1-ALK fusion) has
  a more aggressive course and warrants closer surveillance than typical ALK-positive IMT.

## When to refer

- (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease) Any suspected or
  confirmed IMT should be referred to a sarcoma multidisciplinary team before definitive
  surgery is planned, given the diagnostic difficulty and the anatomic sites involved (thoracic
  and abdominal cavities).
- Refer for molecular fusion testing (ALK, and if ALK-negative, ROS1/NTRK3/RET/PDGFRB) before
  starting systemic therapy in any unresectable, recurrent, or metastatic case, since treatment
  selection in DeVita's section is explicitly fusion-driven.
- Refer for medical oncology input whenever resection is incomplete or not possible, or if
  epithelioid/aggressive histology is identified on pathology.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
