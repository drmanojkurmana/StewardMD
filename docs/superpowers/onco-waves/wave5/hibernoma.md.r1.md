## 1. Safety
No unsafe, misleading, or harmful statements. The narrative correctly frames hibernoma as
entirely benign (no local aggression, no metastasis) and derives management from that biology.
The safest and most important clinical point - that a recurrence or atypical histology should
prompt reconsidering the diagnosis rather than assuming "aggressive hibernoma" - is present and
correct. No absolute directive that could cause harm if followed. No overstep of decision-support
into definitive diagnosis or a fixed order.

## 2. Grounding (DeVita / NCCN standard of care)
Consistent with standard of care:
- Benign brown adipose neoplasm, thorax-predominant, case-report literature - correct.
- Intense FDG avidity on PET expected (high mitochondrial content) and not by itself a trigger for
  malignancy work-up - correct and clinically valuable.
- Complete surgical excision as definitive/curative treatment - correct.
- No defined role for radiotherapy or systemic/targeted therapy - correct.
- Core biopsy to distinguish from lipoma / atypical lipomatous tumour / well-differentiated
  liposarcoma before excision - correct and appropriately cautious.
No fabricated or outdated regimen.
