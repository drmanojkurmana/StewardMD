# Intravascular large B-cell lymphoma (IVLBCL) - Management

## Overview and diagnostic prerequisite

IVLBCL is a rare subtype in which lymphoma cells proliferate within small blood vessels without forming a tumor mass or producing detectable circulating tumor cells. Diagnosis is often difficult because imaging is typically unrevealing; a blind biopsy of normal-appearing skin can be diagnostic, and if that is not informative, biopsy of other sites of suspected involvement (or bone marrow, particularly in the haemophagocytic variant) may be necessary. Management cannot proceed rationally without first securing tissue confirmation, so an early, sometimes empiric, biopsy strategy is itself part of the treatment pathway.

Two clinical patterns are recognized: a Western pattern with more prominent neurologic and cutaneous involvement, and an Asian pattern associated with haemophagocytic syndrome, marrow involvement, hepatosplenomegaly, and cytopenias. A French Canadian series reported more neurologic than cutaneous presentation, so the two patterns are not absolute and should not be used to exclude the diagnosis.

## Treatment approach

- **Systemic chemoimmunotherapy is the backbone of treatment for all patients**, combining an anthracycline-based combination regimen with the anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody rituximab. Before rituximab was available, outcomes were poor, with fewer than 10% long-term survivors; with earlier diagnosis and rituximab-anthracycline-based treatment, reported 2-year progression-free survival and overall survival are approximately 56% and 66%.
- **Central nervous system-directed therapy is added for essentially all patients**, not reserved for those with overt CNS disease at presentation, because of the high risk of neurologic relapse. This is delivered as CNS prophylaxis using either intrathecal chemotherapy or high-dose systemic methotrexate.
- **Patients with secondary brain or spinal cord involvement at diagnosis** may, depending on the clinical situation, need intrathecal chemotherapy, systemic high-dose methotrexate, and/or radiation therapy directed at the involved sites.
- A single-arm phase II trial in Japan treated patients with an anthracycline-based rituximab regimen plus high-dose methotrexate and intrathecal treatment and reported a 2-year progression-free survival of 76%, at the cost of common hematologic toxicity. Within that trial, outcomes for IVLBCL appeared similar to diffuse large B-cell lymphoma treated with comparable intensity.
- There is no staging by conventional anatomic stage in the way solid tumors are staged, because the disease grows intraluminally without forming masses; treatment intent is therefore essentially uniform curative-intent combination therapy for fit patients rather than a stage-adapted approach. (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease)
- Role of surgery: surgery has no role in treating this disease; its only place is diagnostic biopsy of skin or other tissue. (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease)
- Role of radiotherapy: radiation is used selectively, directed at sites of secondary CNS or spinal involvement, rather than as a routine component of first-line therapy.

## Associated haemophagocytic syndrome (Asian-type variant)

When the Asian-type variant is present with an accompanying haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis picture, this complication needs to be identified and managed alongside the lymphoma itself, since haemophagocytic activation can independently drive cytopenias, organ dysfunction, and early mortality.

## Supportive care during treatment

- Transfusion support for anemia and thrombocytopenia.
- Tumor lysis syndrome prophylaxis and infection prophylaxis while patients receive intensive immunochemotherapy. (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease)

## Monitoring response

Response and toxicity should be tracked with serial lactate dehydrogenase (a laboratory abnormality that is characteristically elevated at diagnosis), blood counts, and interval imaging. Hematologic toxicity from combined chemoimmunotherapy and CNS-directed treatment is common and should be anticipated and monitored for proactively.

## Lines of therapy beyond first-line

DeVita's dedicated discussion of IVLBCL does not describe a specific relapsed or refractory treatment pathway for this entity. The general non-Hodgkin lymphoma chapter separately discusses relapsed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma options (including nonmyeloablative allogeneic transplantation in highly selected patients with good performance status after autologous transplant or CAR T-cell therapy, at the cost of meaningful treatment-related mortality), but these were not stated as specific to IVLBCL, so they are noted here only as background context rather than as a grounded recommendation for this disease. When outcomes are unsatisfactory with first-line rituximab-anthracycline-based therapy and CNS-directed treatment, referral to a haemato-oncology/lymphoma specialist center for consideration of intensified or transplant-based approaches on a case-by-case basis is reasonable. (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease)

## When to refer

- Any patient with a suspected diagnosis should be referred urgently to haemato-oncology given how rapidly fatal untreated disease is and how easily the diagnosis is missed.
- Patients with neurologic signs and symptoms warrant concurrent neurology input and early consideration of CNS-directed treatment.
- Patients with an Asian-type presentation and haemophagocytic features warrant critical care and haematology involvement given the risk of multi-organ dysfunction.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
