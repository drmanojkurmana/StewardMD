# Immature teratoma of the ovary — management narrative

## Overview and grounding

Immature teratoma of the ovary is a malignant germ cell tumour, one of the subtypes grouped
under malignant germ cell tumours of the ovary alongside dysgerminoma, yolk sac tumour, mixed
germ cell tumour, embryonal carcinoma, nongestational choriocarcinoma and polyembryoma.
Malignant germ cell tumours as a group account for roughly 5% of ovarian malignancies, present
at a median age in the late teens to early twenties, are almost always unilateral, and are
described as exquisitely chemosensitive. The immature teratoma forms neuroepithelial rosettes
that underpin the Norris grading system, and it can elaborate both alpha-fetoprotein and human
chorionic gonadotropin.

## Treatment approach by stage and grade

- Because malignant ovarian germ cell tumours are almost always unilateral and highly
  chemosensitive, fertility-preserving surgery is described as an option even when metastases
  are present.
- Therapy for malignant germ cell tumours of the ovary is stated to mirror, and to have been
  extrapolated from, the treatment experience in testicular germ cell cancer.
- A clear treatment split by stage and grade is given: with the exception of FIGO surgical
  stage IA dysgerminoma and stage IA, grade 1 or 2 immature teratoma, patients with malignant
  germ cell tumours of the ovary should receive adjuvant combination chemotherapy built on
  bleomycin, etoposide and cisplatin (a platinum-based combination regimen; no numeric dosing
  is given here or reproduced from the source).
- This implies that FIGO stage IA, grade 1 or 2 immature teratoma can be managed with surgery
  alone (fertility-sparing unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy plus staging) without adjuvant
  chemotherapy, while higher grade or higher stage disease is treated with adjuvant
  platinum-based combination chemotherapy.
- Surgical staging (peritoneal washing, careful inspection and biopsy of peritoneal surfaces,
  omental and nodal assessment) as the initial step for any suspected malignant ovarian germ
  cell tumour, with fertility-sparing unilateral surgery preferred over bilateral resection or
  hysterectomy given the near-universal unilaterality of these tumours, is (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Distinguishing purely mature peritoneal implants (gliomatosis peritonei) from immature
  implants when assessing extra-ovarian disease, and reserving additional treatment for
  immature deposits, is (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy and systemic therapy

- Surgery is the primary modality; DeVita's text frames fertility-preserving resection as
  standard even in the presence of metastatic disease for this tumour group.
- No role for radiotherapy in immature teratoma of the ovary is described in the section
  reviewed; unlike the radiosensitive dysgerminoma also discussed there, this histology is not
  characterised as radiosensitive, so no radiotherapy recommendation is made here.
- Systemic therapy, when indicated, is the platinum-based combination described above
  (bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin backbone), used as adjuvant treatment for higher-grade or
  higher-stage disease.
- For relapsed or refractory malignant germ cell tumours, salvage chemotherapy following
  principles extrapolated from testicular germ cell cancer, and consideration of surgical
  resection of residual masses, is (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease); DeVita's ovarian section does not itself detail a salvage regimen for this
  histology.

## Monitoring

- Serial measurement of alpha-fetoprotein and human chorionic gonadotropin, the two markers
  the section states this tumour can elaborate, to help identify a mixed germ cell component
  and to follow disease activity, is a reasonable extension of the grounded biology but the
  specific surveillance schedule is not given in the reviewed section, so no interval is
  specified here.
- Monitoring for return of menstrual function and fertility outcomes is grounded: the section
  states that for this class of ovarian malignant germ cell tumours, return of normal
  menstrual function is the rule and numerous successful pregnancies have been documented
  during follow-up of survivors, supporting the fertility-sparing approach.

  imaging intervals for this histology.

## Prognosis and when to refer

- Prognosis for this class of ovarian germ cell malignancy is described as excellent.
- Given the rarity, the reliance on treatment extrapolated from testicular cancer experience,
  and the fertility-preservation stakes in a typically young patient population, management
  within a gynaecological oncology multidisciplinary team, with fertility-preservation
  counselling before any treatment decision, is (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).
- Any rising tumour markers after treatment, or histologic evidence of growing teratoma
  syndrome, should prompt referral back to a gynaecological oncology team for reassessment and
  consideration of further surgery, since these scenarios are (general oncology standard, not
  from DeVita's section on this disease); DeVita's reviewed ovarian-cancer section does not
  describe growing teratoma syndrome for this specific tumour, though the phenomenon is
  discussed elsewhere in the source in the context of testicular germ cell tumours.
