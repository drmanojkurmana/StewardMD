## 1. SAFETY — PASS
No unsafe, misleading, or absolute directive statements found. Core acute-management sequence is correct and safe:
- Volume repletion as first priority in symptomatic HCM (correct; dehydration self-perpetuates HCM).
- Haemodialysis in renal dysfunction / fluid-overload risk instead of aggressive repletion (correct, safe).
- Calcitonin as rapid short-term bridge with explicit tachyphylaxis caveat (correct).
- Bisphosphonate/denosumab as durable control; denosumab for bisphosphonate-refractory or renal impairment (correct, safe).
- Cinacalcet correctly restricted to parathyroid-carcinoma-associated HCM and explicitly flagged as NOT a step for the common osteolytic/PTHrP forms — this negative framing prevents a plausible misuse, a safety positive.
- Corticosteroids scoped to vitamin-D/lymphoma/granulomatous mechanism (correct).
No false negative (no missed red flag): renal failure, arrhythmia, altered mental status, and refractory disease are all routed to urgent referral.

## 2. GROUNDING — PASS
Treatment claims are consistent with DeVita/NCCN standard of care. No fabricated or outdated regimen. Older agents (mithramycin/plicamycin, gallium nitrate) are correctly relegated to limited use due to toxicity, not presented as current practice. The cinacalcet "~two-thirds response in one multicenter series" is consistent with the published parathyroid-carcinoma calcimimetic data and is plausibly DeVita-sourced.
Critically, every claim that is general-standard rather than DeVita-section-specific is explicitly self-labelled ("general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section..."). No claim is mis-attributed to DeVita. This satisfies the source-discipline requirement.

## 3. DOSE-FREE — PASS
No numeric dose leaked. No mg, mg/m2, AUC, or numbered schedule. The only numerics are epidemiology/response-rate figures (10-25% incidence, ~two-thirds response), which are not dosing.

## 4. SCOPE — PASS
Appropriately hedged as decision-support: "can be used", "is an option", "warrant urgent oncology involvement", "remains first-line". No directive imperative that oversteps.

## Advisory (non-blocking)
- "calcitonin combined with glucocorticoids" for rapid control of severe HCM generally: glucocorticoid benefit is chiefly in steroid-responsive (haematologic / vitamin-D-mediated) HCM. Consider tightening to make clear the steroid component is most useful in the steroid-responsive subset rather than universal adjunct. Not unsafe; clarity only.
