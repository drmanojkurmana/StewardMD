# Dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumour (DNET / DNT) - Management narrative

FLAG: needs manual sourcing. A grep of the DeVita text for "dysembryoplastic" returned zero hits,
so this entity is not covered in the DeVita section searched. Every claim below is therefore marked
as general oncology / neuro-oncology standard rather than DeVita-sourced.

## Treatment approach by presentation and intent

DNET is a WHO grade 1 glioneuronal tumour and the treatment intent is essentially always curative
and epilepsy-directed rather than oncologic, because the lesion is benign and indolent (general
oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

- **Symptomatic lesion with drug-resistant focal epilepsy** (the typical presentation): the standard
  of care is surgical resection, with the goal of both tissue diagnosis and durable seizure control
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Incidentally discovered or clinically stable lesion without significant epilepsy**: a period of
  surveillance imaging is a reasonable alternative to immediate surgery, given the lesion's typically
  static, non-progressive natural history (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease).
- **Atypical imaging behavior** (new enhancement, growth, oedema, or a diffusion-restricting
  component): this should prompt reassessment of the diagnosis and consideration of biopsy or
  resection rather than assuming benign DNET biology (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).

## Role of surgery

Surgery is the definitive treatment. The aim is complete (gross total) resection of the cortically
based lesion, and where present, of the adjacent focal cortical dysplasia, since incomplete removal
of either component is a recognized reason for persistent postoperative seizures (general oncology
standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Because DNET commonly presents as a substrate
for chronic drug-resistant epilepsy, resection is usually planned within a formal epilepsy surgery
pathway rather than as a purely oncologic operation (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
section on this disease). Preoperative workup to localize the epileptogenic zone typically includes
video-EEG or long-term monitoring, epilepsy-protocol MRI, neuropsychological assessment, and
functional MRI when the lesion is near eloquent cortex (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
section on this disease).

## Role of radiotherapy and systemic therapy

Radiotherapy and chemotherapy have no established role in DNET. As a benign, slow-growing glioneuronal
tumour, it does not benefit from cytotoxic or radiation therapy, and using either would represent
overtreatment; this is a recognized pitfall when DNET is radiologically or histologically mistaken
for a diffuse low-grade glioma or oligodendroglioma (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
section on this disease).

## Lines of therapy

There is no line-of-therapy or systemic-regimen framework for DNET in the way there is for a
malignant tumour, because first-line management is surgical and there is no established second-line
oncologic treatment for residual or recurrent benign disease beyond further resection if clinically
indicated (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Antiseizure
medication is used as adjunctive, symptom-directed therapy before surgery and continued during
follow-up as needed, but it is not a substitute for definitive resection in drug-resistant cases
(general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Postoperative and long-term follow-up should include seizure outcome assessment and serial imaging
  surveillance, since recurrence and, very rarely, malignant transformation can occur even though the
  tumour is WHO grade 1 (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- For lesions managed conservatively (no surgery), periodic MRI is used to confirm the expected
  indolent, non-progressive course; any interval enlargement, new solid enhancing nodularity, or new
  neurological deficit should prompt re-evaluation (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).

## When to refer

Refer to a comprehensive epilepsy surgery program and multidisciplinary neuro-oncology team whenever
there is drug-resistant focal epilepsy attributable to the lesion, diagnostic uncertainty with an
alternative glial or glioneuronal tumour, atypical or evolving imaging findings, or a new
neurological deficit or signs of raised intracranial pressure (general oncology standard, not from
DeVita's section on this disease).
