# Granulosa cell tumour of the ovary — management

## Overview and staging intent

Granulosa cell tumours (GCT) are the most common malignant sex cord-stromal tumour of the ovary
(general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease), distinct from the far more
common epithelial ovarian cancers. DeVita notes that adult-type GCT is usually diagnosed in women
in their mid-50s while the juvenile type occurs in children and adolescents, though either can
occur across the age spectrum. Most patients present with early-stage disease, often unilateral (general oncology standard, not
from DeVita's section on this disease), and the overall prognosis for early-stage disease is
favourable (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease), but DeVita is
explicit that over 80 percent of patients with advanced-stage disease will die, so accurate
staging drives everything downstream.

## Surgery is the mainstay

Surgical staging and resection are the foundation of treatment for GCT at every stage:

- Comprehensive staging surgery with salpingo-oophorectomy is the standard of care (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- DeVita specifically notes that lymphadenectomy can probably be omitted from surgical staging
  of these tumours in the absence of grossly enlarged nodes, distinguishing GCT staging from
  the epithelial ovarian cancer staging algorithm.
- Fertility-preserving unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy is a reasonable option in young women
  with disease apparently confined to one ovary (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease). DeVita states this fertility-preserving approach is specifically
  favoured among young patients with the juvenile subtype.
- Because these tumours are oestrogen-secreting, the endometrium should be evaluated (endometrial
  sampling) to exclude concurrent hyperplasia or endometrial carcinoma driven by the hormone
  excess (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Systemic therapy

Systemic therapy has a role mainly in advanced, recurrent, or incompletely resected disease, but
its record in this tumour type is modest:

- DeVita states that the bleomycin-etoposide-cisplatin (BEP) combination, the regimen used for
  malignant germ cell tumours of the ovary, has demonstrable activity in GCT, but the responses
  have not been durable or long-lasting.
- A platinum-based combination is a reasonable option to offer for advanced-stage or incompletely
  resected tumours (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- For hormonally driven recurrent disease, hormonal agents such as aromatase inhibitors have been
  used given the oestrogenic biology of the tumour, though this is standard extrapolated practice
  rather than something DeVita documents specifically for GCT (general oncology standard, not
  from DeVita's section on this disease).
- DeVita does not describe a defined role for radiotherapy in GCT, and no RT-based approach
  should be presented as evidence-based for this tumour without further grounding.

## Monitoring and late relapse

- Serum inhibin (inhibin A, and occasionally inhibin B per DeVita) can assist not only in
  preoperative diagnosis but also in monitoring for recurrence after treatment.
- The characteristic biology of GCT is indolent but with a real risk of late recurrence, so
  DeVita's framing (most patients with early-stage disease are cured, but advanced disease carries
  a high mortality) supports long-term surveillance rather than a short, fixed follow-up window
  (general oncology standard extension of DeVita's point, not stated verbatim in DeVita's section
  on this disease).
- Anti-Mullerian hormone as an additional recurrence marker alongside inhibin is standard
  gynaecological oncology practice (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).

## When to refer

- Any suspected or confirmed GCT should be managed within a gynaecological oncology
  multidisciplinary team given the need for accurate surgical staging and the implications of
  understaging (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Advanced-stage disease, incomplete resection, or recurrence should prompt referral for
  consideration of systemic therapy and, where relevant, clinical trial enrolment, given the
  poor durability of existing regimens noted by DeVita.

## What DeVita does not address for this disease

DeVita's ovarian chapter discusses GCT only briefly within its section on uncommon and rare
ovarian tumours, and does not give a detailed staging-by-stage treatment algorithm, specific
second-line regimens, or numeric outcome data beyond the single advanced-disease mortality
statistic above. Claims about endometrial sampling, aromatase inhibitor use in relapse, AMH
monitoring, and multidisciplinary referral are standard gynaecological oncology practice and are
labelled as such above rather than attributed to DeVita.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
