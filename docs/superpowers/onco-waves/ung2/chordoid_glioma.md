# Chordoid glioma of the third ventricle - management

## Sourcing note

DeVita's text was searched for "chordoid glioma" and related synonyms. The only hit is a single
passing mention of chordoid glioma of the third ventricle in a list of subependymal/intraventricular
tumour types that can cause CSF flow obstruction (in a chapter on increased intracranial pressure in
cancer patients). DeVita does not contain a dedicated management discussion for this tumour. The
narrative below is therefore built on general neuro-oncology and WHO-classification-consistent
management principles, not on a DeVita management section for this disease. Every clinical claim
below is marked accordingly. FLAG: needs manual sourcing against a dedicated neuro-oncology/WHO CNS
tumour reference for full management detail.

## Overview

Chordoid glioma is a rare, slow-growing glial tumour (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
section on this disease) that arises characteristically in the anterior third ventricle, close to the
hypothalamus and optic chiasm. Its management is guided principally by its location rather than by a
high or aggressive histological grade, since it behaves as a low-grade (CNS WHO grade 2) lesion
(general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Because DeVita's own text on
this entity does not extend beyond noting it as a cause of CSF pathway obstruction, the treatment
approach described here should be treated as guideline-general rather than as a synthesis of a DeVita
management discussion.

## Treatment approach by presentation and intent

- **Newly diagnosed, surgically accessible tumour causing mass effect, hydrocephalus, or
  visual/hypothalamic compromise**: the primary and generally only established treatment intent is
  maximal safe surgical resection, aiming for gross total removal when this can be achieved without
  unacceptable risk to the hypothalamus and optic apparatus (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease). Surgery is both diagnostic (histology and PRKCA-mutation testing)
  and therapeutic.
- **Subtotal resection or tumour left in situ because of adherence to hypothalamic structures**: given
  its low proliferative potential but tendency to recur when incompletely removed, adjuvant radiotherapy
  is considered for subtotally resected disease, and again should any residual or recurrent tumour show
  further growth (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). There is no
  established role for cytotoxic chemotherapy or targeted systemic therapy in chordoid glioma, and none
  should be inferred from this narrative.
- **Recurrent disease**: options are re-resection where feasible, or radiotherapy if not already given
  or if further growth occurs after prior irradiation, decided within a neuro-oncology/skull base
  multidisciplinary team (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Unresectable or high-risk lesions in poor surgical candidates**: a more conservative approach
  favouring biopsy plus radiotherapy, or close observation with serial imaging, may be considered when
  the surgical risk to the hypothalamus is judged prohibitive (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).

## Lines of therapy

There is no established sequence of "lines of therapy" analogous to systemic oncology regimens for
this tumour. The realistic sequence is:

1. Maximal safe resection at diagnosis.
2. Adjuvant radiotherapy if resection is subtotal, or at the time of confirmed recurrence/regrowth.
3. Further surgery for accessible recurrence, particularly if it is causing new mass effect or
   hydrocephalus.

(general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, for all of the above.)

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy

- **Surgery** is the definitive treatment. The transcallosal or transcortical approach to the third
  ventricle is used to access the lesion, but the deep location adjacent to the hypothalamus, fornix,
  and optic pathways often prevents complete removal and carries real risk of postoperative
  hypothalamic and endocrine injury (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).
- **Radiotherapy** (typically fractionated external-beam radiotherapy or stereotactic radiosurgery to
  the tumour bed or residual disease) is reserved for subtotally resected or recurrent tumours rather
  than given routinely after complete resection (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section
  on this disease). No specific dose or fractionation schedule is given here; those live in structured
  protocol templates.
- **Systemic therapy** has no defined role in chordoid glioma. No chemotherapy or targeted agent should
  be presented as standard for this tumour; this narrative deliberately omits any such claim because it
  is not grounded.

## Monitoring

- Surveillance brain MRI at intervals to detect recurrence or progression of residual tumour (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Serial formal visual field and visual acuity assessment given the proximity to the optic chiasm
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Ongoing anterior pituitary and hypothalamic endocrine evaluation, since preoperative or
  postoperative hypothalamic-pituitary dysfunction (including diabetes insipidus) is common and may
  evolve over time (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Clinical monitoring for signs of raised intracranial pressure or recurrent hydrocephalus, since
  DeVita specifically lists this tumour among intraventricular lesions that can obstruct CSF flow
  early in their course (this specific point is grounded in DeVita's text).

## When to refer

- Any suspected third-ventricular/suprasellar mass with visual, endocrine, or hydrocephalus features
  should be referred promptly to a neurosurgical service with third-ventricular/skull-base experience,
  ideally within a neuro-oncology multidisciplinary team, given the surgical complexity and morbidity
  risk of this location (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer to endocrinology for perioperative and long-term management of hypothalamic-pituitary
  dysfunction and diabetes insipidus (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).
- Refer to radiation oncology when resection is subtotal or when recurrence occurs, to evaluate
  adjuvant or salvage radiotherapy (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).
- Refer to ophthalmology for baseline and serial visual field/acuity assessment given chiasmal
  proximity (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
