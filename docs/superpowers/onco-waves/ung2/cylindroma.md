# Cylindroma - Management

FLAG: needs manual sourcing. Cylindroma (dermal cylindroma, turban tumour, Spiegler tumour) was
not found in DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.,
which is organised around malignant disease and does not carry a dedicated section on this benign
adnexal skin tumour. Every claim below is therefore marked as general oncology/dermatology
standard and none should be attributed to DeVita.

## Overview

Cylindroma is a benign cutaneous adnexal neoplasm arising from apocrine/eccrine-lineage cells,
most often on the scalp or head and neck (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
this disease). It occurs sporadically as a solitary nodule, or as multiple lesions in the setting
of CYLD-related Brooke-Spiegler syndrome, where confluent scalp nodules can form the classic
"turban tumour" (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Malignant
transformation to cylindrocarcinoma is rare but recognised (general oncology standard, not from
DeVita's section on this disease).

## Treatment approach by presentation

- **Solitary, sporadic lesion:** complete surgical excision is definitive and curative; no
  systemic therapy or radiation is indicated (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section
  on this disease).
- **Multiple or confluent lesions (Brooke-Spiegler syndrome / turban tumour):** staged surgical
  excision with scalp reconstruction (local flaps or skin grafting) for extensive disease; carbon
  dioxide laser or electrosurgical ablation can be used for numerous small lesions where wide
  excision of every nodule is impractical (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease). There is no established role for radiotherapy or systemic anticancer therapy in
  benign cylindroma.
- **Suspected malignant transformation (cylindrocarcinoma):** a lesion with rapid growth,
  ulceration, bleeding, or pain should be re-biopsied/re-excised and managed as a cutaneous
  adnexal malignancy, which may require wider surgical margins and multidisciplinary input;
  DeVita does not cover this entity specifically, so any systemic or radiotherapy decisions for a
  confirmed cylindrocarcinoma should follow general cutaneous adnexal carcinoma practice rather
  than a cylindroma-specific protocol (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease).

## Lines of therapy

There are no defined "lines of therapy" for benign cylindroma in the way there are for malignant
tumours. Surgery is both first and, in most cases, only line of treatment. Repeat excision or
ablation is used for recurrent or new lesions rather than escalating to a different treatment
modality (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy

- **Surgery** is the mainstay and is curative for solitary tumours; it is also the primary
  approach for confluent disease, staged as needed (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).
- **Radiotherapy** has no established role in benign cylindroma management.
- **Systemic therapy** (chemotherapy, targeted agents, immunotherapy) has no established role in
  benign cylindroma; it would only be considered if malignant transformation is confirmed, and
  even then should follow general adnexal-carcinoma management rather than cylindroma-specific
  guidance, since none was found in DeVita (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease).

## Monitoring

- Clinical follow-up to watch for new or recurrent nodules, particularly in syndromic patients who
  develop lesions over a lifetime (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).
- Any lesion that changes rapidly in size, ulcerates, bleeds, or becomes painful warrants prompt
  re-biopsy to exclude malignant transformation (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).
- In confirmed or suspected Brooke-Spiegler syndrome, periodic skin examination of the patient and
  first-degree relatives for cylindromas, spiradenomas, and trichoepitheliomas is reasonable
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Refer to dermatology or dermatopathology for biopsy confirmation of any new adnexal nodule.
- Refer to plastic surgery for staged excision and reconstruction when confluent scalp disease
  requires grafting or flap coverage.
- Refer for CYLD genetic counselling and testing when multiple adnexal tumours, a family history of
  similar lesions, or a turban-tumour presentation raises suspicion for Brooke-Spiegler syndrome.
- Refer urgently for re-excision and multidisciplinary oncology input if a lesion shows features
  suggestive of malignant transformation (rapid growth, ulceration, bleeding, or invasion).
