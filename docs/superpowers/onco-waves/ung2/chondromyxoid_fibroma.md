## Treatment approach by stage and intent

Chondromyxoid fibroma is a benign bone tumour, so there is no "stage" in the malignant sense and no role for systemic anticancer therapy (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

- Confirmed, asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic lesion without cortical breakthrough: definitive surgical treatment is still generally advised because of the risk of continued growth and cortical thinning, rather than watchful waiting (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Standard lesion confined to bone: intralesional curettage with bone grafting (or an equivalent filler) is the mainstay of treatment (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Lesion in an expendable bone (for example a small bone of the foot or a rib), or one that is difficult to curette adequately: en bloc excision is favoured over curettage (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Recurrent or locally aggressive disease, or a lesion where the margin achieved by curettage is felt to be inadequate: wider resection is reserved for this setting rather than used up front (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Pathological fracture through the lesion: this needs to be addressed surgically alongside definitive tumour treatment, generally with stabilisation combined with curettage or excision as appropriate to the fracture pattern (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Lines of therapy

There are no drug "lines of therapy" for chondromyxoid fibroma in the way the term is used for malignant disease. Management is procedural rather than pharmacological (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

- First-line: curettage with bone grafting is the initial approach for most lesions.
- Adjuncts used at the time of curettage to reduce local recurrence: high-speed burring of the cavity wall, and local adjuvants such as cryotherapy or a phenol wash.
- Second-line, for recurrence after curettage: repeat curettage can be considered, but a lower threshold for en bloc or wider excision is generally applied given the demonstrated tendency to recur.
- There is no established role for radiotherapy or systemic (chemotherapy or targeted) therapy in the routine management of this benign tumour.

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy

- Surgery is the entire basis of curative treatment. Curettage with grafting is preferred where feasible; en bloc excision is used in expendable bones or where curettage margins would be unreliable (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Radiotherapy has no established routine role and would only be considered in exceptional circumstances such as an unresectable lesion in a surgically inaccessible site, which is not typical for this tumour (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Systemic therapy (chemotherapy, targeted agents) has no role, since chondromyxoid fibroma is benign and does not metastasise in its usual behaviour (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Histological confirmation before definitive treatment is essential, since the hypercellular, myxoid histology of chondromyxoid fibroma can closely mimic chondrosarcoma, and clinicopathological correlation with imaging is required to avoid overtreatment or undertreatment (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- After treatment, periodic clinical and imaging follow-up (plain radiographs, supplemented by MRI or CT as needed) is used to detect local recurrence, which is more likely after incomplete curettage and in younger patients (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Malignant transformation is very rare; routine screening for metastatic disease is not indicated for a lesion behaving in a typical benign fashion (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Any suspected chondromyxoid fibroma should be referred to, or managed within, an orthopaedic oncology multidisciplinary team, given the difficulty distinguishing it from chondrosarcoma on limited biopsy material (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer for expert bone pathology review whenever the histology shows atypia that cannot be confidently called benign, or when imaging shows features worrying for a malignant cartilage tumour (rapid growth, cortical breakthrough, or an associated soft-tissue mass) (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer promptly if there is a pathological fracture through the lesion, since this changes the surgical planning and urgency (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Recurrence after prior curettage warrants re-referral to the same multidisciplinary team to reconsider the extent of surgery (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
