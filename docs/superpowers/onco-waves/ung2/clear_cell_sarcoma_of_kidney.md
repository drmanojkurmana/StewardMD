# Clear cell sarcoma of the kidney (CCSK) - management narrative

Sourcing note: the DeVita text searched for this wave contains an entry titled
"Clear Cell Sarcoma" (soft tissue chapter), which describes clear cell sarcoma
of soft tissue / "melanoma of soft parts", a tendon-and-aponeurosis tumour of
young adults with melanocytic differentiation and an EWSR1-ATF1 fusion. That
is a histologically and clinically distinct entity from clear cell sarcoma of
the kidney (CCSK), a paediatric renal tumour driven by a BCOR internal tandem
duplication or a YWHAE-NUTM2 fusion. None of that soft-tissue-chapter content
is used below because it does not describe this disease. The DeVita text did
not contain a dedicated section on CCSK of the kidney itself. All clinical
content below is therefore general paediatric oncology / renal-tumour-protocol
standard and is labelled accordingly throughout; nothing here should be read
as DeVita-sourced.

## Overall approach

CCSK is a rare paediatric renal malignancy managed within a paediatric
oncology multidisciplinary team, on a cooperative-group renal-tumour protocol
rather than an individualised regimen (general oncology standard, not from
DeVita's section on this disease). Treatment intent is curative for localised
and most metastatic presentations, combining surgery, systemic chemotherapy,
and radiotherapy, with intensity tailored to stage and, in relapse, to prior
treatment received (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
this disease).

## Surgery

- Radical nephrectomy is the surgical mainstay for resectable disease
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Upfront nephrectomy (rather than pre-operative chemotherapy) is the usual
  approach in this tumour type, in contrast to some other paediatric renal
  tumour protocols, and complete surgical staging (including regional lymph
  node sampling) is required to assign stage correctly (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Surgery for metastatic or relapsed disease (for example pulmonary
  metastasectomy) may have a role case by case within a multidisciplinary
  discussion (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).

## Systemic therapy

- Adjuvant chemotherapy is anthracycline-based and multi-agent, historically
  built around an anthracycline in combination with agents such as an
  alkylating agent and a vinca alkaloid; more intensive contemporary
  protocols also incorporate additional cytotoxic classes for higher-stage or
  higher-risk disease (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section
  on this disease).
- Chemotherapy is given for essentially all stages of CCSK, since even
  stage I disease has a meaningful relapse risk without it; duration and drug
  combination are protocol-defined and increase with stage (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Baseline and surveillance cardiac assessment (echocardiography) is required
  given cumulative anthracycline exposure (general oncology standard, not
  from DeVita's section on this disease).
- For relapsed or refractory disease, retreatment intensity is escalated
  within cooperative-group relapse protocols; there is no single agreed
  standard second-line regimen outside of trial, and enrolment in a
  relapse/refractory clinical trial should be sought where available (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Radiotherapy

- Flank or abdominal (whole-abdomen if there was tumour spill or peritoneal
  disease) radiotherapy is used according to stage and protocol, generally
  for higher-stage local disease or where surgical margins are involved
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Radiotherapy is also directed at sites of distant metastasis, particularly
  bone metastases, given the tumour's classic tropism for skeletal spread
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Staging and treatment intensity

Treatment intensity (chemotherapy duration/drug count and whether
radiotherapy is added) escalates with local stage, presence of nodal
disease, and distant metastasis; accurate surgical-pathological staging and
molecular confirmation of the BCOR internal tandem duplication or
YWHAE-NUTM2 fusion both help confirm the diagnosis and inform protocol
assignment (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
disease).

## Monitoring and surveillance

- Cross-sectional imaging surveillance of the abdomen, together with chest
  imaging, is required to detect local or pulmonary relapse during and after
  treatment (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).
- Because CCSK has a distinctive tendency to late relapse in bone and brain,
  including relapses occurring years after completing treatment,
  surveillance should include periodic skeletal imaging/bone scintigraphy and
  a low threshold for brain MRI, and should extend well beyond the usual
  follow-up window used for Wilms tumour (general oncology standard, not
  from DeVita's section on this disease).
- New bone pain, a pathological fracture, or new neurological symptoms at any
  point after treatment should prompt urgent metastatic work-up rather than
  being attributed to benign causes (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- All suspected or confirmed cases should be referred to a paediatric
  oncology centre with renal-tumour expertise for staging, multidisciplinary
  planning, and enrolment in a cooperative-group protocol or trial before any
  treatment is started (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section
  on this disease).
- Relapsed, refractory, or unusual (for example CNS-relapsed) disease should
  be discussed at a specialist multidisciplinary tumour board and considered
  for a relapse-specific trial (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).
