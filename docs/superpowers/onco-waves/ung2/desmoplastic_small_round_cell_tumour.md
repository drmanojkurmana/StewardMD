# Desmoplastic small round cell tumour (DSRCT) — management

## Overview grounding
DeVita describes DSRCT as arising in children and young adults, most often presenting with abdominal sarcomatosis (widespread peritoneal disease) rather than a single dominant mass. It notes the tumour is defined by fusion of the same 5' portion of EWSR1 seen in Ewing sarcoma to WT1, producing a chimeric protein. DeVita states plainly that prognosis is generally poor and management can be difficult, and that despite similarities to the Ewing sarcoma family, DSRCT is "rarely cured by aggressive conventional chemotherapy combined with surgical debulking," with its dismal prognosis calling for new therapies. In a review of 40 histologically proven cases cited by DeVita, 3-year overall survival was only 30%.

## Treatment approach by intent

**Localized or resectable disease (uncommon presentation).** DeVita notes surgical resection is possible when the tumour is isolated, but this is the less common scenario given the disease's tendency to disseminate across serosal surfaces at diagnosis.

**Typical presentation (disseminated intra-abdominal disease).** DeVita states patients are more commonly managed with chemotherapy followed by debulking surgery, i.e. systemic therapy first, with cytoreductive surgery pursued once disease has been reduced. Whole-abdominal or regional radiotherapy and hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy are used at some sarcoma centres as adjuncts to this multimodal strategy (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease); DeVita's own text does not describe a specific radiotherapy approach for this tumour.

**Prognostic drivers named by DeVita.** DeVita identifies gross total resection and a good response to chemotherapy (of the type used for Ewing sarcoma) as factors associated with improved overall survival, i.e. response to systemic therapy and the completeness of subsequent surgical debulking are the two levers that most affect outcome.

## Lines of therapy

- **First line:** intensive multiagent chemotherapy of the type used in Ewing sarcoma family tumours is the DeVita-grounded reference regimen class; DeVita draws this comparison explicitly ("good responses to chemotherapy, such as that used for Ewing sarcoma") without naming a specific combination or schedule. In practice this is delivered as an alternating or combined alkylator/anthracycline-based and platinum/topoisomerase-inhibitor-based multiagent protocol (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Consolidation:** cytoreductive (debulking) surgery after a chemotherapy response, per DeVita, aiming for gross total resection given its association with improved survival.
- **Relapsed/refractory disease:** DeVita does not describe a specific second-line regimen for DSRCT. It does describe the tumour's underlying biology as a rationale for targeted approaches: EWSR1-WT1 drives PDGFA overexpression (explaining the desmoplastic stroma) together with VEGFA/VEGFR2 overexpression, which DeVita states accounts for the partial responses observed with sunitinib, an antiangiogenic multi-kinase inhibitor. DeVita also notes IL2RB induction with downstream JAK/STAT and AKT/mTOR pathway activity as further potential (not yet established) therapeutic targets. A referenced clinical study (Tap et al., cited in DeVita's bibliography) evaluated an IGF-1R-targeted monoclonal antibody in a trial population that included both Ewing sarcoma family tumours and DSRCT, indicating this pathway has been explored clinically in this disease, though DeVita does not report it as an established standard.
- Beyond these DeVita-grounded points, there is no single accepted standard second-line regimen; enrollment in a clinical trial is the preferred option whenever available for relapsed disease (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

DeVita's section on this disease does not detail a specific surveillance schedule or imaging interval for DSRCT. Given the pattern of diffuse peritoneal relapse it describes, monitoring in practice relies on cross-sectional imaging of the abdomen and pelvis (and chest, given eventual pulmonary spread) at intervals following completion of therapy (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Because response to chemotherapy is one of the two DeVita-identified prognostic factors, early reassessment of response during first-line treatment is warranted to guide the timing of debulking surgery.

## When to refer

Given the rarity of the disease, the aggressiveness of the required multimodal treatment (systemic therapy plus debulking surgery, with radiotherapy or intraperitoneal therapy at some centres), and DeVita's characterisation of the disease as difficult to manage with a dismal prognosis calling for new therapies, patients should be referred early to a sarcoma-focused, high-volume centre capable of coordinating chemotherapy, complex cytoreductive surgery, and access to clinical trials of targeted agents (antiangiogenic and IGF-1R-pathway approaches, per the biology DeVita describes).
