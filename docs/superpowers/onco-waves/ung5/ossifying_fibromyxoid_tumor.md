# Ossifying fibromyxoid tumour (OFMT) - Management

## What DeVita's section on this tumour actually says

DeVita's account of OFMT is almost entirely descriptive rather than therapeutic. It confirms that
OFMT is a rare soft tissue lesion arising in the subcutaneous and muscular layers of the trunk and
extremities, slightly more common in men, with median presentation age 49 to 50 years. Lesions are
categorised as benign or malignant based on cellularity, atypia and mitotic activity, though the
rarity of the disease means that correlation between this grading and actual outcome is not firmly
established. OFMT can metastasise, most often to lung and soft tissue, and distant recurrence has
been documented even years to decades after the primary tumour was removed. Recurrent
translocations are identifiable in roughly 85% of tumours, with PHF1 rearrangements (commonly
fused to EP400, and less often to MEAF6, EPC1 or TFE3) accounting for about half of cases;
CREBBP-BCORL1 and KDM2A-WWTR1 fusions have been reported in malignant-appearing OFMT.

Notably, DeVita's OFMT passage does not itself state a preferred treatment, a systemic therapy
regimen, or a surveillance schedule for this tumour. The management content below is therefore
built from general soft tissue sarcoma principles and is labelled accordingly wherever it is not
literally supported by the OFMT passage.

## Treatment approach by stage/intent

- **Localised, resectable disease (typical or atypical OFMT):** complete surgical excision with
  negative margins is the mainstay of curative treatment (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease). Because OFMT is a soft tissue mass without an established role
  for routine neoadjuvant or adjuvant systemic therapy in this specific entity, surgery alone is
  usually definitive for typical/atypical lesions (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).
- **Malignant OFMT (increased cellularity, atypia and mitoses):** wide local excision remains the
  primary treatment. Given that DeVita records metastasis and late distant recurrence as real risks
  in this group, these patients warrant closer follow-up and a lower threshold for adjuvant
  radiotherapy after resection of high-grade or marginally-resected disease, following the general
  soft tissue sarcoma paradigm (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).
- **Incompletely excised or recurrent disease:** re-excision to achieve clear margins is preferred
  where feasible, again reflecting general soft tissue sarcoma practice rather than an OFMT-specific
  statement in DeVita (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Metastatic disease:** DeVita documents that OFMT can metastasise to lung and soft tissue, but its
  OFMT passage does not describe a specific systemic regimen for this scenario. Where systemic
  treatment is used, it follows the same broad classes applied in other soft tissue sarcomas
  (anthracycline-based regimens, alkylating agents, or other cytotoxic combinations as selected by a
  sarcoma medical oncologist), extrapolated from general soft tissue sarcoma management rather than
  grounded in DeVita's OFMT text (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).

## Lines of therapy

DeVita's OFMT section does not lay out a first-line/second-line systemic therapy sequence for this
tumour, and no such sequence is asserted here. For the rare patient with unresectable or metastatic
malignant OFMT, treatment intent and drug class selection should be individualised by a sarcoma
multidisciplinary team using general soft tissue sarcoma systemic therapy principles (general
oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease), rather than a disease-specific
protocol, because no OFMT-specific regimen is established in the literature grounded here.

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy and systemic therapy

- **Surgery** is the central and, for typical/atypical lesions, usually sufficient treatment.
- **Radiotherapy** has no OFMT-specific data in DeVita's passage; its use should follow general soft
  tissue sarcoma indications (large, deep, high-grade, or marginally resected tumours) (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Systemic therapy** has an uncertain and largely unproven role specifically in OFMT; DeVita's
  section does not mention chemotherapy, targeted therapy or any drug for this tumour at all. Any
  systemic treatment decision in malignant/metastatic OFMT should be made case-by-case in a sarcoma
  centre rather than from a standard regimen.

## Monitoring

Because DeVita explicitly notes that OFMT can recur at distant sites (lung, soft tissue) even years
to decades after the primary resection, long-term surveillance is warranted rather than a short,
fixed follow-up window. Surveillance should combine clinical examination of the operative site with
periodic cross-sectional imaging (chest imaging given the reported lung tropism), with the exact
interval and duration individualised by a sarcoma multidisciplinary team rather than dictated by a
single published schedule (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, for
the specific interval/modality choices; the rationale for long-term follow-up itself is grounded in
DeVita's statement on late distant recurrence).

## When to refer

- Any suspected or confirmed OFMT, and especially any lesion with atypical or malignant histologic
  features, should be referred to a specialist soft tissue sarcoma centre with access to molecular
  testing (PHF1 rearrangement studies) for diagnostic confirmation, as DeVita identifies these
  translocations as characteristic of the entity.
- Cases with equivocal grading, incomplete excision, radiographic concern for local recurrence, or
  evidence of metastatic spread should be discussed in a sarcoma multidisciplinary tumour board
  before further treatment (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
