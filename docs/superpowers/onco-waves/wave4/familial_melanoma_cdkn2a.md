# Familial melanoma (CDKN2A / FAMMM) — Management

Familial melanoma driven by germline CDKN2A pathogenic variants (the familial atypical multiple mole
melanoma, FAMMM, phenotype) is managed on two parallel tracks: lifelong risk-reduction and early
detection for the carrier and family, and standard stage-based oncologic treatment for any melanoma
(or CDKN2A-associated pancreatic cancer) that actually develops. The germline diagnosis changes the
intensity of surveillance and the scope of genetic counselling, but once a melanoma is diagnosed its
treatment follows the same staging-driven pathway as sporadic cutaneous melanoma.

## Confirming the syndrome and counselling

- Germline testing for CDKN2A (sequencing plus deletion/duplication analysis), with CDK4 and a
  broader multigene panel considered when CDKN2A is negative but the family history remains
  suspicious.
- Formal genetic counselling before and after testing, with cascade testing offered to first-degree
  relatives once a pathogenic variant is identified.
- Because large cohort data link CDKN2A carriage to a markedly increased odds of pancreatic
  adenocarcinoma (CDKN2A carried the highest odds ratio among the germline genes assessed in a large
  case-control series of pancreatic cancer patients), any patient presenting with pancreatic cancer
  and a personal or family history of multiple melanomas warrants germline evaluation, and conversely
  known CDKN2A carriers should be considered for pancreatic surveillance discussions, particularly in
  families where pancreatic cancer has already occurred, and counselled on smoking cessation as a
  modifiable cofactor.

## Risk reduction and surveillance (the carrier who has not yet had a melanoma, or who has finished treatment for one)

- Rigorous, ongoing photoprotection (sun avoidance, protective clothing, broad-spectrum sunscreen),
  since ultraviolet exposure and skin type modify penetrance in CDKN2A carriers.
- Structured skin self-examination plus regular total-body skin surveillance by a clinician, using
  dermoscopy and sequential/baseline photography to detect change in the numerous atypical naevi this
  syndrome produces.
- Prompt excisional biopsy of any new, changing, or atypical pigmented lesion rather than watchful
  observation, given the young age of onset and multiplicity of primaries seen in this syndrome.
- Ophthalmological review when uveal melanoma or overlapping BAP1-related features are suspected, since
  BAP1 tumour predisposition is a differential/overlapping consideration in familial melanoma
  presentations.

## Treatment once melanoma is diagnosed (staging-driven, same pathway as sporadic melanoma)

**Localized disease (clinically node-negative, stage I/II):**
- Surgical excision is the definitive treatment, with the excision margin scaled to the depth/thickness
  of the primary lesion.
- Sentinel lymph node assessment is used to stage clinically node-negative disease and guide the need
  for further nodal management.
- Adjuvant systemic therapy is not routinely used for low-risk, completely excised early-stage disease;
  decisions are individualized with the treating oncology team for higher-risk node-negative disease.

**Resected high-risk disease (stage III):**
- Adjuvant systemic therapy with a PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor is standard practice for high-risk resected
  stage III melanoma. This is grounded in phase III adjuvant data: nivolumab improved relapse-free
  survival compared with ipilimumab, and pembrolizumab improved relapse-free survival compared with
  placebo, in patients with resected high-risk stage III disease, and this evidence base established
  PD-1 blockade as standard adjuvant therapy in this setting.
- BRAF and MEK inhibitor combination therapy is an alternative adjuvant option specifically for
  BRAF-mutant resected disease; BRAF/MEK mutation testing of the tumour should be obtained to inform
  this choice.
- Neoadjuvant systemic therapy (checkpoint inhibitor given before definitive surgery/lymphadenectomy)
  is an emerging strategy in nodal disease, with pathologic response after a single pre-operative dose
  of checkpoint therapy correlating with subsequent disease-free outcome in reported experience;
  referral to a centre able to offer this approach is reasonable for bulky nodal presentations.

**Advanced/metastatic disease (stage IV or unresectable):**
- Immune checkpoint inhibition (anti-PD-1 therapy, alone or with anti-CTLA-4 combination) is a standard
  systemic option and has been established through randomized trials in previously treated and
  treatment-naive stage IV disease.
- BRAF and MEK inhibitor combination therapy is a standard alternative for BRAF V600-mutant metastatic
  melanoma; mutation status should be checked at diagnosis of advanced disease to select between the
  targeted and immunotherapy pathways (and to sequence one after the other on progression).
- Management of metastatic disease sits with medical/surgical oncology; surgery and radiotherapy retain
  a role for symptomatic, oligometastatic, or CNS disease alongside systemic therapy, but DeVita source
  material reviewed here does not detail a specific regimen sequence beyond the checkpoint-inhibitor and
  BRAF/MEK pathways above, so a specific line-of-therapy sequence for later relapses is deliberately not
  asserted here.

## Monitoring and follow-up

- Carriers who have had a melanoma continue the same lifelong dermatologic surveillance and
  photoprotection described above, since new primary melanomas are common in this syndrome.
- Follow-up after treatment for melanoma follows standard stage-appropriate oncology surveillance
  (clinical and, where indicated, radiologic follow-up) supervised by the treating oncology team.
- Pancreatic surveillance discussions and smoking-cessation counselling continue for CDKN2A carriers
  independent of melanoma status.

## When to refer

- Any suspected hereditary pattern (multiple primary melanomas, early-onset melanoma, or a family
  history combining melanoma and pancreatic cancer) to clinical genetics for germline testing and
  counselling.
- Any biopsy-confirmed melanoma to surgical/medical oncology for staging and definitive treatment.
- Node-positive or BRAF-mutant disease, or any case being considered for neoadjuvant therapy, to a
  centre with melanoma oncology and dermatologic surgery expertise.
- Suspected pancreatic cancer in a CDKN2A carrier, or any patient with pancreatic cancer plus a
  suggestive family history, for germline testing and genetics/gastrointestinal oncology referral.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
NCCN Guidelines (Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment and Cutaneous Melanoma).
