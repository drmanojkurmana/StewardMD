# Extramammary Paget Disease (EMPD) - Management

Extramammary Paget disease is an intraepithelial adenocarcinoma of apocrine-gland-bearing skin (perianal, vulvar, penoscrotal, axillary and other sites). Before a treatment plan is chosen, the two central questions are whether the disease is confined to the epidermis (in situ) or has invaded the dermis, and whether it is primary cutaneous disease or secondary to an underlying visceral adenocarcinoma (anorectal, urothelial, gynaecologic). Management is overwhelmingly surgical, and every plan should be paired with a search for an associated internal malignancy.

## Treatment approach by extent/intent

- **In situ / non-invasive disease (curative intent):** Surgical excision is the mainstay. Because EMPD spreads subclinically and is frequently multifocal, margins are often positive even after apparently adequate excision, which is the main driver of local recurrence.
- **Extensive or surgically difficult in situ disease:** Non-surgical local options exist for patients who are not good surgical candidates or where excision would be very disfiguring. Per DeVita's Extramammary Paget Disease section, topical imiquimod has been reported as an effective first-line option for primary in-situ disease in selected cases, and radiotherapy has also been reported for EMPD with some success, providing local control in retrospective series. In practice this radiotherapy option is generally reserved for patients who are unfit for, unwilling to have, or whose disease is recalcitrant to other local treatment (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Photodynamic therapy is a further option here (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Dermal invasion or nodal disease:** Once invasion is present, this behaves as an invasive adenocarcinoma of the site rather than an in-situ lesion. Surgery remains the cornerstone, with radiotherapy or systemic chemotherapy considered as adjuncts for locally advanced, node-positive, or margin-positive disease (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Secondary EMPD (epidermotropic spread from an underlying anorectal, urothelial, or other visceral adenocarcinoma):** The underlying primary drives management. Perianal Paget disease is managed surgically at the skin level; per DeVita, progression from perianal Paget disease to invasive disease occurs in approximately 5% of cases overall, and, separately, invasive cancer has been reported in up to 40% of patients whose disease is left untreated (two distinct figures for two different scenarios, not one statistic). A real minority also carry a synchronous internal cancer, so the underlying adenocarcinoma must be identified and treated according to its own site-specific staging and treatment pathway.

## Lines of therapy / modalities

- **Surgery** is first line for essentially all EMPD, whether in situ or invasive. Excision technique should account for the tendency toward subclinical extension beyond the visible lesion; the goal is clear margins, recognizing that recurrence after wide local excision is common (per DeVita, local recurrence has been reported as high as 30% to 40%) precisely because of this multifocal, subclinical spread. Per DeVita, Mohs micrographic surgery has been reported as an alternative surgical technique with lower local recurrence in retrospective series and may be considered where the expertise is available.
- **Radiotherapy** has a role as a non-surgical option for in-situ disease; per DeVita, it has been reported for EMPD with some success and has provided local control of disease in retrospective series. It is generally used in patients who are unfit for, unwilling to have, or whose disease is recalcitrant to other local treatment (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Its use as an adjunct after surgery for invasive, margin-positive, or nodal disease follows general oncology practice rather than being detailed in DeVita's section on this disease (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Topical therapy** (e.g. imiquimod) is an alternative for extensive superficial in-situ disease; per DeVita, it has been reported as an effective first-line option for primary EMPD in selected cases. **Photodynamic therapy** is a further alternative for extensive superficial disease where wide excision would be highly morbid (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Systemic therapy** is reserved for invasive, nodal, or metastatic disease, or for treating an underlying secondary adenocarcinoma; the specific systemic regimen follows the site-specific pathway for the associated cancer (e.g. anorectal or urothelial adenocarcinoma protocols) rather than a distinct "EMPD regimen" (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Long-term surveillance is required after treatment because local recurrence rates are high, driven by the subclinical multifocal nature of the disease rather than true relapse from a new primary.
- Per DeVita's Extramammary Paget Disease section, all patients should undergo colonoscopy and appropriate genitourinary malignancy screening before treatment is initiated, to rule out an internal malignancy and secondary EMPD; DeVita separately notes an association between perianal Paget disease and tubo-ovarian adenocarcinoma and gastrointestinal cancers. The same principle of directed screening for an occult internal primary is reasonably extended to site-specific modalities (e.g. urothelial evaluation for penoscrotal disease, gynaecologic evaluation for vulval disease); DeVita's EMPD text does not itself specify which imaging or endoscopic modality to use at each individual anatomic site (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Any new nodule, induration, ulceration, or growth within a previously treated or observed area should prompt re-biopsy to reassess for dermal invasion.

## When to refer

- Any patient with biopsy-confirmed EMPD should be managed by a multidisciplinary team spanning dermato-oncology/dermatologic surgery, surgical oncology, and the relevant site specialist (colorectal surgery for perianal disease, urology for penoscrotal/periurethral disease, gynaecologic oncology for vulval disease), because the surgical approach and the search for an underlying malignancy are intertwined (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer for radiation oncology and medical oncology input once dermal invasion, nodal involvement, or an associated invasive visceral adenocarcinoma is identified, since treatment then follows the invasive-cancer pathway of the relevant primary site rather than a dermatologic in-situ approach (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer promptly for colonoscopy and genitourinary malignancy screening at diagnosis in all EMPD, per DeVita's Extramammary Paget Disease section, before finalizing a local surgical plan; the specific imaging/endoscopic/urologic modality chosen at penoscrotal or vulval sites is a general oncology standard extension rather than detailed in DeVita's section on this disease.

## What DeVita is silent on (deliberately omitted)

DeVita has a dedicated Extramammary Paget Disease section (Cancer of the Skin chapter) covering incidence/etiology, clinical and pathologic features, and prognosis/management for primary genital and rare axillary EMPD, plus separate Anal Paget and Vulval Paget subsections used elsewhere in this narrative. That EMPD section does discuss surgical excision as the standard of care and Mohs micrographic surgery as an alternative technique with lower recurrence in retrospective series (used above), and it does report topical imiquimod and radiotherapy as nonsurgical/adjunctive options for primary EMPD (used above). It does not, however, detail specific systemic chemotherapy or targeted-therapy regimens, radiotherapy dose/fields/technique, or immunotherapy use in metastatic EMPD; those specifics have been omitted rather than filled in from outside general knowledge. Axillary EMPD is only briefly noted as a rare primary site without site-specific workup detail, and the specific screening modality chosen by anatomic site, the invasive-disease adjuvant framing for radiotherapy/systemic therapy, and the "systemic therapy follows the underlying primary's pathway" framing are general oncology standard rather than DeVita findings; each such statement is labelled inline above rather than carried under the DeVita citation.
