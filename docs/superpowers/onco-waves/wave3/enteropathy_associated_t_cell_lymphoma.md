# Enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL) - Management

## Overview and context
EATL is a rare, aggressive intraepithelial T-cell lymphoma of the small bowel that arises in the
setting of gluten-driven enteropathy. It occurs mainly in older adults and is commonly linked to
clinical or serologic evidence of celiac disease and to the HLA-DQA1*0501/DQB1*0201 genotype.
Among patients with severe or refractory celiac disease, EATL develops in up to roughly 39 percent,
underscoring why refractory celiac disease is treated as a pre-lymphoma state that needs close
follow-up. EATL accounts for a small minority (roughly 15 percent) of small bowel lymphomas overall,
occurring most often in the jejunum and proximal ileum, with an equal male to female ratio.

A related but biologically distinct entity, monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T-cell lymphoma
(MEITL, previously called "type II EATL"), is not associated with celiac disease and has a different
immunophenotype (CD8+/CD56+ rather than the CD3+/CD103+ pattern typical of classic EATL). The two
are distinguished pathologically but are grouped together as intestinal T-cell lymphomas.

## Presentation and initial workup
Patients typically present acutely with small bowel obstruction, perforation, or gastrointestinal
bleeding, sometimes with a preceding history of gluten sensitivity or worsening enteropathy despite
dietary gluten restriction. Because presentation is frequently a surgical emergency, diagnosis is
often made intraoperatively or on resection pathology rather than through elective biopsy.

Guideline-standard aggressive-lymphoma workup applies once EATL is suspected or confirmed:
- Cross-sectional and functional staging (contrast CT and PET/CT) to define nodal and extranodal
  extent, including small bowel and adjacent structures.
- Bone marrow evaluation as part of standard lymphoma staging.
- Endoscopic and pathologic confirmation of celiac disease/enteropathy where not already established,
  including assessment for refractory celiac disease.
- Baseline nutritional assessment, given the frequent background of malabsorption and villous
  atrophy.
- Immunophenotyping (CD3, CD103, CD4/CD8 status, CD30) to confirm the diagnosis, distinguish EATL
  from MEITL, and identify a CD30-positive subset that may be relevant to treatment selection.

## Treatment approach by intent

### Surgery
Surgical resection is central to initial management because many patients present with obstruction,
perforation, or bleeding requiring urgent operative intervention. Resection also provides the tissue
needed for definitive diagnosis and staging. Surgery alone is not curative therapy for this
aggressive lymphoma and is followed by systemic treatment in patients fit enough to receive it.

### Systemic therapy
- The described approach after resection is surgery followed by adjuvant chemotherapy using an
  anthracycline-based regimen, reflecting the aggressive-lymphoma treatment principles applied to
  intestinal T-cell lymphomas.
- EATL is grouped among the mature T-cell/NK-cell neoplasms together with other peripheral T-cell
  lymphomas (PTCL). For these aggressive T-cell lymphomas as a class, most comparative studies have
  not shown an advantage for regimens other than CHOP-type combination chemotherapy; when a
  CD30-positive antibody-drug conjugate-based combination has been directly compared with standard
  chemotherapy in CD30-positive PTCL, the antibody-drug conjugate-containing combination showed
  superior progression-free and overall survival and became a standard first-line option for
  CD30-positive PTCL subtypes. This is relevant to the CD30-positive minority of EATL cases, though
  DeVita does not report EATL-specific trial data for this combination.
- Consolidation with autologous stem cell transplantation in first remission is used in eligible,
  fit patients with aggressive PTCL as a class, based on the poor long-term outcomes with
  chemotherapy alone; this extrapolates general PTCL practice rather than reflecting an EATL-specific
  trial.
- Given the overall poor prognosis of this group of lymphomas, enrollment in a clinical trial should
  be considered whenever available.

### Relapsed or refractory disease
Data specific to relapsed EATL are not detailed in DeVita. By extension from the PTCL group to which
EATL belongs, relapse carries a poor prognosis, and options that have been used in relapsed
aggressive PTCL include antifolate-based therapy and histone deacetylase inhibitor therapy, each
associated with a modest response rate and a limited duration of response, as well as consideration
of allogeneic stem cell transplantation in selected patients able to tolerate it. Salvage
chemotherapy followed by autologous transplant has shown only limited benefit in this broader PTCL
group. These options should be regarded as guideline-consistent extrapolations for EATL rather than
EATL-specific evidence.

### Underlying celiac disease
Because EATL arises from longstanding gluten-driven enteropathy, strict adherence to a gluten-free
diet is the described approach to preventing progression from enteropathy to lymphoma, and
management of underlying refractory celiac disease (nutritional support, monitoring for persistent
villous atrophy) continues alongside oncologic treatment.

## Monitoring
Response assessment follows standard aggressive-lymphoma practice: interval cross-sectional/PET
imaging during and after systemic treatment, together with ongoing surveillance for bowel
complications (obstruction, perforation, bleeding) given the disease's propensity for transmural
bowel involvement. Nutritional status and control of the underlying enteropathy should be followed
longitudinally, since incomplete control of celiac disease is linked to disease persistence or
recurrence.

## When to refer
Given the rarity of EATL, its frequent presentation as a surgical emergency, and its poor overall
prognosis (5-year survival in the range of roughly 8 to 20 percent), patients should be referred
promptly to a center with hematology-oncology expertise in T-cell lymphomas for staging,
consideration of transplant-eligible intensive approaches, and clinical trial availability, with
concurrent gastroenterology involvement for celiac disease management and nutritional support.
Surgical involvement is typically already in place given the emergency presentation, and early
multidisciplinary coordination between surgery, gastroenterology, and hematology-oncology is
appropriate.

## What is deliberately not stated here
DeVita does not provide EATL-specific chemotherapy regimen selection beyond "anthracycline-based,"
does not report EATL-specific transplant outcome data, and does not describe an EATL-specific
surveillance imaging schedule. These gaps are noted rather than filled with invented specifics.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
