# Doege-Potter Syndrome — Management

Doege-Potter syndrome is the paraneoplastic hypoglycemia that arises from a solitary fibrous tumor
(SFT), usually pleural but occasionally pelvic, retroperitoneal, or meningeal, that secretes an
incompletely processed high-molecular-weight IGF-2 (big IGF-2). Management is really the
management of the underlying SFT, with the hypoglycemia treated as a paraneoplastic marker that
should track with tumor burden and resolve after definitive treatment.

## Treatment approach by intent

**Localized, resectable disease.** Complete surgical resection of the SFT is the primary and usually
curative treatment. Resection is expected to correct the hypoglycemia; if hypoglycemia persists after
what was thought to be complete resection, that should prompt a search for residual or metastatic
disease rather than being treated as a separate endocrine problem.

**Locally advanced or borderline-resectable disease.** The tumor should be evaluated for the
technical feasibility of an R0 resection. When resection is not immediately feasible, imaging-based
reassessment and multidisciplinary sarcoma-team input (surgery, medical oncology, radiology,
pathology) are appropriate before deciding on neoadjuvant systemic therapy or a change in surgical
plan.

**Unresectable, metastatic, or recurrent disease.** SFTs are notably resistant to standard
anthracycline-based chemotherapy. Multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors directed against
angiogenic pathways have documented activity in SFT and are the systemic options with the best
evidence in this histology. Because response rates with any systemic therapy are modest, treatment
goals in this setting are disease control and symptom/hypoglycemia palliation rather than cure.

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy

- **Surgery** is the mainstay whenever an R0 resection is achievable, including for isolated
  recurrences or resectable metastases, since SFT behavior after resection can still be indolent even
  in patients with high-risk histologic features.
- **Radiotherapy** is not established as a first-line curative modality for SFT and is not substantiated
  as an evidence-based option here; its use should be individualized by the sarcoma multidisciplinary
  team rather than applied routinely.
- **Systemic therapy** is reserved for unresectable or metastatic disease, given the demonstrated
  resistance of SFT to standard anthracycline-based regimens and the activity seen with angiogenesis-
  targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors.

## Risk stratification and monitoring

Tumor size greater than 10 cm and a mitotic rate above 4 per 10 high-power fields identify SFTs at
higher risk of metastasis, and these features should inform the intensity of post-treatment
surveillance. Retroperitoneal SFTs in particular carry a high risk of distant recurrence and
disease-specific death over a 10-year horizon, so long-term imaging follow-up is warranted for this
site even after apparently complete resection.

Because the paraneoplastic hypoglycemia in Doege-Potter syndrome parallels tumor bulk, clinical and
biochemical monitoring for hypoglycemia recurrence (fasting glucose, symptoms of neuroglycopenia)
can serve as an adjunct signal of tumor recurrence alongside imaging, though it should not replace
structured oncologic surveillance imaging.

## When to refer

Any patient presenting with a large SFT and unexplained hypoglycemia should be referred promptly to
a sarcoma-experienced surgical oncology or multidisciplinary sarcoma team for staging and resection
planning, given the size and site-related recurrence risks above. Patients with unresectable, locally
advanced, or metastatic disease should be referred to medical oncology for consideration of
tyrosine-kinase-inhibitor-based systemic therapy, and endocrinology input is useful for interim
management of hypoglycemia (for example, glucocorticoids and continuous carbohydrate provision)
while definitive oncologic treatment is arranged.
