# Cutaneous B-cell Lymphoma (Primary Cutaneous B-cell Lymphoma, PCBCL) — Management

## Overview and approach by subtype
Management of primary cutaneous B-cell lymphoma is driven almost entirely by histologic subtype rather than by a single staging system, because the indolent and aggressive forms follow very different clinical courses. Before treatment, systemic (nodal) lymphoma with secondary skin involvement must be excluded, since the treatment intent and philosophy differ from a truly skin-confined process.

- Indolent subtypes (marginal zone lymphoma of the skin, follicle centre lymphoma): these tend to be persistent and bothersome to the patient but rarely take an aggressive clinical course. Management is generally conservative and skin-directed.
- Aggressive subtype (diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, leg type): this behaves much less favourably and is treated more assertively from the outset.

## Indolent disease (marginal zone / follicle centre)
- Options include radiotherapy, surgical excision, or observation alone, chosen according to lesion number, site, and patient preference.
- Radiotherapy technique and side-effect profile mirror that used for cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (mycosis fungoides). Short courses of low-dose radiotherapy can give palliative benefit and complete responses in a proportion of patients, reflecting the exquisite radiosensitivity of these indolent clones.
- For marginal zone lymphoma of the skin specifically, Borrelia burgdorferi has been implicated in a subset of cases; where a Borrelia association is suspected, antibiotic therapy directed at the organism is a reasonable first step in appropriate regions before committing to radiotherapy or excision (guideline-general extension of the Borrelia association; DeVita does not detail an antibiotic protocol).
- Rituximab (an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody) has been used in patients with primary cutaneous B-cell lymphoma, including those with more widespread cutaneous disease, but the supporting evidence is anecdotal and drawn from small series, so it should be regarded as an option for multifocal or relapsed indolent disease rather than a default first-line choice.
- Outcomes for localized cutaneous B-cell lymphoma are excellent: complete response rates approach 100 percent, with five-year disease-free survival of roughly 50 percent, so recurrence in the skin is common but is not equated with treatment failure or need for escalation to systemic therapy.

## Aggressive disease (diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, leg type)
- This subtype is treated more aggressively because outcomes with radiotherapy alone are relatively poor.
- Combined modality therapy is generally favoured, following the therapeutic approach used for nodal diffuse large B-cell lymphoma of similar histology (i.e., a systemic anthracycline-based, rituximab-containing immunochemotherapy regimen combined with regional radiotherapy), since leg-type lesions behave biologically like their nodal counterpart rather than like indolent cutaneous lymphoma.
- If diffuse large B-cell histology is present but not the leg-type presentation, radiotherapy alone can be considered as sole therapy, reflecting a somewhat better prognosis for large-cell lymphoma arising outside the lower leg.
- Rituximab-based therapy, as above, has also been used in this group, particularly where disease is widespread.

## Lines of therapy
- First line for indolent disease: local radiotherapy or surgical excision for a solitary or few lesions; observation is reasonable for asymptomatic, non-progressive disease.
- Relapse/multifocal indolent disease: repeat local therapy to individual lesions, or systemic anti-CD20 therapy (rituximab) if disease is widespread, recognising the evidence base here is limited.
- Leg-type diffuse large B-cell lymphoma: combined systemic immunochemotherapy plus radiotherapy as first line, following nodal diffuse large B-cell lymphoma treatment paradigms.
- Non-leg diffuse large B-cell lymphoma: radiotherapy alone can suffice; escalate to combined modality if there is multifocal or bulky disease, extrapolating from how leg-type disease is handled.

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy
- Surgery: excision is a reasonable option for a solitary indolent lesion, on par with radiotherapy; it is not curative for multifocal skin disease and has no established role in leg-type disease given its more aggressive behaviour.
- Radiotherapy: the mainstay of local control for indolent PCBCL, and a component (rather than sole modality) of treatment for leg-type disease in most cases; used as sole therapy only for non-leg diffuse large B-cell lymphoma.
- Systemic therapy: reserved for widespread indolent disease (rituximab) or as part of combined modality treatment for leg-type diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (anthracycline-based, rituximab-containing regimen); DeVita does not specify a preferred agent set beyond rituximab and the general nodal diffuse large B-cell lymphoma paradigm, so specific regimen selection should follow current haemato-oncology / NCCN guidance rather than being asserted here.

## Monitoring
- Given that even successfully treated indolent PCBCL has a meaningful rate of cutaneous relapse (five-year disease-free survival of about 50 percent despite near-universal complete response), regular skin surveillance for new or recurrent lesions is warranted after treatment.
- Relapse in the skin alone in indolent subtypes should not automatically be treated as systemic progression; re-staging to confirm the disease remains skin-confined is appropriate before escalating therapy.
- For leg-type disease, monitoring should follow the pattern used for nodal diffuse large B-cell lymphoma given the shared biology and treatment approach, with attention to both local (skin) and systemic relapse.

## When to refer
- Refer to haemato-oncology for confirmation of histologic subtype and exclusion of systemic lymphoma with secondary skin involvement, since this fundamentally changes management intent.
- Refer for radiation oncology input whenever radiotherapy is being considered, particularly for leg-type disease where combined modality planning is needed.
- Refer promptly to haemato-oncology for any diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, leg type, or any multifocal/widespread cutaneous B-cell lymphoma being considered for rituximab-based or combined systemic therapy, given the narrower and less forgiving course of aggressive disease.
