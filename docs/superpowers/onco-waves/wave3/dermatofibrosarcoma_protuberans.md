# Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP) - Management

## Overview relevant to treatment planning

DFSP is a rare, low-grade cutaneous sarcoma with a strong tendency toward local invasion and recurrence but a low potential for metastasis. Most cases (roughly 90%) are low-grade; a minority show a fibrosarcomatous component, which behaves more aggressively and is staged higher. There is no universally accepted dedicated staging system; AJCC groups DFSP with other soft tissue sarcomas, with localized low-grade disease generally corresponding to stage I, higher-grade or larger fibrosarcomatous-variant tumors to stage II or III, and rare regional or distant metastatic disease to stage IV. More than 90% of DFSPs carry a COL1A1-PDGFB fusion that drives PDGF receptor signaling, which is the basis for targeted therapy in advanced disease.

## Treatment by stage and intent

### Localized disease (the great majority of cases)
Surgery is the primary and, for most patients, the only treatment needed.
- Wide local excision (WLE) with a generous margin (most series describe a minimum of about 3 cm, including the underlying fascia) is a standard first-line approach. Elective lymph node dissection is not indicated, since nodal spread is rare.
- Mohs micrographic surgery (MMS) is an alternative first-line option, particularly where tissue conservation matters (face, other cosmetically or functionally sensitive sites) or where margin control needs to be maximized. Comparative series suggest MMS achieves at least comparable, and in some series lower, local recurrence rates than WLE, with fewer positive margins on the initial resection.
- The likelihood of local recurrence tracks directly with the adequacy of the surgical margin. Narrow or incomplete excisions have been associated with substantially higher recurrence rates than wider excisions.
- Positive or narrow margins should prompt re-excision when feasible, given the high recurrence risk of incompletely resected DFSP.

### When surgery is not possible, incomplete, or would cause major functional/cosmetic deficit
- Radiation therapy has been used selectively as an adjunct or alternative when resection cannot achieve adequate margins or would produce a major cosmetic or functional deficit, with reports of good local response. DeVita does not specify a dose regimen for this indication, and none is given here.

### Locally advanced, unresectable, or metastatic disease
- A PDGF receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (imatinib) has documented clinical activity in advanced or metastatic DFSP, reflecting the underlying COL1A1-PDGFB driver biology, and has produced durable responses including in case series of locally advanced or metastatic disease. This is the systemic therapy with the clearest grounding for DFSP specifically.
- Conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy (agents such as vinblastine or methotrexate have been reported) has only limited supporting clinical data in DFSP and should be considered a fallback rather than a preferred option.
- Because distant metastasis is rare but the disease is locally aggressive, management of advanced/unresectable disease should be multidisciplinary, involving surgical oncology, radiation oncology, and medical oncology.

### Fibrosarcomatous DFSP variant
This variant follows a more aggressive course, with higher local recurrence and a nontrivial metastasis rate reported in case series. Treatment still centers on surgical excision with adequate margins, but these patients warrant closer follow-up given the higher risk of recurrence and metastasis, and earlier consideration of the systemic options above if the disease becomes unresectable or metastatic.

## Monitoring and follow-up

- DFSP has a high reported local recurrence rate (up to roughly 60% in some series), strongly dependent on the extent of the original resection; most recurrences appear within the first 3 years.
- Head and neck DFSP recurs locally more often than trunk/extremity disease, likely reflecting the smaller margins used in cosmetically sensitive regions.
- Lymph node metastasis is uncommon (around 1% of cases), and distant metastasis, when it occurs, is principally to the lungs (around 4% of cases); multiple local recurrences appear to predispose to eventual distant metastasis.
- Overall disease-specific mortality is low.
- Given this recurrence pattern, patients should be followed with regular clinical examination of the surgical site (and regionally) for an extended period, with vigilance concentrated in the first several years after resection; DeVita does not specify an exact follow-up interval for DFSP, so a fixed schedule is not asserted here.

## When to refer

- Any tumor where clear margins cannot be obtained by standard excision, or where excision would risk major functional or cosmetic compromise (face, hands, other sensitive sites), warrants referral to a center with Mohs surgery expertise or a multidisciplinary sarcoma/skin oncology team.
- Locally advanced, unresectable, recurrent-after-resection, or metastatic disease should be referred to medical oncology for consideration of PDGFR-targeted therapy and to a multidisciplinary sarcoma team for combined surgical/radiation/systemic planning.
- The fibrosarcomatous variant, given its more aggressive behavior, merits earlier multidisciplinary sarcoma input even when apparently localized.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
