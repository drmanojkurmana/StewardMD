# Succinate Dehydrogenase-Deficient Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumour (SDH-deficient GIST) - Management

## Background relevant to management
SDH-deficient GIST is a distinct molecular subtype of GIST defined by loss of succinate dehydrogenase complex function (negative SDHB staining on immunohistochemistry), arising either from SDHC promoter hypermethylation (sporadic) or from germline mutations of the SDHA, SDHB, SDHC, or SDHD subunit genes. It lacks KIT or PDGFRA mutations and is typically encountered in the "wild-type" GIST work-up once KIT/PDGFRA mutational analysis is negative. It occurs predominantly in young girls and women, is characteristically gastric and often multifocal, and can arise in the context of the Carney triad (GIST, pulmonary chondroma, extra-adrenal paraganglioma) or Carney-Stratakis syndrome (GIST and familial paraganglioma). Compared with KIT-mutated GIST, its natural history is generally more indolent, and it is much less responsive to standard tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs).

## Diagnostic work-up that shapes management
- Any GIST negative for KIT and PDGFRA mutations should have SDHB immunohistochemistry performed to identify SDH-deficient disease.
- Once SDH deficiency is confirmed, genetic counseling and testing for germline SDHA/SDHB/SDHC/SDHD mutations is indicated, since nonsporadic germline SDHX mutations carry implications for the patient and family.
- All SDH-deficient patients carry a risk of paraganglioma, which is the stated rationale for consideration of annual whole-body MRI surveillance.
- Because these tumors can be multifocal (especially gastric), imaging and surgical planning should account for the possibility of additional lesions beyond the index tumor.

## Localized disease
- Surgery remains the primary treatment when disease is localized, as in GIST generally.
- Because SDH-deficient GIST is often multifocal and confined to the stomach, and behaves indolently in many patients but can still have individual aggressive lesions, the extent of resection should be individualized rather than following a fixed template used for KIT-mutated GIST. Judicious, case-by-case surgical planning is advised, balancing the risk of recurrence against the lack of benefit from currently available TKIs in this subtype.
- Unlike conventional adult KIT-mutated GIST, where lymph node involvement is rare and lymphadenectomy is not routinely required, DeVita identifies SDH-deficient (and other non-KIT/PDGFRA syndromic) GIST as the recognized exception with a propensity to metastasize to lymph nodes. Regional nodes should therefore be evaluated rather than assumed benign by analogy to KIT-mutated disease, and suspicious or involved nodes should be resected; routine prophylactic lymphadenectomy is still not mandated in the absence of suspicious nodes.
- Adjuvant TKI therapy (the standard being adjuvant imatinib in KIT/PDGFRA-mutated GIST) is not currently recommended for SDH-deficient (formerly "wild-type") GIST, because this subtype is much less sensitive to imatinib and adjuvant trial data in this population are lacking; its generally less aggressive natural history is also cited as a reason these patients are not selected for adjuvant treatment.

## Advanced, unresectable, or metastatic disease
- When SDH-deficient GIST is metastatic or locally unresectable, systemic therapy is far less reliably effective than in KIT-mutated disease, since these tumors are essentially not sensitive to imatinib as a class, though occasional responses have been reported.
- Sunitinib and regorafenib are alternative TKI options that have shown activity specifically in SDH-deficient GIST and are the agents to consider in this setting rather than first-line imatinib.
- The clinical decision to treat (and how aggressively) should take into account the generally indolent natural history of this subtype relative to KIT-mutated GIST; some patients with limited or slowly progressive metastatic disease may be managed expectantly or with a more measured approach than would apply to KIT-mutated disease.
- Given the relative lack of TKI sensitivity in this subtype, surgery may have a broader role in some presentations of metastatic or locally advanced SDH-deficient GIST than it would in TKI-sensitive, KIT-mutated GIST.
- General GIST principles that reasonably extend to this subtype: when a TKI is used and disease responds, therapy is continued indefinitely, as discontinuation trials in GIST overall show progression resuming within months of stopping; and monitoring on therapy should include attention to tolerability alongside imaging response assessment (with PET able to demonstrate metabolic response within weeks where relevant) (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, extrapolated from DeVita's GIST discussion of KIT-mutated disease).

## Monitoring
- Longitudinal whole-body MRI is specifically called out for SDH-deficient patients because of their paraganglioma risk, independent of GIST surveillance itself.
- Genetic counseling and, where indicated, cascade testing of relatives applies to patients found to carry a nonsporadic germline SDHX mutation.
- Because multifocality is characteristic, follow-up imaging should be alert to new or additional gastric lesions, not only growth of the known tumor.

## When to refer
- DeVita states that GIST treatment strategy should be planned by a multidisciplinary expert team; given the genotyping complexity, atypical surgical decision-making for multifocal presentations, and limited TKI sensitivity that characterize SDH-deficient GIST specifically, referral to centers or networks with specific expertise in this rare subtype is a reasonable extension of that principle (synthesis from DeVita's chapter themes, not a verbatim statement).
- Patients with suspected or confirmed germline SDHX mutations, or with features of Carney triad or Carney-Stratakis syndrome, should be referred for genetic counseling and for paraganglioma/other syndrome-associated tumor surveillance.

## What DeVita's section does not specify
