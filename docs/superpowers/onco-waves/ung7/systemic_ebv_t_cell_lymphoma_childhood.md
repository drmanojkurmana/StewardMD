# Systemic EBV-positive T-cell lymphoma of childhood - management

## Disease context

Systemic EBV-positive T-cell lymphoma of childhood is a rare, clonal, EBV-driven cytotoxic T-cell
neoplasm arising in children and young adults, typically after acute or chronic active EBV infection, and
it is almost always complicated by haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (general oncology standard, not
from DeVita's section on this disease). Because of its rarity there is no single established randomised
evidence base or standard regimen; management is extrapolated from HLH treatment protocols and paediatric
T-cell lymphoma/lymphoproliferative disease experience (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
section on this disease).

## Treatment approach by intent

There is no accepted "early stage, surgery-only" pathway for this disease; it behaves as a systemic,
rapidly progressive illness from presentation, so surgery has no curative role and is limited to
diagnostic biopsy (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Radiotherapy is
not part of standard management, since disease is systemic rather than localized (general oncology
standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

- **Intent is curative wherever possible**, but the disease is frequently fatal within weeks without
  prompt intensive therapy, so treatment is urgent from the time of diagnosis (general oncology standard,
  not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **First priority: control the hyperinflammatory/HLH component.** This uses HLH-directed
  immunochemotherapy, combining an etoposide-based cytotoxic agent with corticosteroids and a
  calcineurin inhibitor such as ciclosporin, aimed at suppressing the cytokine-driven macrophage
  activation before it causes irreversible multi-organ damage (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).
- **In parallel or in sequence: lymphoma-directed combination chemotherapy** targeting the clonal
  EBV-positive cytotoxic T-cell population itself, since HLH-directed therapy alone does not eliminate the
  malignant T-cell clone (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Definitive/curative step: allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT)** in patients who
  achieve disease control, as this is regarded as the main route to durable cure; chemotherapy alone is
  not considered curative in most reported experience (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).

## Lines of therapy

- **Induction/first line:** simultaneous or rapidly sequential HLH-directed therapy (etoposide-based
  regimen plus corticosteroid plus ciclosporin) and lymphoma-directed combination chemotherapy, intended
  to bring the hyperinflammatory state and the T-cell clone under control quickly enough to proceed to
  transplant (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Consolidation:** allogeneic HSCT as soon as a patient is fit enough and a donor is available; given
  the pace of the disease, transplant referral should happen early, ideally in parallel with induction
  rather than only after chemotherapy is complete (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease).
- **Relapsed/refractory or transplant-ineligible disease:** no established standard salvage regimen exists
  in the literature reviewed here; this content cannot state a specific salvage approach without inventing
  one, so DeVita and general sourcing are both silent on this point and it is deliberately omitted.

## Supportive care

- Intensive supportive care for multi-organ dysfunction, including correction of coagulopathy and
  cytopenias and organ support in a critical-care setting, is a core part of management given the
  frequency of hepatic, haematologic, and haemodynamic compromise (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).
- Blood-product support and infection prophylaxis are needed through the periods of profound cytopenia and
  immunosuppression that accompany both the disease and its treatment (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Serial assessment of the HLH markers (ferritin, triglycerides, fibrinogen, soluble interleukin-2
  receptor), blood counts, liver function, and coagulation studies to track response to HLH-directed
  therapy and detect early organ deterioration (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease).
- EBV viral load and, where available, T-cell clonality/EBER status to track the underlying
  lymphoproliferative clone (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Close monitoring for transplant candidacy and timing, since delay while awaiting a donor or full
  organ recovery can allow disease progression (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease).

## When to refer

- Refer immediately to a specialist paediatric haemato-oncology centre with transplant capability at the
  time of diagnosis; this is not a disease to manage step-wise in a general paediatric or adult oncology
  setting given its speed and the central role of allogeneic HSCT (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).
- Early critical-care/organ-support referral for patients developing multi-organ dysfunction or
  haemodynamic instability from the cytokine storm (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section
  on this disease).
- Family counselling and evaluation for an underlying genetic predisposition to abnormal EBV handling,
  particularly relevant given the disease's higher incidence in East Asian and Latin American populations
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
