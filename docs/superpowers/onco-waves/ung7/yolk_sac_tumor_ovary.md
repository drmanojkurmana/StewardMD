# Ovarian yolk sac tumour (endodermal sinus tumour) - management narrative

## Overview and grounding

DeVita classifies ovarian yolk sac tumour under malignant germ cell tumours (MGCT) of the ovary, which together account for a small minority of ovarian malignancies and present at a median age in the late teens to early twenties. These tumours are almost always unilateral, and DeVita states this unilaterality is the reason fertility-preserving surgery is always an option for this disease, even when metastases are present. The yolk sac tumour subtype is described as spreading hematogenously, elaborating alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), and being characterised histologically by Schiller-Duval bodies.

## Treatment approach by stage and intent

Surgery is the first step and is fertility-preserving whenever feasible, given the near-universal unilaterality of disease and the young age of most patients. DeVita is explicit that this fertility-sparing approach holds even in the presence of metastatic disease, since the tumour class overall is exquisitely chemosensitive.

Adjuvant systemic therapy follows surgery for essentially all patients with ovarian yolk sac tumour. DeVita states that, with the exception of FIGO stage IA dysgerminoma and stage IA grade 1 or 2 immature teratoma, patients with malignant germ cell tumours of the ovary (including yolk sac tumour, which does not fall into either of those two excepted categories) should receive adjuvant combination chemotherapy built on a platinum agent together with etoposide and bleomycin. Because yolk sac tumour is not one of the two histology/stage combinations DeVita names as safely observed without chemotherapy, essentially every stage of pure ovarian yolk sac tumour should be treated with this adjuvant regimen (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, is that observation after surgery alone is sometimes considered for a strictly confined, completely resected stage IA yolk sac tumour in select low-volume centres with close marker/imaging follow-up capability, but DeVita's own text does not carve out this exception for yolk sac tumour the way it does for dysgerminoma and low-grade immature teratoma, so this should be treated cautiously).

Advanced or recurrent/metastatic disease is still approached with curative intent given the marked chemosensitivity of this tumour class, and DeVita notes that the treatment framework for ovarian MGCT as a whole mirrors and has been extrapolated from the more extensively studied testicular germ cell tumour experience.

## Role of surgery

- Unilateral salpingo-oophorectomy with preservation of the uterus and contralateral ovary is the standard surgical approach, reflecting the near-universal unilaterality of the tumour.
- Fertility-preserving surgery is appropriate even when metastatic disease is present, per DeVita.
- Surgical staging (including assessment of the abdomen and pelvis) informs the extent of disease but does not override the chemosensitive nature of this tumour class in deciding on adjuvant therapy.

## Role of radiotherapy

DeVita's discussion of this disease does not describe a role for radiotherapy in ovarian yolk sac tumour; the radiosensitive subtype DeVita specifically calls out among ovarian germ cell tumours is dysgerminoma, not yolk sac tumour. In the absence of a grounded role, radiotherapy should not be considered part of standard management of ovarian yolk sac tumour.

## Role of systemic therapy

A platinum-etoposide-bleomycin combination is the adjuvant regimen DeVita names for malignant germ cell tumours of the ovary (excepting the two specific exceptions above), extrapolated from testicular germ cell tumour experience. No numeric dosing or cycle schedule is reproduced here; those specifics belong in a structured, access-gated protocol template rather than this narrative.

## Monitoring

- Serum AFP is the key tumour marker for this histology (per DeVita and general oncology standard) and should be followed serially during and after treatment to assess response and detect recurrence.
- DeVita notes that prognosis for this class of ovarian cancer is excellent, with return of normal menstrual function being the rule and numerous successful pregnancies documented during follow-up of survivors, supporting long-term surveillance that also attends to fertility and reproductive outcomes.

## When to refer

Because ovarian yolk sac tumour is rare, occurs in a young population where fertility preservation is a central concern, and requires coordinated surgical staging plus combination chemotherapy, management should involve a gynaecologic oncology and germ cell tumour multidisciplinary team from the point of diagnosis (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Any suspicion of a mixed germ cell tumour component, rising markers on surveillance, or recurrent disease should prompt prompt referral back to that team.

## Deliberate omissions

No specific chemotherapy drug doses, cycle counts, or treatment durations are given, per the dose-free requirement. Statements about second-line or salvage regimens for platinum-resistant or relapsed ovarian yolk sac tumour were omitted because DeVita's section on this disease does not describe them explicitly; only the general extrapolation from testicular germ cell tumour experience is grounded.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
