# Sertoli-Leydig cell tumour (SLCT) - management

Sertoli-Leydig cell tumour (also called androblastoma or arrhenoblastoma) is a rare ovarian sex-cord
stromal tumour that classically produces androgens and causes a virilising syndrome. Management is
staged surgery first, with adjuvant systemic therapy reserved for higher-risk histology or disease
extent, and follow-up built around the tumour's hormone markers.

## Treatment approach by stage and grade

- **Well-differentiated, stage-confined disease in a young woman:** fertility-sparing unilateral
  salpingo-oophorectomy with surgical staging is appropriate. DeVita states plainly that surgical
  resection is usually curative for Sertoli-Leydig cell tumours, and the KB's own reference notes
  well-differentiated tumours behave as essentially benign once removed.
- **Advanced-stage or poorly differentiated disease, or disease with heterologous mesenchymal
  elements or tumour rupture:** total hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy and surgical
  debulking is the surgical standard (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease), reflecting the higher recurrence and metastatic risk described for these subgroups.
- **Lymphadenectomy** can reasonably be omitted from staging surgery for sex-cord stromal tumours
  in the absence of grossly enlarged nodes; this is stated in DeVita for the sex-cord stromal tumour
  group as a whole (granulosa cell and Sertoli-Leydig tumours), not specifically re-derived for SLCT
  alone.

## Lines of systemic therapy

- DeVita's own text on Sertoli-Leydig cell tumour is limited to two systemic-therapy statements:
  surgical resection is usually curative, and patients with advanced Sertoli-Leydig cell tumours
  may respond to gonadotropin-releasing hormone analogs. DeVita does not, in this section, name a
  specific chemotherapy regimen for SLCT.
- For poorly differentiated tumours, heterologous mesenchymal elements, tumour rupture, or advanced
  stage, adjuvant platinum-based combination chemotherapy is used (general oncology standard, not
  from DeVita's section on this disease); this mirrors the approach used across sex-cord stromal and
  malignant germ cell ovarian tumours in the same DeVita chapter, but is not stated by DeVita for
  SLCT specifically, so it is marked as general standard rather than DeVita-sourced.
- Hormonal therapy with a gonadotropin-releasing hormone analog is a reasonable option in advanced or
  recurrent disease per the DeVita statement above, particularly when further surgery or cytotoxic
  therapy is not suitable.

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy

- Surgery is the primary and, for well-differentiated stage-confined disease, usually definitive
  treatment, per DeVita ("surgical resection is usually curative").
- There is no mention of radiotherapy for Sertoli-Leydig cell tumour in the DeVita section reviewed;
  radiotherapy is not considered a standard part of management for this tumour type (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease) and its absence here should not be
  read as an oversight.
- Systemic therapy is reserved for advanced, recurrent, or biologically higher-risk disease (poorly
  differentiated histology, heterologous elements, rupture) rather than used routinely after complete
  resection of low-risk tumours.

## Monitoring

- Virilising features often regress, at least partially, once the tumour is resected; this matches
  the KB reference's own clinical pearl and is consistent with the hormone-driven biology described in
  DeVita for this tumour class.
- Surveillance should combine clinical review with serial androgen markers (serum testosterone) and
  pelvic imaging (ultrasound or MRI) to detect recurrence, since the tumour is hormonally active in
  most cases (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, though consistent
  with how DeVita describes using inhibin to monitor the related granulosa cell tumour for
  recurrence).
- Given the biology described for related DICER1-associated tumours, germline DICER1 mutation testing
  and genetic counselling should be considered, particularly in young patients or those with retiform
  or heterologous histology (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Refer to a gynaecological oncology multidisciplinary team for surgical staging decisions,
  particularly whenever fertility preservation is being weighed against completeness of staging, or
  when histology returns as poorly differentiated, heterologous, or ruptured.
- Refer for genetics assessment when DICER1 syndrome is suspected (personal or family history of
  related tumours, or retiform/heterologous histology in a young patient).
- Advanced or recurrent disease, or disease being considered for hormonal therapy with a
  gonadotropin-releasing hormone analog, should be managed by a gynaecological oncology team rather
  than in general gynaecology follow-up.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
