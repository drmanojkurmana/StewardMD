# Atypical Fibroxanthoma (AFX) — Management Expansion

## Framing the diagnosis before treating it
Atypical fibroxanthoma is defined, by convention, as the superficial variant of a fibrohistiocytic
spindle cell tumor that stays confined to the dermis, without extension into subcutis or deeper
structures, tumor necrosis, or lymphovascular or perineural invasion. If any of those aggressive
features are present, the lesion is reclassified as pleomorphic dermal sarcoma (PDS), which behaves
much more aggressively. Because the distinction depends on evaluating the full depth of the lesion,
a definitive management plan should wait until the specimen has been completely excised and fully
assessed histologically, since a superficial or incisional biopsy alone cannot reliably exclude PDS.

Diagnosis is one of exclusion: immunohistochemistry is used to rule out spindle-cell squamous cell
carcinoma, desmoplastic or spindle-cell melanoma, and leiomyosarcoma, since AFX has no distinctive
clinical appearance and overlaps with these and other entities (pyogenic granuloma, basal cell
carcinoma, amelanotic melanoma, Merkel cell carcinoma, cutaneous metastasis).

## Treatment approach by stage/intent

**Localized, true AFX (confined to dermis, no adverse histologic features)**
- Primary treatment is surgical removal, with complete excision as the goal.
- Both wide local excision (WLE) and Mohs micrographic surgery (MMS) are used; comparative
  retrospective data favor MMS for superior margin control and tissue conservation, with lower
  reported local recurrence and no reported metastases in MMS-treated cohorts versus WLE.
- There is no established role for adjuvant radiotherapy or systemic therapy in histologically
  confirmed, completely excised, localized AFX. This is a surgical disease at this stage.

**Lesions reclassified as pleomorphic dermal sarcoma (deep invasion, necrosis, or lymphovascular/
perineural invasion found on the excision specimen)**
- These carry meaningfully higher rates of local recurrence and metastasis than true AFX and should
  be managed with the more aggressive intent that a deep dermal/subcutaneous sarcoma warrants,
  including wider surgical margins and closer surveillance.
- The literature does not provide a clear, standardized systemic or radiation protocol specific to
  PDS; management should be individualized.

**Unresectable, recurrent, or metastatic disease**
- Adjuvant treatment with radiation therapy or chemotherapy should be considered, but there are no
  clear guidelines for this setting, and conventional chemotherapy has proven largely ineffectual in
  reported experience.
- Because activating RAS pathway mutations have been identified in some tumors, targeted molecular
  inhibitors are discussed as a potential future direction, but this is not an established, defined
  line of therapy at present. No specific agent or drug class can be named here as a validated
  standard.
- Given the lack of guideline-directed systemic options, these cases warrant multidisciplinary
  sarcoma-team discussion rather than a fixed treatment algorithm.

## Lines of therapy
There is no formal first-line/second-line systemic therapy sequence established for AFX or PDS in
the source material. Surgery is effectively the only therapy with a clearly favorable evidence base;
beyond that, treatment is individualized and not protocolized.

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy
- **Surgery** is the mainstay and, for true AFX, is generally curative. Margin-controlled excision
  (Mohs) is preferred where available.
- **Radiotherapy** has a role only in the unresectable, recurrent, or metastatic setting, and is
  used without clear guideline support, on a case-by-case basis.
- **Systemic therapy** (conventional chemotherapy) has a poor track record and is not a reliable
  option; it should not be presented to patients as a proven benefit.

## Monitoring
- Follow up for local recurrence after excision.
- In immunosuppressed patients (organ transplant recipients, chronic immunosuppression), recurrence
  and metastatic rates are substantially higher, so surveillance should be more vigilant in this
  group.
- Because these tumors arise on chronically sun-damaged skin, ongoing photoprotection and
  surveillance for other ultraviolet-driven skin cancers in the same field is part of long-term care.
- Imaging should be arranged if deep or recurrent disease is suspected, particularly to reassess
  whether the lesion should be reclassified as PDS.

## When to refer
- Refer to dermatology/dermatopathology for diagnostic confirmation with a broad immunohistochemical
  panel and for surgical excision (ideally Mohs-trained surgeon where feasible).
- Refer to a multidisciplinary sarcoma team if the excision specimen shows features of pleomorphic
  dermal sarcoma, or if disease is unresectable, recurrent, or metastatic, since there is no
  standardized non-surgical pathway and management decisions (radiotherapy, any systemic approach)
  should be individualized by a team experienced with cutaneous sarcomas.
- Refer immunosuppressed or transplant patients for closer specialist follow-up given their higher
  reported rates of recurrence and metastasis.

## What DeVita is silent on (deliberately omitted)
DeVita does not give a defined chemotherapy regimen, a specific radiotherapy indication/technique,
or a validated targeted agent for AFX/PDS; it explicitly states there are no clear guidelines and
that conventional chemotherapy has been largely ineffectual. No regimen, drug name, or statistic
beyond what is stated above has been added, and no dose, schedule, or numeric drug quantity is
included anywhere in this document.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
