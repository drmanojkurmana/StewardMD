## 1. Safety
No unsafe, misleading, or harm-causing statement found.
- Core claim — surgery (wide en bloc resection with clear margins) is the definitive and essentially only reliably effective modality — is correct and the safe default for this tumour. A clinician following it would not be led into harm.
- "Intralesional curettage should be avoided … high risk of local recurrence" is a correct safety-positive caution, not a harmful directive.
- The chemo/RT statements are framed as "no established role" / "not standard," not as absolute prohibitions that would block individualized specialist decisions. The metastatic paragraph explicitly defers to MDT individualization rather than asserting "do not treat," which is the right hedge and avoids a false-negative that could deny a patient specialist-directed therapy.
- Late-recurrence/late-metastasis surveillance guidance (line 34-36) is safety-positive: it warns against assuming new findings are benign and prompts re-biopsy/re-staging.
No definitive claim oversteps decision-support scope.

## 2. Grounding (DeVita 12th ed / NCCN standard of care)
Consistent with standard orthopaedic-oncology teaching:
- Low-grade malignant bone tumour, tibial/fibular diaphyseal predilection, adolescents/young adults — correct.
- Histologic spectrum with osteofibrous dysplasia / OFD-like adamantinoma and cytokeratin/epithelial-marker IHC dependence — correct and appropriately drives the "biopsy before definitive surgery" and specialist-referral logic.
- Metastatic pattern (late, pulmonary + regional nodal) and metastasectomy for resectable pulmonary disease — consistent.
- No fabricated or outdated regimen: the draft deliberately does NOT import osteosarcoma/Ewing chemotherapy evidence, and the "What was deliberately left out" section correctly flags that DeVita's detailed chemo discussion in that chapter applies to those distinct high-grade sarcomas, not adamantinoma. This is exactly right — carrying that evidence over would have been the dangerous fabrication, and it was avoided.
