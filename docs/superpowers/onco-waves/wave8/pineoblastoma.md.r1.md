# R1 Clinical-Safety Review - pineoblastoma

VERDICT: APPROVE

Goldens changed: no (intended: n/a - narrative content, no engine/rule change).
Adversarial .verdict.md: NOT PRESENT (no outstanding flags to re-check).

## 1. Safety
No unsafe, misleading, or harmful directives. Resection is consistently hedged as
"maximal safe resection... rather than resection at any cost," correctly reflecting the
vascular/central-location risk of pineal surgery. No absolute cure/mortality claims.
Infant RT-deferral, MDT individualisation of recurrent disease, and urgent-referral
triggers (obstructive hydrocephalus, progressive Parinaud) are all clinically sound and
safe. Nothing a clinician could follow to patient harm.

## 2. Grounding (DeVita/NCCN standard of care)
Strong. DeVita-attributed claims are consistent with the 12th-ed pineal-region content:
difficulty of resection, resection-matters-vs-germinoma-biopsy distinction, the induction
-> CSI+boost -> high-dose chemo with autologous stem-cell rescue series (incl. infants
who avoided RT), investigational framing, poorer HDC results at relapse, and endocrine
deficiencies attributed mainly to tumour. Every claim NOT in DeVita's disease-specific
section is explicitly relabelled "general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
this disease" (RB1/DICER1 predisposition, platinum/alkylator extrapolation, disseminated-
CNS-tumour craniospinal principle, endocrine monitoring). No DeVita mis-attribution found.
No fabricated regimen.

## 3. Dose-free
Confirmed. No mg, mg/m2, AUC, or numbered schedules. The only numerics are outcome
statistics ("high-60s to low-70s percent" 4-yr PFS/OS) and an epidemiologic "roughly
half" - these are prognostic/epidemiologic, not dosing, and are permitted.

## 4. Scope
Appropriately hedged as decision-support: "suggested treatment approach," "encouraging but
still investigational," recurrent disease "individualised through a paediatric neuro-
oncology MDT rather than following a single default pathway." Not directive.

## Advisory (non-blocking)
- Classification line calls pineoblastoma "a variant of primitive neuroectodermal tumour
  (PNET)." WHO CNS 2016/2021 retired the CNS-PNET category; pineoblastoma is now an
  embryonal/pineal parenchymal tumour. This is outdated nomenclature but does NOT affect
  the management recommendations (CSI + chemo remain correct) and is not a safety issue.
  Consider softening to "historically grouped with... PNET" on a future pass.

APPROVE: clinically safe, grounded with correct source attribution, dose-free, and
appropriately scoped as decision-support.
