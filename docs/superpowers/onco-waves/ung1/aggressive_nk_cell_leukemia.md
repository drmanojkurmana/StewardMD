# Aggressive NK-cell leukaemia (ANKL) - Management

## Sourcing note
DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed. lists Aggressive NK-cell leukemia only as an entry in the WHO classification table of mature T- and NK-cell neoplasms; it does not carry a dedicated management section for this specific entity. The chapter does, however, discuss treatment for the closely related entity extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type (a biologically related EBV-driven NK/T-cell neoplasm covered in the same textbook), and that content is used below only where explicitly flagged as borrowed from a different disease's section. All other management claims are marked as general oncology standard.

## Overview and intent
ANKL is a fulminant, EBV-driven systemic proliferation of natural killer cells with an extremely aggressive course (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Because of the speed of deterioration, the treatment intent for essentially all patients fit enough to receive it is disease control with an attempt at cure through transplant; there is no local or "watch and wait" option analogous to indolent lymphoproliferative disorders (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Systemic therapy: lines of treatment
- First line: intensive asparaginase-containing chemotherapy is used up front because ANKL cells characteristically resist conventional anthracycline-based regimens, likely through drug efflux mechanisms (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, though consistent with a related-chapter observation that anthracycline-containing CHOP produces low complete-response rates and short survival in disseminated NK/T-cell neoplasms and that asparaginase-containing regimens have shown promise in this disease family - borrowed from DeVita's chapter on extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type, not this disease's own section).
- Regimens combining asparaginase with other cytotoxic classes (for example with a folate antagonist and a corticosteroid backbone) have been studied in the related NK/T-cell lymphoma population for relapsed/refractory disease (borrowed from DeVita's chapter on extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type, not this disease's own section). No drug doses or schedules are specified here; those live in structured protocol templates.
- Second line/relapsed disease: given the rarity and rapid course of ANKL, there is no well-established standard second-line regimen; management at relapse is generally individualized in consultation with a haemato-oncology/transplant centre (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of surgery and radiotherapy
- Surgery has no role; ANKL is a systemic, leukaemic disease rather than a localized mass lesion (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Radiotherapy is not part of standard ANKL management. (Note: radiotherapy has an established role in the related but distinct entity of localized extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type - this is a different disease and that RT-based approach should not be extrapolated to ANKL; borrowed from DeVita's chapter on extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type, not this disease's own section.)

## Haematopoietic stem cell transplantation
Allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation is offered to fit patients who respond to induction chemotherapy, as it represents the only realistic prospect of durable disease control given the otherwise near-uniform relapse and death within weeks to months.

## Managing disease-associated emergencies
- Haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis frequently complicates ANKL and should be treated as an oncologic emergency with immunosuppressive and cytotoxic therapy alongside organ support, run in parallel with disease-directed chemotherapy rather than sequentially.
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation is corrected with fresh frozen plasma, cryoprecipitate, and platelet transfusion as needed.
- Broad supportive care is required from presentation, including transfusion support, tumour lysis syndrome prophylaxis, and antimicrobial prophylaxis against opportunistic infection given the profound immune compromise.

## Monitoring
- Plasma EBV DNA quantification is tracked serially as a marker of disease activity and response.
- Ferritin, triglycerides, and fibrinogen are followed to detect and monitor haemophagocytic activity.
- Coagulation parameters, cytopenias, and organ function (particularly hepatic) are monitored closely given the risk of rapid multi-organ deterioration.

## When to refer
Refer emergently, at the point of suspicion or diagnosis, to a transplant-capable haemato-oncology unit; do not delay for staging completeness given the explosive tempo of this disease. Early and parallel palliative care involvement is appropriate given the very poor prognosis (survival often measured in weeks to a few months even with intensive therapy), so that goals-of-care discussions happen alongside, not after, disease-directed treatment.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
