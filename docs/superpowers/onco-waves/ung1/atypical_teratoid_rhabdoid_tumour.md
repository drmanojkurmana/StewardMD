# Atypical teratoid rhabdoid tumour (AT/RT) - management

## Sourcing note

DeVita's text does not contain a dedicated management section for atypical teratoid/rhabdoid
tumour. Searches on "atypical teratoid rhabdoid tumor", "AT/RT", and "rhabdoid tumour" only
surface the entity in passing, as one member of the embryonal CNS tumour family (alongside
medulloblastoma, supratentorial PNETs, ETANTRs, and pineoblastoma), as an occasional brain
stem tumour mimic, and in a WHO soft-tissue tumour list and an EZH2/SMARCB1 discussion under
chordoma. No treatment protocol, sequencing, or outcome data specific to AT/RT was found in
DeVita. FLAG: needs manual sourcing against a paediatric neuro-oncology text or COG/SIOP
protocol; everything below is marked accordingly and every clinical claim here is labelled
'(general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease)'.

## Treatment approach by intent

AT/RT is treated with curative intent whenever feasible, using a coordinated multimodal
approach through a paediatric neuro-oncology team (general oncology standard, not from
DeVita's section on this disease). Because most patients are infants or very young children,
treatment must balance tumour control against the neurocognitive cost of therapy, particularly
radiotherapy, in a developing brain (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
this disease).

- **Surgery**: maximal safe resection is the foundation of initial management; extent of
  resection is one of the strongest modifiable prognostic factors (general oncology standard,
  not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Systemic therapy**: intensive multi-agent chemotherapy follows surgery, often incorporating
  a high-dose chemotherapy phase with autologous stem-cell rescue in appropriately selected,
  fit patients; regimens are risk- and, increasingly, molecular-subgroup-informed (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Radiotherapy**: focal or craniospinal radiotherapy is used where the child's age allows,
  reserved or delayed in the very youngest infants because of the disproportionate
  neurocognitive toxicity of cranial irradiation in this age group (general oncology standard,
  not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Intrathecal/CNS-directed therapy**: intrathecal chemotherapy and directed treatment of
  leptomeningeal ("drop") metastases are used given the tumour's propensity for early
  cerebrospinal fluid dissemination (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease).

## Lines of therapy

There is no dedicated discussion in DeVita of first-line versus salvage regimens for AT/RT. In
general oncology practice, first-line treatment is the multimodal surgery-chemotherapy-(radiotherapy)
sequence above; relapsed or refractory disease is managed on a case-by-case basis, often through
clinical trial enrolment given the poor salvage outcomes historically reported for this tumour
(general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). DeVita's chordoma
discussion notes that EZH2-targeting agents (which exploit the epigenetic consequences of
SMARCB1/INI1 loss) are being studied in clinical trials across SMARCB1-deficient neoplasms,
including malignant rhabdoid tumour; this is a trial-stage, investigational approach rather
than established therapy, and DeVita does not describe its use specifically in AT/RT.

## Molecular and germline considerations

AT/RT is defined by loss of SMARCB1 (INI1), or rarely SMARCA4 (BRG1). DeVita's related chordoma
discussion (in the context of poorly differentiated pediatric chordoma, a separate SMARCB1-deficient
entity) confirms that SMARCB1/INI1 loss drives disease through dysregulation of the SWI/SNF
chromatin-remodelling complex and elevation of the polycomb methyltransferase EZH2. A meaningful
proportion of AT/RT arises against a germline SMARCB1/SMARCA4 mutation (rhabdoid tumour
predisposition syndrome); when suspected or confirmed, this warrants genetic counselling and
screening for synchronous extracranial rhabdoid tumours (general oncology standard, not from
DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Surveillance imaging of the whole neuraxis (brain and spine) given the tumour's propensity for
  cerebrospinal fluid dissemination (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).
- Cerebrospinal fluid cytology as part of relapse surveillance where safe to obtain (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Ongoing neurocognitive, endocrine, and developmental follow-up given the young age at
  treatment and the toxicity of intensive multimodal therapy (general oncology standard, not
  from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

Refer promptly to a specialist paediatric neuro-oncology centre with neurosurgery, radiation
oncology, and access to intensive/high-dose chemotherapy and stem-cell rescue support, given the
rarity, aggressiveness, and complexity of AT/RT management (general oncology standard, not from
DeVita's section on this disease). Refer for genetic counselling wherever a germline
SMARCB1/SMARCA4 alteration is identified or suspected, both for the patient's own extracranial
screening and for family counselling (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
this disease).
