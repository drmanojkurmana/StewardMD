# Acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia (AMKL / AML-M7) - Management

## Sourcing note

A targeted search of DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles and Practice of Oncology,
12th edition, did not surface a dedicated management section for acute megakaryoblastic leukaemia.
The only text hits for "megakaryocytic leukaemia" are incidental mentions in the mediastinal germ
cell tumour chapter (as a rare associated second haematologic malignancy) and do not describe its
treatment. The general "Management of Acute Leukemias" chapter of the same text covers acute
myeloid leukaemia (AML) as a whole, including risk-stratified induction, consolidation, and the
role of allogeneic transplant; those general AML principles (e.g. the cytarabine/anthracycline
induction backbone) are applied to AMKL below and ride under the DeVita citation only where they
come directly from that general chapter. The remaining AMKL-specific content below (Down syndrome
reduced-intensity rationale, fusion genetics, transplant escalation for high-risk non-Down-syndrome
disease, trephine/reticulin monitoring) reflects standard heme-onc and paediatric AML management
practice consistent with NCCN guidance and the KB's own AMKL reference notes, and is labelled inline
as general oncology standard rather than attributed to DeVita, since DeVita has no AMKL-specific
section to attribute it to.

## Overview of treatment intent

AMKL is a biologically heterogeneous subtype of AML with megakaryocytic differentiation, and
treatment intent differs sharply by the clinical context in which it arises:

- **Down-syndrome-associated AMKL in children** (typically presenting after transient abnormal
  myelopoiesis of the newborn): treated with curative intent using reduced-intensity chemotherapy,
  because this disease is notably chemosensitive and reduced-intensity approaches limit toxicity
  while preserving high cure rates.
- **Non-Down-syndrome paediatric AMKL** (often driven by fusions such as RBM15-MKL1 or
  CBFA2T3-GLIS2): treated with curative intent using standard intensive paediatric AML-type
  chemotherapy, escalated to allogeneic transplant in high-risk disease given its more aggressive
  behaviour.
- **Adult AMKL** (frequently arising from prior myelodysplasia or as therapy-related disease):
  treated with curative intent where feasible, but outcomes are generally poor and treatment
  decisions must weigh fitness, comorbidity, and disease-related adverse features (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, drawn from DeVita's general AML chapter
  which stresses that patient-related fitness and disease-related adverse cytogenetics govern
  treatment selection across AML as a whole).

There is no surgical or radiotherapy role in AMKL; it is a systemic marrow disease and management
is entirely medical (haematology-oncology led).

## Lines of therapy

- **First-line / induction**: AML-type intensive chemotherapy combining cytarabine with an
  anthracycline-based regimen is the mainstay across AMKL subtypes.
- **Down-syndrome-associated disease**: the same cytarabine/anthracycline backbone is given at
  reduced intensity, reflecting the marked chemosensitivity of GATA1-mutated blasts and the aim of
  limiting excess toxicity in this population.
- **Consolidation / post-remission therapy**: allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation is
  used for high-risk non-Down-syndrome paediatric disease and for most adult cases, reflecting their
  poorer chemosensitivity and higher relapse risk relative to Down-syndrome-associated disease.
- **Relapsed/refractory disease**: DeVita's AMKL-specific notes do not describe a defined salvage
  regimen for this subtype; salvage decisions should follow general relapsed/refractory AML
  principles (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease), incorporating
  transplant consolidation if not already performed and clinical trial referral where available.

## Supportive care

- Red cell and platelet transfusion support for marrow failure-related cytopenias.
- Antimicrobial prophylaxis during neutropenia.
- Tumour lysis syndrome prophylaxis at treatment initiation, particularly with higher marrow blast
  burden or organomegaly.

## Monitoring

- Surveillance of infants with Down syndrome who have had transient abnormal myelopoiesis, since a
  proportion later develop overt AMKL and require early recognition.
- Serial marrow assessment (trephine biopsy with reticulin staining, given the high rate of dry
  aspirate) to confirm remission status, since fibrosis can obscure residual disease on aspirate
  alone.
- Monitoring for cytopenias, bleeding, and infection throughout treatment given the underlying
  marrow failure and myelofibrosis that characterise this disease.
- Baseline and interval organ function assessment and imaging for hepatosplenomegaly, both before
  and during treatment.

## When to refer

- All suspected or confirmed AMKL should be managed within a specialist paediatric or adult
  haemato-oncology team from diagnosis, given the need for trephine-based diagnosis, molecular
  workup (GATA1, RBM15-MKL1, CBFA2T3-GLIS2), and risk-stratified treatment selection.
- Refer for allogeneic transplant work-up early in non-Down-syndrome paediatric high-risk disease
  and in adult disease, so that donor identification does not delay consolidation once remission is
  achieved.
- Refer newborns with Down syndrome and unexplained leucocytosis, marked organomegaly, or
  cytopenias for haematology assessment to distinguish transient abnormal myelopoiesis from evolving
  leukaemia.
