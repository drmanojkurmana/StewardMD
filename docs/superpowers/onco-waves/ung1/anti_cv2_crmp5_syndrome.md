# Anti-CV2/CRMP5 Paraneoplastic Syndrome (Anti-CRMP5 syndrome, CV2 antibody syndrome) - Management

## Overview and treatment intent

Anti-CV2/CRMP5 is an antibody-associated paraneoplastic neurologic syndrome (PNS), most commonly linked to small cell lung carcinoma and thymoma. DeVita classifies CV2/CRMP5 as one of the "high-risk" antineuronal antibodies (over 70% association with an underlying cancer), placing it in the same tier as anti-Hu, meaning a positive antibody result should trigger an urgent, structured search for the causative tumor rather than isolated neurologic treatment. Because the phenotype targets an intracellular antigen (a cytoplasmic protein), DeVita's general PNS treatment discussion notes that antibodies against intracellular antigens as a class tend to respond less robustly to immunotherapy than antibodies against cell-surface antigens.

There is no disease-specific staging system for anti-CV2/CRMP5 itself; "stage" in this context refers to (a) whether the underlying malignancy has been found and can be staged, and (b) how advanced the neurologic deficit is at presentation. Treatment intent is therefore twofold and delivered in parallel: oncologic (find and treat the tumor) and neuro-immunologic (limit further antibody/T-cell-mediated injury and support function).

## Tumor-directed therapy

- Identifying and treating the underlying small cell lung carcinoma or thymoma is an essential parallel component of management, delivered alongside immunotherapy rather than as a substitute for it. DeVita's paraneoplastic-syndromes overview notes that successful treatment of the underlying malignancy typically resolves syndromes driven by ectopic hormone production, but that tumor-directed treatment is less effective in immune-mediated syndromes such as PNS, so immunotherapy remains a necessary parallel track rather than an afterthought to tumor control.
- Staging and treatment of the identified malignancy should follow standard histology-specific small cell lung cancer or thymoma pathways (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease); this document does not restate those disease-specific regimens.
- If no tumor is found on initial screening despite a high-risk antibody, DeVita's general PNS section recommends repeating malignancy screening at intervals for roughly two years, since this is the window in which most PNS-associated tumors surface.

## Immunotherapy

DeVita's general treatment discussion of paraneoplastic neurologic syndromes (which covers CV2/CRMP5 as a high-risk antibody within that syndrome group) describes an escalating approach:

- Patients with strong clinical suspicion for a PNS should not wait for antibody confirmation; treatment is started once viral and bacterial infectious mimics have been reasonably excluded.
- First-line (acute) options are corticosteroid pulse therapy, intravenous immunoglobulin, or plasma exchange.
- For patients who do not respond to first-line therapy, DeVita's PNS treatment passage lists second-line options including rituximab, azathioprine, mycophenolate mofetil, or cyclophosphamide, and the same passage goes on to note tocilizumab as an option in rituximab-refractory autoimmune encephalitis.
- For anti-CV2/CRMP5 specifically, a subset of patients shows modest stabilisation or partial improvement with tumor treatment plus immunotherapy; some case-series literature suggests this may trend somewhat more favorably than what is typically reported in anti-Hu or anti-Yo disease, though this comparison is not a quantified or rigorously established finding (general neuro-oncology literature, not from DeVita's section on this disease, which does not report CV2/CRMP5 outcome data).

## Phenotype-directed symptomatic management

DeVita's CV2/CRMP5 table entry lists the phenotype as encephalomyelitis and sensory neuronopathy. The wider neuro-oncology literature also describes cerebellar ataxia, chorea, cranial neuropathy, myelopathy, and a mixed sensorimotor peripheral neuropathy as part of the antibody's clinical spectrum (general neuro-oncology literature, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Supportive management is tailored to the dominant clinical problem:

- Chorea, when present, can be managed symptomatically with dopamine-depleting or antidopaminergic agents (general neuro-oncology literature, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Visual involvement (optic neuritis, retinitis, uveitis) has been described with this antibody and needs a formal ophthalmological assessment and directed treatment to preserve vision if present; early ophthalmology referral is reasonable for any visual complaint (general neuro-oncology literature, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Cerebellar ataxia, myelopathy, and peripheral neuropathy, where present, are supported with physiotherapy and general supportive care, since these deficits are typically slower to reverse even with successful tumor and immune treatment (general oncology/neurology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Consider testing for coexisting anti-Hu or anti-amphiphysin antibodies at diagnosis; the neuro-oncology literature describes anti-CV2/CRMP5 as capable of coexisting with these, which can change the clinical picture and the tumor association and broaden ongoing surveillance (general neuro-oncology literature, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Maintain longitudinal tumor surveillance jointly with neurology and oncology, given the guarded but variable natural history and the chance that the causative tumor is not found on the first screen.
- Track the neurologic exam serially (visual acuity/fields, ataxia, chorea, neuropathy findings) to judge response to immunotherapy and tumor treatment, since improvement in this antibody is more often partial stabilization than full reversal.

## When to refer

- Any patient with a new high-risk antibody result (including CV2/CRMP5) warrants prompt referral to neuro-oncology or neuro-immunology for coordinated immunotherapy decisions alongside oncology-directed tumor treatment.
- Refer to ophthalmology urgently for any visual complaint, given the risk of painless, vision-threatening optic neuritis or retinopathy.
- Refer for staging (chest imaging, mediastinal imaging) as soon as the antibody is identified, so tumor-directed treatment can proceed in parallel with immunotherapy without delay.

## What DeVita's section does not specify

DeVita's disease-specific content on CV2/CRMP5 is limited to a single risk-tier table entry: phenotype "encephalomyelitis, sensory neuronopathy," associated tumors small cell lung carcinoma and thymoma, and a high-risk (over 70%) antibody-cancer association tier. DeVita does not give a CV2/CRMP5-specific immunotherapy sequencing or preferred agent order (the general PNS treatment ladder above was used for that drug-class detail rather than inventing a disease-specific protocol), and it does not describe, for this antibody specifically, the expanded phenotype (cerebellar ataxia, chorea, cranial neuropathy, myelopathy, peripheral neuropathy), chorea-specific symptomatic treatment, the ophthalmic phenotype, coexistence with anti-Hu or anti-amphiphysin antibodies, or a comparative outcome versus anti-Hu or anti-Yo disease. All of those items are drawn from general neuro-oncology literature rather than DeVita's CV2/CRMP5 entry, as flagged inline above.
