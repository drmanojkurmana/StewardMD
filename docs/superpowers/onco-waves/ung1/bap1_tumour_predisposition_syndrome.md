# BAP1 Tumour Predisposition Syndrome - Management

## Overview of the DeVita grounding

DeVita's chapter on cancers of the genitourinary tract (Chapter 43) covers BAP1-associated tumour
predisposition syndrome specifically as one of the inherited renal cancer syndromes, alongside VHL
disease, HPRC, HLRCC, Birt-Hogg-Dube and tuberous sclerosis. The text states that germline BAP1
mutations were identified in a small number of families with predisposition to clear cell kidney
cancer, and that carriers are also at risk of uveal and cutaneous melanoma and malignant mesothelioma.
It confirms BAP1 as a classic two-hit tumour suppressor gene (loss of heterozygosity demonstrated in
carrier tumours), and notes that carriers develop an aggressive, early-onset form of renal cell
carcinoma (RCC) associated with poor survival. DeVita explicitly states that no dedicated clinical
trials of therapy for BAP1-associated RCC have been reported, but that in the COMPARZ trial cohort of
treatment-naive advanced or metastatic RCC patients treated with VEGF-targeting therapy, BAP1
mutation status was associated with reduced progression-free and overall survival. DeVita's coverage
of this syndrome is genetics- and epidemiology-focused; it does not lay out a surveillance protocol or
organ-specific treatment algorithm for BAP1 carriers, so that content below is marked as general
oncology standard rather than DeVita-sourced.

## Treatment approach by organ and stage

There is no treatment that reverses the underlying germline BAP1 defect; management is organised
around early detection and standard oncological treatment of each tumour type as it arises (general
oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

### Renal cell carcinoma
- DeVita specifies that BAP1-mutated RCC tends to present at an earlier age and behaves more
  aggressively, with worse survival than sporadic clear cell RCC.
- For advanced or metastatic clear cell RCC, DeVita's COMPARZ-cohort data show that BAP1 mutation
  carriers treated with VEGF-targeted systemic therapy had shorter progression-free and overall
  survival than non-carriers, though DeVita is explicit that no BAP1-specific treatment trial exists,
  so no preferred agent or line of therapy for this subgroup can be drawn from the text.
- Localised, resectable renal tumours are managed with surgical resection (nephron-sparing surgery
  where feasible given the tendency to multifocal and bilateral disease over a lifetime); locally
  advanced or metastatic disease follows standard clear cell RCC systemic therapy pathways (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

### Uveal melanoma
- Not covered by DeVita's section on this syndrome beyond noting the association.
- Localised uveal melanoma is generally managed with eye-preserving therapy (plaque brachytherapy or
  other radiotherapy techniques) or enucleation for larger tumours, with surveillance for hepatic
  metastasis given the tumour's tropism for liver spread (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).

### Cutaneous melanoma and BAP1-inactivated melanocytic tumours
- Not covered by DeVita's section on this syndrome beyond noting the association.
- Suspicious pigmented or the syndrome's characteristic small dome-shaped melanocytic papules are
  managed by excisional biopsy; confirmed cutaneous melanoma follows standard melanoma staging and
  treatment pathways, including consideration of immunotherapy for advanced disease (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

### Malignant mesothelioma
- Not covered by DeVita's section on this syndrome beyond noting the association; general knowledge
  in the field describes BAP1-associated mesothelioma as more often peritoneal, arising at a younger
  age with limited or absent asbestos exposure, and following a more indolent course than sporadic
  mesothelioma (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Management follows standard mesothelioma pathways: surgical debulking or cytoreduction where
  feasible for localised or peritoneal disease, combined with systemic oncological therapy; an
  antifolate-platinum doublet with or without an anti-angiogenic agent, or immunotherapy-based
  regimens, are used in advanced disease (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).

## Lines of therapy

DeVita does not define a line-of-therapy sequence specific to BAP1-associated tumours. For each
component cancer (RCC, melanoma, mesothelioma), the carrier's tumour is treated according to the
standard staging-based and biomarker-based therapy sequence for that cancer type once it has arisen;
DeVita only flags that BAP1 status appears to be a negative prognostic marker within VEGF-targeted
therapy for advanced RCC, without prescribing an alternative sequence (general oncology standard for
the sequencing statement, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy and systemic therapy
- Surgery is the primary curative modality across the tumour spectrum whenever disease is localised:
  nephron-sparing renal surgery, enucleation or eye-preserving surgery for uveal melanoma, wide local
  excision for cutaneous lesions, and cytoreductive surgery for peritoneal mesothelioma.
- Radiotherapy has a defined role in uveal melanoma (plaque brachytherapy or other radiotherapy as an
  eye-preserving alternative to enucleation).
- Systemic therapy is reserved for locally advanced, unresectable or metastatic disease within each
  tumour type, following that cancer's standard regimen classes (for example VEGF-targeted therapy or
  immunotherapy in advanced RCC, immunotherapy in advanced melanoma, platinum-based systemic therapy in
  mesothelioma).
(All of the above is general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, except the
observation that BAP1 carriers treated with VEGF-targeted therapy for advanced RCC had worse
progression-free and overall survival, which is DeVita-sourced.)

## Monitoring and surveillance

DeVita's section does not lay out a surveillance schedule. General oncology practice for confirmed
BAP1 carriers centres on lifelong, coordinated multi-organ surveillance (general oncology standard, not
from DeVita's section on this disease):
- Regular dilated ophthalmological examination and ocular imaging for uveal melanoma.
- Periodic whole-skin dermatological examination with dermoscopy for cutaneous melanoma and
  BAP1-inactivated melanocytic tumours.
- Periodic cross-sectional abdominal or renal imaging for clear cell RCC, given the tendency toward
  early-onset, multifocal and aggressive disease that DeVita describes.
- Clinical vigilance for symptoms suggesting peritoneal or pleural mesothelioma (unexplained ascites,
  abdominal distension, or pleural effusion).
- Loss of nuclear BAP1 expression on tumour immunohistochemistry can flag a tumour as syndromic and
  should prompt germline testing discussion, an approach consistent with DeVita's description of BAP1
  as a two-hit tumour suppressor detectable by loss of the wild-type allele in tumour tissue.

## When to refer

- Refer to clinical genetics for germline BAP1 testing and cascade testing of at-risk relatives once
  the diagnostic pattern (early-onset RCC, uveal or cutaneous melanoma, mesothelioma, or a suggestive
  family history) is recognised, or when tumour immunohistochemistry shows loss of nuclear BAP1
  staining.
- Refer to ocular oncology for any new or enlarging pigmented ocular lesion.
- Refer to a specialist mesothelioma or thoracic/peritoneal surgical oncology service for suspected
  mesothelioma, particularly when it arises at a young age or without clear asbestos exposure.
- Refer to urological oncology for a renal mass, particularly given the aggressive, early-onset
  behaviour of BAP1-associated RCC noted in DeVita.
(General oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, except where DeVita's
description of aggressive early-onset RCC is cited as the rationale for prompt urological referral.)

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
