## Overview

Trichoepithelioma is a benign adnexal tumour of follicular differentiation. It occurs either as a
solitary sporadic papule or, in its multiple familial form (linked to CYLD mutations and the
Brooke-Spiegler spectrum), as clusters of skin-coloured papules on the central face (general oncology
standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Because it is benign, management is guided by
diagnostic certainty, cosmetic burden, and surveillance for the histologic mimic it is most often
confused with, basal cell carcinoma, rather than by oncologic staging or systemic therapy.

## Treatment approach by presentation

- **Solitary lesion:** complete surgical excision is both diagnostic and curative. Excision confirms
  the histology (ruling out basal cell carcinoma) and removes the lesion in one step (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Multiple familial lesions (Brooke-Spiegler spectrum):** treatment is directed at cosmetic burden
  rather than cure, since lesions are numerous and recur. Ablative/destructive modalities such as
  carbon dioxide laser, electrosurgery, or dermabrasion are used to reduce the visible burden of
  facial papules, with an understanding that recurrence is expected and repeat treatment is often
  needed over time (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- There is no established role for radiotherapy or systemic (cytotoxic, targeted, or immunotherapy)
  treatment in trichoepithelioma; this is a benign tumour and such therapies are not part of its
  management (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Lines of therapy

Trichoepithelioma does not have oncologic "lines of therapy" in the systemic-treatment sense.
Sequencing is procedural: excisional biopsy first for any solitary or diagnostically uncertain
lesion, followed, for the multiple familial phenotype, by staged destructive/ablative treatment of
the residual cosmetic burden, with retreatment as lesions recur (general oncology standard, not
from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy

- **Surgery** is the definitive tool for solitary lesions and for any lesion whose behaviour raises
  concern for malignancy (enlargement, ulceration, infiltrative change), where excisional biopsy both
  treats and rules out basal cell carcinoma (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease).
- **Ablative/destructive procedures** (laser, electrosurgery, dermabrasion) substitute for excision
  in the multifocal familial form, where excising every lesion is impractical; these are palliative
  for cosmesis, not curative, and recurrence is the expected trade-off (general oncology standard,
  not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Radiotherapy and systemic therapy** have no defined role; this reflects the benign nature of the
  tumour rather than a treatment gap (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).

## Monitoring

- Clinical follow-up through dermatology (and dermatopathology for any biopsied lesion) to watch for
  new or changing lesions, since trichoepithelioma and basal cell carcinoma can closely resemble one
  another and a stable lesion that begins to enlarge, ulcerate, or infiltrate should prompt rebiopsy
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- In the multiple familial/CYLD-related form, monitoring extends to screening the patient and at-risk
  relatives for the other tumours of the Brooke-Spiegler spectrum, cylindromas and spiradenomas
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Malignant transformation of trichoepithelioma itself is rare, but any lesion behaving atypically
  should be treated with a low threshold for rebiopsy rather than reassurance (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Refer to dermatology/dermatopathology for any new, changing, or diagnostically uncertain facial
  papule, and for histologic confirmation whenever basal cell carcinoma cannot be confidently excluded
  clinically (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer for CYLD genetic counselling and testing when multiple familial trichoepithelioma or a wider
  Brooke-Spiegler phenotype (coexisting cylindromas/spiradenomas, a suggestive family history) is
  suspected (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer to plastic surgery for reconstruction or staged cosmetic management when facial lesions are
  numerous or disfiguring, and consider psychological support for the cosmetic morbidity of extensive
  disease (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
