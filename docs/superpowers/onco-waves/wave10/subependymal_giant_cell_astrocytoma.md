# Subependymal giant cell astrocytoma (SEGA) — Management

## Overview and biology
SEGA arises in patients with tuberous sclerosis complex, driven by germline TSC1 or TSC2 mutations. DeVita groups SEGA together with pilocytic astrocytoma, ganglioglioma and pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma as an "astroglial variant" form of low-grade glioma (grade I). These circumscribed, non-diffusely-invasive lesions less frequently progress to a higher grade, and for this group of tumours DeVita states that surgery alone is often curative. SEGA's periventricular, subependymal or intraventricular location gives it a predilection to obstruct cerebrospinal fluid flow and cause hydrocephalus early in its course, which is the main driver of morbidity and of urgency in management (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, for the hydrocephalus-driven urgency specifically, though the intraventricular-location/CSF-obstruction association itself is stated by DeVita in its chapter on raised intracranial pressure).

## Treatment approach by clinical scenario

**Acute or symptomatic hydrocephalus, or a lesion causing significant mass effect**
- Surgical resection is the definitive treatment; for this astroglial-variant group of low-grade tumours (which DeVita explicitly includes SEGA within), resection alone is often curative.
- If hydrocephalus needs urgent relief independent of tumour removal, cerebrospinal fluid diversion (ventriculoperitoneal shunt or endoscopic third ventriculostomy) is used (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Escalate urgently to neurosurgery for any sign of raised intracranial pressure (worsening headache, vomiting, papilloedema, decline in consciousness or cognition) (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

**Growing lesion not requiring emergency surgery**
- An mTOR inhibitor, everolimus, is FDA approved specifically for SEGA and is a therapeutic option here; DeVita states everolimus is approved for use in SEGA, among other mTOR-driven conditions, and that its mechanism is inhibition of mTOR complex 1, the pathway constitutively activated when the TSC1/TSC2 (hamartin/tuberin) complex is lost. Everolimus can be used to reduce tumour volume in lesions that do not need emergency surgical decompression (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, for the volume-reduction indication as distinct from the FDA-approval fact itself).
- Everolimus is generally continued as long as it controls tumour growth, with surgery reserved for lesions that progress on medical therapy or that develop acute complications (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

**Stable, non-growing subependymal lesion (subependymal nodule)**
- No specific oncologic treatment; the distinguishing feature from SEGA is a lack of growth on serial imaging, and these are followed rather than treated (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Lines of therapy
DeVita does not lay out a numbered first-line/second-line sequence specific to SEGA. What DeVita does support is: surgery as the definitive, often-curative treatment for this astroglial-variant tumour group, and everolimus as an FDA-approved medical option for SEGA. In practice, surgery is used first when there is an emergency or a resectable, symptomatic lesion, and an mTOR inhibitor is used first when surgery is not immediately required and volume reduction is the goal; radiotherapy has no defined established role for SEGA in the material reviewed and is not mentioned by DeVita for this tumour, so it is omitted here rather than presented as a treatment line.

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy and systemic therapy
- **Surgery**: definitive and, per DeVita's description of this tumour group, often curative on its own; the appropriate choice for symptomatic tumours or acute hydrocephalus.
- **Radiotherapy**: DeVita's text reviewed here does not describe a role for radiotherapy in SEGA; no such claim is included.
- **Systemic (targeted) therapy**: the only systemic agent DeVita explicitly ties to SEGA is the mTOR inhibitor everolimus, used to shrink tumour volume in lesions not needing emergency surgery. DeVita does not describe conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy having a role in SEGA.

## Monitoring
- Serial contrast-enhanced MRI to track the size of the periventricular/foramen-of-Monro lesion, since growth on serial imaging is what distinguishes a SEGA from a stable subependymal nodule (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Ongoing surveillance imaging as part of tuberous sclerosis complex follow-up, aiming to detect enlarging lesions before they cause hydrocephalus (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Monitoring should also cover the wider tuberous sclerosis complex phenotype (skin, cardiac, renal, retinal manifestations) and seizure control, since SEGA occurs in this hereditary syndrome (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease; the TSC1/TSC2-driven hereditary association itself is supported by DeVita's chapter on hereditary CNS tumour syndromes).

## When to refer
- Refer urgently to neurosurgery for any feature of raised intracranial pressure or acute hydrocephalus (worsening headache, vomiting, papilloedema, declining consciousness, new focal deficit or a marked change in seizure pattern) (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer to neuro-oncology or a centre experienced with mTOR inhibitor therapy when a lesion is growing but does not need emergency surgery, to weigh medical therapy against elective resection (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer for tuberous sclerosis complex multidisciplinary care and genetic counselling once a SEGA is found, given its autosomal dominant TSC1/TSC2 basis (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease; the TSC1/TSC2 hereditary link itself is grounded in DeVita).

## Deliberate omission
DeVita's reviewed text does not describe a role for radiotherapy, cytotoxic chemotherapy, or any staging/line-of-therapy sequence specific to SEGA beyond surgery and everolimus; these were omitted rather than inferred.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
