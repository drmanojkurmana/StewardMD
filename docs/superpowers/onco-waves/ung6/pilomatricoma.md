# Pilomatricoma - Management

**Grounding note:** DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed. was searched for "pilomatricoma", "pilomatrixoma", and "pilomatrix carcinoma" and contains no dedicated coverage of this entity. Pilomatricoma is an overwhelmingly benign adnexal skin tumour that falls outside the scope of a general medical oncology textbook, so this content is guideline-general and drawn from standard dermatology/dermatopathology and surgical oncology practice rather than from DeVita. ## Treatment approach by presentation

Almost all pilomatricomas are managed as benign lesions (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

- **Typical solitary pilomatricoma (firm, mobile, slow-growing nodule, classic tent sign):** complete surgical excision is both diagnostic and curative. No systemic staging or oncology referral is needed for a lesion with a classic clinical and/or ultrasound appearance (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Small, asymptomatic, cosmetically acceptable lesions in a patient who prefers to avoid surgery:** clinical observation with interval follow-up is a reasonable alternative, provided the lesion behaves in an indolent, stable fashion (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Atypical, rapidly enlarging, ulcerated, fixed, or infiltrative lesions:** these features should prompt suspicion for the rare malignant counterpart, pilomatrix carcinoma, and change management toward wider excision and closer surveillance (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of surgery

- Excision (simple or elliptical, with margins adequate to remove the lesion intact) is the mainstay and definitive treatment for benign pilomatricoma; recurrence after complete excision is uncommon (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- For lesions with any clinical concern for pilomatrix carcinoma (rapid growth, size, fixation, ulceration, recurrence after prior excision), wide local excision with attention to margin clearance is preferred over simple excision, given the malignant variant's potential for local recurrence and, rarely, metastasis (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- All excised specimens, even those clinically typical, should go to histopathology to confirm the diagnosis (basaloid matrix cells maturing into anucleate "shadow"/"ghost" cells with dystrophic calcification) and to exclude malignant transformation (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of radiotherapy and systemic therapy

- There is no established role for radiotherapy or systemic (chemotherapy/targeted) therapy in benign pilomatricoma; surgery alone is curative in the great majority of cases (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- For pilomatrix carcinoma with nodal or distant spread, management follows a multidisciplinary, case-by-case approach analogous to other cutaneous adnexal carcinomas (wide excision plus consideration of regional lymph node evaluation), but this is not a standardized regimen and specific systemic agents are not well-established for this rare tumour (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Do not present a specific chemotherapy or radiotherapy regimen as established for pilomatrix carcinoma, as this is not grounded in a standard reference.

## Monitoring

- After excision of a classic benign pilomatricoma, routine long-term oncologic surveillance is not required beyond standard post-surgical wound follow-up, given the excellent prognosis and low recurrence rate (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- For lesions excised with any atypical feature, or for confirmed pilomatrix carcinoma, closer clinical follow-up for local recurrence is warranted, with imaging and nodal assessment guided by clinical findings (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- When multiple pilomatricomas are present, evaluate for associated syndromic conditions (for example myotonic dystrophy), which changes the monitoring frame from a purely dermatologic problem to one involving broader systemic follow-up (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Refer to dermatology or general/plastic surgery for excisional biopsy of any suspected pilomatricoma; this is typically sufficient for both diagnosis and cure (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer to surgical oncology (and consider a multidisciplinary tumour board) for any lesion with clinical or histopathological features raising concern for pilomatrix carcinoma, given the malignant variant's capacity for local recurrence and metastasis (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer for genetic/syndromic evaluation when pilomatricomas are multiple, particularly in the context of myotonic dystrophy or other recognised associations (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Deliberate omissions

No specific chemotherapy regimen, radiotherapy dose/field, biomarker-directed therapy, or staging system for pilomatrix carcinoma is included, because none of these were found in DeVita and no other verified reference was consulted for this pass; inventing regimen names or statistics for a tumour this rare would risk misinformation.
