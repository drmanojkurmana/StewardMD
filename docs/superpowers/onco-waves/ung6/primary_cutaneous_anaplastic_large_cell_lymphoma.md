# Primary Cutaneous Anaplastic Large-Cell Lymphoma (C-ALCL) - Management

## Staging and initial workup
All patients presenting with lesions suggestive of C-ALCL should undergo careful staging to rule out
systemic (nodal or extracutaneous) involvement before the disease is classified as primary cutaneous
disease, since C-ALCL is defined precisely by the cutaneous presentation in the absence of systemic
disease. Most patients present with a solitary nodule, tumor, or ulcerating lesion, and these lesions
may spontaneously regress; multifocal skin disease is seen in a minority of patients (up to about 20%).

## Localized disease: first-line approach
- The overall prognosis of C-ALCL is excellent, and most patients are managed with surgical excision
  and/or local radiotherapy directed at the lesion(s).
- Local radiotherapy alone may be adequate for definitive control of a solitary or localized lesion;
  dose and fractionation are set by the treating radiation oncologist per institutional protocol.
- Because lesions can regress spontaneously, a period of observation is a reasonable option in a
  patient with an asymptomatic, regressing lesion (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).

## Recurrent or multifocal skin disease
- For recurrent disease confined to the skin, low-dose oral methotrexate or other cytotoxic agents
  may be used.
- Multifocal cutaneous disease at presentation, while less common, does not by itself indicate
  systemic disease, but should prompt the same careful staging as solitary disease to exclude nodal
  or visceral spread.

## Relapsed/refractory disease and systemic therapy
- Brentuximab vedotin, a CD30-directed antibody-drug conjugate, has an established role in relapsed
  or refractory C-ALCL. In the ALCANZA trial, patients with CD30-expressing cutaneous lymphoma
  (including C-ALCL) were treated with brentuximab vedotin, and the response rate was notably high
  (87%) in the subset with only cutaneous involvement.
- Based on this trial, brentuximab vedotin is approved for patients with C-ALCL who progress after
  radiotherapy or after at least one systemic therapy, i.e. it is positioned as a later-line option
  rather than routine first-line therapy for localized disease.
- Peripheral neuropathy is the most frequent adverse event associated with brentuximab vedotin and
  is reversible in the majority of affected patients; this should be monitored for during therapy.
- Multi-agent cytotoxic chemotherapy (regimens historically used in refractory cutaneous T-cell
  lymphoma more broadly, such as an etoposide/vincristine/anthracycline/cyclophosphamide/oral
  corticosteroid combination) can produce high response rates but responses are typically not durable,
  and such combination chemotherapy carries substantial myelosuppression and infection risk; it is
  generally reserved for patients who are refractory to more targeted approaches or who have extensive
  disease requiring rapid palliation, rather than used as routine therapy for localized C-ALCL (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Nucleoside analogs (for example pentostatin) and gemcitabine have documented activity in the broader
  category of refractory cutaneous T-cell lymphoma and may be considered in refractory systemic-type
  presentations, generally as single agents given the modest incremental benefit and added toxicity of
  combinations; patients receiving nucleoside analogs require prophylactic antimicrobial and antiviral
  coverage given the risk of opportunistic infection (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).

## Distinguishing from systemic ALCL and lymphomatoid papulosis
- C-ALCL is histopathologically similar to systemic ALCL but differs in that it lacks the ALK
  translocation that is characteristic of (and common in) systemic ALK-positive ALCL, and it does not
  express epithelial membrane antigen, which systemic ALCL does express. These features support the
  pathologic distinction from systemic disease and should inform the staging workup.
- C-ALCL and lymphomatoid papulosis (LyP) can be clinically and histopathologically indistinguishable,
  and up to about 20% of CD30+ lymphoproliferative cases may be preceded by or followed by another
  lymphoma (including mycosis fungoides, ALCL, or Hodgkin lymphoma). Ongoing dermatologic surveillance
  for evolution or a second lymphoma is therefore appropriate.

## Monitoring
- After treatment of a localized lesion, patients should be followed clinically for local recurrence,
  new cutaneous lesions, and any signs suggesting systemic spread, given the recognized potential for
  overlap with systemic lymphomas noted above.
- Patients treated with brentuximab vedotin should be monitored for peripheral neuropathy and other
  infusion-related adverse events during and after treatment.
- Routine surveillance intervals and imaging schedules are not specified in DeVita's section on this
  disease; specific follow-up cadence should follow institutional/NCCN cutaneous lymphoma surveillance
  practice (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer
- Refer to (or manage jointly with) a dermatology/cutaneous-lymphoma specialist center at diagnosis,
  given the need for careful staging to distinguish C-ALCL from systemic ALCL and from lymphomatoid
  papulosis.
- Refer for radiation oncology assessment when local radiotherapy is being considered for a localized
  lesion.
- Refer to a lymphoma/hematology-oncology specialist for evaluation of systemic therapy (brentuximab
  vedotin or cytotoxic options) in patients with recurrent, multifocal, or refractory disease, or in
  any patient in whom staging raises concern for systemic involvement.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
