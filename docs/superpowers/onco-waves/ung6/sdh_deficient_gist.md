# SDH-deficient gastrointestinal stromal tumour (SDH-deficient GIST) - management narrative

## Overview and treatment philosophy

SDH-deficient GIST is a biologically distinct, wild-type (KIT/PDGFRA-negative) subgroup of GIST arising from loss of function of the succinate dehydrogenase complex, confirmed by negative SDHB immunohistochemistry. It typically arises in the stomach in children, adolescents and young women, is frequently multifocal, and can metastasise to lymph nodes, yet often runs an indolent, protracted course even with metastatic disease. Because tumour growth is independent of KIT/PDGFRA signalling, this subtype is characteristically resistant to imatinib and to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) in general, so the treatment approach differs substantially from conventional KIT-mutant GIST: surgery is the dominant modality, medical therapy plays a secondary and less effective role, and lifelong surveillance is required because of multifocality and hereditary tumour risk.

## Treatment by stage/intent

**Localised or multifocal resectable disease.** Complete surgical resection is the mainstay of treatment. Surgery is typically a wedge or segmental gastric resection; more extensive resection (for example a more extensive gastrectomy) is used only when required for complete removal of a large or multifocal lesion. Because SDH-deficient GIST is often confined to the stomach and can be multifocal, the extent of surgery must be individualised, weighing the risk of recurrence, the lack of benefit from currently available TKIs, and the tumour's often indolent natural history: some lesions can be observed or managed conservatively while others behave more aggressively and warrant resection. Routine lymphadenectomy is not required in GIST generally; nodes are resected only when clinically suspicious. Preoperative (neoadjuvant) TKI downsizing, which is a recognised strategy in KIT/PDGFRA-mutant GIST to allow more limited surgery, is not established for the SDH-deficient subtype because of its resistance to standard TKIs.

**Adjuvant therapy.** Adjuvant imatinib is standard practice in KIT/PDGFRA-mutant GIST with significant relapse risk, but wild-type (including SDH-deficient) GISTs are much less sensitive to imatinib and have a natural history that is often less aggressive; for these reasons, patients with SDH-deficient GIST are not currently selected for adjuvant TKI therapy.

**Unresectable or metastatic disease.** When disease is metastatic or locally unresectable, standard GIST practice is indefinite medical therapy with a TKI, but SDH-deficient tumours are essentially not KIT/PDGFRA-driven and are largely insensitive to imatinib. Sunitinib and regorafenib have shown some activity specifically in SDH-deficient GIST and can be considered in this setting. The treatment decision should account for the frequently indolent natural history of this subtype compared with KIT-mutated GIST, meaning observation or staged, judicious surgery for individual metastatic lesions may be reasonable in appropriately selected patients rather than reflexive continuous TKI therapy.

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy

- Surgery is the primary and most effective treatment modality for SDH-deficient GIST, including judicious resection of localised metastatic deposits, given the limited effectiveness of available TKIs in this subtype.
- Radiotherapy has no described role in the GIST management approach found; GIST treatment is built around surgery and TKI systemic therapy (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Systemic therapy (imatinib) is largely ineffective in this subtype; antiangiogenic TKIs (sunitinib, regorafenib) are the systemic options with reported activity in SDH-deficient GIST specifically.
- Management should occur within a specialist sarcoma multidisciplinary team given the rarity and syndromic associations of this subtype (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Hereditary and syndromic evaluation

A substantial proportion of SDH-deficient GIST is associated with germline mutations of the SDH subunit genes (SDHA, SDHB, SDHC, SDHD) and with recognised syndromes: the Carney triad (GIST, pulmonary chondroma, paraganglioma, often linked to SDHC promoter hypermethylation rather than germline mutation) and the Carney-Stratakis syndrome (an autosomal dominant condition of GIST plus paraganglioma from germline SDH mutation). Because of this, when mutational analysis excludes KIT and PDGFRA mutations, SDHB (and, if negative, SDHA) immunohistochemistry should be assessed, and patients with confirmed SDH deficiency should undergo genetic counselling and germline SDHX testing. All SDH-deficient patients carry a risk of paraganglioma, which can justify periodic whole-body imaging (for example annual MRI), while those found to carry a non-sporadic germline SDHX mutation require ongoing genetic counselling and cascade testing of at-risk relatives.

## Monitoring

- Lifelong surveillance imaging is warranted given the persistent but often indolent metastatic potential of this subtype.
- Periodic whole-body imaging (for example annual MRI) is reasonable for paraganglioma screening in confirmed SDH-deficient patients.
- Tumour response to any systemic therapy used should be monitored closely; PET imaging can demonstrate responsiveness within weeks where systemic therapy is trialled (general oncology standard extrapolated from GIST TKI-response monitoring, not specific to the DeVita section on this disease's own management).

## When to refer

- Refer to a specialist sarcoma multidisciplinary team for surgical planning, particularly given multifocality and the judgement required to balance resection against the tumour's often indolent course.
- Refer for genetic counselling and germline SDH testing whenever SDHB (or SDHA) immunohistochemistry confirms SDH deficiency, given the frequent hereditary basis and implications for family screening.
- Refer for paraganglioma/phaeochromocytoma work-up (biochemical and imaging) in any confirmed SDH-deficient patient, and evaluate for pulmonary chondroma if a Carney triad is suspected.

## What DeVita's section does not specify

DeVita's GIST chapter does not give a disease-specific staging system, dose or schedule for sunitinib or regorafenib in SDH-deficient GIST, nor does it specify a fixed surveillance interval or duration of systemic therapy for this subtype; these gaps are stated here rather than filled in.
