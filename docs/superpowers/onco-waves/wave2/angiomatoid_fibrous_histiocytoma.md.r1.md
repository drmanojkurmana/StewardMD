# R1 Clinical-Safety Review — angiomatoid_fibrous_histiocytoma

VERDICT: REVISE

## Summary
Clinically SAFE, well-hedged, and DOSE-FREE, but it fails GROUNDING: two claims
not present in DeVita's AFH text are presented as DeVita-sourced and are NOT
disclosed in the closing caveat. The adversarial verdict flagged both; both are
still present and still undisclosed. Per the R1 rule, that is a mandatory REVISE
(the doc cites DeVita as its sole source for content DeVita does not contain).
The claims are clinically uncontroversial and safe, so this is REVISE, not REJECT.

## 1. SAFETY — PASS
- No unsafe, misleading, or harmful directive. Surgery-first framing, "curative
  in most patients," selective RT, no routine systemic therapy — all match the
  benign natural history and would not cause harm if followed.
- Strong scope hygiene: metastatic care routed to sarcoma MDT, explicit warning
  NOT to extrapolate high-grade sarcoma cytotoxic regimens onto AFH. Good.
- No absolute/overreaching decision-support claims; language is appropriately
  probabilistic ("most," "usually," "consider," "individualized").

## 2. GROUNDING — FAIL (drives the verdict)
DeVita's only AFH content is: WHO "intermediate, rarely metastasizing" tier +
metastatic risk "typically <2%, usually to lymph nodes or lung," plus the bare
name in the classification table. The core surgery/RT/systemic framing is
consistent with DeVita/NCCN standard of care. But:

- **EWSR1/FUS molecular confirmation + Ewing/clear cell sarcoma mimics** (line
  11): true pathology, but NOT in DeVita's AFH text and presented as a flat
  recommendation. Not disclosed in the line-43 caveat, which only covers
  surgery/RT/follow-up. Reads as DeVita-attributed when it is not.
- **"Systemic symptoms as a management marker" section** (lines 28-29): the
  cytokine-mediated paraneoplastic syndrome is a real published AFH feature but
  is absent from DeVita, yet is promoted to its own top-level section and reused
  as a monitoring signal (line 34) — i.e., presented as established
  DeVita-grounded fact, not flagged as extra-source.
- Cross-tier "wide excision... mirrors other intermediate, locally aggressive
  soft-tissue tumours" (line 10): borrowed from a DIFFERENT WHO tier than AFH's
  own. Acceptable as hedged ("mirrors") but should be named in the caveat for
  consistency. Borderline, not blocking on its own.

## 3. DOSE-FREE — PASS
No mg, mg/m2, AUC, or numbered schedule. Only numerics are the "<2%" metastatic
epidemiology (matches DeVita) — not dosing. Clean.

## 4. SCOPE — PASS
Appropriately hedged as decision-support: MDT/referral triggers, "individualized
through a sarcoma tumour board," "no fixed protocol," explicit non-extrapolation
warning. Not directive.

## Required changes to reach APPROVE
1. Relabel the EWSR1/FUS + mimics claim as general pathology/molecular standard
   (NOT DeVita-specific), or move it into the "what is deliberately not stated"
   disclosed-extrapolation section. It is uncontroversial and may stay as
   clinical content — it must simply stop being attributed to DeVita.
2. Either cut the "Systemic symptoms as a management marker" section or
   explicitly flag it as published-literature content beyond DeVita. As written
   it mis-attributes a paraneoplastic-syndrome claim (and its downstream
   monitoring use) to DeVita.
3. (Minor) Name the cross-tier "wide excision" inference in the closing caveat
   for consistency.

Both flagged claims are clinically uncontroversial and safe; the block is purely
mis-attribution to DeVita, not clinical danger.

Source checked against: DeVita, Hellman & Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles &
Practice of Oncology, 12th ed. (only AFH mentions: WHO tier + <2% met risk).
