# Juvenile myelomonocytic leukaemia (JMML) - Management

## Overview of treatment intent

JMML is an aggressive clonal disorder of early childhood with a RAS-pathway driver mutation (PTPN11, NRAS, KRAS, NF1 or CBL). Left untreated it is rapidly progressive and fatal in most children, so for the majority of patients the intent is curative, built around transplant rather than chemotherapy alone (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Treatment by disease category

- **Typical (RAS-driven, progressive) JMML:** allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation is the principal curative treatment for most children (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **CBL-mutated and Noonan-syndrome-associated forms:** a subset behaves indolently or self-resolves, so a period of watchful waiting/observation rather than immediate transplant is appropriate once these genotypes are confirmed (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **NF1-associated disease:** transplant remains the mainstay, alongside recognition and management of the underlying neurofibromatosis type 1 (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Lines of therapy

1. **Pre-transplant cytoreduction:** a hypomethylating agent or low-intensity chemotherapy approach is used to reduce disease burden and control organomegaly/cytopenias ahead of transplant, rather than as definitive therapy on its own (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
2. **Allogeneic stem cell transplantation:** the definitive, potentially curative step for most children with progressive disease (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
3. **Relapse after transplant:** managed with donor lymphocyte infusion or a second transplant, reflecting that relapse is a recognised and treatable event rather than automatically terminal (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of surgery and radiotherapy

- **Splenectomy** has a selected, adjunctive role for symptomatic splenomegaly or to facilitate engraftment; it is not a primary curative measure (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Radiotherapy** has no established primary role in JMML management; conditioning-related irradiation, where used, falls under transplant protocols rather than disease-directed radiotherapy and is not detailed here.

## Role of systemic therapy

Systemic therapy in JMML is chiefly a bridge to transplant (hypomethylating agent or low-intensity chemotherapy for cytoreduction) rather than a stand-alone curative modality, and supportive systemic care (blood product transfusion, prompt treatment of infection) is needed throughout the disease course (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Close monitoring for relapse after transplant, since relapse is recognised and salvage options (donor lymphocyte infusion, second transplant) exist (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Ongoing surveillance of blood counts, organomegaly and infection risk during cytoreduction and the peri-transplant period (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- For CBL-mutated/Noonan-associated disease managed with watchful waiting, monitoring is needed to detect any transition to a more progressive course (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Any child with suspected JMML (monocytosis, hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenias, characteristic rash) should be referred promptly to a paediatric haematology/oncology and transplant centre given the disease's rapid progression and the central role of transplant (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer for genetic counselling and family evaluation where an underlying germline predisposition syndrome (neurofibromatosis type 1, Noonan syndrome) is identified (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Delay in referral for transplantation is a recognised pitfall and should be avoided (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
