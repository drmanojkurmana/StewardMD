# Indolent T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder of the GI tract (iTLPD-GI) - Management

## Sourcing note

DeVita's Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma chapter lists "Indolent T-cell lymphoproliferative disorder of the gastrointestinal tract" only as a named entity within the WHO classification table of mature T- and NK-cell neoplasms. It gives no dedicated clinical, staging, or treatment discussion for this specific entity. The same chapter does discuss the related, distinct aggressive entities enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL) and monomorphic epitheliotropic intestinal T-cell lymphoma (MEITL) in detail, but iTLPD-GI is explicitly a separate, indolent condition and that EATL/MEITL treatment content does not apply to it. Because of this, essentially all management content below is general oncology / haematology standard, not DeVita-sourced, and is marked accordingly. This disease should be flagged for manual sourcing against WHO classification guidance and haemato-oncology society statements, since DeVita does not substantively cover it.

## Treatment approach by stage and intent

There is no formal staging system specific to iTLPD-GI because it is a diffuse mucosal process rather than a nodal lymphoma with anatomic stages (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Intent of management is determined by symptom burden and disease behaviour rather than by stage:

- Asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic disease found incidentally on endoscopy: a watch-and-wait approach with clinical and endoscopic surveillance, since the condition is indolent and often does not progress for years (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Symptomatic disease (chronic diarrhoea, abdominal pain, malabsorption, weight loss): symptom-directed and nutritional management first, reserving systemic therapy for those with significant or progressive symptoms (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Suspected transformation to an aggressive lymphoma: re-biopsy and re-staging, then management as the aggressive lymphoma it has transformed into rather than as iTLPD-GI (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Lines of therapy

- First line for symptomatic disease: supportive and nutritional measures (antidiarrhoeal agents, nutritional supplementation, correction of deficiencies) rather than cytotoxic therapy (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- If systemic therapy is required, low-intensity approaches are generally favoured over aggressive combination chemotherapy, which has not been shown to be effective for this indolent process and risks disproportionate toxicity (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- In cases with identified activating STAT3-JAK2 fusions or other JAK-STAT pathway lesions, JAK-STAT pathway-directed therapy has been proposed as a rational, targeted option, though this is based on the underlying molecular biology of the disease rather than large trial data (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Aggressive multi-agent chemotherapy regimens used for peripheral T-cell lymphomas or for EATL/MEITL are not appropriate first-line choices for iTLPD-GI and should be reserved for confirmed transformation to an aggressive lymphoma (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of surgery and radiotherapy

- Surgery has no routine role in iTLPD-GI itself; it is reserved for complications such as bowel obstruction, perforation, or bleeding, which are more typical of the aggressive intestinal T-cell lymphomas than of the indolent disorder (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Radiotherapy is not a standard component of management for this indolent, diffusely infiltrative mucosal process (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Systemic therapy, when needed, is directed at controlling symptoms or treating confirmed transformation rather than achieving eradication of the clonal infiltrate (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Long-term endoscopic and clinical surveillance is central to management, given the chronic, relapsing but generally stable course (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Surveillance should watch specifically for signs of transformation: rapidly progressive symptoms, a new mass lesion, bowel obstruction or perforation, development of B symptoms, or a rising lactate dehydrogenase (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Nutritional status and markers of malabsorption should be monitored periodically, since refractory malabsorption with severe weight loss is itself a red flag warranting reassessment (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Coeliac serology and response to a gluten-free trial can be reassessed if the diagnosis is in doubt, since lack of response to a gluten-free diet is a distinguishing feature from coeliac-associated enteropathy (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Diagnosis and ongoing management should sit within a haemato-oncology multidisciplinary team given the rarity and diagnostic overlap with coeliac disease, inflammatory bowel disease, and the aggressive intestinal T-cell lymphomas (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer promptly for reassessment (repeat endoscopy and biopsy, cross-sectional imaging) if there is any change to a more rapidly progressive course, a new mass, obstructive symptoms, B symptoms, or rising lactate dehydrogenase, as these suggest transformation to a higher-grade lymphoma requiring management as an aggressive lymphoma (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer to specialist molecular pathology where available if targeted therapy directed at a STAT3-JAK2 or related fusion is being considered (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
