# Krukenberg tumour - management narrative

## Grounding note

DeVita has no dedicated chapter or subsection on Krukenberg tumour management. The term
appears only as an incidental cross-reference inside the Stomach, Ovarian, and Gallbladder
cancer chapters (bilateral ovarian involvement as a presentation of advanced gastric cancer,
the "Blumer shelf" pelvic implant, and Krukenberg tumour listed among metastatic tumours to
the ovary). None of these passages describe how to treat the ovarian metastasis itself. The
narrative below is therefore built on the existing KB reference entry (WHO Classification of
Female Genital Tumours / Robbins & Cotran) plus general oncology principles, and every
management claim is labelled per the sourcing rule below rather than attributed to DeVita.

## Overview

A Krukenberg tumour is a metastatic, mucin-producing signet-ring cell carcinoma involving
the ovary, most commonly seeded from a gastric primary and less often from colon, appendix,
breast, gallbladder/biliary, or cervical primaries. It is by definition secondary disease, and
its presence signals that the underlying primary is already disseminated. Management is
therefore directed at the primary malignancy and its overall stage, not at the ovarian
deposit as an isolated problem (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
disease).

## Treatment approach by intent

- **Identify and stage the primary first.** Before committing to a treatment plan, confirm the
  site of origin (upper and lower endoscopy, breast assessment, cross-sectional imaging of
  chest/abdomen/pelvis, and immunohistochemistry such as CK7/CK20/CDX2) since therapy is
  chosen according to the primary tumour type, not the ovarian metastasis (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Systemic (palliative) intent is the default**, because a Krukenberg tumour indicates
  stage IV disease of the primary cancer in essentially all cases. Treatment aims at disease
  control and symptom palliation rather than cure (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Curative intent is rare** and would only be considered in the unusual scenario of a
  solitary, resectable primary with isolated, completely resectable ovarian disease and no
  other metastatic sites; this is an exception rather than the rule and decisions of this kind
  belong with a multidisciplinary tumour board (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).

## Lines of therapy / systemic treatment

- First-line treatment follows the standard regimen for the identified primary. For a gastric
  or gastroesophageal primary this is typically a fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-based
  combination (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Biomarker-directed therapy should be considered where applicable to the primary, including
  HER2 status and mismatch-repair/microsatellite-instability or PD-L1 status, which can direct
  the addition of targeted or immune checkpoint therapy in gastric and other gastrointestinal
  primaries (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Subsequent lines of therapy are chosen on progression using the same principles that apply
  to metastatic disease from that primary site, with therapy intensity and eligibility guided
  by performance status and organ function (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).

## Role of surgery

- Surgery for the ovarian mass itself (bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, sometimes with
  cytoreduction) is generally palliative, undertaken to relieve pain, adnexal torsion, mass
  effect, bowel obstruction, or hormonally driven bleeding/virilisation from the metastatic
  stroma, rather than to achieve cure. This is consistent with the existing KB reference entry
  for this disease.
- Surgery on the ovaries should be considered only once the primary is characterised and, where
  feasible, when the primary disease is being controlled, so the operation is not undertaken in
  isolation from the overall disease burden (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).
- Definitive surgery directed at the primary tumour (for example gastrectomy) follows the
  standard indications and resectability criteria for that primary cancer and is not altered
  simply because an ovarian metastasis is present, beyond confirming that overall disease is
  not too extensive to benefit from resection (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).

## Role of radiotherapy

DeVita's incidental references to Krukenberg tumour do not describe a role for radiotherapy in
this setting, and there is no well-established primary role for radiotherapy directed at the
ovarian metastasis itself. Radiotherapy may occasionally be used for localised symptom control
(for example pelvic pain or bleeding) as part of palliative care, following the same principles
used for palliation of pelvic metastatic disease from other primaries (general oncology
standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Serial cross-sectional imaging and tumour markers appropriate to the primary (for example
  CA19-9 and CEA for a gastrointestinal primary, with CA125 sometimes followed given ovarian
  involvement) are used to track response and detect progression. This is consistent with the
  existing KB reference entry for this disease.
- Monitoring should also track the hormonally active stroma clinically, since resolution or
  recurrence of abnormal uterine bleeding or virilisation can reflect disease activity (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Ongoing surveillance for and management of complications common to advanced intra-abdominal
  malignancy, including bowel obstruction, ascites, and venous thromboembolism, should be built
  into follow-up (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Refer promptly to a multidisciplinary oncology team spanning upper gastrointestinal surgery,
  medical oncology, and gynaecological oncology as soon as a Krukenberg tumour is suspected or
  confirmed, since the primary site drives both surgical and systemic decision-making (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer to gynaecological oncology specifically when ovarian mass-related symptoms (pain,
  torsion, obstruction, abnormal bleeding, virilisation) require surgical assessment, and to
  palliative/supportive care early given the generally poor prognosis, to address nutrition,
  pain, ascites, and obstruction (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).

## Deliberate omissions

No specific regimen names, response rates, survival statistics, or drug doses are given
because DeVita's text does not contain a dedicated management discussion for Krukenberg tumour
to ground them against, and dose information is out of scope for this narrative.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
NCCN Guidelines (general oncology standard where cited above).
