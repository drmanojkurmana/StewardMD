# Management: Nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL)

## Grounding note

DeVita's Hodgkin Lymphoma chapter (Chapter 66) discusses NLPHL mainly in the pathology,
biology and staging sections. It confirms that NLPHL is biologically distinct from
classical Hodgkin lymphoma (CHL), arising from a B cell lineage with the lymphocyte-predominant
(LP) "popcorn" cell retaining a full B-cell immunophenotype (CD20, CD79a, PAX5 positive),
and it notes that the pattern of spread differs from CHL: NLPHL often presents as a single
isolated node, most often in the neck, and when it does disseminate it tends to spread to
distant sites without the orderly, contiguous nodal progression seen in CHL, behaving in this
respect more like a low-grade B-cell lymphoma. The chapter's detailed treatment-approach
sections (early-stage management principles, consolidation radiotherapy, novel frontline
agents such as brentuximab vedotin, rituximab, lenalidomide, and anti-PD-1 antibodies) are
written for classical Hodgkin lymphoma and do not carry NLPHL-specific treatment
recommendations in this text. Every management claim below that is not the distribution
pattern described above is marked as general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
this disease, and no NLPHL-specific regimen, trial, or statistic is invented.

## Overview of approach

NLPHL is treated as a distinct entity from classical Hodgkin lymphoma because of its indolent
behaviour and its retained CD20 expression (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
section on this disease). DeVita's chapter does support that NLPHL commonly presents as
localised, single-node disease (often cervical) and, when it progresses, can disseminate to
distant sites without the stepwise nodal spread typical of classical disease, which is a
reason staging and follow-up strategies are considered separately from CHL. Because outcomes
are generally favourable, treatment intensity is matched to disease extent and to the
avoidance of long-term toxicity, an over-arching principle DeVita states for Hodgkin lymphoma
management broadly.

## Treatment by stage and intent

- **Localised (stage I-II), non-bulky disease**: management is commonly radiotherapy to the
  involved site alone, without systemic chemotherapy, given the indolent, often single-node
  presentation (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Localised disease where a solitary node has been completely excised**: a period of
  observation without further treatment is a recognised option in some cases (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Advanced-stage or symptomatic disease**: systemic therapy is used, drawing on
  anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody therapy alone or combined with a multi-agent chemotherapy
  backbone similar in structure to that used for indolent B-cell lymphomas or classical
  Hodgkin lymphoma, followed by consolidative radiotherapy to residual or bulky sites when
  indicated (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Relapsed or refractory disease**: retreatment with anti-CD20 antibody therapy,
  chemoimmunotherapy, or, in select transformed or heavily pretreated cases, more intensive
  approaches including autologous stem cell transplantation, mirroring the approach used in
  relapsed indolent B-cell lymphoma (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease).
- **Transformation to aggressive large B-cell lymphoma**: recognised as a possible event over
  the disease course and, when it occurs, is managed as de novo aggressive large B-cell
  lymphoma with anthracycline-based combination chemoimmunotherapy (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy and systemic therapy

- **Surgery/excisional biopsy** serves a diagnostic role first (architecture cannot be
  assessed on needle sampling) and can, in a fully excised solitary node, also serve a
  treatment role by removing all known disease before a decision on further therapy (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Radiotherapy** is the mainstay for localised disease and, in advanced-stage disease
  generally, DeVita describes its use as consolidation after chemotherapy for residual masses,
  with the benefit concentrated in patients who have not achieved a complete response, while
  those in complete remission after modern regimens derive little added benefit; the more
  PET-guided and intensive the chemotherapy, the less radiotherapy has been needed in
  practice.
- **Systemic anti-CD20 antibody therapy** is central to NLPHL specifically because the LP
  cells retain B-cell surface antigens including CD20, unlike classical Hodgkin
  Reed-Sternberg cells; DeVita's biology sections support the immunophenotypic basis for this
  targeting even though the chapter's treatment-outcome discussion of antibody-based frontline
  therapy is presented in the classical Hodgkin lymphoma context.
- **Combination chemotherapy** is reserved for higher-volume, advanced, or symptomatic
  disease rather than used routinely for localised NLPHL (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).

## Monitoring

- Because NLPHL has an indolent course with a recognised tendency to late relapse, prolonged
  clinical follow-up beyond the timeframes typically used for classical Hodgkin lymphoma is
  appropriate (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Follow-up should include periodic clinical examination and imaging as indicated, with a low
  threshold to re-biopsy any node that enlarges rapidly, becomes symptomatic, or behaves in a
  manner discordant with the known indolent pattern, given the risk of transformation to
  aggressive large B-cell lymphoma (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
  this disease).
- Attention to late treatment effects (radiotherapy field, cumulative systemic therapy
  exposure) is part of long-term survivorship care, consistent with DeVita's stated principle
  for Hodgkin lymphoma overall that long-term follow-up and avoidance of preventable late
  toxicity are core goals of treatment.

## When to refer

- Refer to a haematology/haemato-oncology specialist and to a centre with lymphoma pathology
  expertise at diagnosis, since excisional biopsy and expert histopathological review are
  needed to distinguish NLPHL from classical Hodgkin lymphoma and from mimics such as
  T-cell/histiocyte-rich large B-cell lymphoma and progressive transformation of germinal
  centres (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer promptly for re-biopsy and oncology reassessment if a node enlarges rapidly, new
  systemic symptoms develop, or imaging suggests a pattern atypical for indolent NLPHL, to
  rule out transformation (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).
- Refer to radiation oncology for involved-site radiotherapy planning in localised disease and
  to medical oncology/haematology for systemic antibody-based or combination therapy in
  advanced or relapsed disease (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).

## What DeVita does not address for this disease

DeVita's Chapter 66 text does not provide NLPHL-specific treatment protocols, regimen names,
response rates, or survival statistics; those elements above are stated as general oncology
standard rather than attributed to DeVita, and no such number or regimen has been invented
where grounding was absent.

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed.
