# Osteofibrous dysplasia — management

## Sourcing note
A targeted search of DeVita's chapter on sarcomas of bone found no dedicated
discussion of osteofibrous dysplasia (OFD) management. The name appears only
in a bibliography citation (Most, Sim, Inwards, "Osteofibrous dysplasia and
adamantinoma") and adamantinoma, the malignant lesion on the same
biological spectrum, is mentioned only once for its anatomic predilection for
the tibia and/or fibula, with no treatment detail. DeVita's bone sarcoma
chapter is organized around malignant entities (osteosarcoma, chondrosarcoma,
Ewing sarcoma, chordoma) and does not carry a section on this benign
fibro-osseous lesion. Every management claim below is therefore marked
'(general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease)'
except where explicitly noted otherwise. FLAG: needs manual sourcing against
a bone-pathology-specific reference (WHO Classification of Tumours: Soft
Tissue and Bone, or Enzinger and Weiss) for a fuller evidence base.

## Treatment approach by presentation

Osteofibrous dysplasia is a benign, self-limited lesion in most children, and
the overall management posture is watchful observation rather than early
intervention (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
disease).

- Asymptomatic or incidentally found lesions in a growing child: observation
  with serial radiographs, since many lesions stabilise or regress as the
  child approaches skeletal maturity (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).
- Anterior tibial bowing without deformity progression, fracture, or
  significant pain: continued observation, protective bracing, and activity
  modification rather than surgery (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).
- Progressive deformity, recurrent or impending pathological fracture, or
  a lesion that fails to stabilise near skeletal maturity: corrective
  surgery, typically curettage/resection with bone grafting or osteotomy for
  established deformity (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).
- Any lesion where biopsy raises concern for an epithelial (keratin-positive)
  component, i.e. OFD-like adamantinoma or frank adamantinoma: management
  shifts toward that of a low-grade to intermediate-grade bone malignancy,
  with wide surgical resection rather than observation (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). DeVita notes only
  that adamantinoma characteristically arises in the tibia and/or fibula; it
  does not detail adamantinoma's systemic or surgical treatment in the
  passage located.

## Role of surgery

Surgery in pure OFD is reserved, not first-line, because resection in young
children carries a high recurrence rate (general oncology standard, not from
DeVita's section on this disease). When surgery is undertaken, it is aimed at
correcting mechanical problems, deformity, or fracture, and is generally
deferred until the child is older or closer to skeletal maturity to reduce
recurrence risk (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
disease). Adequate tissue sampling at any biopsy or resection is important so
that an epithelial component is not missed, since this determines whether
the lesion is treated as benign OFD or as adamantinoma-spectrum disease
(general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## Role of radiotherapy and systemic therapy

There is no established role for radiotherapy or systemic (cytotoxic,
targeted, or immune) therapy in the treatment of osteofibrous dysplasia
itself (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
disease). If histology confirms transformation along the adamantinoma
spectrum, treatment intent and modality follow that of the malignant entity,
which is managed as a bone sarcoma with surgery as the mainstay; DeVita's
located passage does not specify adamantinoma's systemic regimen, so no
drug class or regimen can be attributed to DeVita here, and none should be
inferred without a dedicated adamantinoma-specific reference.

## Monitoring

- Serial clinical and radiographic follow-up through childhood and
  adolescence to track lesion behaviour, tibial bowing, and any change in
  radiographic appearance (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's
  section on this disease).
- Long-term surveillance is warranted given the lesion's relationship to
  adamantinoma; any increase in size, new pain, or radiographic change
  concerning for an epithelial component should prompt re-biopsy (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- After any surgical intervention, continued follow-up for recurrence,
  which is more common when resection is performed at a young age (general
  oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

Refer to an orthopaedic oncology service for any of the following: biopsy
planning where adamantinoma cannot be excluded, progressive tibial
deformity or fracture risk requiring surgical correction, or any histologic
finding of a keratin-positive epithelial component that would reclassify the
lesion as OFD-like or classic adamantinoma (general oncology standard, not
from DeVita's section on this disease). Musculoskeletal pathology review
with cytokeratin immunohistochemistry is warranted whenever the epithelial
question is unresolved on initial biopsy (general oncology standard, not
from DeVita's section on this disease).
