# Myxofibrosarcoma (MFS) - Management

## Disease context (grounded in DeVita's dedicated MFS section)

Myxofibrosarcoma is a common soft tissue sarcoma of elderly adults, presenting typically as a
painless mass of the limbs or trunk. On histology it is a fibroblastic tumor with pleomorphism, a
characteristic curvilinear vascular pattern, and a myxoid stromal component; the degree of myxoid
component correlates with behavior. Local recurrence is frequent (reported up to 50% in tumors with
a high myxoid fraction in older series), and in a larger regression analysis of primary high-grade
extremity/truncal MFS, tumor site tracked with recurrence risk: lower-extremity tumors recurred
locally less often than upper-extremity tumors, which in turn recurred less often than truncal
tumors. Disease-specific survival also differed by myxoid content and was additionally influenced
by tumor size and patient age. Low-grade MFS can progress to high grade with successive local
recurrences, which raises its metastatic potential over time. The most common sites of distant
spread are lung, bone, and lymph nodes. Genomically, MFS is a copy-number-driven tumor with complex,
often triploid/tetraploid karyotypes rather than a single defining fusion, and shares a genetic
spectrum with undifferentiated pleomorphic sarcoma (UPS) rather than being a fully distinct entity.

These genomic findings have generated early-phase and preclinical treatment leads specific to MFS
that clinicians should be aware of as investigational, not standard of care: RICTOR/mTOR pathway
activation (via integrin-alpha10/TRIO-RICTOR signaling) informed a phase II trial (Alliance) of an
mTOR inhibitor in patients with MFS and UPS, and preclinical work has flagged neddylation/Skp2
inhibition as a possible strategy in RB1/TP53-deficient MFS and UPS. Neither is established therapy
and neither is grounded in this document as a treatment recommendation.

## Treatment approach by stage and intent

- **Localized, resectable disease (any grade).** Wide local excision with clear margins is the
  definitive treatment (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Because
  MFS characteristically infiltrates along fascial planes well beyond the palpable mass, resection
  planning should assume disease extends further than imaging or palpation suggests (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease); this pattern is a recognized feature of MFS
  biology but the specific surgical-margin recommendation is general soft-tissue-sarcoma practice
  rather than a statement made in DeVita's MFS-specific text.
- **High-grade, large, deep, or marginally resectable tumors.** Perioperative (neoadjuvant or
  adjuvant) radiotherapy is commonly added to reduce local recurrence in this group (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Locally advanced disease at high risk of recurrence or in patients with high-grade,
  large/deep tumors.** Cytotoxic systemic therapy (an anthracycline-based regimen, alone or combined
  with an alkylating agent such as ifosfamide) may be considered as part of a multidisciplinary plan
  in selected high-risk cases (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease);
  DeVita's MFS-specific section does not itself describe a standard cytotoxic regimen for this
  disease and instead focuses on the tumor's biology and early investigational targets described
  above.
- **Metastatic disease.** For isolated, resectable pulmonary metastases, metastasectomy can be
  considered; for disseminated disease, systemic therapy and supportive/palliative measures are the
  mainstay (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). DeVita's MFS
  section establishes that lung, bone, and lymph node are the characteristic sites of spread, which
  should guide staging and surveillance imaging, but it does not lay out a specific systemic regimen
  for metastatic MFS.
- **Recurrent disease.** Because repeated local recurrence in MFS is associated with grade
  progression and rising metastatic risk (grounded in DeVita's MFS section), each recurrence should
  prompt re-staging and, where feasible, repeat pathological grading rather than assuming the
  original grade still applies (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease, though it follows directly from the grounded biology).

## Lines of therapy

DeVita's MFS-specific section does not describe a sequence of standard lines of systemic therapy for
this disease; the trial and preclinical data it presents (mTOR inhibition, Skp2/neddylation
inhibition) are investigational and specific to ongoing or early-phase research rather than an
established treatment ladder. Where cytotoxic systemic therapy is used for high-risk or advanced
disease, this follows general soft-tissue-sarcoma practice (anthracycline-based therapy as initial
systemic treatment, with other cytotoxic or targeted options considered on progression) rather than
MFS-specific evidence in the grounded source (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on
this disease).

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy

- **Surgery** is the backbone of curative treatment for localized MFS, with the extent of resection
  planned around the tumor's known infiltrative, fascial-plane-tracking growth pattern (biology
  grounded in DeVita's MFS section; the specific surgical-margin practice is general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Radiotherapy** functions mainly as an adjunct to surgery to reduce local recurrence, particularly
  in higher-risk tumors (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Systemic therapy** in MFS today is largely either (a) cytotoxic treatment used selectively in
  high-risk or advanced disease, following general soft-tissue-sarcoma practice, or (b) investigational
  targeted approaches (mTOR pathway inhibition, Skp2/neddylation inhibition) that emerged directly
  from MFS-specific genomic findings described in DeVita but remain trial-stage rather than standard
  care (grounded in DeVita's MFS section for the rationale; not grounded as an approved therapy).

## Monitoring

- Because local recurrence is common and can signal progression to a higher grade with increased
  metastatic potential (grounded in DeVita's MFS section), structured surveillance should include
  regular clinical examination of the surgical site and periodic local imaging (general oncology
  standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease, for the specific imaging modality and
  interval).
- Because lung, bone, and lymph nodes are the characteristic sites of distant spread (grounded in
  DeVita's MFS section), surveillance for metastatic disease should prioritize chest imaging, with
  further staging directed by symptoms (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease, for the specific interval and modality choice).
- At any local recurrence, repeat pathological assessment of grade is reasonable given the documented
  tendency for recurrences to be higher grade than the primary tumor (grounded in DeVita's MFS
  section for the underlying biology; the specific recommendation to re-biopsy or re-grade at
  recurrence is general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Any suspected or biopsy-confirmed myxofibrosarcoma should be referred to a specialist
  multidisciplinary soft-tissue-sarcoma team (surgical oncology, radiation oncology, medical
  oncology, and soft-tissue pathology) before definitive treatment, given the disease's infiltrative
  growth pattern and the risk of inadequate initial resection (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer promptly for re-evaluation at any local recurrence, given the documented risk of grade
  progression and increasing metastatic potential with repeated recurrence (grounded in DeVita's MFS
  section for the rationale).
- Patients with high-grade, large, or metastatic disease, or those being considered for
  investigational targeted therapy (for example within a trial of mTOR-pathway inhibition), should be
  referred to a center with access to sarcoma clinical trials (grounded in DeVita's MFS section for
  the existence of this trial-stage approach).
