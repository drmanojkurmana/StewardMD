# Oligodendroglioma (IDH-mutant, 1p/19q-codeleted) - Management

## Diagnosis first, treatment second

Management cannot be planned without molecular confirmation. Oligodendroglioma is now defined by the combination of an IDH1 or IDH2 mutation and whole-arm 1p/19q codeletion; morphology alone is not sufficient, and interobserver agreement on histology-only diagnosis has been shown to be poor even among expert neuropathologists. Tumours are graded 2 (lower grade) or 3 (higher grade); there is no grade 4 oligodendroglioma. Both grades are often treated with similar principles because they carry a broadly similar favourable prognosis relative to astrocytic tumours, although grade 3 disease is more likely to warrant upfront combined chemoradiotherapy.

## Surgery

Maximal safe resection remains the principal treatment modality, as with other diffuse gliomas. Because these tumours have a long natural history and a strong response to medical therapy, the decision about how aggressive to be with resection should take the 1p/19q status into account: in tumours that can only be partially resected safely, giving chemotherapy first can allow a treatment response and time for brain plasticity, sometimes permitting a more complete resection in a later, staged procedure. Upfront radiotherapy reduces this plasticity, which is a reason surgical and radiotherapy timing are considered together rather than in isolation.

## Radiotherapy and chemotherapy: combined modality is the backbone

Oligodendrogliomas are notably sensitive to both radiotherapy and chemotherapy, and this chemosensitivity, driven by the 1p/19q codeletion, underlies their relatively favourable outcome.

- For grade 3 (anaplastic) oligodendroglioma, postoperative combined chemoradiotherapy is recommended, using a radiotherapy approach similar to that used for other malignant gliomas.
- Randomised data in molecularly defined, 1p/19q-codeleted, IDH-mutant anaplastic oligodendroglioma show a clear and durable survival benefit for chemoradiotherapy over radiotherapy alone; upfront radiotherapy alone should not be the preferred approach in these patients.
- Long-term follow-up of that trial found that patients with codeleted tumours receiving combined chemotherapy (an alkylating-agent-based regimen) plus radiotherapy lived roughly twice as long as those given radiotherapy alone, whereas non-codeleted tumours showed no such benefit; this is the direct rationale for combined therapy in confirmed oligodendroglioma.
- There is no level 1 evidence to support chemotherapy alone as a substitute for radiotherapy in newly diagnosed disease. Although chemotherapy-alone strategies are sometimes used in practice, an interim safety analysis of a large intergroup trial requiring radiotherapy be given in this population found substantially more progression with chemotherapy alone than with radiotherapy, and chemotherapy-only approaches should be regarded as unproven outside a trial setting.
- Adjuvant treatment roughly doubles progression-free survival, but because survival is already long (years to decades), the long-term toxicity of treatment, particularly cognitive effects of radiotherapy, must be weighed carefully against benefit.
- Watchful waiting after resection may be considered in select lower-risk patients; features associated with a less favourable course include CDKN2A/B homozygous loss, incomplete resection, large tumour size, and tumour crossing the midline.
- For lower-grade (grade 2) disease more broadly, early postoperative radiotherapy improves progression-free survival compared with deferring radiotherapy to progression, although this has not translated into a clear overall survival difference in trial follow-up; seizure control at one year was also better with early radiotherapy. Dose escalation beyond standard fractionation regimens has not been shown to add benefit.

## Lines of therapy at recurrence

- After progression following radiotherapy, a majority of patients with anaplastic oligodendroglioma respond to chemotherapy, with response rates in the range of roughly half to two-thirds in prospective series.
- In patients who progress on an alkylating combination regimen, switching to temozolomide (an oral alkylating agent) has produced meaningful response rates in reported series.
- There is no evidence that the order in which an alkylating combination regimen and temozolomide are used changes overall efficacy; because temozolomide lacks the cumulative bone marrow suppression seen with prolonged multi-agent alkylating regimens, it is a reasonable initial choice at first recurrence for that tolerability reason.
- Targeted approaches directed at mutant IDH1, and IDH1-directed vaccine strategies, have been evaluated in clinical trials in IDH-mutant gliomas generally; specific outcome data for oligodendroglioma were not found in the grounded material, so this should be framed as investigational rather than standard.

## Monitoring

Surveillance relies on periodic contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain to detect progression or transformation to a higher grade, since oligodendrogliomas can progress in tempo over a long natural history and new nodular enhancement is a marker of higher-grade transformation. Seizure control with antiseizure medication is an ongoing management need, since seizures are the most common presenting feature and can persist or recur independent of tumour status (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). Formal neurocognitive assessment over time is reasonable given the long survival window and the cognitive burden that can come from both the tumour itself and from treatment, in particular radiotherapy.

## When to refer

- Any new or progressive focal neurological deficit, escalating seizure frequency or status epilepticus, or signs of raised intracranial pressure warrants urgent neuro-oncology or neurosurgical referral (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- New nodular enhancement on surveillance imaging, suggesting higher-grade transformation, should prompt re-referral for consideration of re-resection, further chemotherapy, or re-irradiation.
- All patients with a possible oligodendroglioma should be managed within a multidisciplinary neuro-oncology framework, given the specialised molecular diagnostics and combined-modality treatment decisions involved, and with supportive and palliative input introduced over time since the disease remains eventually progressive and incurable despite its relatively favourable course.
