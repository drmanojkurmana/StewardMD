# MUTYH-associated polyposis (MAP) - Management

## Grounding note

DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles and Practice of Oncology, 12th ed. has no dedicated
chapter section on MUTYH-associated polyposis. The text mentions MAP only in passing, inside other
chapters: the Small Bowel Cancer chapter's risk-factor list, the Gastric Cancer chapter's list of familial
syndromes with elevated gastric-cancer risk, and a single note in the SCLC chapter about germline MUTYH
variants and platinum sensitivity in an unrelated lung-cancer population. There is no DeVita-authored
surveillance schedule, surgical threshold, or systemic-therapy discussion specific to MAP. The narrative
below therefore leans heavily on general oncology/hereditary-colorectal-cancer standards (marked
inline) and is FLAGGED for manual sourcing against a genetics-focused reference (e.g. NCCN
Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment: Colorectal, or GeneReviews) before clinician sign-off.

## Overview and intent of management

MAP is not treated with cytotoxic or targeted cancer therapy in the way an established malignancy is;
it is a hereditary polyposis syndrome, and "management" means lifelong cancer-risk reduction through
surveillance, endoscopic polyp clearance, and, when polyp burden or cancer supervenes, surgery. DeVita
records that MAP is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern and carries a lifetime colorectal cancer
risk in the range of roughly 43 to 100 percent when surveillance is not undertaken in a timely fashion,
along with an approximately 4 percent lifetime risk of duodenal cancer (DeVita, Small Bowel Cancer
chapter). These figures are the rationale for early, structured surveillance rather than a "watch and
wait" approach.

## Colorectal management by polyp burden

- Attenuated polyposis with a manageable number of adenomas: colonoscopic surveillance with
  polypectomy to clear adenomas as they arise is the mainstay (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease). This is consistent with MAP's typical phenotype of an attenuated
  polyp count rather than the florid polyposis of classic familial adenomatous polyposis.
- Polyp burden that cannot be reliably cleared endoscopically, or a confirmed colorectal cancer:
  risk-reducing colectomy or proctocolectomy is considered (general oncology standard, not from
  DeVita's section on this disease). The choice between segmental colectomy, subtotal colectomy, and
  proctocolectomy with or without ileal pouch reconstruction is individualised to polyp distribution,
  rectal sparing, age, and patient preference, mirroring surgical decision-making in other polyposis
  syndromes (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Established colorectal cancer arising in a MAP patient is staged and treated according to standard
  colorectal cancer principles for that stage (surgical resection with or without adjuvant chemotherapy
  for node-positive or high-risk disease) (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease). DeVita does not describe a MAP-specific systemic therapy regimen, and none should be
  inferred; the underlying MUTYH defect does not, on the material reviewed, change first-line colorectal
  cancer drug selection.

## Extracolonic (duodenal and gastric) surveillance

- Upper gastrointestinal endoscopy with duodenal examination is warranted because of the increased
  duodenal cancer risk noted above (DeVita, Small Bowel Cancer chapter, risk figure only; endoscopic
  interval and technique are general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Any advanced-histology duodenal adenoma found on surveillance should prompt closer endoscopic
  follow-up or endoscopic/surgical resection depending on size and grade (general oncology standard,
  not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- MUTYH-associated polyposis is separately listed by DeVita among the familial syndromes associated
  with increased gastric cancer risk, alongside familial adenomatous polyposis, Lynch syndrome, Cowden
  syndrome, juvenile polyposis, Li-Fraumeni syndrome, and Peutz-Jeghers syndrome (DeVita, Gastric
  Cancer chapter). DeVita does not specify a gastric surveillance protocol for MAP itself in that passage,
  so any gastric screening interval offered to patients should be sourced from a genetics/GI guideline
  rather than presented as DeVita-derived.

## Lines of therapy

There are no "lines of therapy" in the systemic-oncology sense for MAP as a syndrome; the closest
analogue is an escalation ladder: surveillance and polypectomy as first-line management, escalating to
segmental or extended colectomy for polyp burden that outstrips endoscopic control, and to
standard-of-stage cancer treatment if a colorectal, duodenal, or other MAP-associated cancer develops
(general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease). One incidental, disease-unrelated
DeVita finding worth flagging for context (not for MAP management): in a small-cell lung cancer
population, DeVita notes that germline MUTYH variants were the most common pathogenic germline
variant identified and were associated with greater platinum sensitivity in that SCLC cohort (DeVita,
SCLC chapter). This describes a biomarker association in lung cancer patients who happen to carry
MUTYH variants; it is not evidence for, and should not be extrapolated to, any systemic therapy
recommendation for MAP-associated colorectal or duodenal cancer.

## Monitoring

- Colonoscopy at intervals appropriate to prior polyp findings, continued for life given persistent risk
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Upper endoscopy with duodenal assessment on a periodic schedule once biallelic MUTYH variants are
  confirmed (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease; DeVita supports only
  the underlying duodenal cancer risk figure).
- Surveillance for iron-deficiency anaemia and occult gastrointestinal blood loss, since either can signal
  a missed lesion (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Refer for germline genetic testing and counselling whenever attenuated adenomatous polyposis is
  identified, particularly when inheritance does not fit a dominant pattern, since MAP is recessive and
  both parents warrant testing (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- Refer to a hereditary colorectal cancer or clinical genetics multidisciplinary service for lifelong
  surveillance coordination, cascade testing of siblings, and reproductive counselling once biallelic
  pathogenic MUTYH variants are confirmed (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this
  disease).
- Refer to colorectal surgery when polyp burden becomes endoscopically unmanageable or a cancer is
  found, and to medical oncology for standard-of-stage treatment if invasive cancer is diagnosed
  (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

Sources: DeVita, Hellman, and Rosenberg's Cancer: Principles & Practice of Oncology, 12th ed. NCCN
Guidelines: Genetic/Familial High-Risk Assessment - Colorectal.
