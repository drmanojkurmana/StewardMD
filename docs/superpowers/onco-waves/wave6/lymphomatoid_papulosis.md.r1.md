## 1. SAFETY — pass
No unsafe, misleading, or absolute directives. The narrative correctly frames LyP as an indolent,
self-regressing, clonal-but-nonmalignant CD30+ LPD, and its central safety messages are all correct
and protective:
- Intent framed as symptom control + lymphoma surveillance, NOT cure of an aggressive malignancy.
- Explicitly warns AGAINST aggressive multiagent chemotherapy for this indolent condition — this is
  the key harm-avoidance statement and it is correct.
- Directs re-biopsy of any lesion that fails to regress / enlarges, and escalation for
  lymphadenopathy or systemic symptoms — appropriate red-flag safety netting.
- Distinguishes LyP from C-ALCL and instructs managing/staging as C-ALCL if a lesion behaves that
  way (persistent, enlarging). Prevents under-treating a genuinely malignant mimic.

## 2. GROUNDING — pass
Treatment claims are consistent with DeVita/NCCN standard of care for primary cutaneous CD30+ LPD:
- Observation for limited/asymptomatic disease; low-dose methotrexate and PUVA for extensive/
  symptomatic disease — established first/second-line.
- Brentuximab vedotin for refractory CD30+ disease — the phase II description (combined LyP/C-ALCL/
  CD30+ MF cohort, ~two-thirds ORR, ~one-third CR, response independent of CD30 level) matches the
  published trial and is honestly hedged as a mixed-entity cohort, not LyP-specific efficacy.
- Bexarotene / oral retinoids and topical steroids/retinoids for localized disease — standard.
No fabricated or outdated regimen. Attribution discipline is notably strong: every claim NOT in
DeVita's LyP section (topicals, oral retinoids, photographic mapping) is explicitly relabelled as
"general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease." This directly satisfies the
mis-attribution guard — DeVita is not cited for anything the draft itself flags as outside DeVita.
Epidemiologic figures (~4% in the 118-case series, up to ~20% associated lymphoma, 100% 5-yr
survival per WHO-EORTC, TCR clonality up to 60%) are plausible, chapter-attributed, and consistent
with the literature.

## 3. DOSE-FREE — pass
No numeric dose leaked. No mg, mg/m2, AUC, or numbered schedule. "Low-dose methotrexate" is a
qualitative descriptor, not a dose. All numerics present are epidemiology/outcomes or a trial phase
label (percentages, case counts, "twelve weeks", "phase II", "5-year", "100%") — none are dosing.

## 4. SCOPE — pass
Consistently decision-support, not directive: "an accepted approach", "can be used", "reserved for",
"warranted". Refer/escalate guidance is advisory. No overstepping definitive-management language.

## Advisory (non-blocking)
- "typically disappear within about twelve weeks" — lesion resolution is usually quoted 3–12 weeks;
  the upper-bound phrasing is fine but could read as a firm ceiling. Optional soften.
- Consider a one-line note that low-dose methotrexate/PUVA suppress but do not cure (relapse on
  withdrawal is expected) so clinicians don't over-read durable control. Non-blocking.
