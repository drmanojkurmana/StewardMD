# Li-Fraumeni Syndrome: Management

Li-Fraumeni syndrome is a rare, highly penetrant hereditary cancer predisposition phenotype caused by germline mutations in TP53, the gene encoding the p53 tumor suppressor. Roughly 80 percent of affected patients develop a cancer by age 45, and in about 36 percent of patients the index tumor is a soft tissue or bone sarcoma of diverse histology. Li-Fraumeni is not a single tumor type but a lifelong multi-organ cancer risk state, so its "management" is best understood as two linked tracks: treating whichever cancer the mutation carrier develops, and lifelong surveillance to catch subsequent primaries early. There is no disease-specific systemic therapy for "Li-Fraumeni syndrome" itself; treatment is dictated by the organ site and histology of each tumor that arises.

## Cancers associated with the syndrome

Tumors reported in TP53 germline carriers, and referenced in the disease-site chapters, include:
- Breast cancer, often HER2-positive, arising at a young age
- Osteosarcoma and other primary bone sarcomas
- Soft tissue sarcomas of diverse histology
- Malignant gliomas and choroid plexus carcinoma
- Adrenocortical carcinoma (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease)
- Other cancers at younger-than-expected ages, consistent with a general hereditary cancer predisposition pattern

## Treatment approach by tumor type

There is no dedicated systemic regimen for Li-Fraumeni syndrome; each cancer that a carrier develops is treated according to standard site-specific protocols for that histology and stage (surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy as indicated for that tumor), with two important modifications driven by the underlying TP53 mutation:

- **Radiotherapy avoidance where possible.** In breast cancer, radiotherapy is avoided when possible in patients who carry the p53 mutation of Li-Fraumeni syndrome, because the excess risk of a second malignancy induced by radiotherapy may exceed 20 percent, albeit based on very limited data. The same principle of avoiding DNA-damaging radiotherapy exposure is described for sarcoma patients with Li-Fraumeni syndrome (and separately for RB1 germline carriers), because of the elevated risk of a radiation-induced second cancer.
- **Consider non-radiotherapy local control strategies.** Because radiotherapy carries this elevated second-malignancy risk in TP53 carriers, surgical options for local control (for example, mastectomy rather than breast-conserving therapy plus radiotherapy) warrant particular consideration, and this trade-off should be discussed explicitly with the patient (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).
- **Systemic therapy follows the treated cancer's own standard of care.** Chemotherapy, targeted therapy, or hormonal therapy choices are driven by the tumor being treated (for example, HER2-directed therapy for HER2-positive breast cancer), not by the TP53 mutation status itself; DeVita's disease-site chapters do not describe a TP53-specific systemic regimen.

Lines of therapy for any individual cancer in a Li-Fraumeni patient therefore mirror the lines of therapy for that cancer in the general population; sequencing (surgery, then adjuvant or neoadjuvant systemic therapy, with radiotherapy minimized or avoided when feasible) should follow the site-specific standard while explicitly weighting against radiotherapy exposure.

## Role of surgery, radiotherapy, and systemic therapy

- **Surgery** is the primary local treatment modality of choice across tumor types in Li-Fraumeni patients, since it does not carry the mutagenic second-cancer risk that radiotherapy does.
- **Radiotherapy** should be minimized or avoided when a reasonable alternative exists, given the disproportionate second-malignancy risk in TP53 carriers described above; when radiotherapy cannot be avoided (for example, unresectable disease), the decision should be individualized and clearly discussed with the patient.
- **Systemic therapy** (chemotherapy, targeted agents, hormonal therapy) is selected and sequenced according to the specific cancer diagnosed, not according to TP53 status; DeVita does not describe a p53-targeted systemic agent or class specific to this syndrome.

## Screening and surveillance

Because Li-Fraumeni carriers face lifelong, multi-organ cancer risk, structured surveillance is central to management:
- Whole-body MRI-based sarcoma screening recommendations exist specifically for Li-Fraumeni syndrome, reflecting the elevated risk of bone and soft tissue sarcoma in this population.
- Annual breast MRI (in addition to mammography) is recommended for women with Li-Fraumeni syndrome and their first-degree relatives, based on expert opinion, given the elevated and early-onset breast cancer risk in this group.
- Genetic counseling and consideration of germline TP53 testing should be offered when personal or family cancer history is suggestive (young-onset breast cancer, multiple primaries, sarcomas, or a family history consistent with Li-Fraumeni syndrome), as part of broader hereditary cancer risk assessment.
- Because ionizing radiation itself carries added carcinogenic risk in TP53 carriers, imaging-based surveillance protocols should favor MRI over CT or other ionizing-radiation-based studies where feasible (general oncology standard, not from DeVita's section on this disease).

## When to refer

- Refer to clinical genetics/genetic counseling for germline TP53 testing and cascade testing of first-degree relatives whenever Li-Fraumeni syndrome is suspected or confirmed, and before undertaking genetic testing so the implications for the patient and family can be discussed.
- Refer to a dedicated hereditary cancer surveillance program (breast MRI screening, whole-body MRI sarcoma screening) once a germline TP53 mutation is confirmed.
- Refer to the relevant disease-site oncology specialist (breast, orthopedic oncology/sarcoma, neuro-oncology, etc.) for treatment of any specific tumor that develops, with the treating team informed of the TP53 mutation status so that radiotherapy use is reconsidered.
- Multidisciplinary discussion is warranted when radiotherapy appears to be the only feasible local control option, to weigh oncologic benefit against the documented excess second-malignancy risk in this population.

## What DeVita's text does not specify

DeVita's chapters describe Li-Fraumeni syndrome primarily in the context of risk, screening, and radiotherapy-avoidance across several tumor sites (breast, sarcoma/bone, CNS, choroid plexus tumors), but do not lay out a syndrome-specific systemic drug regimen, staging system, or line-of-therapy sequence, because treatment is site-and-histology driven rather than syndrome-driven. Any specific chemotherapy regimen, drug class, or numeric outcome statistic for a given Li-Fraumeni-associated cancer has therefore been omitted here rather than inferred, since that content belongs to the specific cancer's own disease chapter and structured protocol templates, not to this syndrome-level summary.
