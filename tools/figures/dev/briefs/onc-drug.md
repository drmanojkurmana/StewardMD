
ANTICANCER DRUG MECHANISM SCENE (extra rules for this queue). This scene explains ONE drug: how it kills the tumour and, equally, why its side effects happen. Students must understand it at a glance.
- Registry entry (`<scene>.reg.json`): `"drugs": {"<generic lowercase>": "d=1"}` so the Drugs database and OncQis show a "How it works" card for that generic (the key is the drug's generic name, one word where the generic is one word; for 5-fluorouracil use "fluorouracil" and also "5-fluorouracil"; for L-asparaginase use "asparaginase" and "pegaspargase"; for ATRA use "tretinoin"; for arsenic trioxide use "arsenic trioxide"). The id must be `<drug>-mechanism-adverse-effects` as given. Terms must be specific (drug generic and common brand names, not bare common words).
- Model: `level` slider = course of treatment (early, middle, late, cumulative) for toxicities that build up, or the sensitivity of the tumour where that fits better; treatment select = no treatment, the drug, and then one or two options that show the key protective or rescue measure for that drug's worst toxicity (for example dexrazoxane for doxorubicin cardiotoxicity, mesna and hydration for cyclophosphamide cystitis, leucovorin for methotrexate, hydration and magnesium for cisplatin, atropine and loperamide for irinotecan diarrhoea, dexamethasone for ATRA differentiation syndrome, steroids for immune adverse events, premedication for paclitaxel hypersensitivity). "With" = the most informative drug-plus-protection state.
- Stage: left half the TARGET (tumour cell with the actual molecular target drawn and named: DNA, topoisomerase, tubulin, DHFR, thymidylate synthase, ribonucleotide reductase, BCR-ABL, HER2, CD20, PD-1, VEGF, EGFR, PARP, proteasome, asparagine, PML-RARa as appropriate) showing the drug acting step by step with animated particles; right half the TOXICITY ORGANS (use organ(...) art: heart, lung, kidney, liver, brain, bowel, bladder, skin, marrow-as-bone where relevant) showing the mechanism of each main side effect animated (for example anthracycline redox cycling making radicals in heart mitochondria, acrolein in the bladder, bleomycin in lung with low hydrolase, tubulin damage in axons). Each toxic organ has a label whose hover/tap text states the MECHANISM of that side effect in one plain sentence, not just its name.
- Gauges panel: tumour kill, then one gauge per main side effect (for example cardiotoxicity, myelosuppression, neuropathy), so a student sees benefit and harm change together with the course slider and the protective option.
- Caption in the stage changes with state: what the drug does now and what harm builds. Keep at most 8 labels, short, easy for a first-year student.
- Facts must be standard teaching. No doses, numbers, thresholds or cumulative limits. Flag AI-assisted for review in the sources footer.
